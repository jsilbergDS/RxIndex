L66: ## I. OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF FINDINGS AND RECOMMENDATIONS
L67: Clinical data from an ongoing, open-label trial (Study BLU-667-1101, NCT03037385) were submitted to the Agency in support of a new drug application (NDA 213721) for pralsetinib for the above proposed indication. Three clinical investigators were selected for site inspections: Drs. Justin Gainor (Site 2603), Christina Baik (Site 2610), and Gregory Kalemkerian (Site 2611). There were no significant inspectional findings for Drs. Christina Baik (Site 2610) and Gregory Kalemkerian (Site 2611).
L68: Dr. Justin Gainor's site did not report the deaths of two study subjects (Subjects [REDACTED](b)(6) and [REDACTED](b)(6)) to the Sponsor and subsequently the deaths were not included in the datasets submitted with the NDA. Both subjects had died prior to the database lock date, and both were no longer in the study at the time of death: Subject [REDACTED](b)(6) had been withdrawn for progressive disease, and Subject [REDACTED](b)(6) had withdrawn due to inability to tolerate the study drug and interest in a different treatment. In the case of Subject [REDACTED](b)(6) the overall
L69: survival (OS) was reported as 16.5 months in the submission. The correct OS based on the date of death would have been 11.5 months. In the case of Subject [REDACTED](b)(6) the reported OS was correctly reported, as the subject was censured on the date of death (listed as date last known alive), even though the death was not reported by the site.
L70: At the time of both subjects' deaths, Protocol Amendment 7 (Amendment date 12/12/2018) was in effect at the site. According to Protocol Amendment 7, follow-up after the end of treatment was as follows: "patients without documented progressive disease will be followed for disease assessment approximately every 3 months until documentation of progressive disease, initiation of another antineoplastic therapy, death, or closure of the study." For Subject [REDACTED](b)(6) there was no protocol requirement to acquire or report follow-up information due to the presence of progressive disease. Subject [REDACTED](b)(6) may have been started on an alternate antineoplastic therapy, and thus may also not have required follow-up or the reporting of follow-up information according to the protocol.
L71: On 7/3/2019, Protocol Amendment 9 came into effect for Study BLU-667-1101. Protocol Amendment 9 removed the previous wording regarding follow-up after end of treatment and replaced it with: "All patients will be contacted every 3 months for PFS and overall survival."
L72: Because of the absence of instructions in the protocol prior to Protocol Amendment 9 regarding follow-up of subjects who had progressive disease or who withdrew to participate in an alternate treatment, there may be additional instances of unreported subject deaths at uninspected clinical sites for subjects who ended treatment prior to [REDACTED](b)(6).
L73: The second inspection finding at Site [REDACTED](b)(6) was that two unscheduled computed tomography (CT) assessments were not sent to Central Radiology until after the data lock date, resulting in the absence of blinded independent central radiology (BICR) assessments for two imaging studies for two study subjects. The unscheduled CT scan performed on [REDACTED](b)(6) for Subject [REDACTED](b)(6) was sent to Central Radiology over a year after it was performed; the unscheduled CT scan performed on [REDACTED](b)(6) for Subject [REDACTED](b)(6) was sent to Central Radiology 7 months after it was performed. In both cases, the CT scans were interpreted as progressive disease (PD) by the local assessment. The study protocol does not provide instruction for the handling of unscheduled imaging assessments and thus it is possible that there may be additional instances of failure or delay of transmission of unscheduled imaging assessments to Central Radiology at other study sites. As unscheduled imaging may occur due to the development or worsening of symptoms, the failure to include these imaging studies in the study data could affect efficacy outcomes in favor of the study drug in this single-arm trial.
L74: ## Recommendations:
L75: Based on the results of Dr. Justin Gainor's inspection, we suggest that you consider conducting an analysis of submitted datasets to identify instances of missing BICR assessments at time points for which local RECIST assessments were done for all study subjects with confirmed response. Missing BICR assessments for subjects with confirmed response could potentially impact the duration of response endpoint described in the labeling, and a BICR assessment of any such missing data may be needed.
L76: The missing date of death information for two subjects at Site 2603 and potentially at other uninspected sites, does not appear to impact safety or efficacy claims made in the current proposed label. However, the findings may have the potential to affect future survival analyses based on subjects who ended treatment prior to Protocol Amendment 9.
L77: ## II. BACKGROUND
L78: Study BLU-667-1101, entitled: "A Phase 1/2 Study of the Highly Selective RET Inhibitor, BLU667 in Patients with Thyroid Cancer, Non-Small Cell Lung Cancer (NSCLC) and Other Advanced Solid Tumors," is an ongoing, first-in-human, open-label clinical trial designed to evaluate the safety, tolerability, pharmacokinetics, and preliminary antineoplastic activity of pralsetinib in subjects with rearranged during transfection (RET)-fusion NSCLC and other advanced solid tumors. A portion of the data from Study BLU-667-1101 were submitted to the Agency in support of an original new drug application (NDA 213721) for pralsetinib for the treatment of NSCLC.
L79: Pralsetinib is an orally-administered selective RET inhibitor. RET is a receptor tyrosine kinase normally expressed in several tissue types. RET-fusions occur as a result of chromosomal rearrangement and are thought to drive tumor growth and proliferation. RET-fusions have been found in multiple tumor types including NSCLC.
L80: Study BLU-667-1101 is a phase 1 followed by phase 2 study. The primary objective of phase 1 is to determine the maximum tolerated dose and the safety and tolerability of pralsetinib in subjects with unresectable, RET-altered solid tumors. The primary objective of phase 2 is to determine the overall response rate (ORR) in subjects with different tumor types, and to further define the safety and tolerability of the study drug.
L81: Eligible subjects were required to be \ge 18 years of age. In phase 2, subjects were additionally required to have measurable disease per RECIST v1.1 and RET-fusion mutation (excluding synonymous, frameshift, and nonsense mutations) solid tumor, as determined by local testing of tumor or circulating tumor nucleic acid in blood. Phase 2 included 7 enrollment groups based on disease type and prior therapy status. The NSCLC phase 2 groups were further required to have advanced disease and were placed into one of two cohorts based on whether they had been previously treated with a platinum-based chemotherapy.
L82: The primary efficacy endpoint for Phase 2 of the study is the overall response rate (ORR) by blinded independent central review (BICR) of CT and/or MRI using RECIST v1.1 criteria. ORR was defined as the proportion of subjects with confirmed best overall response (BOR) of complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) for \ge 2 assessments with \ge 28 days apart and no progressive disease (PD) in-between. Secondary efficacy endpoints include duration of response (DOR), clinical benefit rate (CBR), disease control rate (DCR), progression free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS).
L83: All subjects in Phase 2 were scheduled to receive pralsetinib 400 mg QD on a continuous schedule, with every 28 days as a complete cycle. Subjects continued receiving pralsetinib until progression of disease, intolerance, noncompliance, withdrawal of consent, death, or closure of the study by the Sponsor. Tumor assessments for the primary endpoint were to be performed with CT with IV contrast of the chest, and CT or MRI with IV contrast of the brain, abdomen and pelvis at baseline (Day -28 to Day -1), on D1 of every odd numbered cycle starting with C3 \pm 4 days, and at end of treatment (EOT).
L84: The efficacy population for the submitted clinical study report (CSR) consists of subjects with NSCLC enrolled in either Phase 1 or Phase 2 who received a starting dose of 400 mg pralsetinib per day with first dose prior to [REDACTED](b) (6) From [REDACTED](b) (6) through [REDACTED](b) (6), the study enrolled 132 NSCLC patients who received a pralsetinib starting dose of 400 mg QD in Phases 1 and 2. Safety results for all subjects who received pralsetinib are included in the CSR. The submission includes data from 53 clinical investigator sites, including 22 in Europe, 17 in the USA, and 14 in Asia. In the efficacy population, 49% of the subjects were enrolled in Europe, 44% in the USA, and 39% in Asia. The data cutoff date for the analyses reported in this NDA was November 18, 2019.
L85: Three participating clinical investigators (CI) were selected for inspection: Drs. Justin Gainor (Site 2603), Christina Baik (Site 2610), and Gregory Kalemkerian (Site 2611). The selected sites enrolled a relatively high number of subjects among the study sites and had relatively higher efficacy of the primary endpoint (ORR) than the reported ORR for the study.(b) (6)
