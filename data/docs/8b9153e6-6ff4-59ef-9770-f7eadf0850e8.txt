L367: ## 1. Background
L368: This memorandum evaluates two new self-administration studies in support of NDA 210,493 for the drug product Akynzeo for Injection, which is under review in the Division of Gastroenterology and Inborn Errors Products (DGIEP). Akynzeo is a combination product containing 235 mg of fosnetupitant (a NK1 antagonist) and 0.25 milligrams of palonosetron (a 5HT3 antagonist). Akynzeo for Injection is proposed for the prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with initial and repeat courses of highly emetogenic cancer chemotherapy. Palonosetron is thought to prevent nausea and vomiting during the acute phase and fosnetupitant is thought to prevent nausea and vomiting during both the acute and delayed phase after cancer chemotherapy.
L369: This product is formulated as a lyophilized powder contained in a vial that is reconstituted in 50 ml of 5% Dextrose Injection, USP or 0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection, USP. Dosing recommendations propose that the reconstituted solution of Akynzeo be infused intravenously over a 30-minute period immediately prior to the initiation of chemotherapy. Dexamethasone (12 mg) should also be administered 30 minutes prior to chemotherapy, with additional dosing of 8 mg dexamethasone once daily on Days 2 to 4.
L370: A similar drug product, Akynzeo capsules (NDA 205,718), was approved by FDA on October 10, 2014. Akynzeo capsules are formulated as a fixed combination of netupitant (a NK-1 antagonist), and palonosetron, and is similarly indicated for the prevention of acute and delayed nausea and vomiting associated with initial and repeat courses of moderately and highly emetogenic cancer chemotherapy. (Notably, the fosnetupitant in Akynzeo for Injection is a prodrug for netupitant). CSS reviewed this NDA and concluded from the preclinical and clinical abuse-related data that the drug combination did not have abuse potential and should not be recommended for scheduling under the Controlled Substances Act. The NDA for Akynzeo for Injection is a 505(b)1 submission.
L371: At a pre-IND meeting in 2012, CSS informed the Sponsor that, “It is not necessary to conduct abuse potential studies with fosnetupitant, as long as you submit data establishing that the fosnetupitant rapidly converts to the CNS-active metabolite, netupitant; that netupitant is responsible for the drug’s activity; and that the pro-drug makes no contribution to the activity of the drug product.” The Sponsor subsequently submitted pharmacokinetic information showing that fosnetupitant converts completely to netupitant in rats, dogs, and monkeys. They also provided information showing that fosnetupitant is not CNS-active because it cannot cross the blood-brain barrier (although its metabolite, netupitant, does penetrate the CNS).
L372: In an IND meeting in 2014, CSS informed the Sponsor that additional self-administration studies with fosnetupitant alone or with the combination of fosnetupitant + palonosetron would not be necessary. However, the Sponsor conducted these studies anyway and submitted them in the current NDA.
L373: ## 2. Conclusions
L374: In two self-administration studies in baboons that were conducted with netupitant (the active metabolite of fosnetupitant) and with netupitant + palonosetron, neither drug treatment produced self-administration that was statistically significantly different than that produced by placebo. This suggests that these drug treatments do not produce rewarding effects.
L375: The clinical review by Dr. Charles Line of DGIEP confirms that there were no abuse-related AEs observed during clinical studies with intravenous fosnetupitant + palonosetron.
L376: ## 3. Recommendations
L377: (b) (4)
L378: the lack of abuse-related signals from netupitant alone and from netupitant + palonosetron.
L379: The drug product Akynzeo for Injection should not be recommended for control under the CSA, because the results from the preclinical abuse potential studies show that the drug combination lacks abuse potential. This is similar to the lack of abuse potential of Akynzeo capsules (netupitant + palonosetron), which was also not recommended for CSA scheduling when it was approved in 2014.
L380: ## 4. Discussion
L381: ## Self-Administration Study (Studies #NETU-13-73 and NETU-13-74)
L382: The Sponsor conducted a self-administration drug study in which netupitant was tested for its ability to induce self-administration as a measure of the rewarding property of the drug.
L383: ## *Study Design*
L384: Male olive baboons (n = 3) were trained to self-administer cocaine (0.32 mg/kg/infusion, i.v.) during 24 hour continuous access sessions. After stable cocaine self-administration was produced using a fixed ratio (FR) 100 or FR 160 with a 3 hour timeout between injections, which allowed up to 8 injections per day. Animals self-administered cocaine 6 to 8 times per day during training.
L385: Animals were allowed access to netupitant (0.6, 1.0, 1.3, 1.8 mg/kg, i.v.), netupitant + palonosetron (0.6 + 0.01 mg/kg, i.v.) or vehicle (either saline or Captisol vehicle). The test doses and testing times were selected based on the results of the pharmacokinetics/
L386: pharmacodynamics study. This is the first study in which netupitant alone has been tested in self-administration.
L387: Cocaine, vehicle and each fixed-dose netupitant, or the combination of netupitant + palonosetron were available sequentially for self-administration 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, for a total of 15 contiguous days. The last 5 days of each period of access to netupitant alone, netupitant + palonosetron or saline/vehicle was used to characterize the reinforcing effects of the treatment.
L388: ## *Results*
L389: Cocaine was self-administered 7 to 8 times in 24 hours, which was significantly higher than that shown for saline or the Captisol-based vehicle (0 to 3.0 times in 24 hours). These data validate the study.
L390: The number of self-administrations of netupitant alone or netupitant + palonosetron (0.4 to 1.8 times in 24 hours) was similar to or less than that produced by saline/vehicle alone (0 to 3.0 times) at all doses of the drug treatments that were tested.
L391: ## *Conclusion*
L392: Netupitant alone or netupitant + palonosetron was not self-administered, suggesting these treatments do not appear to have rewarding properties. This outcome is similar to the outcome seen in the previous self-administration studies conducted with netupitant + palonosetron submitted for Akynzeo capsules under NDA 205,718.
L393: ## Abuse-Related Adverse Events
L394: The clinical review by Dr. Charles Line of DGIEP (DARRTS, 12/15/17) confirms that there were no abuse-related AEs observed during clinical studies with intravenous fosnetupitant + palonosetron.

L466: ## Netupitant
L467: In animal reproduction studies with netupitant, no effects on embryo-fetal development were observed following daily oral administration in pregnant rats during the period of organogenesis at doses up to 3.7 times the human AUC (area under the plasma concentration-time curve) at the recommended single human dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy. L468: ## Fosnetupitant
L469: In animal reproduction studies with fosnetupitant, delayed ossification of pubis occurred after intravenous administration in rats during the period of organogenesis at a dose 3 times the human AUC for netupitant (fosnetupitant is rapidly distributed and metabolically eliminated to form netupitant) at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy. L525: ## Risk Summary
L526: Limited available data with AKYNZEO use in pregnant women are insufficient to inform a drug associated risk of adverse developmental outcomes.
L527: In animal reproduction studies with netupitant, no effects on embryo-fetal development were observed following daily oral administration in pregnant rats during the period of organogenesis at doses up to 3.7 times the human AUC (area under the plasma concentration-time curve) at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy. However, a dose-dependent increase in adverse effects on embryo-fetal development was observed following daily oral administration of netupitant in pregnant rabbits during the period of organogenesis with doses at least 0.2 times the human AUC at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy. Daily oral administration of netupitant in rats up to 3.7 times the human AUC at the recommended dose during organogenesis through lactation produced no adverse effects in the offspring.
L528: In animal reproduction studies with fosnetupitant, delayed ossification of pubis occurred after intravenous administration in rats during the period of organogenesis at a dose 3 times the human AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy. In pregnant rabbits, an increase in resorptions was observed with daily
L529: intravenous administration of fosnetupitant during the period of organogenesis at doses up to 9 times the human AUC for fosnetupitant and 0.4 times the human AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy. Daily intravenous administration of fosnetupitant (3 times the human AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy) in rats during organogenesis through lactation produced lower bodyweight in offspring at birth through maturation, and delayed physical development.
L530: In animal reproduction studies with palonosetron, no effects on embryo-fetal development were observed following oral administration during the period of organogenesis at doses up to 921 and 1841 times the recommended oral dose in rats and rabbits, respectively (see *Data*).

L534: ## *Animal Data*
L535: ## Netupitant
L536: Daily oral administration of up to 30 mg/kg netupitant in rats (3.7 times the human AUC at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy) during the period of organogenesis produced no effects on embryo-fetal development. However, an increased incidence of external and skeletal abnormalities in rabbit fetuses was observed following daily oral administration of netupitant in rabbits at 10 mg/kg/day and higher (0.2 times the human AUC at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy) during the period of organogenesis. These abnormalities included positional abnormalities in the limbs and paws, and fused sternebrae. Reduction in fetal rabbit weight occurred at 30 mg/kg/day. Maternal toxicity in rabbits (i.e., loss of bodyweight during the treatment period) was also observed at 30 mg/kg/day. Daily oral administration of up to 30 mg/kg netupitant (3.7 times the human AUC at the recommended dose) in rats during organogenesis through lactation produced no adverse effects in the offspring.
L537: ## Fosnetupitant
L538: Daily intravenous administration of 39 (b)(4) mg/kg/day fosnetupitant in rats (3 times the human AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy) during the period of organogenesis produced delayed ossification of pubis. No effects on embryo-fetal development were observed with daily administration of up to 13 (b)(4) mg/kg fosnetupitant in rats (2 times the human AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy). Due to the limited systemic exposure to fosnetupitant in pregnant rats, it is not possible to provide an AUC-based comparison of fosnetupitant exposure in rats and humans. An increase in resorptions was observed with daily intravenous administration of fosnetupitant at 6 (b)(4) mg/kg/day and higher in rabbits (9 times the human AUC for fosnetupitant and 0.4 times the human AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy) during the period of organogenesis. No effects were observed in rabbits at 3 (b)(4) mg/kg/day (5 times the human AUC for
L539: fosnetupitant and 0.4 times the human AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy). Daily intravenous administration of 39 (b) (4) mg/kg fosnetupitant in rats (3 times the AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy) during organogenesis through lactation produced lower bodyweight in offspring at birth through maturation, and delayed physical development (pinna detachment, eye opening, and preputial separation). These effects were associated with maternal toxicity (reduced weight gain and food consumption). No effects occurred in offspring or dams at 13 (b) (4) mg/kg/day (2 (b) (4) times the human AUC for netupitant at the recommended single dose to be given with each cycle of chemotherapy).
L540: ## Palonosetron
L541: In animal reproduction studies with palonosetron, no effects on embryo-fetal development were observed in pregnant rats given oral doses up to 60 mg/kg/day (921 times the recommended oral dose based on body surface area) or pregnant rabbits given oral doses up to 60 mg/kg/day (1841 times the recommended oral dose based on body surface area) during the period of organogenesis.

L801: ## 4.2.5 Study Description
L802: ## 4.2.5.1 Design
L803: PNET-12-23
L804: This single center randomized Phase I study was performed in healthy male and female subjects in different parts: a parallel group single ascending dose (SAD) part with a crossover extension (SAD-CO) (where applicable), a pilot crossover part (P-CO), and a final crossover part (F-CO).
L805: ## Overview of study parts
L806: A randomized, double-blind (within dose cohorts), 2-period, 2-sequence, crossover design was the basis of each study part. No crossover arm was however performed for low pro-netupitant doses (i.e., doses below 130 mg) in the SAD/SAD-CO part because these doses were investigated mainly for safety reasons.
L807: All parts were performed under double-blind (within dose cohorts), double dummy conditions.
L808: Unbalanced randomization was applied in the SAD/SAD-CO part (4:1 ratio for pro-netupitant vs. oral FDC), while 1:1 randomization was applied in the P-CO and F-CO extensions.
L809: The SAD/SAD-CO part was conducted in dose cohorts of 10 subjects each with single ascending doses. In total, 8 doses (i.e. 19.5, 32.5, 65, 130, 195, 260, 325, 390 mg pro-netupitant) were pre-defined in the clinical study protocol and the crossover extension was planned for doses from 130 mg to 390 mg pro-netupitant (inclusive).
L810: The P-CO part was conducted in dose cohorts of 20 subjects each with the doses selected during the course of the study on the basis of the PK data obtained from the SAD/SAD-CO cohorts. Two doses (i.e. 195 and 260 mg) were selected for the P-CO study extension.
L811: ## NEPA-15-18
L812: This was a multicenter, multinational, randomized, active-controlled, double-blind, double-dummy, parallel group, stratified study evaluating the safety and efficacy of a single dose of IV NEPA FDC (260 mg/0.25 mg) infused in 30 min, with oral dexamethasone, prior to initial and repeated cycles of HEC. The control group was oral netupitant/palonosetron (300 mg/0.50 mg) fixed-dose combination (Oral NEPA FDC) administered orally 60 min before start of reference HEC.
L813: ## 4.2.5.2 Controls
L814: There was no separate placebo only arm in both the trials. Also, there was no positive control in either of the studies.
L815: ## 4.2.5.3 Blinding
L816: ## PNET-12-23
L817: The study was performed according to a double-blind double-dummy design (within the dose cohorts). Therefore, the subjects received a placebo capsule together with the active pro-netupitant infusion and a placebo infusion together with the oral active netupitant/palonosetron FDC capsule to guarantee the double-blind nature of the study.
L818: ## NEPA-15-18
L819: The blinding of the study drugs was guaranteed by the use of identical placebos to the respective active drugs (double-dummy technique).

L829: ## 4.2.6.2 Sponsor's Justification for Doses
L830: ## PNET-12-23
L831: ## Selection of starting dose
L832: Based on the results obtained in a 2-week toxicity study in rats (PNET-11-21), the dose level of 13.16 mg/kg/day is the no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) for systemic effects and the dose level of 39.47 mg/kg/day, which was the highest dose tested in this study, is the NOAEL for local effects. Moreover, a 2-week toxicity study in dogs was also performed (PNET-11-22), and in this study, the dose level of 13.16 mg/kg/day (the highest dose level) was the NOAEL for systemic and for local effects.
L833: Following the FDA guideline "Estimating the maximum safe starting dose in initial clinical trials for therapeutics in adult healthy volunteers", the NOAEL was converted to the human equivalent dose (HED), and a dose of 19.5 mg was calculated.
L834: Therefore, based on the above mentioned animal toxicity data, the starting dose for IV administration in human volunteers was proposed to be 19.5 mg pro-netupitant (equivalent on molar basis to 15 mg netupitant, based on a conversion factor rounded to 1.3).
L835: ## Selection of single ascending doses
L836: Dose escalation above the starting dose of 19.5 mg pro-netupitant was proposed as follows: 32.5 mg, 65 mg, 130 mg, 195 mg, 260 mg, 325 mg, and 390 mg pro-netupitant (equivalent on molar basis to 25 mg, 50 mg, 100 mg, 150 mg, 200 mg, 250 mg, and 300 mg netupitant).
L837: Based on exposure data obtained from oral netupitant 300 mg/palonosetron 0.5 mg FDC studies, together with exposure data obtained in NETU-11-01 study with previous IV netupitant formulation, the oral availability of netupitant in human was estimated as equal to 87%. Therefore, it was expected that an IV dose ranging between 200 mg and 300 mg of netupitant (expected value of 261 mg considering 87% availability factor) would give an equivalent exposure (AUC0-inf) to the oral dose of 300 mg netupitant. This corresponded to a dose of pro-netupitant between 260 mg and 390 mg.
L838: The highest predefined proposed dose in this study was 390 mg pro-netupitant. This dose was defined in accordance with the suggestions of the competent authority and in reference to the specification limits of the active ingredient.
L839: After each cohort of 10 subjects (SAD part) had completed the SAD treatment period, the DSMB assessed the safety (and PK, if relevant) data obtained and determined if dose escalation could proceed to the next predefined dose or if a dose adjustment was necessary.
L840: ## Selection of doses in the pilot crossover part
L841: Pro-netupitant doses to be tested in the P-CO part depended on the PK data obtained from the previous crossover cohorts (SAD/SAD-CO part from dose 130 mg and previous P-CO cohorts, if any) for which data were already available.
L842: The dose of oral fixed dose combination (FDC) that was equivalent by exposure to netupitant from the current IV pro-drug dose was computed from the last (i.e. the current) estimate of F_{rel} IV. The extended equivalence interval spanned from 80% of the lower 50% confidence limit of this estimate to 125% of its upper 50% confidence limit. If this interval included 300 mg, then the IV pro-netupitant dose was further tested in a P-CO cohort. Otherwise no P-CO cohort was performed with this dose.
L843: ## Selection of doses in the final crossover part
L844: Pro-netupitant doses to be tested in the F-CO part were determined once all the subjects in the crossover SAD/SAD-CO and P-CO parts completed the study.
L845: After re-estimation of F_{rel} IV, derivation of the target dose, and calculation of the number of subjects needed, half of these subjects were treated at the “estimated target dose – 10%” (i.e., 90% of the estimated target dose) and the other half of the subjects was treated with the “estimated target dose +10%” (i.e., 110% of the estimated target dose), provided that such dose was lower or equal to the highest dose tested and considered safe by the DSMB. In case the 110% of the estimated target dose was above such dose, a lower dose was to be selected for the F-CO part.
L846: ## NEPA-15-18
L847: The dose of fosnetupitant in the IV NEPA FDC was selected based on the results of a pharmacokinetic (PK) study in healthy volunteers (PNET-12-23). In this study, a dose of 257 mg fosnetupitant was shown to be equivalent in exposure (AUC) to the netupitant dose contained in oral NEPA FDC (i.e., 300 mg netupitant). The dose of IV fosnetupitant was then rounded to 260 mg for further development of the IV NEPA FDC.
L848: The dose of palonosetron selected for the IV NEPA FDC was based on the original palonosetron IV development program. The IV palonosetron dose showing to be effective in the prevention of acute nausea and vomiting associated with HEC, and approved in the US and EU, is the 0.25 mg administered as an IV 30-sec bolus.
L849: ## Reviewer's Comments:
L850: *There are two completed TQT studies demonstrating that netupitant at therapeutic exposures in IV NEPA FDC and palonosetron at supratherapeutic exposures had no effect on the QTc interval.*
L851: The TQT study with IV palonosetron during the single agent Aloxi program revealed no effect on ECG parameters at doses up to 2.25 mg. In this study, the supratherapeutic dose of 2.25 mg of palonosetron was infused within 2 min (IV bolus infusion) and provided 9-fold exposure margin over therapeutic dosing of 0.25 mg Aloxi (IV bolus administered as 30 sec infusion). Thus, this study would provide &gt;9-fold exposure margin over the exposures anticipated with therapeutic dosing of 0.25 mg palonosetron given as 30 min IV infusion in IV NEPA FDC product.
L852: The assessments in the current program have adequate exposure margin to cover the highest clinically relevant exposure of fosnetupitant with therapeutic dose, because fosnetupitant clearance (conversion to netupitant) is likely mediated by non-saturable enzymes, such as phosphatases and esterases, distributed throughout the body and none of the intrinsic/extrinsic factors are expected to increase its exposure over those observed in its clinical studies. The study PNET-12-23 did not show a positive C-QTc relationship for fosnetupitant. Moreover, the preclinical cardiac safety studies did not identify any potential issues for fosnetupitant.
L853: The netupitant C_{max} observed with the therapeutic dosing of IV NEPA FDC [Fosnetupitant 260 mg/palonosetron 0.25 mg] is similar to the netupitant C_{max} observed with supratherapeutic dose (600 mg) of netupitant used in TQT study of oral Akynzeo combination product (300 mg netupitant/0.5 mg palonosetron; supratherapeutic dose of 600 mg netupitant /1.5 mg palonosetron was studied in that study). Although the previous TQT study of oral Akynzeo does not cover the worst case scenario of possible increase in netupitant exposure up to 1.4-fold that could be expected with hepatic impairment, these exposures are covered by 390 mg IV fosnetupitant (1.5-fold the proposed therapeutic dose of 260 mg fosnetupitant in IV NEPA FDC) that is studied in dose-escalation study PNET-12-23, and data from this study did not show a positive C-QTc relationship for netupitant.
L854: Moreover, in the Phase 3 study (NEPA-15-18) in more than 400 patients receiving IV NEPA FDC or oral NEPA FDC (oral Akynzeo), the data showed similar cardiac safety profile between the two products, similar values for QTcF and \DeltaQTcF in by-time and outlier analyses with no clinically significant QTc prolongation signal. Furthermore, the sponsor has stated that “oral Akynzeo has been marketed for 2 years and after more than 14,000 patient exposures with more than 85,000 doses, no QTc reactions have been reported in the post marketing surveillance data.”

L899: ## PNET-12-23

L1030: The intensive PK sampling in Study PNET-12-23 showed that the C_{max} for netupitant metabolites M1, M2 and M3 are around 12 h (steady conc. from 8 to 24 h), 2-3 h and 24 h, respectively. Although the ECG sampling in either of the studies (PNET-12-23 and NEPA-15-18) was not adequate to capture the effects near T_{max} of these metabolites, these metabolite exposures with therapeutic dosing of IV NEPA FDC were well covered by supratherapeutic dose of 600 mg netupitant in TQT study for approved oral NEPA FDC (2-fold [54 vs. 26 \mug/L], 1.5-fold [228 vs. 156 \mug/L] and 1.9-fold [102 vs. 53 \mug/L] exposure margin for M1, M2 and M3, respectively). Furthermore, there was no statistically significant positive E-R relationship seen in C-QTc analysis with each of these 3 metabolites in the TQT study (Previous QT-IRT review under IND 73493 for TQT study for oral Akynzeo (netupitant/palonosetron), dated 01/20/2010 in DARRTS). Thus, further exploration of E-R relationship for these metabolites was not carried out in this review.
L1031: The PK data also demonstrated that the C_{max} of fosnetupitant and netupitant occur at the end of the 30 minute infusion of IV NEPA FDC, indicating the fast formation of netupitant from its prodrug. Mean concentrations of netupitant remained at nearly the same level until 4 h post-infusion and declined slowly thereafter. Netupitant C_{max} after the IV 260 mg fosnetupitant (therapeutic dose in IV NEPA FDC) was ~2-fold of that after oral 300 mg netupitant (823 vs. 421 \mug/L). Also this netupitant C_{max} after the IV 260 mg fosnetupitant was similar to the C_{max} achieved with supratherapeutic oral dose (600 mg) of netupitant previously studied as a part of TQT study for oral Akynzeo (supratherapeutic dose of 600 mg netupitant /1.5 mg palonosetron studied in that study). Although the previous TQT study of oral Akynzeo does not cover the worst case scenario of possible increase in netupitant exposure up to 1.4-fold that could be expected with hepatic impairment, these exposures are covered by 390 mg IV fosnetupitant (1.5-fold the proposed therapeutic dose of 260 mg fosnetupitant in IV NEPA FDC) that is studied in Study PNET-12-23. So the relationship between \Delta QTcF and netupitant concentrations is explored in this review in order to take into account these higher exposure levels.
L1032: Further, this review also focused on the new moiety of fosnetupitant, which has not been studied in any prior TQT studies. The preclinical cardiac safety studies did not identify any potential issues for fosnetupitant. The assessments in the current program have adequate exposure margin to cover the highest clinically relevant exposure of fosnetupitant with therapeutic dose, because fosnetupitant clearance (conversion to
L1033: netupitant) is likely mediated by non-saturable enzymes, such as phosphatases and esterases, distributed throughout the body and none of the intrinsic/extrinsic factors are expected to increase its exposure over the observed therapeutic exposures in its clinical studies.

The results for netupitant are consistent with the prior TQT study where no positive C-QTc relationship was identified for netupitant.

Maximum tolerated dose	In rats, the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) after intravenous repeated administration was 78.94 mg/kg/day (equivalent to 60 mg/kg/day of netupitant) due to a slight decrease in body weight, a slight increase in the weight of liver and some local changes at the injection site (Study PNET-11-06, Appendix 18). In dogs, after intravenous repeated administration, the dose level of 39.47 mg/kg/day (equivalent to 30 mg/kg/day of netupitant) was considered the MTD due to the effects on body weight (a slight reduction) and food consumption (marked reduction up to 70%) (Study PNET-11-07, Appendix 18).
Principal adverse events	
Cancer Patients
A total of 404 chemotherapy naïve cancer patients were treated in up to 4 consecutive cycles in the Ph III, double-blind, randomized, multicentre safety study NEPA-15-18. A total of 203 patients received the fixed dose combination (FDC) of fosnetupitant 260mg/palonosetron 0.25mg as 30-minute infusion (IV NEPA) and 201 patients received the oral netupitant/palonosetron (300mg/0.5mg) FDC (oral NEPA FDC), each in addition to oral dexamethasone. Throughout the study, the most frequent TEAE was neutropenia reported in 30.0% of patients in the IV NEPA FDC group and 28.9% of patients in the oral NEPA FDC group. Other frequently reported TEAEs were anemia (16.3% and 18.9% of patients, respectively), alopecia (15.8% and 14.4% of patients, respectively), constipation (10.3% and 12.9% of patients, respectively), and leukopenia (10.3% and 10.9% of patients, respectively).
Healthy Volunteers
In the two healthy volunteer studies conducted with fosnetupitant as a single agent, the most frequently reported TEAE after both administration routes was headache (all IV doses: 8.1% of subjects; oral: 15.7% of subjects) in PNET-12-23 (Single IV ascending dose study from 19.5 mg up to 390 mg versus oral NEPA FDC) and flushing (17.9% with dexamethasone alone, 30%, 35% and 27.8% after 130, 195 and 260 mg fosnetupitant plus dexamethasone, respectively) in PNET-13-63 (DDI study).

Elimination	Route	Primary elimination route for fosnetupitant is metabolism. The prodrug is rapidly and completely hydrolyzed to netupitant by non-CYP450 hydrolytic enzymes. Netupitant is then primarily eliminated by CYP3A4 mediated metabolism with formation of the main metabolites M1, M2 and M3. After oral administration of radiolabeled netupitant, 86.5% of administered radioactivity was excreted via the feces in 30 days post-dose.
Primary elimination route for palonosetron is via the kidneys. After a single intravenous dose of radiolabeled palonosetron, approximately 80% of the dose was recovered within 144 hours in the urine, with unchanged palonosetron representing approximately 40% of the administered dose.
Other elimination routes for fosnetupitant are unknown. After oral administration of radiolabeled netupitant, 4.7% of administered radioactivity was excreted in urine in 30 days post-dose. Palonosetron is eliminated by CYP2D6 mediated metabolism with formation of two primary metabolites: N-oxide-palonosetron (M9) and 6-S-hydroxy-palonosetron (M4).

Age	No data evaluating the effect of age on the PK of fosnetupitant are available. A study in the pediatric population is planned. Based on a population PK study, age was not found to be a significant covariate for netupitant and palonosetron PK after oral administration of netupitant-palonosetron FDC (Study NETU-10-02). In study NETU-10-12 the effect of age on the PK parameters of netupitant was investigated after oral administration of netupitant-palonosetron FDC. In elderly subjects, netupitant and palonosetron C_{max} increased by 36% and 10%, respectively, whereas AUC_{inf} increased by 25% and 37%, respectively as compared to younger adults. A study in the pediatric population with the oral netupitant-palonosetron FDC is in progress (Protocol NEPA-15-31). A previous study in pediatric cancer patients following intravenous infusion of palonosetron at 20 \mug/kg over 15 min showed in age groups between 2 and <17 years an exposure (AUC_{inf}) approximately 3-fold higher than that achieved in adult cancer patients after intravenous infusion of 0.25mg palonosetron (Study PALO-10-20, Package Insert).
Sex	Study PNET-12-23 showed that in healthy subjects receiving a single 260mg fosnetupitant infusion, fosnetupitant mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} were superimposable in females (n=13) and in males (n=19), their mean ratio being 1.006 and 1.009, respectively (less than 1% difference). As far as released netupitant is concerned, mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} were 16% and 4% higher in females than in males, respectively. Study NEPA-15-19 showed that in cancer patients receiving a single 260mg fosnetupitant / 0.25mg palonosetron infusion, fosnetupitant mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} were slightly higher in females (n=13), 19% and 22%, respectively, than in males (n=11). On the other hand, C_{max} and AUC_{inf} of netupitant released from fosnetupitant were superimposable in females and males, mean ratios being 1.07 and 0.99, respectively.
L1050: Palonosetron mean C_{max} was similar in females and males (ratio M/F = 1.01) and mean AUC_{inf} was slightly higher, i.e., 26%, than in males.
A post-hoc analysis (Study NEPA-13-11) of pooled data from 3 studies with oral netupitant 300mg/palonosetron 0.5mg FDC (NETU-09-07, NETU-11-02 and NETU-11-23), indicated that netupitant C_{max} and AUC_{0-4} were 35.29% and 2.35% greater in females than in males, respectively. Mean palonosetron C_{max} and AUC_{0-4} were 30.53% and 39.88% higher in females than in males, respectively.

Race	
No data assessing the effect of race on the PK of fosnetupitant are available. However, no impact of race can be predicted considering that the hydrolytic enzymes involved in the rapid biotransformation of the prodrug into netupitant do not belong to the CYP450 superfamily and, likely, are not subject to genetic polymorphism (e.g., phosphatases, esterases).
The effect of race on netupitant PK is also unlikely based on the following theoretical considerations and experimental results. Netupitant is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4. With the exception of alleles *1B in Afro-americans and *19 in Indo-Pakistans, genetic polymorphism of CYP3A4 may occur in a small proportion of the population across all of the affected alleles, with either no influence on enzyme activity or its reduction. In a recent study in 18 Chinese healthy subjects (study NEPA-14-02) receiving an oral administration of 300mg netupitant / 0.5mg palonosetron FDC, mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} of netupitant in Chinese were marginally higher, i.e., 17% and 10%, respectively, than those observed in the Caucasian population after administration of oral NEPA FDC (Study NETU-11-02, 22 fasted HVs).
Palonosetron is primarily metabolized by CYP2D6, known to be a polymorphic CYP isoform. Racial and ethnic studies of drug metabolism have shown substantial inter-population differences in the polymorphic distribution of CYP2D6 activity. A study conducted in CYP2D6 poor (PM) and extensive metabolizers (EM) after administration of a single IV dose of 0.75 mg palonosetron showed similar values of C_{max} (PM/EM=0.98) and AUC_{inf} (PM/EM=0.85) for the two groups (Study PALO-99-39). Also, intravenous palonosetron PK was characterized in 24 healthy Japanese subjects over the dose range of 3 – 90 µg/kg. Total body clearance was 25% higher in Japanese subjects compared to Caucasians. Finally, in Chinese healthy subjects (study NEPA-14-02) receiving an oral administration of 300mg netupitant / 0.5mg palonosetron FDC, mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} of palonosetron were higher, i.e., 42% and 68%, respectively, than those observed in the Caucasian population after administration of oral NEPA FDC (Study NETU-11-02, 22 fasted HVs).

Hepatic & Renal Impairment	
No studies have been performed with fosnetupitant in hepatic and renal impairment. However, no change in the PK profile of fosnetupitant can be predicted in both hepatic and renal impairment because the prodrug is not expected to be excreted through the urine and it is likely metabolized by non-saturable enzymes systems, such as phosphatases and esterases, largely distributed throughout the body and not only in the liver.
Netupitant and palonosetron PK was investigated in hepatic
L1051: impairment after oral netupitant/palonosetron FDC administration (study NETU-10-10). In patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment, mean AUC_{inf} of netupitant was 67% and 86% higher, respectively, than in healthy subjects, and the mean C_{max} for netupitant was 40% and 41% higher, respectively than in healthy subjects. In patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment, mean AUC_{inf} of palonosetron was 33% and 62% higher, respectively, than in healthy subjects, and the mean C_{max} for netupitant was 14% higher and unchanged, respectively as compared to healthy subjects. In light of the negligible renal excretion of netupitant and its metabolites (4.7% of the administered dose), no specific PK studies have been performed in renal impairment. In a population PK analysis, mild and moderate renal impairment did not significantly affect the pharmacokinetics of netupitant in cancer patients. After a single IV bolus of palonosetron 0.75 mg to the patients over 30 seconds, mild to moderate renal impairment did not significantly affect palonosetron PK parameters, whereas AUC_{inf} increased by approximately 28% in patients with severe renal impairment relative to healthy subjects (Study PALO-99-35).

Extrinsic Factors	Drug interactions	
Fosnetupitant is administered intravenously and is efficiently hydrolyzed by non-saturable enzymatic systems, such as phosphatases and esterases, abundantly distributed throughout the body. Therefore no interactions are expected between fosnetupitant itself and concomitant drugs known to be inhibitors or inducers of CYP450 enzymes that could alter fosnetupitant PK behavior.
In in vitro studies using human liver microsomes (study PNET-15-49), fosnetupitant showed weak inhibition towards some CYP450 isoforms, e.g., CYP2C9 (IC_{50}=27.9 \muM), CYP2C19 (IC_{50}=46.1 \muM), CYP2C8 (IC_{50}=56.7 \muM). Also, fosnetupitant showed weak inhibition towards transporters, i.e., P-gp (IC_{50}=50 \muM), OATP1B1 (IC_{50} 19.1 \muM), OATP1B3 (IC_{50}=17.37 \muM) (study PNET-15-27).
A PBPK modeling study (Study PNET-16-29) indicated, based on in vitro K_i estimates, physicochemical properties and in vivo PK data for fosnetupitant, no in vivo interaction between fosnetupitant and CYP2C9, CYP2C19, OATP1B1, OATP1B3 and P-gp substrates is expected. This prediction is justified by the short permanence of fosnetupitant in the systemic circulation determined by a high systemic prodrug clearance. The elevated fosnetupitant concentrations observed in a very short timeframe at the end of the infusion appeared to have no significant impact on the PK profile of drug substrates of relevant CYP450 isoforms and transporters weakly inhibited in vitro by fosnetupitant. For the same reason, no drug interactions are expected between fosnetupitant as substrate of OATP1B1 and OATP1B3 and inhibitors of these two transporters.
However, metabolism of fosnetupitant generates netupitant, which has been found to be a moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor. Hence, an in vivo drug interaction study was performed
L1052: between fosnetupitant, administered as a single IV infusion of 130, 195 and 260mg over 30 minutes and dexamethasone, a CYP3A4 probe substrate (Study-13-63). On Day 1, dexamethasone C_{max} did not increase when given with a single administration of 260mg fosnetupitant (adjusted geometric mean ratio = 0.97) whereas AUC_{0-24} showed a 50% increase. On Day 4, dexamethasone C_{max} and AUC_{0-24} increased 1.7- and 2.4 fold, respectively, when given with a single administration of 260mg fosnetupitant. Another study demonstrated that CYP3A4 inhibition lasts up to 6 days from the single oral netupitant administration (Study NEPA-14-39).
In vivo interaction studies have also been performed with oral netupitant/palonosetron FDC co-administered with CYP3A4 drug substrates.
Dexamethasone (Study NETU-06-07). After co-administration of a single dose of netupitant (300 mg) with a dexamethasone regimen (20 mg on Day 1, followed by 8 mg b.i.d. from Day 2 to Day 4), mean C_{max} and AUC_{0-24} of dexamethasone were increased by 1.1 and 1.7-fold, respectively, on Day 1 and up to 1.7 and 2.4-fold on Day 4 as compared to exposure after dexamethasone alone. Increased dexamethasone exposure as a consequence of CYP3A4 inhibition lasted up to Day 6 (Study NEPA-14-39).
Midazolam (Study NP16599). When co-administered with netupitant 300 mg, the mean C_{max} and AUC of midazolam after single dose oral administration of 7.5 mg midazolam was 36% and 126% higher, respectively.
Erythromycin (Study NP16599). When 500 mg erythromycin was co-administered with netupitant 300 mg, the systemic exposure of erythromycin was highly variable and the mean C_{max} and AUC of erythromycin were increased by 92% and 56%, respectively.
Oral Contraceptives (Study NETU-10-08). A single oral dose of netupitant/palonosetron FDC, when given with a single oral dose of 60 \mug ethinyl estradiol and 300 \mug levonorgestrel, increased the AUC of levonorgestrel by 46%, whereas C_{max} was unchanged.
No significant effect on C_{max} and AUC of ethinyl estradiol was observed. The point estimates of C_{max} and AUC_{inf} were 1.05 and 1.12, respectively.
Chemotherapeutic Agents (docetaxel, etoposide, cyclophosphamide) (Study NETU-10-09). With co-administration of oral netupitant/palonosetron FDC, the mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} of docetaxel were 49% and 35% higher, respectively, whereas mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} of etoposide were increased by 10% and 28%, respectively compared to when co-administered with palonosetron alone. Mean C_{max} and AUC for cyclophosphamide after co-administration with oral netupitant/palonosetron FDC was 27% and 20% higher compared to when co-administered with palonosetron alone.

L1053: The effects of CYP3A4 inducers and inhibitors on the PK of netupitant and palonosetron were assessed.
Rifampicin (Study NETU-10-11). A single oral dose of netupitant/palonosetron FDC was administered with rifampicin, a strong CYP3A4 inducer, following once daily administration of 600 mg rifampicin for 17 days. Co-administration of rifampicin decreased the mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} of netupitant by 62% and 82%, respectively, compared to those after netupitant/palonosetron alone. Co-administration of rifampicin decreased the mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} for palonosetron by 15% and 19%, respectively.
Ketoconazole (Study NETU-10-11). A single oral dose of netupitant/palonosetron FDC was administered with ketoconazole, a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor, following once daily administration of 400 mg ketoconazole for 12 days. Co-administration with ketoconazole increased mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} of netupitant by 25% and 140%, respectively, compared to after administration of netupitant/palonosetron FDC alone. The mean C_{max} and AUC_{inf} of palonosetron were 15% and 10% higher, respectively, when co-administered with ketoconazole.
Palonosetron is eliminated from the body through both renal excretion and metabolic pathways with the latter mediated via multiple CYP enzymes. In vitro studies indicated that palonosetron is not an inhibitor of CYP1A2, CYP2A6, CYP2B6, CYP2C9, CYP2D6, CYP2E1 and, CYP3A4/5 (CYP2C19 was not investigated) nor does it induce the activity of CYP1A2, CYP2D6, or CYP3A4/5.
Coadministration of 0.25 mg I.V. palonosetron and 20 mg I.V. dexamethasone in healthy subjects revealed no PK drug-interactions between palonosetron and dexamethasone.
In an interaction study in healthy subjects where palonosetron 0.25 mg (I.V. bolus) was administered on day 1 and oral aprepitant for 3 days (125 mg/80 mg/80 mg), the pharmacokinetics of palonosetron were not significantly altered (AUC: no change, Cmax: 15% increase).
A study in healthy volunteers involving single-dose I.V. palonosetron (0.75 mg) and steady state oral metoclopramide (10 mg four times daily) demonstrated no significant pharmacokinetic interaction.
An in vitro interaction study was done to assess the substrate or inhibitor properties of palonosetron (PALO-15-28) for transporters. Palonosetron resulted to be substrate of P-gp and a weak inhibitor of the MATE1, MATE2-K, OCT1, OCT2 and OAT3. Application of principles and basic models described in the DDI guidance (2012), indicated no in vivo relevance of these interactions.

fosnetupitant is immediately and rapidly hydrolyzed to netupitant;

no drug interactions that could overexpose patients to the prodrug are expected;
no change in the PK profile of fosnetupitant could be expected in both hepatic and renal impairment;

Possible increase of netupitant exposure due to drug interactions with strong inhibitors of the CYP3A4-mediated metabolism or hepatic impairment, are covered by safety data at supratherapeutic doses, as discussed in the oral Akynzeo® dossier.

Preclinical Cardiac Safety	
Based on the rapid conversion of fosnetupitant to netupitant the cardiac safety of fosnetupitant relies on a comprehensive cardiac safety program performed for oral Akynzeo® (Appendix 18).
In vitro studies. Effect of Fosnetupitant on hERG Current (I_{Kr}) was assessed in vitro (Study PNET-12-06). Fosnetupitant up to 30 \mu\text{M} had no effects on the delayed rectifier potassium current (I_{Kr}) encoded by hERG.
In vivo studies. Cardiovascular function was assessed in vivo in telemetered dogs in a 2-week study with IV fosnetupitant (Study PNET-12-35). Fosnetupitant was administered at 1.32, 3.95 and 13.16 mg/kg/day (equivalent to 1, 3 and 10 mg/kg/day of netupitant). All electrocardiographic tracings recorded during the study did not show any abnormality which could be considered treatment-related. The intravenous administration of combination fosnetupitant/palonosetron 1.32/1 mg/kg/day, 13.2/6 and 3.95/3 for 2 weeks (NEPA-13-76) did not induce any effect on arterial blood pressure, heart rate and body temperature in a study in telemetered dogs. Alterations in atrio-ventricular conduction, ventricular depolarisation and repolarisation characterised by increases in PR, PQ, QT interval and QRS complex durations, ST segment, QT corrected QT_c and QTshift were observed in the high-dose group (13.2/6 mg/kg/day) at Day 14. There were no effects on QT at low (1.32/1 mg/kg/day) and mid-dose (3.95/3 mg/kg/day) levels at any time during the study. Changes in QT and QRS complex were due to the main netupitant metabolite, M1.
In the 4-week toxicology study PNET-13-27, the cardiovascular effects of fosnetupitant were assessed in conscious dogs dosed at 1.316, 3.95 or 13.16 mg/kg/day, equivalent to 1, 3 and 10 mg/kg/day of netupitant, by intravenous route once daily for 4 weeks. Electrocardiographic tracings recorded during the dosing period did not show any abnormality which could be considered to be treatment-related.
In the 4-week toxicology study NEPA-13-52, dogs were treated with fosnetupitant/palonosetron at doses of 0/0, 1.32/1, 3.95/3, or 13.16/10/6 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks. Palonosetron was administered at 10 mg/kg/day during 14/13 days (males/females, respectively); due to convulsive episodes the dose level was then reduced to 6 mg/kg/day until the end of the study. Intravenous administration of the combination of fosnetupitant-palonosetron caused transient and reversible increased heart rate at mid dose (females only) and at high dose (both sexes) levels. These changes were associated with a transient and reversible increase, without dose-related effect, in QT_c (QT_cF and QT_cV) intervals at the high dose level (male and female animals) and at the mid dose (males only).
