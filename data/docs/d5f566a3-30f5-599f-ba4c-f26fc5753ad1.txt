L11: Torsades de pointes (TdP) is a specific type of abnormal heart rhythm that can lead to ventricular fibrillation and sudden death. QT prolongation, a surrogate marker for risk of developing TdP, is an established serious side effect of many approved therapeutic products.
L12: Ribociclib 600mg QD in combination with letrozole provides clinically meaningful and statistically significant improvement in the progression-free survival of post-menopausal women with HR+, HER2-advanced or metastatic breast cancer as initial endocrine-based therapy. However, QT prolongation is an important identified safety risk for ribociclib.
L13: 2. Describe the particular review issue and the goal of the study/clinical trial. If the study/clinical trial is a FDAAA PMR, describe the risk. If the FDAAA PMR is created post-approval, describe the “new safety information.”
L14: Ribociclib can prolong QT interval in a concentration-dependent manner, with mean increase in QTc interval exceeding 20ms at 600 mg once daily dosing. The Agency believes appropriate measures should be taken to mitigate the risk for QT prolongation, which includes studying an alternative dosing regimen which could prove to have a lower risk of QT prolongation with comparable efficacy.

L30: Conduct an open-label, randomized, parallel trial to compare the efficacy and safety of an alternative dosing regimen (such as 300mg BID or 400mg QD) to the proposed 600mg QD ribociclib dose. The goal of conducting this clinical trial is to find an alternative dosing regimen which is able to mitigate the risks for QT prolongation without compromising efficacy.
L31: The primary objective should compare the overall response rate (ORR) between tested dosing regimen to the proposed 600mg QD dose. The ORR is defined as the percentage of subjects with a confirmed response (CR) or partial response (PR) by local investigator assessment as per RECIST criteria. PFS and OS should be included as exploratory endpoints for efficacy.
L32: The secondary objective should evaluate the safety and tolerability of the tested dose, including QT prolongation, hepatobiliary toxicity and neutropenia. Adequate ECG monitoring should be conducted to ensure the correct assessment of risks for QT prolongation.
L33: The trial should include a minimum sample size of 100 total subjects per arm. The protocol and statistical analysis plan should be submitted prior to initiation of the study for full Agency review.

L151: ## II. BACKGROUND
L152: Novartis Pharmaceuticals Corporation (Novartis) seeks approval of Ribociclib tablets, in combination with letrozole, for the treatment of postmenopausal women with hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative advanced or metastatic breast cancer as initial endocrine-based therapy. This clinical inspection summary is based on the results from primarily Study CLEE011A2301.
L153: The following overview of the Study CLEE011A2301 is intended as background context for interpreting the inspectional findings. Study CLEE011A2301 is a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo controlled study evaluating the efficacy and safety of treatment with ribociclib plus letrozole versus placebo plus letrozole in postmenopausal women with HR-positive, HER2-negative advanced breast cancer who received no prior therapy for advanced disease. The study randomized 668 subjects at 223 clinical centers in 29 countries.
L154: Study Period: Study initiation date (first subject enrolled): December 17, 2013
L155: Data cut-off date for analysis: January 29, 2016
L156: Primary efficacy endpoint: PFS as determined by the Clinical Investigator review of radiology data using RECIST version 1.1 criteria. PFS was defined as the time from the date of randomization to the date of the first documented disease progression or death due to any cause.
L157: Inspections of clinical sites were considered essential to verify the quality of conduct of the studies for this NME application.

- Identification, documentation, and reporting of adverse events (AEs) for a sample of enrolled subjects.

L370: ## Cardioelectrophysiology
L371: Serial, triplicate ECGs were collected following a single dose and at steady-state to evaluate the effect of ribociclib on the QTc interval in patients with advanced cancer. A pharmacokinetic-pharmacodynamic analysis included a total of 267 patients treated with ribociclib at doses ranging from 50 to 1200 mg, including 193 patients treated with ribociclib 600 mg. L533: The mean concentration-time profile of ribociclib is illustrated in Figure 1.
L534: The relationship between \Delta QTc and ribociclib was investigated by a log-linear mixed-effects model with QTcF change from baseline (\Delta QTcF) as the dependent variable and log transformed concentrations as the predictor (Figure 4). The final C-QTc model was a log-linear model with random effect on the intercept and slope, an effect of baseline QTcF, Study and time of data collection on the intercept. Including study and time of data collection as categorical factors in the final model and random effect on the slope was able to fit data better (\Delta AIC= -399)
L535: compared to the model proposed by the sponsor. Summary of results of the final ribociclib concentration-\DeltaQTcF analysis is shown in Table 14.
L536: According to the final model, a significant relationship between \DeltaQTcF and ribociclib concentrations was observed (p&lt;0.0001) and the mean (90% CI) \DeltaQTcF at mean steady-state C_{max} of 2236.5 ng/ml following therapeutic dosing regimen 600 mg QD is estimated to be 22.64 (20.19, 25.09) ms. At the mean steady-state C_{max} of 3460 ng/ml with the supratherapeutic dosing regimen of 900mg QD, the mean \DeltaQTcF (90% CI) of 27.75 (24.74, 30.75) ms is estimated. The risks for QT prolongation were also predicted for alternative dosing regimen (300mg BID, 400mg BID and 400mg QD according to the review team's interest). The predicted \DeltaQTcF at estimated steady-state C_{max} for 300mg BID, 400mg BID and 400mg QD were 15.8, 15.9 and 19.7 ms respectively (Table 15). A relative reduction of 13% to 30% in QT prolongation was predicted compared to 600mg QD.

L545: ## 5.3 CLINICAL ASSESSMENTS
L546: ## 5.3.1 Safety assessments
L547: Ribociclib prolong the mean QTc interval by &gt;20 ms at therapeutic exposures and has substantial increased likelihood of being proarrhythmic. Sponsor's summary of AEs related to QTc interval prolongation is described in section 4.1.7.3. There was an imbalance of AEs related to QTc prolongation such as syncope, ventricular tachycardia and one sudden death. These AEs can signal potential proarrhythmic effects of ribociclib. The lack of torsades de pointes in this small clinical database (n=381) is not considered sufficient to dismiss possible arrhythmogenic risks.
L548: ## 5.3.2 ECG assessments
L549: Overall ECG acquisition and interpretation in 4 studies submitted (A2301, X1101, X2101, and X2107) appears acceptable.
L550: ## 5.3.3 Other ECG Interval
L551: No clinical relevant effect on heart rate, PR or QRS intervals.

Expected High Clinical Exposure Scenario	Co-administration with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor [Study A2101] represents the worst-case scenario where ribociclib Cmax increased 1.67-fold and AUCinf increased 3.21-fold following a single dose of ribociclib.
At the clinically relevant dose (600 mg), the geometric mean Cmax and AUC0-24h at steady-state were 1820 ng/mL and 23800 hr*ng/mL, respectively, and the maximum Cmax and AUC0-24h observed was 6170 ng/mL and 90600 hr*ng/mL, respectively [Study X2101-Table 14.2-2.1].
Using the worst-case increase observed from Study A2101 and the geometric mean exposures at 600 mg, a 1.67-fold increase in Cmax and a 3.21-fold increase in AUC would result in exposures below the maximum exposures observed at 600 mg.
In addition, at the MTD (900 mg), the maximum Cmax and AUC0-24h observed were 7930 ng/mL and 135000 hr*ng/mL, respectively, providing an additional safety margin in the scenario of co-administration of a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor.
Concomitant use of ribociclib should be avoided with strong CYP3A4 inhibitors. If co-administration of ribociclib with a strong CYP3A4 inhibitor cannot be avoided, a dose (b) (4) [SCP-Section 3.10.1.1.1].

L560: In Vivo
Ribociclib
Dog (GLP) telemetry study:Ribociclib caused dose-dependent prolongation of QTc intervals when compared to vehicle.
Mean changes of QTc interval at ribociclib doses of 20, 50 and 100 mg/kg were +12.1 ms (+5.3 %), +23.4 ms (+10.2 %), and +37.9 ms (+16.5 %), respectively.
No other ECG findings and no effects on heart rate, blood pressure or core body temperature.
The lowest ribociclib Cmax following 20 mg/kg where QTc interval prolongation was observed was 929 ng/mL. At 100 mg/kg, the Cmax range was 2570 to 7250 ng/mL.
Dog (non-GLP) non-invasive telemetry study:A single dose of ribociclib 100 mg/kg produced QTc interval prolongation (up to 31 ms, or 12%) when compared to vehicle.
One dog did exhibit QRS prolongation (up to 13 ms or 33%), heart rate increase and an increase in the incidence of premature ventricular contractions (PVCs) (isolated or bigeminal; total of 45 PVCs/ 24 h period).
The Cmax in the single dog dosed at 100 mg/kg, and in which an increased incidence of PVCs was observed, was 9720 ng/mL; exposure in this dog was 2-fold higher than those observed in the GLP study dosed with 100 mg/kg ribociclib.
LEQ803
Dog (non-GLP) non-invasive telemetry study:LEQ803 prolonged mean QTc intervals (up to 16%) at 5 mg/kg after single oral administration in a non-GLP study.
LEQ803 had no effect on heart rate, RR, PR and QRS intervals of the ECG and no arrhythmias were observed.
At 5 mg/kg, LEQ803 Cmax ranged from 13.4 to 86.6 ng/mL. At 600 mg in patients, for LEQ803, total Cmax at steady-state was 116 ng/mL [SCP-Table 2-6].
Conclusion
In vivo cardiac safety studies in dogs demonstrated dose and concentration-related QTc interval prolongation at an exposure that would be expected to be achieved in patients following the recommended dose of 600 mg. There is the potential to induce incidences of PVCs at elevated exposures (~ 5-fold the clinical Cmax). There were no effects on heart rate, blood pressure, core body temperature, or other ECG findings.

L588: ## 3 OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF THE MATERIALS REVIEWED
L589: We evaluated the proposed Kisqali container labels and carton labeling and noted that all packaging configurations are labeled with a prominent “200 mg per tablet” strength statement. This may lead to wrong selection errors because it is not obvious which Kisqali blister pack should be chosen for a patient’s prescribed dose. We recommend revising the container labels and carton labeling to prominently display the daily dose followed by the quantity of tablets required for the dose (e.g., “600 mg daily dose (three 200 mg tablets)”, “400 mg daily dose (two 200 mg tablets)”, and “200 mg daily dose (one 200 mg tablet)”) to prevent wrong selection errors. We also noticed that NDC numbers are presented as placeholders (0000-0000-00). Similarly, to prevent wrong selection errors, we recommend the Applicant to consider using different and non-sequential product code (middle four digits -0000-) for the NDC number to differentiate the different package configurations (“600 mg daily dose”, “400 mg daily dose”, and “200 mg daily dose”), and to ensure that the package code (last two digits -00) of the NDC number is different for including taking into account the blister cards of 21 or 14 tablets and the carton that contains 3 blister packs. Additionally, the dosing instructions on the container label and carton labeling should be improved to ensure patients take the correct number of tablets per day for their intended dose.
L590: We reviewed the proposed PI and noticed the use of “x” in the Dosage and Administration section of the PI, and recommend to revise the statement “600 mg (3 x 200 mg film-coated tablets)” to “600 mg (three 200 mg film-coated tablets)” for clarity and to prevent misinterpretation and confusion. We also noticed that NDC numbers weren’t provided in the How Supplied/Storage and Handling Section of the full PI and recommend Applicant to provide the NDC number information in this section.
L591: We noticed the use of “film-coated tablets” throughout labels and labeling, and we defer to Office of Pharmaceutical Quality (OPQ) for the determination of the appropriate dosage form for the product on labels and labeling.
