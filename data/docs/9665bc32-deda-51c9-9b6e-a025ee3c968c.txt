L11: ## 1. Background
L12: Defencath is a catheter lock solution (CLS) which contains taurolidine (13.5 mg/mL) and heparin (1,000 USP units/mL) as the active ingredients and citrate as a pH buffer. Taurolidine is a derivative of aminosulfonamide-taurinamide. A proposed mechanism of action for the antimicrobial activity is via transfer of methylol groups that interact with bacterial and fungal cell walls causing damage to the cell walls and inhibiting adherence of microorganisms to biological surfaces. Heparin is included in the solution for its anticoagulant properties. Defencath is instilled within the lumens of the hemodialysis (HD) catheter after HD treatment and withdrawn prior to the next HD session. It is not intended for systemic administration.
L13: The Applicant, CorMedix, originally submitted NDA 214520 on June 30, 2020, and received a Complete Response (CR) letter on February 26, 2021, due to product quality deficiencies. As for clinical data, the Applicant provided adequate information to support the effectiveness of Defencath in reducing the incidence of CRBSIs in patients with kidney failure receiving chronic HD through a CVC. Substantial evidence of effectiveness is provided by data from a single, randomized, active-controlled phase 3 clinical trial. In this trial, Defencath met the primary endpoint of time to CRBSI in demonstrating a 71% risk reduction in CRBSIs (95% confidence interval, 38%, 86%, p value 0.0006) in the Defencath arm versus the heparin comparator arm. Please see the NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation
L14: signed into DARRTS on February 26, 2021, for additional details related to the original submission.
L15: The Applicant resubmitted the NDA on February 25, 2022, and provided a response to the product quality deficiencies, a request for approval under the Limited Population Pathway for Antibacterial and Antifungal Drugs (LPAD), resubmission of request for proprietary name review, clinical safety update report, revised container and carton labeling, and draft labeling (prescribing information). The Applicant received another CR on August 4, 2022, due to unacceptable status of the drug substance (heparin sodium) and drug product manufacturing facilities. Please see the NDA review signed into DARRTS on August 3, 2022, for additional details.

L26: ## I. Safety Update
L27: The Applicant has submitted results from a literature review in April 2023 evaluating adverse events related to the use of the product. Two papers were found which included one case report in a patient receiving long-term parenteral treatment and a retrospective study in adults receiving home parenteral nutrition. In the retrospective study, the study drug was instilled and left in the catheters after administration of parenteral nutrition. Upon re-access of the catheters the study drug was flushed into the patients and not withdrawn. There were mild to severe adverse events (AEs) related to the study drug with no deaths. The events included severe AE's such as dyspnea, flushing, rash, and dizziness and more mild symptoms such as dysgeusia, nausea, and malaise. In many of the patients, re-challenge of the study drug did not result in repeat AEs. In the case report, the study drug was instilled into a chest tube catheter and aspirated prior to administering treatment. The Sponsor concludes that these adverse events will not alter the labeling of study drug and in these cases the study drug was not used as intended. All reported AEs were also consistent with the AEs seen in the clinical trial previously submitted by the Applicant.
L28: In both the case report and retrospective study, the study drug was not used as intended by the Sponsor. The study drug is only indicated for renal failure patients who are receiving hemodialysis through a central venous catheter and is meant to be withdrawn and not flushed into the patient. The use, catheter type, and patient population in both studies are not consistent with intended indication of the study drug and there is agreement with the Applicant's evaluation.

L151: ## 1. Executive Summary
L152: ## 1.1. Product Introduction
L153: Defencath® is a catheter lock solution (CLS) which contains taurolidine (13.5 mg/mL) and heparin (1,000 USP units/mL) as the active ingredients and citrate as a pH buffer. Taurolidine is a derivative from aminosulfonamide-taurinamide. A proposed mechanism of action for the antimicrobial activity is via transfer of methylol groups that interact with bacterial and fungal cell walls (Torres-Viera et al. 2000; Shah et al. 2002) causing damage to the cell walls and inhibiting adherence of microorganisms to biological surfaces. Heparin is included in the solution for its anticoagulant properties. Defencath is instilled within the lumens of the hemodialysis (HD) catheter after HD treatment and withdrawn prior to the next HD session. It is not intended for systemic administration. The product has Class III Drug-Device CE Mark approval in Europe as a catheter lock solution since 2013 (https://www.cormedix-europe.com/files/cto_layout/PDFs/E-NEU01-01-2015_IFU_English.pdf).
L154: ## 1.2. Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
L155: The Applicant has provided adequate information to support the effectiveness of Defencath in reducing the incidence of catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSIs) in patients with kidney failure receiving chronic HD through a central venous catheter (CVC). Substantial evidence of effectiveness is provided by data from a single, randomized, active-controlled Phase 3 clinical trial, LOCK-IT-100. In this trial, Defencath met the primary endpoint of time to CRBSI in demonstrating a 71% risk reduction in CRBSIs (95% confidence interval, 38%, 86%, p value 0.0006) based on the clinical adjudication committee (CAC)-assessed incidence of CRBSIs in the Defencath arm versus the heparin comparator arm.
L156: A single adequate and well-controlled trial was considered adequate to support the safety and effectiveness of Defencath in reducing the incidence of CRBSIs in patients with kidney failure receiving chronic HD through a CVC based on the following:
L157: - The persuasive and highly statistically significant findings on the primary endpoint of time to CRBSI. CRBSI as defined in the trial is a clinically meaningful endpoint and development of CRBSIs may result in significant morbidity and mortality, particularly in this patient population who rely on their CVCs for life-saving HD.
L158: At the interim analysis which was conducted when 28 CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs had occurred, there were 6 CRBSI cases in the Defencath arm and 22 CRBSI cases in the heparin arm with a statistically significant difference in favor of Defencath (p=0.0034). In the final analysis conducted once all ongoing subjects completed the study, there were 9 CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs in the Defencath arm and 32 CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs in the heparin arm (71% risk reduction, 95 % confidence interval, 38%, 86%, p=0.0006).

L165: ## Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
L166: In NDA 214520, the Applicant is seeking approval of Defencath for prevention of CRBSI in patients with kidney failure receiving hemodialysis (HD) through a central venous catheter (CVC). The labeled indication will be reduction in the incidence of CRBSI in patients with kidney failure receiving chronic HD through a CVC. Defencath contains taurolidine (13.5 mg/mL), a thiadiazinane antimicrobial, and heparin (1,000 USP Units/mL), an anticoagulant, as the active ingredients, and citrate as a pH buffer. Defencath has not been previously approved or marketed in the United States.
L167: The efficacy and safety of Defencath were evaluated in a single, multicenter, double-blind, randomized, active-controlled Phase 3 trial, LOCK-IT-100, in patients with kidney failure receiving HD through a CVC. LOCK-IT-100 evaluated the efficacy and safety of Defencath as a catheter lock solution (CLS) compared to the active control, heparin (1,000 USP units/mL), in reducing the incidence of CRBSIs. The primary efficacy endpoint was time to CRBSI. Due to low capture of CRBSI events during the trial, there were changes implemented in the protocol while the blind was maintained; these changes included introduction of a clinical adjudication committee (CAC) for ascertaining CRBSIs, revision of the definition of the primary endpoint to incidence of CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs, and introduction of an interim analysis. The CAC reviewed subjects' medical records to ensure that they met the clinical and microbiological criteria for a protocol-defined CRBSI event. To confirm the validity of the CAC adjudication, the FDA conducted a readjudication of the cases reviewed by the CAC and performed a sensitivity analysis based on the results.
L168: The interim analysis, which was conducted when 28 CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs had occurred, showed 6 CRBSI cases in the Defencath arm and 22 CRBSI cases in the heparin arm with a statistically significant difference in favor of Defencath (p=0.0034). The Data and Safety Monitoring Board recommended early termination of the study based on the results of the interim analysis. The final analysis at the completion of the study showed 9 CRBSI cases in the Defencath arm and 32 CRBSI cases in the heparin arm. This result was statistically significant in favor of Defencath (71% risk reduction, 95 % confidence interval, 38%, 86%, p=0.0006). The event rate per 1,000 catheter-days for the final analysis population was 0.13 and 0.46 in the Defencath and the heparin arms, respectively. A number of sensitivity analyses were conducted including a sensitivity analysis with the FDA readjudication of CRBSI events. Three cases were adjudicated differently by the CAC and the FDA; however, the overall number of CRBSI cases remained the same in the CAC and FDA assessments. A sensitivity analysis using the original, more stringent protocol definition of CRBSI prior to CAC adjudication also demonstrated a significant result in favor of Defencath with no CRBSI cases in the Defencath arm and six CRBSI cases in the heparin arm (p=0.0251). A sensitivity analysis evaluating CRBSIs due to gram-positive organisms showed
L169: 8 CRBSI cases in the Defencath arm and 18 CRBSI cases in the heparin arm; however, this difference was not statistically significant (p=0.0551).
L170: Loss of catheter patency occurred more frequently in the Defencath arm (63 subjects, 16%) than in the heparin arm (48 subjects, 12%) and is a serious concern as patients with kidney failure may have limited options for vascular access in the absence of a mature arteriovenous (AV) graft or fistula. Thrombolytic therapy such as tPA usage was also higher among subjects in the Defencath arm, occurring in 46 subjects (12%) in the Defencath arm and 33 subjects (8%) in the heparin arm. The mechanism by which taurolidine may affect loss of catheter patency is not clear; however, increased catheter malfunction/loss of patency and need for thrombolytic treatment have been previously reported in publications with use of CLSs containing taurolidine. There was a numerically increased number of catheter removals observed in the Defencath arm, with 236 subjects (59%) in the Defencath arm and 225 subjects (57%) in the heparin arm reporting catheter removal during the study follow-up period. Of note, differences in the number of catheter removals between the two arms did not appear to be secondary to loss of catheter patency. Although there were differences in rates of CRBSI between the two treatment arms, the main driver of the observed difference in catheter removal rates between the two arms was the greater number of patients with AV graft/fistula maturation (a positive reason for catheter removal) in the Defencath arm with 156 subjects (39%) in the Defencath arm compared to 129 subjects (32%) in the heparin arm reporting catheter removal for AV graft/fistula maturation.
L171: The remainder of the adverse reactions observed in the LOCK-IT-100 trial were consistent with prior knowledge of the safety of taurolidine, heparin, and the citrate excipient and included heparin-induced thrombocytopenia, hypersensitivity reactions, hemorrhage/bleeding, hemodialysis catheter malfunction, thrombocytopenia, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, musculoskeletal chest and neck pain, hypocalcemia, and dysgeusia.
L172: The Applicant has provided substantial evidence of the effectiveness of Defencath and adequate safety for the indication of reduction in the incidence of CRBSI in patients with kidney failure receiving chronic HD through a CVC. However, as the status of the drug product manufacturing facility, ROVI Pharma Industrial Services S.A., was found unacceptable by the Office of Pharmaceutical Manufacturing Assessment (OPMA) (see section 4.2 of this review for additional details) the NDA will receive a complete response action in this review cycle.

L180: ## 2. Therapeutic Context
L181: ## 2.1. Analysis of Condition
L182: Catheter-related bloodstream infections (CRBSIs) are a significant public health problem, resulting in mortality and significant morbidity, including increased hospital length of stay and healthcare costs (Mermel 2000; Warren et al. 2006; Allon 2008; O'Grady et al. 2011). CRBSIs are of particular concern in kidney failure patients who require vascular access for hemodialysis (HD). Approximately 25% of U.S. HD patients use temporary or permanent central catheters as their primary vascular access for dialysis (Tokars et al. 2000; Allon 2004). More recent data suggest that up to 80% of patients still initiate HD with a tunneled HD catheter despite efforts to promote the use of functional arteriovenous fistulas at the start of dialysis (Lok 2007; System 2017). Among HD patients, bloodstream infections occur 2.5 to 5.5 times per 1,000 catheter-days, translating to approximately 0.9 to 2.0 episodes per year (Allon 2004); thus making prevention of CRBSI in the HD population an important challenge.
L183: Strategies to reduce the risk of CRBSIs include routine infection control practices, such as use of proper hand hygiene, aseptic technique, and appropriate skin preparation regimens (O'Grady et al. 2011). While there are no FDA-approved prophylactic antimicrobial lock solutions, the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) guidelines for the prevention of intravascular catheter-related infections recommend the use of prophylactic antimicrobial lock solutions in patients with long-term catheters with a history of multiple CRBSIs despite adherence to best practices (O'Grady et al. 2011).
L184: ## 2.2. Analysis of Current Treatment Options
L185: There are currently no FDA-approved antimicrobial or antiseptic catheter lock solutions (CLSs) for prophylactic use in the prevention of CRBSI. However, a number of antimicrobial and antiseptic solutions are used off-label as CLSs to reduce the risk of CRBSI in susceptible patients (Labriola and Pochet 2017; Golestaneh and Mokrzycki 2018). Antibacterial drugs which have been used as CLSs for CRBSI prophylaxis alone or in combination include vancomycin, gentamicin, ciprofloxacin, minocycline, amikacin, cefazolin, cefotaxime, and ceftazidime. Other products, such as alcohol, taurolidine, and trisodium citrate, have also been used as CLSs (O'Grady et al. 2011).

L209: ## 4.3. Clinical Microbiology
L210: Defencath contains taurolidine (1.35%), heparin (1,000 Units/mL), and citrate (3.5%) (as an excipient) and is intended to be instilled in the CVC lumen and remain in place between HD treatment sessions. It is not intended to be systemically administered. Regarding the ingredients of Defencath:
L211: - The taurolidine concentration (1.35%) is equivalent to 13,500 µg/mL and has been shown to have antimicrobial activity, making the environment inside the CVC hostile to bacterial and fungal growth.
- Heparin at a concentration of 1,000 Units/mL does not contribute to the antimicrobial activity (Capdevila et al. 2001) of Defencath. It is an anti-thrombolytic agent and is added as an anticoagulant to prevent clot formation.
- Citrate is not an active ingredient but has been added to the drug product to adjust the pH and enhance the solubility of taurolidine in solution. The 3.5% concentration is below the level that would be required for antimicrobial activity. Citrate concentrations of 2.2% are able to completely kill *Staphylococcus aureus* and *Staphylococcus epidermidis*. However, complete killing of *Escherichia coli*, *Pseudomonas aeruginosa*, and *Candida albicans* was only achieved at 30% (Weijmer et al. 2002), which is 10 times
L212: ## NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation – NDA 214520 Defencath (taurolidine 1.35% and heparin 5,000 units/5 mL (1,000 Units/mL))
L213: the amount found in Defencath. In a meta-analysis of 13 randomized-controlled studies, citrate (4% to 46.7%) failed to show an advantage to heparin as a CLS in the prevention of CRBSI (Zhao et al. 2014).
L214: For the purposes of the review, this section will focus on taurolidine, the active ingredient associated with the antimicrobial activity of Defencath. Taurolidine, a derivative of the amino acid taurine, is unstable in biological fluids and is rapidly hydrolyzed to formaldehyde, methylene glycol, taurultam, and taurinamide in aqueous solution, ultimately breaking down to taurine (Myers et al. 1980; Gong et al. 2007). Formaldehyde causes denaturation of surface proteins and alteration of membrane lipids. Methylene glycol causes methoxylation of proteins and lipids (Myers et al. 1980; ATSDR 1999). This nonspecific toxic mechanism of action has been studied in vitro and in vivo. Based on these studies, taurolidine:
L215: - Reacts with the cell wall constituents of gram-negative bacteria and decreases microbial adhesion (Gorman et al. 1987a; Gorman et al. 1987b). This results in decreasing bacterial adherence to human cells. Chemical changes in bacterial cells are more destructive to bacterial function than changes to human host cells (Gorman et al. 1987c; Jurewitsch et al. 1998).
- Neutralizes endotoxin activity in vitro (Blenkharn 1987) and in vivo (Pfirrmann and Leslie 1979).
- Inhibits microbial (bacteria, yeast) adherence to buccal epithelial and uroepithelial cells, and suppresses the formation of bacterial fimbriae (Gorman et al. 1987a). Taurolidine has a higher affinity than mannose (the natural substrate) for bacterial fimbriae protein that is responsible for bacterial adhesion to human cells (Caruso et al. 2010).
L216: The necessary inhibitory and bactericidal concentrations of taurolidine are in the range of 125 to 2048 mg/L, and it appears that taurolidine requires contact times with bacteria greater than 4 to 24 hours at least 4x MIC to show bactericidal activity. There is a low frequency of resistant mutants (&lt;10^{-9}) at 2x and 4x MIC against *E. coli*, *S. aureus*, *Enterococcus faecalis*, *Enterococcus faecium*, and *P. aeruginosa*. For further information on antibacterial activity and resistance (see Section 16.3)
L217: The study design and results of the single Phase 3 trial are described elsewhere in the review (see Section 8.1). In this study, although there was a higher number of gram-positive infections in the both groups, the infection frequency for gram-negative organisms was significantly lower in the Defencath group compared to the heparin group. The most predominant gram-positive organisms were *S. aureus* followed by *S. epidermidis*, which were identified in both groups (Table 2). In this trial, the one gram-negative organism in the Defencath group was *Klebsiella pneumoniae*, whereas, the 14 gram-negative isolates in the heparin arm were a wide variety of organisms, including members of *Enterobacteriales*, *P. aeruginosa*, and *Stenotrophomonas maltophilia*. There were no anaerobes or yeasts associated with CRBSI events in this study.

L231: ## 5.1. Executive Summary
L232: There are CLSs containing 1,000 units/mL of heparin approved for marketing in the United States. A CLS containing taurolidine and heparin, similar to Defencath, is approved in the European Union. Thus, there is significant human clinical experience with the components of Defencath in CLSs.
L233: In addition to its use in CLS, heparin has been approved for systemic use in the United States, contributing to the clinical experience with this drug substance. Although taurolidine is not present in any drug products or devices marketed in the United States, there are published studies demonstrating that up to 5 g have been given intravenously over 0.5 to 2 hours to human subjects without significant toxicity. The amount of taurolidine in 5 mL of Defencath is 0.0675 g, about 74 times less than the 5 g tolerated by acute intravenous administration. Thus, if the maximum catheter fill volume of Defencath was accidentally pushed intravenously into the circulation rather than pulled out of the catheter and discarded according to the labeling instructions, it is unlikely to harm a patient. A rat study conducted with a diluted solution of Defencath did not use high enough levels of the active ingredients to adequately explore the toxicity of taurolidine and identify possible target organs of toxicity (if any). However, the medical reviewers found that previous clinical experience with taurolidine (particularly as a component of CLS) was adequate to support the clinical development of Defencath and the toxicology reviewer deferred to their judgement. Thus, additional acute or repeat-dose general toxicology testing in animals was not required for the components of Defencath.
L234: Defencath is not intended for systemic administration, so the Division agreed to waive reproduction toxicity studies for taurolidine and deemed that these data were not needed for labeling.
L235: Genotoxicity studies were required for taurolidine to label Defencath as a drug product because it is considered a new molecular entity. Although taurolidine is positive for genotoxicity in vitro regardless of metabolic activation (mouse lymphoma and Ames tests), it is negative in vivo. Data from a study in rats demonstrated that micronuclei were not induced in bone marrow polychromatic erythrocytes, and DNA damage was not detected in liver cells in a comet assay when taurolidine was administered intravenously up to a maximum tolerated dose of 600 mg/kg/day for three days. This suggests that in vivo, taurolidine is quickly metabolized and deactivated so that genotoxic substances related to this molecule are not able to reach DNA and cause damage.
L236: At the levels where taurolidine exposure could be achieved in the clinic during use of the Defencath CLS, the risk of genotoxicity appears low.
L237: NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation – NDA 214520 Defencath (taurolidine 1.35% and heparin 5,000 units/5 mL (1,000 Units/mL))
L238: In vitro studies using human blood showed that taurolidine did not interfere with the anti-coagulant properties of heparin. In addition, Defencath was not associated with hemolysis and it did not activate complement in vitro in human plasma.
L239: Defencath appears reasonably safe to use as a CLS in patients with kidney failure receiving hemodialysis through a CVC. There is no objection to its approval from the pharmacology/toxicology review team.

L309: ## 8.1. Review of Relevant Individual Trials Used To Support Efficacy
L310: LOCK-IT-100 was entitled, “A Phase 3, prospective, multicenter, double-blind, randomized, active control study to demonstrate the safety and effectiveness of Defencath in preventing catheter-related bloodstream infections in subjects receiving hemodialysis therapy as treatment for end stage renal disease.”
L311: ## 8.1.1. LOCK-IT-100
L312: ## Trial Design
L313: The study was a Phase 3, prospective, multicenter, double-blind, randomized, active control study. It evaluated the efficacy and safety of Defencath (taurolidine 13.5 mg/mL and heparin 1,000 USP units/mL) as a CLS as compared to the active control, heparin (heparin sodium 1,000 USP units/mL, benzyl alcohol 9.45 mg/mL and sodium chloride 9.0 mg/mL), in preventing CRBSIs in subjects receiving HD for kidney failure. Heparin is the current standard of care as a CLS in the U.S. The Defencath lock solution matched the control lock solution in terms of volume, color, viscosity, and smell and was packaged in identical containers. The blind was not broken until all subjects completed the study and the database was locked.
L314: The inclusion criteria were as follows:
L315: - Subject had ESRD (also referred to as kidney failure in this review) and undergoes chronic HD at least two times per week
- Subject had an HD catheter that has demonstrated the ability to achieve a minimum blood flow of at least 250 mL/min for at least two consecutive dialysis sessions to enable successful HD
- The HD CVC was placed in a jugular or subclavian vein, with the tip of the catheter typically sitting at the junction of the superior vena cava and right atrium
- The subject was not expected to expire within 180 days
- The subject was likely to require the use of a CVC for at least 60 days
- The subject (or the legal guardian) understood the nature of the study and provided written informed consent prior to the study enrollment
- The subject was willing to comply with specified follow-up evaluations and prescribed dialysis therapy
L316: (8) The subject was receiving adequate HD as assessed by the investigators and based on a single pool Kt/V measurement &gt;1.2 (within the last 30 days)

(9) All females of childbearing potential must have a negative pregnancy test at the screening visit (i.e., subject is not pregnant). Females of childbearing potential must agree to use an acceptable method of contraception for the duration of the study. Reliable forms of birth control are defined as:

- Abstinence as documented in the medical records and CRF.
- Depo-Provera, or other hormonal contraceptive (oral, implant, ring, patch) for the duration of the study. (NOTE: The subject must have used the chosen method of birth control for at least 1 month/cycle prior to enrollment into the study).
- Double barrier method (spermicidal jelly or foam with condoms or diaphragm).
- Subjects who would not be required to use the required birth control methods are those who are:Postmenopausal, defined as:12 months of spontaneous amenorrhea or
- 6 months of spontaneous amenorrhea with serum follicle-stimulating hormone levels &gt;40 Units/mL or
- Surgically sterilized (i.e., previous tubal ligation or hysterectomy).
L317: The exclusion criteria were as follows:
L318: - Subjects who received antibacterial drugs within the last 14 days
- Visible evidence of compromised skin integrity is present at the catheter exit site or evidence of a catheter exit site infection
- Subject had received any thrombolytic treatment (i.e., tissue plasminogen activating factor [tPA] – Cathflo®) in their current catheter within 30 days of randomization
- Fill volume of HD catheter was unknown or cannot be determined
- Subjects using any type of antimicrobial-coated or heparin-coated catheter
- Documented chronic bleeding diathesis, active or recurrent bleeding within 1 month prior to randomization
- Documented history of an atrial thrombus or known hypercoagulable state
- Subjects with open, nonhealing skin ulcers
- Current requirement for systemic immunosuppression that would increase risk of infection; such as:Steroid dose higher than 10 times the physiologic dose (&gt;50 mg Prednisone/day)
- Methotrexate dose sufficiently high to suppress WBC count below 5,000
- Biologic immunomodulators (anti-TNF, anti-CD4, etc.) within 1 month
- Azathioprine dose greater than 2.5 mg/kg/day (175 mg for average adult)
- Calcineurin inhibitors:Cyclosporine dose greater than 8 mg/kg (560 mg for average adult)
- Tacrolimus dose greater than 0.4 mg/kg (30 mg for average adult)
- Sirolimus dose greater than 10 mg daily for average adult

L320: (10) Active malignancy requiring or anticipated to require chemotherapy likely to cause leukopenia and/or immunosuppression

(11) Known allergy or absolute contraindication to citrate, taurolidine, or heparin, or a history of heparin-induced thrombocytopenia

(12) Unstable malignancy

(13) Cirrhosis with encephalopathy

(14) Subject was currently taking another medication with known systemic drug interaction with citrate, taurolidine, or heparin

(15) Subject was currently enrolled in another investigational device and/or drug trial or has participated in another investigational device and/or drug trial within 30 days prior to enrollment

(16) Subject was anticipated to receive a renal transplant within 90 days (subjects can be on the transplant list, but a subject with a known or anticipated transplant date within the next 90 days should be excluded)

(17) Any other medical condition which rendered the subject unable to or unlikely to complete the study, or which would interfere with optimal participation in the study or produce significant risk to the subject.
L321: Eligible subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either Defencath or heparin as a CLS. The assigned study medicine was instilled into the HD CVC at the end of all dialysis sessions and discarded prior to the initiation of the next dialysis session. All subjects received other standard of care consistent with current clinical guidelines for HD therapy during the study. Study visits occurred on HD treatment days. Every four weeks at one of these dialysis visits, a more extensive visit occurred including the collection of vital signs and labs. Subjects remained in the study until the occurrence of one of the following events, whichever occurred first: 1) development of a CRBSI, 2) catheter removal, 3) death, 4) transfer to a nonstudy site, 5) termination of dialysis, or 6) the end of the study. The primary efficacy endpoint was time to CRBSI.
L322: The final assessment of CRBSI was made by a CAC. According to the CAC charter, the CAC members were blinded to the treatment assignment. The study was designed to continue until approximately 56 subjects had experienced a CRBSI. The interim analyses for efficacy and futility of the primary endpoint were planned to be conducted by an independent statistical team when 28 CAC-adjudicated CRBSI events occurred. The Data Safety Monitoring Committee periodically reviewed the accumulating study data. Their primary responsibilities were to monitor subject safety and study conduct, oversee the interim analysis, and make a recommendation to stop the study or not based on the interim analysis.
L323: ## Study Endpoints
L324: The primary efficacy endpoint was time to a CAC-adjudicated CRBSI. The definition of CRBSI was modified over the course of the trial but prior to unblinding (Appendix 16.4), and the reasons for the changes are discussed in Section 3.2. The major changes appeared to be for clarification purposes.
L325: ## NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation – NDA 214520 Defencath (taurolidine 1.35% and heparin 5,000 units/5 mL (1,000 Units/mL))
L326: According to the protocol, the definition of CRBSI required that the same organism be grown from at least one blood culture from:
L327: - A peripheral site or bloodline sample and
- Either the arterial or venous catheter hub (or the venous or arterial dialysis circuit blood lines if on dialysis)
L328: Blood cultures were to be taken from two sites to enhance the validity of the culture testing results. One aerobic and one anaerobic blood culture bottle were to be taken from each site. If the subject had clinical symptoms, while on or off dialysis, blood cultures were to be obtained using peripheral venipuncture from a source not intended for future vascular access, and the venous or arterial hub of the HD CVC (or the venous or arterial dialysis circuit blood line if on dialysis). If a peripheral site was not available, as soon as the patient was evaluated, blood cultures could be obtained from both the venous and arterial hubs of the HD CVC (or the dialysis circuit blood lines if on dialysis), preferably before the study subject received antibacterial drugs. Ten to 20 mL of blood were to be collected, without discarding any of the CLS.
L329: Necessary clinical indicators for suspicion of infection included the following:
L330: - One of the following symptoms:Fever (defined as \ge 37.8^\circ \text{C})
- Rigors, defined as shivering from a feeling of being cold, often with copious sweating, as documented by a medical professional
- Two or more of the following symptoms:Tachycardia defined as a heart rate greater than 100 beats per minute
- Tachypnea defined as a respiratory rate greater than 24 breaths per minute
- Low blood pressure defined as a systolic blood pressure less than 90, as measured by a clinician (RN or MD) with a blood pressure cuff, or a decrease in blood pressure greater than 30 mm Hg
- An obvious change in mental status from previously documented baseline.
L331: All attempts were made to document as many signs and/or symptoms as possible at the time of suspected CRBSI.
L332: An investigator could choose to obtain blood cultures on other suspicions of CRBSI, but the CRBSI case definition required the clinical indication for suspicion of infection criteria listed above in addition to the blood culture criteria. An alternate source of infection should not be clinically suspected.
L333: As mentioned in Section 3.2, the Applicant was concerned about the low event rate, thus a Clinical Adjudication Committee (CAC) (see section below) was established to adjudicate for CRBSI.

L335: The two secondary efficacy endpoints were as follows:
L336: - catheter loss for any reason
- loss of catheter patency defined as required use of a tPA or removal of the catheter due to dysfunction.
L337: ## Clinical Adjudication Committee (CAC)
L338: The CAC was composed of five members, two board-certified in internal medicine and nephrology, one board-certified in pediatrics and infectious diseases, one board-certified in internal medicine and infectious diseases, and one board-certified in medical microbiology. The CAC reviewed the clinical documentation and the blood culture results to determine if a suspicious case was a CRBSI. The trigger for clinical suspicion included the signs and symptoms as detailed in the section above PLUS a positive blood culture (one positive blood culture would suffice for any organism with the exception of coagulase-negative *Staphylococcus*, which required 2 positive cultures). Blood samples were obtained from a peripheral site (or either the arterial or venous catheter hub or the arterial or venous dialysis blood line). Subjects had to show signs and symptoms of CRBSI and no other apparent source of bloodstream infection.
L339: The CAC was convened between the dates of December 2017 and October 2018 to review and adjudicate potential CRBSI cases used for the primary endpoint analysis. The committee evaluated the subjects for suspicious CRBSI events by reviewing the subjects' medical records to ensure that they met the clinical and microbiological criteria for a protocol-defined CRBSI event.
L340: ## Statistical Analysis Plan
L341: ## Analysis Sets
L342: The primary efficacy analysis population was the full analysis population (FAP) including all randomized subjects who received at least one dose of study medication. Subjects were analyzed according to their allocated group determined by randomization. The safety population included all patients who took at least one dose of study medication. For this population, subjects were analyzed according to the treatment they actually received.
L343: ## Control of Multiplicity
L344: The Pocock method was used to derive the interim efficacy boundary. The two-sided significance level of 0.0294 was used for both interim and final analyses. The fixed sequence testing procedure was utilized in the efficacy analyses to control the overall type I error. The comparison of the secondary endpoint of catheter loss for any reason between the treatment groups was formally performed only if the analysis of the primary efficacy endpoint demonstrated that Defencath significantly reduced CRBSI, and the analysis of another secondary efficacy endpoint of loss of catheter patency was formally conducted only if the

L346: analysis of catheter loss for any reason showed that Defencath significantly reduced the incidence of catheter loss.
L347: ## Analysis of Primary Endpoint
L348: For the primary efficacy endpoint of time to CAC-adjudicated CRBSI, if a subject had a CRBSI adjudicated by the CAC, then time to CRBSI was the number of days between randomization and the occurrence of a CAC-adjudicated CRBSI. Otherwise, a subject was censored at the earliest of the following dates: date of first catheter removal, date of last study drug dose plus three days, and date of study completion. Note that, according to the Applicant, three days was added to the last study drug dose because the study drug would be withdrawn at the next dialysis session, which would typically be about three days later.
L349: A log-rank test was performed to evaluate the treatment difference. A Cox proportional hazard model was utilized to obtain the hazard ratio of Defencath over heparin and its 95% CI for the CAC-adjudicated CRBSI. The incidence rate of CAC-adjudicated CRBSI per 1,000 catheter-days was calculated for each treatment group. The 95% CI for the incidence rate of the event per 1,000 catheter-days was also computed based on the assumption that the number of catheter-days followed an exponential distribution.
L350: *Reviewer's comments: There were censored data because subjects completed the study due to catheter removal, death, being transferred to a nonstudy site, termination of dialysis, or when subjects withdrew early from the study. The Applicant assumed all censored data was noninformative, which implied that censoring was unrelated to the study treatment. This assumption may not be valid. The statistical reviewer conducted sensitivity analyses to evaluate the robustness of the study results by including death as an event in addition to CRBSI and also by considering nonpositive disposition events as events. These analyses will be presented in the Sections of Efficacy Results – Primary Endpoint and Additional Analyses Conducted on the Individual Trial.*
L351: ## Analysis of Secondary Endpoints
L352: The secondary efficacy endpoints were analyzed as time to catheter removal for any reason and time to loss of catheter patency. Time to catheter removal for any reason was defined as the number of days between randomization and first catheter removal if a subject had a catheter removed; otherwise, a subject was censored at the study completion date or last study drug dose date plus three days. Similarly, time to loss of catheter patency was defined as the number of days between randomization and date of loss of catheter patency if a subject had an event; otherwise, a subject was censored at the earliest of the following dates: date of first catheter removal, date of last study drug dose plus three days, or date of study completion. The secondary efficacy endpoints were analyzed using the same statistical approaches as those for the primary endpoint.

These observations raised the possibility of differential activity of Defencath with more activity against gram-negative organisms vs. gram-positive organisms; however, the taurolidine MIC values were similar for gram-positive and gram negative organisms, and ranged from 64 to 1024 µg/mL against gram-positive bacteria and 512 to 1024 µg/mL against the gram-negative bacteria (see section 4.3 Clinical Microbiology for additional details). A sensitivity analysis based on CAC-adjudicated CRBSI with gram-positive organisms did not show a statistically significant difference between the two arms (p=0.0551); however, the small number of CRBSI events in the study may be a factor. This result is consistent with previous studies in the literature, including a systematic review, which reported that taurolidine-based CLSs significantly decreased the incidence of CRBSIs due to gram-negative bacteria (p=0.004), but were associated with a nonsignificant decrease in gram-positive infections (p=0.07) (Liu 2013). One possible explanation for the difference in the number of CRBSIs due to gram-positive versus gram-negative organisms following treatment with a taurolidine-based lock solution may be related to whether the source of the CRBSI is an extra- or intraluminal source. CLSs combat intraluminal contamination and if the source is extra-luminal, for example, secondary to an exit-site infection from a gram-positive organism, the CLS may not be effective. The literature on intra-versus extraluminal sources of CRBSI is further discussed in section 16.3 Additional Clinical Microbiology Information. L585: ## Categorization of Adverse Events
L586: Adverse events in LOCK-IT-100 were defined as any untoward medical occurrence that did not necessarily have a causal relationship with the treatment. Collection of AE data began after a subject had signed the informed consent and ended 28 days (+7 days) after removal of the last dose of the investigational product. Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were defined as AEs that started or worsened after the subject's first administration of investigational product. A serious adverse event was an adverse event that was fatal, life-threatening, led to a persistent or significant disability, congenital anomaly or birth defect, or required or prolonged an inpatient hospitalization. AEs were coded with MedDRA Version 21.0 in LOCK-IT-100.

L855: There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of Defencath in pregnant women. A consult from the Division of Pediatric and Maternal Health (DPMH) was requested as part of this review and is summarized below.

L879: ## 8.3. Statistical Issues
L880: There were no major statistical issues with the Phase 3 trial. A planned interim analysis was performed when 28 CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs had occurred. The results demonstrated a statistically significant treatment difference in the primary efficacy endpoint in favor of Defencath, and therefore the study was stopped early using an acceptable method for controlling the type I error. Additional subjects were randomized and treated between the interim analysis and termination of the study. The final analysis based on all data through the study completion yielded similar results to the interim analysis.
L881: The primary endpoint was changed during the blinded study. The Division was notified of the change and agreed. However, the treatment effect of Defencath appeared consistent across various definitions of outcomes. Death is an important efficacy outcome. The additional analyses where the deaths were considered as part of the event were performed, and the results were consistent with the primary endpoint.
L882: ## 8.4. Conclusions and Recommendations
L883: Defencath is being considered for approval under the LPAD pathway due to its potential to reduce the incidence of a serious and life-threatening infection in a limited population of patients, that is, kidney failure patients receiving HD through a CVC. Effectiveness in other populations has not been demonstrated. A streamlined approach involving a single Phase 3 trial was considered acceptable given the statistically persuasive results in the trial. For the purposes of this NDA, the safety database of 398 subjects exposed to Defencath was considered sufficient. The efficacy and safety review for this NDA focused on the Phase 3 trial.
L884: ## Efficacy
L885: The efficacy of Defencath was demonstrated in a single, randomized, controlled Phase 3 trial in a population of patients with kidney failure receiving HD through a CVC. The interim analysis, which was conducted when 28 CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs had occurred, showed 6 CRBSI cases in the Defencath arm and 22 CRBSI cases in the heparin arm, with a statistically significant difference in favor of Defencath (p=0.0034). The Data and Safety Monitoring Board recommended early termination of the study based on the results of the interim analysis. In the
L886: ## NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation – NDA 214520 Defencath (taurolidine 1.35% and heparin 5,000 units/5 mL (1,000 Units/mL))
L887: final analysis, Defencath met the primary endpoint of time to CRBSI with 9 cases of CAC-adjudicated CRBSIs in the Defencath arm and 32 in the heparin arm (p=0.0006) and an event rate per 1,000 catheter-days of 0.13 and 0.46 in the Defencath arm and the heparin arm, respectively. An FDA re-adjudication of the CRBSI cases evaluated in this trial showed a few differences as compared with the CAC-adjudicated CRBSI cases; however, the overall results of the FDA re-adjudication were consistent with the CAC-adjudicated results. The treatment effect of Defencath remained consistent in several sensitivity analyses performed by the Agency and is further detailed in Section 8.1.2 Study Results.
L888: ## Safety
L889: The safety of Defencath was supported by the single Phase 3 trial.
L890: The safety finding of concern in LOCK-IT-100 was the difference in loss of catheter patency, which occurred more frequently in the Defencath arm versus the heparin arm. Loss of catheter patency is a serious concern for patients with kidney failure who may have limited vascular access options. The mechanism by which taurolidine affects loss of catheter patency is not clear but has been noted previously and was a specific safety endpoint for LOCK-IT-100 due to this concern. The difference in rates of catheter removal between the Defencath arm and the heparin arm however, do not appear to be secondary to loss of catheter patency. Although there were differences in the rates of CRBSI between the two treatment arms, the main driver of the overall observed difference in catheter removal rates between the two arms was the greater number of CVC removals due to AV graft/fistula maturation in the Defencath arm.
L891: Certain AEs, including HIT, hypersensitivity reactions, and hemorrhage/bleeding were noted more frequently in the heparin arm than the Defencath arm; however, because heparin is a component of Defencath, these AEs may potentially occur with Defencath administration and were thus highlighted in this review, and will be communicated in the Defencath labeling.
L892: The remainder of the AEs occurring in LOCK-IT-100 with a plausible relationship to Defencath and its components were consistent with prior knowledge of the safety of taurolidine, heparin and the citrate excipient and included hemodialysis catheter malfunction, thrombocytopenia, nausea, vomiting, dizziness, musculoskeletal chest pain, hypocalcemia, and dysgeusia.
L893: Overall, there were no major differences noted between the Defencath arm and the heparin comparator arm with regard to the number of reported AEs, SAEs, and discontinuations. No deaths in either treatment arm were attributed to the study drug.

L981: ## 16.3. Additional Clinical Microbiology Information
L982: Further evidence of the nonspecific toxic mechanism of action is the relatively high concentration of taurolidine required for microbial inhibition and killing. Data from surveillance studies showed taurolidine minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) values:
L983: - Against gram-positive bacteria, including methicillin-resistant *Staphylococcus aureus*, methicillin-susceptible *Staphylococcus aureus* (MSSA), streptococci (Groups A, B, C and G), viridans streptococci, *Pediococcus* spp., *Lactobacillus* spp., *Leuconostoc* spp., *L. monocytogenes*, and diphtheroids, ranged from 128 to 2048 µg/mL with modal values of 512 µg/mL (Report#19-CMX-01; Zimmermann and Preac-Mursic 1992; Torres-Viera et al. 2000; Shah et al. 2002; Olthof et al. 2013).
L984: ## NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation – NDA 214520 Defencath (taurolidine 1.35% and heparin 5,000 units/5 mL (1,000 Units/mL))
L985: - Against *Enterobacteriales* isolates, including *E. coli*, *K. pneumoniae*, *E. aerogenes*, *C. freundii*, *S. marcescens*, *P. mirabilis*, *P. vulgaris*, and *M. morganii*, ranged from 256 to 2048 µg/mL with modal values of 512 µg/mL (Report#19-CMX-01; Torres-Viera et al. 2000; Shah et al. 2002; Olthof et al. 2013).
- Against *P. aeruginosa* ranged from 256 to 2048 µg/mL with a one-dilution shift in modal value of 1024 µg/mL (Report#19-CMX-01; Torres-Viera et al. 2000).
- Against other nonfermenters, including *Acinetobacter* spp., *S. maltophilia*, *B. cepacia*, other gram-negative isolates (*M. catarrhalis*), and anaerobes, ranged from 500 to 4,000 µg/mL (Zimmermann and Preac-Mursic 1992; Torres-Viera et al. 2000).
- Against fungal organisms, such as *C. glabrata* taurolidine, were 512 µg/mL; however, taurolidine showed less activity against *C. albicans* with 8-fold higher MIC50/90 values of 4096 µg/mL (Report#19-CMX-01; Olthof et al. 2013).
- Against other fungal organisms, such as *Cryptococcus neoformans*, *Aspergillus* species, *Trichophyton rubrum*, *Epidermophyton floccosum* and *Pityrosporum ovale*, ranged from 256 to 5,000 µg/mL.
L986: Time kill studies showed that taurolidine is bactericidal (&gt;3 log10 CFU reduction) between 1 to 4x MIC. One group (Torres-Viera et al. 2000) tested 5 isolates with a taurolidine MIC of 500 µg/mL (*E. coli*, n=2); *E. faecalis*, n=1; *S. aureus*, n=2), and an *E. faecium* isolate with a MIC of 250 µg/mL. At the MIC, only 3 isolates showed a 0.1 to 3.2 log10 reduction at 24 hours (2 *S. aureus* and 1 *E. coli* isolate). The remaining isolates had a 0.8 to 1.8 log10 increase in growth (1 *E. coli*, *E. faecium*, *E. faecalis*). In contrast, at 4x MIC (2,000 µg/mL), five of the six isolates showed bactericidal activity, with the *E. faecium* isolate showing a 1.3 log10 increase in growth. Similarly, (Traub et al. 1993) evaluated 37 isolates, including *S. aureus* (n=9), *E. faecium* (n=17) and Enterobacteriaceae (n=11), with taurolidine MICs &lt;1250 µg/mL and showed that the minimum bactericidal concentrations corresponded to 1 to 2x MIC with the exception of 3 gram-negative isolates, which showed minimum bactericidal concentrations of 4x MIC.
L987: A study by (Shah et al. 2002) evaluated the in vitro activity of taurolidine solution (containing either 0.675% or 1.35% taurolidine and citrate 2.61%) against bacterial pathogens. Taurolidine at a concentration of 675 µg/mL following 24 hours of exposure showed a &gt;3 log10 CFU reduction in bacterial load against *S. aureus*, *S. epidermidis*, and *P. aeruginosa* compared to controls (p&lt;0.05). However, a 2 log10 reduction was observed against *E. faecalis* compared to control (p&lt;0.05). In contrast, taurolidine at a concentration of 13500 µg/mL resulted in a 4.2 log10 reduction compared to controls after 24 hours exposure. Similar results were shown in the (Report# [REDACTED](b)(4)-10-6115-N14) using the Defencath formulation, which resulted in a 6.04 log10 reduction against *S. aureus* and 6.28 log10 reduction against *P. aeruginosa* at 18 to 24 hours compared to controls. In contrast, *E. faecalis* had a 3.32 log10 reduction and *C. albicans* had a 2.78 log10 reduction compared to controls after 18 to 24 hours.
L988: A study (Traub et al. 1993) showed that taurolidine at a concentration of 2,000 and 1,000 µg/mL in the presence of bovine serum (65% v/v) and human serum (50% v/v) marginally affected the bactericidal activity against selected gram-positive and gram-negative isolates.
L989: NDA Multidisciplinary Review and Evaluation – NDA 214520 Defencath (taurolidine 1.35% and heparin 5,000 units/5 mL (1,000 Units/mL))
L990: However, (Blenkharn 1990) reported an increase in taurolidine activity with up to a 4-fold reduction in MIC values when tested in the presence of 25% (v/v) pooled human serum against test strains of *S. aureus*, *E. coli*, and *P. aeruginosa*. It was not stated whether testing was conducted using fresh or frozen serum to ensure the preservation of all components of the complement system required for activation of the classical and/or alternative pathways.
L991: The development of taurolidine resistance has not been reported in vitro or with other taurolidine lock solutions used in humans. One study (Torres-Viera et al. 2000) evaluated the emergence of resistant subpopulations of 6 selected organisms (*E. coli*, n=2; *E. faecalis*; *E. faecium*; *S. aureus*, n=2). No resistant subpopulations or mutants to taurolidine grew when organisms were plated on agar containing 1,000 µg/mL or 2,000 µg/mL taurolidine. The frequency of resistant mutants was &lt;10-9 at 2x and 4x MIC for two strains each of *E. coli*, *S. aureus*, *E. faecalis*, *E. faecium*, and *P. aeruginosa*. The authors failed to find bacterial resistance, despite attempts to induce bacterial resistance experimentally. However, a study by (Kummerle et al. 1996) reported resistance against the disinfectant formaldehyde, a breakdown product of taurolidine partially responsible for its mechanism of action, which is most often found in gram-negative bacteria, such as members of the *Enterobacteriaceae* family and *Pseudomonas* spp. This was demonstrated against the plasmid-mediated formaldehyde-resistant clinical *E. coli* strain, VU3695, which carries a glutathione and NAD-dependent formaldehyde dehydrogenase plasmid. Studies were not conducted to evaluate the in vitro activity of taurolidine against formaldehyde-resistant gram-negative isolates.
L992: A study (Blenkharn 1987) evaluated taurolidine in combination with gentamicin, amikacin, piperacillin, cefuroxime, and ceftazidime against aerobic and facultative gram-negative isolates. The study showed an additive or indifferent effect, with a fractional inhibitory concentration index (FICI) ranging from 0.7 to 1.8. Against *B. fragilis*, taurolidine and metronidazole were additive (FICI 1.4). Against *S. aureus*, combinations of taurolidine and gentamicin or amikacin were synergistic (FICI 0.3 and 0.4, respectively) while combinations with vancomycin were antagonistic (FICI 2.3).
L993: The use of taurolidine in CLSs for the prevention of CRBSIs has been studied over the last 25 years. The reported event rate of CRBSIs per 1,000 catheter-days in taurolidine CLSs (with or without an anticoagulant) ranged from 0 to 1.35 compared to 0.21 to 3.44 in heparin CLS (Liu et al. 2013), indicating a trend towards benefit in patients who received taurolidine CLS. Information on the type of microorganisms leading to CRBSIs was available from five published clinical trials.
L994: - In the five studies, the number of CRBSI episodes due to gram-negative bacteria after taurolidine CLS use trended lower in incidence (range, 0 to 4) compared to heparin (0 to 11). One study (Betjes and van Agteren 2004) in hemodialysis patients reported no CRBSI episodes due to gram-negative bacteria in either the taurolidine or heparin CLS groups. This is in contrast to another study in a hemodialysis patient population (Solomon et al. 2010), which reported 2 and 9 gram-negative organisms in the taurolidine and heparin CLS arms, respectively. In this study the 2 infections in the
L995: taurolidine CLS group were due to *E. coli*, whereas, the 9 in the heparin arm were caused by *Klebsiella oxytoca*, *Delftia acidovorans*, *Enterobacter aerogenes*, *Haemophilus parainfluenzae*, *Serratia* spp., and *Stenotrophomonas maltophilia*.
L996: - The most predominant gram-positive bacteria was *S. epidermidis* followed by *S. aureus*. In the four trials, the number of CRBSI episodes due to gram-positive bacterial infection was not significantly different between the taurolidine or heparin CLS groups (range, 0 to 9 versus 4 to 13, respectively).
- The incidence of pathogenic organisms cultured from the exit sites were reported in two clinical trials (Betjes and van Agteren 2004; Solomon et al. 2010). (Betjes and van Agteren 2004) reported exit site infections at a similar frequency in both the taurolidine (n=2) and heparin (n=4) CLS groups. Of note, in the one taurolidine patient the *S. aureus* CRBSI episode was preceded by an exit site infection with *S. aureus*. One study (Solomon et al. 2010) reported seven positive exit site cultures in the taurolidine CLS group, with all cultures due to *S. aureus*. The heparin group included six positive exit site cultures: four were *S. aureus* and two were coliforms. Four of the 13 exit site cultures were associated with positive blood cultures for the same organism (2 taurolidine and 2 heparin). The finding of approximately equal numbers of positive blood cultures associated with exit site infections suggest that these CRBSIs may have a different pathogenesis. Bacteremia related to dialysis catheters can develop from an extra- or intraluminal source. CLSs combat intraluminal contamination and cannot be expected to eliminate bacteremia originating from the exit site.
- Three studies reported on intraluminal growth in the catheter at the time of removal. None of the studies showed a significant difference in bacterial growth on the catheters. A study (Dumichen et al. 2012) reported biofilm growth on the catheters in which 6 of 25 catheters in the taurolidine CLS and 7 of 26 catheters in the heparin CLS group were colonized by bacteria at the time of removal. Similarly, a study (Handrup et al. 2013) using scanning electron microscopy found no difference in the development of biofilm on catheters that contained taurolidine versus heparin CLS. In contrast, another study (Solomon et al. 2010) examined select catheters (4 taurolidine, 10 heparin) by transmission electron microscopy which showed bacteria in 2 catheters: 1 in the heparin arm had an *S. aureus* observed in the tunnel and tip segments; and in 1 catheter in the taurolidine arm, 1 gram-negative rod was observed among red blood cells at the tip, this was not associated with clinical or microbiological evidence of infection. There was no bacterial biofilm in any catheter; the authors stated it was not possible to determine whether taurolidine CLS destroys established bacterial biofilms.
- There have been no reports of bacterial resistance with taurolidine CLS treatment.

Version 1
(September 22, 2014)	
The definition of CRBSI used in this study requires that the same organism is grown from at least one blood culture from:
A peripheral site or bloodline sample
The arterial and venous catheter hub, or the catheter tip if/when the catheter is ultimately removed.
If a peripheral site is not available when the subject is not on dialysis, then at the next dialysis session, the venous or arterial dialysis port blood line can be substituted, preferably prior to the study subject receiving antibacterial drugs.
If the patient has symptoms of CRBSI while undergoing hemodialysis, as defined by the clinical indications below, blood from the hemodialysis circuit, both the arterial and venous dialysis port blood line, can substitute for a peripheral site collection.
Necessary clinical indication for suspicion of infection includes the following:
One of the following symptoms:
Fever (defined as \ge 37.8^{\circ} \text{C}) or
Rigors, defined as shivering from a feeling of being cold, often with copious sweating, as documented by a medical professional
Or two or more of the following symptoms:
Tachycardia defined as a heart rate greater than 100 beats per minute
Tachypnea, as defined as a RR greater than 24 breaths per minute
Low blood pressure as defined as a systolic blood pressure less than 90, as measured by a clinician (RN or MD) with a BP cuff, or a decrease in blood pressure greater than 30 mm Hg
An obvious change in mental status from previously documented baseline
All attempts will be made to document as many signs and/or symptoms as possible at the time of suspected CRBSI.
An investigator may choose to obtain blood cultures on other suspicions of CRBSI, but the case definition is based on the criteria listed above. An alternate source of infection should not be clinically suspected.

L1020: *Reviewer Comment: There is some evidence suggesting efficacy of taurolidine lock solutions based on relatively small, open label, randomized trials from the literature. Studies 2, 3, 4 referenced in Table 46 evaluated taurolidine lock solutions in non-HD populations. Study 1 was conducted in an HD population similar to Defencath, however, it had several limitations including that the study enrolled a small number of patients at a single-center and was unblinded. L1021: ## 16.5.2. Observational Studies
L1022: The Applicant proposed using the results from two observational studies reported by Murray et al. (2014) and Solomon et al. (2012) to support the efficacy of a combined taurolidine and heparin CLS in reducing the risk of CRBSI.
L1023: The Murray study evaluated the effect of a heparin CLS versus a combined taurolidine and heparin CLS on the rate of staphylococcal bacteremia. Data for 565 patients on chronic HD were obtained from the UK National Health Service Greater Glasgow and Clyde and the National Health Service Forth Valley Renal Unit Patient Record database. The information on incident staphylococcal-positive blood cultures was collected for 27 months between April 1, 2011 and June 30, 2013. The first 15 months of observation evaluated the incidence of bacteremia while heparin was used as the CLS. On July 1, 2012, a combined taurolidine and heparin CLS replaced heparin and the incidence of bacteremia was assessed for an additional 12 months. The results demonstrated that the bacteremia rate per 1,000 HD days was higher before the introduction of the combined taurolidine and heparin CLS as compared with after its initiation of use (0.69 [95% CI: 0.51, 0.87] versus 0.40 [95% CI: 0.25, 0.54], respectively).
L1024: The Solomon study assessed whether a combined taurolidine and heparin CLS reduced the incidence of CRBSIs in patients with HD. All 106 patients in the study received the combined taurolidine and heparin CLS at a clinical site in the UK. The incidence rate of CRBSI per 1,000 HD days in these patients was then compared with 34 patients who received a heparin CLS in a prior randomized clinical trial. The results showed that the rate in the patients who received the combined taurolidine and heparin CLS was less than half of the rate observed in the patients who received the heparin CLS (1.33 versus 3.25).
L1025: *Reviewer's comments: Both studies were observational and single-arm studies. Because neither study contained a randomized control, we cannot be sure that other differences did not exist between the test group and the control group that might be the reason for some or all of the differences seen. Additionally, patient level data were not supplied so we were unable to conduct additional analyses to explore the potential for bias.*
