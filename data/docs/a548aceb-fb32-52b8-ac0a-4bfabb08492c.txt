L24: At the present time, all issues raised in the response to CR have been adequately addressed by the Applicant. Of note, one of the deficiencies (Deficiency 1) in the Response to CR letter concerned the integrity of the clinical data. Based on the FDA inspection of the BioGeneriX AG facility (b) (4), there was a concern that the integrity of the database for Study XM02-2-INT, the pivotal trial submitted to support the efficacy of the product may have been compromised. In addition, the FDA asked TEVA to provide documentation, including justification and the audit trail for all changes made to the database after unblinding and a detailed analysis of the impact of all changes made to the data base after initial data lock and unblinding on the safety and efficacy data.

In the December 16, 2011 report of that inspection, there were no concerns identified relating to the integrity of the data base used for Study XM02-02-INT. During this inspection, the quality control and quality assurance safeguards were physically demonstrated to the FDA inspectors and found to be satisfactory. Inspection of the audit trail at that visit also confirmed that no inappropriate changes were made to the data base. Following review of the data and reports from OSI and discussion with the OSI reviewer and the CDTL, this reviewer concludes that it is unlikely that the violations noted during the initial inspection impact the results of the data submitted as part of the BLA.

L41: ## 1. Introduction/Background
L42: Neutroval®, a granulocyte colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) secreted by genetically-engineered bacteria containing the gene for human G-CSF, is seeking U.S. marketing approval under 351(a) of the Public Health Service (PHS) Act. The proposed indication is for the "reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia and the incidence of febrile neutropenia in patients treated with established myelosuppressive chemotherapy". Neupogen® (filgrastim), currently marketed in the U.S., is also a human G-CSF product. Other related products marketed in the U.S., which work through the same or similar receptors on granulocytes for the proposed indication for Neutroval, are Neulasta® (pegfilgrastim) and Leukine® (sargramostim). Neutroval was approved in the EU under an abbreviated pathway for biosimilar products; however, at the time of BLA submission (November 30, 2009), there was no mechanism for abbreviated approval in the U.S. Subsequent to the BLA submission, the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act (PPAC Act) was signed into law (March 23, 2010), which amended the PHS Act to create an abbreviated approval pathway for biological products.
L43: ## 2. Single Trial to Support Efficacy and Other Clinical Trials
L44: Five trials were submitted in support of this BLA, which included two Phase 1 studies (XM02-01 and XM02-05) conducted in a total of 200 healthy volunteers, one study (XM02-02) designed to investigate efficacy and safety in 348 patients, and two additional safety studies (XM02-03, XM02-04) in 329 patients randomized 2:1 to XM02 versus Neupogen. A brief description of these studies are listed below.
L45: - **XM02-01-LT**, Comparative study of pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic parameters of XM02 and Neupogen when formulations are given to healthy volunteers
- **XM02-05-DE**, Study on the bioequivalence of 5 µg/kg or 10 µg/kg of XM02 and Neupogen, each after intravenous or subcutaneous administration, in healthy female and male subjects. A multi-center, randomized, single dose, single blind, two-way crossover design
- **XM02-02-INT**, Efficacy and safety of XM02 compared to Neupogen in patients with breast cancer receiving chemotherapy, a multinational, multi-center, randomized, controlled study. The single trial conducted to establish the efficacy of XM02.
- **XM02-03-INT**, Safety and efficacy of XM02 in patients with small cell or non-small-cell lung cancer receiving platinum-based chemotherapy, a multi-national multi-center, randomized, controlled study
- **XM02-04-INT**, Safety and efficacy of XM02 in patients with Non-Hodgkin's-Lymphoma receiving chemotherapy, a multi-national, multicenter, randomized, controlled study
L46: Studies XM02-02, XM02-03, XM02-04 were conducted in patients with cancers who were scheduled to receive myelosuppressive chemotherapy. Studies XM02-03 and XM02-04 were randomized (1:1), double blind, active-controlled trials that enrolled 240 patients with lung cancer and 92 patients with NHL. These studies were designed to provide an assessment of comparative safety and activity of XM02 to an active control (EU-marketed Neupogen) to aid in the design of a definitive efficacy trial (XM02-02 INT).
L47: A single trial, XM02-02, was conducted to establish the efficacy of XM02 for the proposed indication of reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN). Study XM02-02 is a multinational, multicenter, randomized (2:2:1), active- and placebo-controlled trial conducted in women with high-risk stage II, or with stage III or IV breast cancer receiving initial chemotherapy as adjuvant treatment or as treatment of metastatic disease. The trial included two planned comparisons of the effect on the duration of severe neutropenia following myelosuppressive therapy; these were reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) in the Neutroval arm as compared to placebo arm and non-inferiority of the mean DSN in the Neutroval arm as compared to EU-marketed Neupogen.
L48: A reduction in mean DSN has been accepted by FDA as a valid surrogate for reduction in the incidence of infection and febrile neutropenia when the duration of severe neutropenia in the absence of growth factors is approximately 4-5 days. The major efficacy trial reportedly shows both a significant reduction in the mean DSN and fell within pre-specified bounds set out in the protocol for determination of non-inferiority against an active comparator. FDA did not agree with the non-inferiority margin specified in the clinical protocol.
L49: ## *Treatment Plan*
L50: Study drug treatment with XM02/EU-Neupogen/Placebo was administered at a dose of 5 mcg/kg subcutaneously, daily for 5-14 days, beginning on study day 2. Treatment was to be continued until study day 15 or a post-nadir ANC of \ge 2.0 \times 10^9/L. Patients in the placebo arm were switched to XM02 during chemotherapy cycles 2-4. Concomitant chemotherapy consisted of doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 and docetaxel 75 mg/m2 on day 1 of a 21-day cycle for up to 4 cycles.
L51: ## *Analysis Plan*
L52: The primary efficacy endpoint was the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) in cycle 1. There was no plan specified for testing of secondary endpoints in the protocol which adjusted for alpha spending. Based on this lack of a pre-specified plan for preservation of alpha, the FDA statistical reviewers identified all secondary endpoints as "exploratory".
L53: A single planned interim analysis was planned after 50% of the patients had completed cycle 1 to assess the validity of the assumptions regarding variability, with a proposal for sample size re-estimation in the event that the variability in the DSN was underestimated. The proposal for the interim analysis was revised in protocol amendment 3 to limit the analyses to data from the two active treatment arms.
L54: ## *Results - Efficacy Analyses*
L55: Study XM02-02 registered and randomized 350 subjects at 52 centers in 10 countries. Results demonstrated that the mean duration of severe neutropenia was significantly shorter in XM02-treated patients compared to placebo-treated patients following docetaxel/doxorubicin chemotherapy in women receiving initial chemotherapy treatment for metastatic or high-risk localized breast cancer. After adjustments for "treatment", "country" and "adjuvant vs. metastatic therapy", and baseline ANC value, the mean duration of severe neutropenia in cycle 1 was 1.1 and 3.8 days (p &lt; 0.0001, \chi^2 test) in the XM02 and placebo arms, respectively, with a difference of -2.7 days (95% CI -3.2 days, -2.2 days).
L56: Concerns regarding Teva's methods for imputation of missing data or "replacement" were noted in Dr. Lu's review where she conducted a series of sensitivity analyses to examine the robustness of the treatment effects of XM02 on the duration of severe neutropenia. Dr. Lu determined that the primary analysis conducted by Teva is robust and consistent with the results of the sensitivity analyses.
L57: As noted in Section 2 of Dr. Keegan's summary review, Teva did not provide adequate justification for the non-inferiority margin of a 1-day difference in mean DSN between the XM02 and the active control arm. In addition, Teva did not provide adequate justification that a margin based on treatment effects with US-marketed Neupogen could be extrapolated to treatment effects with the EU-marketed product. Therefore, claims of non-inferiority to Neupogen will not be permitted, however the active-controlled data does provide supportive data for XM02.
L58: Based on the results obtained in the full analysis set, the mean DSN for both XM02 and for EU-marketed Neupogen was 1.1 days. The difference in the mean DSN was 0.03 days and the 95% confidence intervals around this observed difference were -0.26 days and + 0.33 days.
L59: ## 3. Safety
L60: The application contained safety data from five clinical trials; however, data from two pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic studies in which healthy volunteers received a single dose of XM02 are not included below in analyses of safety due to the limited exposure. Safety data are presented in this summary that includes 543 XM02-treated patients enrolled in three studies (XM02-03 (SCLC or NSCLC population), XM02-04 (NHL population) and XM02-02 (metastatic breast cancer population)). Of these 534 patients, 356 patients received only XM02 in combination with 4-6 cycles of chemotherapy. Protocols XM02-03 and XM02-04 had an active control for the first cycle only (n=115) and Protocol XM02-02 INT had a placebo-control for the first cycle only (72 patients) and an active control (134 patients) for the treatment cycles 1-4. Brief synopses of each of these three studies are provided below:
L61: Across the 677 patients enrolled in these three studies, the median age of the safety population was 56 years (range: 20 years to 80 years) and 65% were female. Based on the known toxicity profile of other G-CSF products, Teva anticipated that bone pain would be the most common adverse reaction, and Teva developed a standardized medical query (SMQ) for bone pain. Across all three studies conducted in patients with cancer, the incidence of bone pain was 23.5% in the 534 XM02-treated patients. No patients discontinued the study drug due to bone pain. The safety database was also assessed for reports of adverse reactions identified with other G-CSF products. There were no cases of acute respiratory distress syndrome, sickle cell crisis, or splenic rupture in the XM02 integrated safety database.
L62: The clinical program for XM02 was not adequate in design (placebo-controlled for only one cycle in one study) or size to rule out the possibility of the stimulation or promotion of malignancies involving the myeloid lineage. There were no reported cases of secondary myeloid cancers in the integrated safety database.
L63: ## *Immunogenicity*
L64: The results submitted and performed with the unvalidated assays showed that no patients had binding antibodies or neutralizing antibodies during the study treatment with XM02 during Study XM02-02 (N = 195). Please see Dr. Keegan's summary review for Immunogenicity results for Studies XM02-03 and XM02-04.
L65: The incidence of anti-XM02 antibody formation obtained in clinical studies is not considered reliable since it was not assessed using validated assay methods. The unvalidated immunogenicity assays yielded the following results: five of the 534 patients exposed to XM02 tested positive for binding antibodies during study treatment; 4 of these 5 patients also tested positive for neutralizing antibodies. No evidence of toxicity profile or clinical response was associated with binding antibody or neutralizing antibody development. The impact of immunogenicity on XM02 PK could not be assessed since XM02 PK data were not collected in patients who tested positive for binding or neutralizing antibodies. In order to characterize pharmacologic aspects of the product now and for assessment of planned or inadvertent changes to manufacturing that impact quality, development and characterization of the immunogenic properties with validated assays will be required as post-marketing required studies, if this product is approved in the future.

L72: ## 5. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology
L73: There is an outstanding pharm/tox issue that precludes approval. The application lacks a nonclinical assessment of developmental and reproductive toxicity of XM02 in DART studies conducted in at least one relevant animal species. Teva has not provided adequate scientific justification of why such studies would not be informative or infeasible. Teva has provided information from the published literature; however, since Teva is seeking approval for their product under 351(a) of the PHS Act, the application may not rely on data from other products to establish safety and effectiveness. The published data from other products in this class report abortifacient properties. Characterization of the extent and severity of these properties should be performed prior to approval and included in product labeling.
L74: There were no genotoxicity or carcinogenicity studies performed with XM02 and none were required to support the BLS. The genotoxicity of large molecular weight proteins cannot be assessed using current assay methods and carcinogenicity assessments are not required given the proposed indication and intended patient population.
L75: ## 6. Clinical Pharmacology/Biopharmaceutics
L76: There are no outstanding clinical pharmacology issues that preclude approval. The clinical pharmacology reviewer staff had determined that Teva must conduct an assessment of the impact of Neutroval on QT prolongation. This is based on the assessment of the submitted EKG data and is consistent with advice conveyed to Teva during the pre-IND/pre-BLA meeting.
L77: Minimal dose-exploration was conducted with this product and there was no attempt to explore the dose-response or dose-toxicity relationships or to identify a maximum tolerated dose. The selection of the dose and dose regimen for the efficacy and safety trials were supported by pharmacokinetic and bioavailability studies (bulleted below) conducted in healthy volunteers at two doses and by two routes of administration along with an active comparator (n=176 subjects) and based on the evidence of similar pharmacokinetics and
L78: pharmacodynamic activity to the doses of EU-marketed Neupogen (which are also the same as the recommended doses of the FDA-approved Neupogen®).
L79: - XM02-01 LT was a randomized, dose-comparison, active-controlled, two-arm, two-period, cross-over study comparing the PK and pharmacodynamics (PD) of XM02 and EU-marketed Neupogen in healthy male volunteers.
- XM02-05 DE was a randomized, four-arm, dose- and schedule-comparison of the PK and PD parameters of XM02 and EU-marketed Neulasta. The study was intended to establish bioequivalence between XM02 and EU-marketed Neupogen and also characterized the bioavailability of subcutaneously administered XM02.
L80: The pharmacokinetic profile of XM02 was also assessed in a subgroup of patients with cancer (XM02-02, XM02-03, XM02-04) in two active-controlled trials and in the three-arm, active- and placebo-controlled efficacy trial.
L81: The median T_{max} was 6 hours and the median half-life (t_{1/2}) was 8.9 hours for the 5 mcg/kg dose in healthy volunteers. Increasing the dose of XM02 from 5 to 10 mcg/kg resulted in an approximately 3-fold increase in both C_{max} and AUC 0-48h. In patients with cancer receiving chemotherapy, the median T_{max} of XM02 ranged from 4 to 6 hours and the median t_{1/2} ranged from 3.2 to 3.8 hours. Accumulation after repeated daily dosing was not observed. Based on this cross-study comparison, the pharmacology reviewer concluded that there were no interactions regarding dose adjustment based on the underlying cancer or chemotherapy regimen, across the limited numbers of cancer and chemotherapy regimens included in these studies.
L82: No gender-related differences were observed in the pharmacokinetics of XM02 administered by the subcutaneous route of administration. No dose adjustment is recommended for mild renal impairment. The pharmacokinetic profile in patients with moderate and severe renal impairment has not been assessed. However, based on the safety margin of XM02 and the lack of relationship between the incidence of the major adverse event (bone pain) and degree of renal impairment, an XM02 dosage adjustment would not be clinically warranted. The pharmacokinetic profile in patients with hepatic impairment has not been studied.

L97: ## 12. Decision/Action/Risk Benefit Assessment
L98: **Regulatory Action: Complete Response.** Multiple review disciplines have identified concerns with this application, resulting in recommendations that the application not be approved. There were no disagreements
L99: within disciplines regarding these recommendations and I concur with the individual discipline recommendations as well with Dr. Keegan's recommendations. The issues surrounding a Complete Response action are:
L100: - Based on the FDA inspection of BioGeneriX AG (b) (4) there is concern that the integrity of the database for Study XM02-02-INT, the single trial submitted to support the efficacy of Neutroval, may have been compromised. Specifically, after the initial database lock on January 2, 2006, and subsequent data unblinding, the database was unlocked and the data were altered on at least two separate dates, i.e. January 17th and January 23rd, 2006.

- (b) (4)

- The sponsor has not provided adequate information concerning their device closure system. Based on FDA's assessment, the sponsor appears to be relying solely on the fill weight as the definitive property to decide if the correct amount of therapy is being delivered through the syringe. There are physical aspects of syringes and needles such as dead space/volume, bond strength between the syringe/needle, and spacing of volumetric graduation markings that can impact the performance of the device. Additionally, based on FDA's review of DMF (b) (4) (Drug Master File for (b) (4)), it appears that the syringes may not conform to current FDA consensus standards regarding syringes and needles.

- The literature assessment of the potential reproductive toxicity of granulocyte colony stimulating factor(s) provided in support of BLA 125294 does not fulfill the regulatory requirements for nonclinical developmental and reproductive toxicity studies with Neutroval. This BLA submitted under section 351(a) of the PHS Act may not rely on published literature describing studies of other biological products, including studies regarding a licensed biological product, to fulfill this requirement for approval.

- The proposed proper name for Neutroval, (b) (4) is not acceptable for this BLA submitted under section 351(a) of the PHS Act, (b) (4)
L101: Also, outlined below are areas of product characterization that will require further assessment, either prior to approval or as post-marketing requirements if the five issues above are satisfactorily addressed:
L102: - Characterization of the potential for binding and neutralizing anti-product antibody responses using validated assay methods.
- Further characterization of critical attributes and narrowing of specifications with increased manufacturing experience, including specifications for in-process testing to ensure microbial sterility of the final product.
- Assessment in pediatric patients as required under PREA. Teva plans to assess the pharmacokinetic [systemic exposure (AUC_{0-t})] and pharmacodynamic [absolute neutrophil counts (ANC)] comparability of Neutroval in 50 pediatric patients between the ages of 1 month and 16 yrs 11 months, in three cohorts defined by age with at least 8-10 children per age cohort.
- Characterization of the effects of Neutroval on the corrected QT interval as a predictor for arrhythmic potential in a thorough QT study.
L103: **Risk Benefit Assessment:** Provided that the issues regarding data integrity can be satisfactorily addressed in the resubmission, the findings of a study XM02-02 demonstrate that the benefits of reduction in duration of febrile neutropenia, supported by the finding of a reduction in the incidence of febrile neutropenia, is clinically meaningful and outweigh the risks of transient bone pain which is both modest in severity and self-limited.

L120: ## 1. Introduction
L121: The Biologics License Application (BLA) for Neutroval® seeks U.S. marketing approval under 351(a) of the Public Health Service (PHS) Act for a biologic product, granulocyte colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) secreted by genetically-engineered bacteria containing the gene for human G-CSF. This would be the second human G-CSF product to be marketed in the U.S.; the first product is Neupogen® (filgrastim), manufactured by Amgen, Inc. Related products, which work through the same or similar receptors on granulocytes, that are marketed in the U.S. for the proposed indication sought by Teva, are Neulasta® (pegfilgrastim) also marketed by Amgen, Inc, and Leukine® (sargramostim), marketed by Bayer Healthcare Pharmaceuticals. Neutroval was approved in the EU under an abbreviated pathway for biosimilar products; however at the time of submission of the BLA on November 30, 2009, there was no mechanism for abbreviated approval in the U.S.
L122: The major efficacy data supporting this application are derived from the results of a single well-conducted, placebo- and active-controlled trial that included two planned comparisons of the effect on the duration of severe neutropenia following myelosuppressive therapy; these were reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) in the Neutroval arm as compared to placebo arm and non-inferiority of the mean DSN in the Neutroval arm as compared to EU-marketed Neupogen. A reduction in mean DSN has been accepted by FDA as a valid surrogate for reduction in the incidence of infection and febrile neutropenia when the duration of severe neutropenia in the absence of growth factors is approximately 4-5 days. The major efficacy trial reportedly shows both a significant reduction in the mean DSN and fell within pre-specified bounds set out in the protocol for determination of non-inferiority against an active comparator. As will be discussed in Section 2 and 7 below, FDA did not agree that with the non-inferiority margin specified in the clinical protocol.

L158: ## 4. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology
L159: I concur with the conclusions reached by the pharmacology/toxicology reviewer that there is one outstanding pharm/tox issue that precludes approval. The application lacks a nonclinical assessment of developmental and reproductive toxicity of XM02 in DART studies conducted in at least one relevant animal species. Teva has not provided adequate scientific justification for as to why such studies would not be informative or infeasible; Teva has provided information from the published literature. Since Teva is seeking approval for their product under 351(a) of the PHS Act, the application may not rely on data from other products to establish safety and effectiveness (literature references) and cannot rely on FDA's previous findings of safety and effectiveness for another product without appropriate right of reference. The review staff considered these data necessary to obtain prior to approval in order to ensure that it is accurately labeled. The published data from other products in this class (granulocyte colony stimulating factors) report abortifacient properties. Characterization of the extent and severity of these properties should be performed prior to approval and included in product labeling.
L160: The nonclinical safety program for XM02 consisted of two GLP 26-week repeat dose toxicity studies with 4-week recovery periods in rats (0, 5, 50, and 500 mcg/kg/day) and monkeys (0, 5, 25, and 125 mcg/kg/day), and in a standard battery of GLP safety pharmacology studies. The rat was determined to be the most sensitive species, with a maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of XM02 of 50 mcg/kg/day, based on the observation of a 20% mortality (deaths in 12 of 60 rats) in the 125 mcg/kg/day dose cohort. Mortality in these animals was attributed to severe pain caused by enlarged, swollen joints in hind paws and limbs. The MTD was not reached in the monkey, with no adverse clinical signs at doses up to 125 mcg/kg/day.
L161: The major toxicities associated with XM02 treatment in rats and monkeys resulted from the exaggerated pharmacology of G-CSF and consisted of bone marrow hypercellularity, enlarged spleens, increased alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and extramedullary hematopoiesis (rat)/granulocyte infiltrations (monkey). These effects were dose-dependent and reversible. There were no detectable effects on the respiratory or central nervous system in rats or the cardiovascular system in dogs after a single dose of 3500 mcg/kg XM02 in the GLP safety pharmacology studies.
L162: There were no genotoxicity or carcinogenicity studies performed with XM02 and none were required to support the BLS. The genotoxicity of large molecular weight proteins cannot be assessed using current assay methods and carcinogenicity assessments are not required given the proposed indication and intended patient population.

L165: XM02 is non-glycosylated protein, produced by recombinant DNA technology in *E. coli*. XM02 contain the same amino acid sequence as the native human granulocyte colony stimulating factor (G-CSF) with the exception of an N-terminal methionine necessary for transcription in *E. coli*. Human G-CSF regulates the proliferation and differentiation of myeloid progenitor cells, resulting in an increase in the number of mature functional neutrophils in the peripheral blood. XM02 exerts its effects through the same specific, high-affinity receptor on the surface of myeloid progenitor cells and mature myeloid cells as human G-CSF.

L184: Five trials were submitted in support of this BLA. L185: Three studies were conducted in patients with cancers who were scheduled to receive myelosuppressive chemotherapy. Two of these trials were randomized (1:1), double blind, active-controlled trials, XM02-03 and XM02-04 that enrolled 240 patients with conducted in lung cancer and 92 patients with NHL, respectively. Study XM02-03 and Study XM02-04 were designed to provide an assessment of comparative safety and activity of XM02 to an active control (EU-marketed Neupogen) to aid in the design of a definitive efficacy trial (XM02-02 INT). The design of these trials and results of pharmacokinetic data are summarized in section 5 and the safety information from these trials is included in the integrated safety data discussed in section 8 of this summary review.
L186: There was a single trial, XM02-02-INT, conducted to establish the efficacy of XM02 for the proposed indication of reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN). Study XM02-02 is a multinational, multicenter, randomized (2:2:1), active- and placebo-controlled trial conducted in women with high-risk stage II, or with stage III or IV breast cancer receiving initial chemotherapy as adjuvant treatment or as treatment of metastatic disease. Due to the protocol-specified body-weight dosing (5 mcg/kg body weight) of study drug and the different fill volumes of the XM02 and EU-Neupogen syringes, the drug administrator and the pharmacist were not blinded to study drug treatment.
L187: The co-primary objectives of Study XM02-02 were to confirm assay sensitivity with respect to duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) by comparing XM 02 versus placebo and to demonstrate the equivalence of XM02 and EU-marketed Neupogen during the first cycle of chemotherapy with respect to DSN. DSN was defined as the number of days with an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) of less than or equal to 0.5 \times 10^9 neutrophils/L. As discussed below, these endpoints were tested hierarchically.
L188: Additional study objectives were
L189: - Incidence of febrile neutropenia per cycle and across all cycles (febrile neutropenia defined as body temperature of &gt; 38.5^{\circ}\text{C} for more than one hour, axillary measurement with a calibrated standard device, and absolute neutrophil count (ANC) &lt; 0.5 \times 10^9/\text{L})
- DSN in cycles 2 to 4
- Depth of ANC nadir in cycles 1 to 4
- Times to ANC recovery in cycles 1 to 4
- Mortality
- Incidence of adverse events AEs
- Changes of safety laboratory parameters
- Immunogenicity (development of antibodies against XM 02 and EU-Neupogen)
L190: ## *Treatment Plan*
L191: Study drug treatment with XM02/EU-Neupogen/Placebo was administered at a dose of 5 mcg/kg subcutaneously, daily for 5-14 days, beginning on study day 2. Treatment was to be continued until study day 15 or a post-nadir ANC of \ge 2.0 \times 10^9/\text{L}. Patients in the placebo arm were switched to XM02 during chemotherapy cycles 2-4.
L192: Concomitant chemotherapy consisted of doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 and docetaxel 75 mg/m2 on day 1 of a 21-day cycle for up to 4 cycles. Appropriate dose modifications for chemotherapy-related toxicity were included in the protocol. Treatment could be delayed up to 14 days to allow recover from myelosuppression, defined as ANC \ge 1.5 \times 10^9/\text{L} and platelets \ge 100 \times 10^9/\text{L}.
L193: ## *Study Monitoring Plan*
L194: Study monitoring during cycle 1, included measurement of ANC within 24 hours prior to chemotherapy, then daily until study day 15 or an ANC of \ge 2.0 \times 10^9/\text{L}, whichever was longer. In cycles 2, 3, and 4, ANC was assessed within 24 hours before chemotherapy and then daily starting on Day 5 until Day 15, or longer until ANC reached \ge 2.0 \times 10^9/\text{L}. In each cycle, body temperature (axillary) was measured daily until Day 15, or longer until ANC reached \ge 2.0 \times 10^9/\text{L}. Safety assessments (laboratory parameters, antibody sampling, physical examination, vital signs) were performed at screening, within 24 hours before chemotherapy in each cycle (Day 1), and at the end of study visit. Patients were monitored for adverse events (AEs) and concomitant medication throughout the study.
L195: Three weeks after the last chemotherapy infusion (Day 85 \pm 1), end of study assessments were performed. On Day 180 \pm 5, there was an additional assessment for determination of antibodies against XM 02 or EU-Neupogen.
L196: ## *Analysis Plan*
L197: The primary efficacy endpoint was the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) in cycle 1. There was one planned interim analysis for re-estimation of sample size in the event that the assumptions regarding
L198: The analysis was to be conducted in two stages, with the efficacy comparison (bioequivalence) to be conducted only if the assumptions regarding assay sensitivity with respect to the DSN
L199: were confirmed. The first comparison was of DSN in cycle 1 between the XM02 and placebo arms. If this comparison demonstrated a statistically significant difference on DSN (ANCOVA, p&lt;0.05, two-sided), in favor of the XM02 arm, then the analysis for equivalence in DSN in cycle 1 between the XM02 and the EU-marketed Neupogen arms could be conducted. Due to this hierarchical testing procedure, no adjustment of the significance was required.
L200: Based on the analysis plan, the two products (XM 02 and EU-marketed Neupogen) would be determined to be equivalent if the two-sided 95% confidence interval for the difference in DSN between the two arms excluded 1 day.
L201: There was no plan specified for testing of secondary endpoints in the protocol which adjusted for alpha spending. Based on this lack of a pre-specified plan for preservation of alpha, the FDA statistical reviewers identified all secondary endpoints as “exploratory”.
L202: The study sample size of 140 patients per active treatment arm was based on the following assumptions.
L203: - 109 patients per active treatment arm will be required to reject the null hypothesis (that the difference in the mean DSN between the treatment and active control arm is greater than 1.0 days) with 90% power, based on the assumption that the observed difference in mean DSN will be less than 0.25 days and that the standard deviation is 1.7 days.
- 20% of the patients registered and randomized will not be eligible for per-protocol analysis. A single planned interim analysis was planned after 50% of the patients had completed cycle 1 to assess the validity of the assumptions regarding variability, with a proposal for sample size re-estimation in the event that the variability in the DSN was underestimated. The proposal for the interim analysis was revised in amendment 3 to the protocol to limit the analyses to data from the two active treatment arms.
L204: The statistical analysis plan identified three patient populations for data analysis:
L205: - **Full analysis set:** The full analysis set comprises all randomized patients
- **Safety set:** The safety set comprises all patients of the full analysis set who received at least one dose of study treatment.
- **Per protocol set:** The per protocol set comprises all patients of the full analysis set, who received at least one cycle of chemotherapy, who received their study treatment (XM 02 or Filgrastim) for at least one cycle and who did not have any major protocol violations including violations of inclusion and exclusion criteria.
L206: Demographic and baseline characteristics were to be evaluated for the full analysis set and for the per-protocol set.
L207: The efficacy analyses were to be conducted in the per-protocol set (primary efficacy analysis) and for the full analysis set (supportive analyses).

L216: ## *Efficacy Analyses*
L217: Study XM02-02 demonstrated that the mean duration of severe neutropenia was significantly shorter in XM02-treated patients compared to placebo-treated patients following docetaxel/doxorubicin chemotherapy in women receiving initial chemotherapy treatment for metastatic or high-risk localized breast cancer. After adjustments for “treatment”, “country” and “adjuvant vs. metastatic therapy”, and baseline ANC value, the mean duration of severe neutropenia in cycle 1 was 1.1 and 3.8 days (p &lt; 0.0001, \chi^2 test) in the XM02 and placebo arms, respectively, with a difference of -2.7 days (95% CI -3.2 days, -2.2 days).
L218: As noted in Dr. Lu's review, due to her concerns regarding Teva's methods for imputation of missing data or “replacement”, she conducted a series of sensitivity analyses to examine the robustness of the treatment effects of XM02 on the duration of severe neutropenia. There were two specific imputation methods that concerned Dr. Lu. First, in Teva's primary analysis comparing with XM02 arm with the placebo arm, the observed DSN for placebo patients who received therapeutic G-CSF treatment was replaced with the median DSN for all placebo patients who received no G-CSF treatment. Dr. Lu noted that this approach could underestimate the variability of DSN in placebo arm and exaggerate the statistical significance of the difference in DSN. Second, Dr. Lu raised concerns regarding imputing values for missing ANCs, based on linear interpolation using the ANC values immediately before and after the missing value. Dr. Lu raised questions regarding the validity of this method since it assumes an ANC value to be in-between the ANC values before and after. Based on the results of the sensitivity analyses (summarized in Tables 6 and 7 in Section 3.1.4 of Dr. Lu's review), Dr. Lu determined that the primary analysis conducted by Teva is robust and consistent with the results of the sensitivity analyses.
L219: The mean duration of severe neutropenia observed in the placebo-control arm is lower than anticipated based on historical controls. Thus the inclusion of a placebo-arm, as recommended by the EU regulatory authorities to confirm assay sensitivity, was warranted. Historical data available at the time of the approval of Neulasta showed that, in the absence of growth factor support, chemotherapy regimens similar to the one used in XM02-02 (i.e., doxorubicin 60 \text{mg}/\text{m}^2 and docetaxel 75 \text{mg}/\text{m}^2 administered every three weeks) resulted in a 100% incidence of severe neutropenia with a mean duration of severe neutropenia of 5-7 days and incidence of febrile neutropenia of 30-40%. Only with chemotherapeutic regimens that have a prolonged duration of severe neutropenia (5-7 days) is a reduction in mean duration of severe neutropenia a valid surrogate for reduction in febrile neutropenia. Due to the inclusion of a placebo control, confirmation that the surrogate was valid was possible.
L220: Febrile neutropenia was defined as body temperature of &gt;38.5^\circ\text{C} for more than one hour, and \text{ANC} &lt; 0.5 \times 10^9/\text{L}, occurring on the same day. In addition, patients receiving systemic antibiotics in a cycle were considered as having FN in this cycle. If these two conditions (temperature &gt;38.5^\circ\text{C} for &gt;1 hour and \text{ANC} &lt; 0.5 \times 10^9/\text{L}) were met, regardless of whether systemic antibiotics were administered, this was categorized as “observed FN”. If systemic antibiotics were given, regardless of whether the two “threshold conditions” were met, this was categorized as “protocol defined FN”. The majority of the events were defined by administration of systemic antibiotics in all three arms (see Dr. Lu's review for details). The incidence of observed or of protocol-defined febrile neutropenia was lower in the Neuroval arm compared to the placebo arm (12.1% vs. 36.1%). Since the protocol did not prospectively specify the method for adjustment of secondary
L221: endpoints, comparative claims for the reduction in the incidence of febrile neutropenia in the package insert or promotional labeling should not include p-values. However, FDA finds this comparison compelling because it confirms that the surrogacy of reduction for DSN for reduction in febrile neutropenia is present even with the shorter than anticipated DSN in the placebo-arm.
L222: As noted in Section 2 of this summary review, Teva did not provide adequate justification for the non-inferiority margin of a 1-day difference in mean DSN between the XM02 and the active control arm. In addition, Teva did not provide adequate justification that a margin based on treatment effects with US-marketed Neupogen could be extrapolated to treatment effects with the EU-marketed product. Therefore, claims of non-inferiority to Neupogen will not be permitted, however the active-controlled data does provide supportive data for XM02.
L223: Based on the results obtained in the full analysis set, the mean DSN for both XM02 and for EU-marketed Neupogen was 1.1 days. The difference in the mean DSN was 0.03 days and the 95% confidence intervals around this observed difference were -0.26 days and + 0.33 days. The results in the per-protocol set were essentially the same.

L225: The application contained safety data from five clinical trials. The size of the safety database and duration of exposure is sufficient for characterization of treatment-emergent signals although data are somewhat limited by the use of active-controlled study designs and limited amount of controlled data beyond chemotherapy cycle 1, as will be discussed further in this section of the review. L227: Data from two pharmacokinetic/pharmacodynamic studies in which healthy volunteers received a single dose of XM02 are not included below in analyses of safety due to the limited exposure. Safety data are presented in this summary that includes 541 XM02-treated patients enrolled in three studies conducted in patients with cancer, Studies XM02-03, XM02-04 and XM02-02. Of these 541 patients, 356 patients received only XM02 in combination with 4-6 cycles of chemotherapy. Analyses of safety conducted by Dr. Herndon evaluated results within individual studies and pooled analyses across all studies conducted in patients with cancer. The dose and duration of exposure was adequate to assess safety for the intended use. The safety data in this application is limited by the availability of internally-controlled data to cycle 1 in two of the three studies and availability of a placebo-control for only one study, which is also limited to the first cycle. The design of these studies limits the ability to detect safety signals, since it is contingent on detection of signals that occur at higher rates than the active control. Protocols XM02-03 and XM02-04 had an active control for the first cycle only (n=115) and Protocol XM02-02 INT had a placebo-control for the first cycle only (72 patients) and an active control (136 patients) for the treatment cycles 1-4. Brief synopses of each of these three studies are provided below:
L228: - **Study XM02-03:** There were 240 patients with small cell lung cancer (SCLC) or advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) who were previously untreated or had received only one prior chemotherapy regimen enrolled in this trial, of these 237 received at least one dose of study drug in following chemotherapy. Concomitant chemotherapy consisted of platinum-based chemotherapy administered in 3- or 4-week cycles for up to 6 cycles. Patients were randomized (2:1) to receive XM02 (n=158) or EU-marketed Neupogen (n=79) during cycle 1; in all subsequent chemotherapy cycles, patients received open-label XM02. The dose of XM02 was 5 mcg/kg/day subcutaneously administered following chemotherapy for 14 days or until evidence of neutrophil recovery.

- **Study XM02-04:** There were 92 patients with previously untreated aggressive NHL enrolled in this randomized, active-controlled trial enrolled in this trial; all patients received at least one dose of study drug in following chemotherapy. Concomitant chemotherapy consisted of CHOP (cyclophosphamide IV 750 mg/m2, doxorubicin IV 50 mg/m2, and vincristine IV 1.4 mg/m2 on day 1 of each treatment cycle and prednisolone 100 mg/day days 1-5 of each cycle) for up to 6 21-day cycles. Patients were randomized (2:1) to receive XM02 (n=63) or EU-marketed Neupogen (n=29) during chemotherapy cycle 1; in all subsequent chemotherapy cycles, patients received open-label XM02. The dose of XM02 was 5 mcg/kg/day subcutaneously administered following non-steroid chemotherapy for 14 days or until evidence of neutrophil recovery.

- **Study XM02-02:** There were 350 patients with high-risk stage II or metastatic breast cancer who had received no previous systemic treatment for breast cancer, enrolled in this trial; of these 350 patients, 348 received at least one dose of study drug in following chemotherapy. Concomitant chemotherapy consisted of docetaxel 75 mg/m2 and doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 in 3-week cycles for up to 4 cycles. Patients were randomized (2:2:1) to receive XM02 (n=140), EU-marketed Neupogen (n= 136), or placebo (n= 72) during cycle 1; in all subsequent chemotherapy cycles, patients randomized to placebo received XM02. The dose of XM02 was 5 mcg/kg/day subcutaneously administered following chemotherapy for 14 days or until evidence of neutrophil recovery.

L249: ## 9. Advisory Committee Meeting
L250: This is the fourth product indicated for the prevention of severe neutropenia and febrile neutropenia in patients with cancer receiving myelosuppressive therapy and the third product which acts specifically on the G-CSF receptor. The primary endpoint, a reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia following myelosuppressive chemotherapy, is an accepted surrogate for febrile neutropenia when the duration of severe neutropenia in the absence of a neutrophil growth factor is at least 4-5 days but less than 14 days (i.e., not prolonged as is the case with induction chemotherapy for acute leukemia or following hematopoietic progenitor cell transplantation). There were no unexpected clinical safety or efficacy issues requiring the advice of the Oncologic Drugs Advisory Committee.
L251: ## 10. Pediatrics
L252: I concur with the CDTL and the PeRC's recommendations regarding Teva's plans to conduct of pediatric studies required under PREA. The BLA contained a request for deferral of pediatric studies and proposed to conduct trial in pediatric patients as required post-marketing commitment. Specifically, Teva requested a partial waiver of pediatric studies under PREA for neonates up to 1 month of age, deferral of studies in children between the ages of 1 month to (b)(4) years and provided a proposed plan for assessing the comparability of Neutroval pharmacokinetics and pharmacodynamics between adults and children as deferred studies. This request was evaluated by the clinical review team and reviewed by the PeRC PREA Subcommittee on August 11, 2010. The PeRC agreed with the Division to grant a partial waiver and the deferral for this product.
L253: I do not concur with the PeRC's premise which underlies their recommendation that Teva be required to conduct a study to establish a safe and effective dose in pediatric patients if the 5 mcg/kg/day dose does not result in "similar responses as compared to adults". There is no way to determine a safe and effective dose based on pharmacokinetic comparability since the exposure-response relationship for this product has not been established. Further, the exposure-response relationship is particularly difficult for this product due to age-related
L254: differences in marrow cellularity and the unique receptor-mediated clearance of this product. Therefore, an efficacy study will be required to establish a safe and effective dose for pediatric patients for purposes of describing a recommended dose for pediatric patients/subpopulations in product labeling. Regardless of the outcome of the PK/PD study, efficacy studies will be required to support a specific claim for pediatric patients or subpopulations of pediatric patients.
L255: ## 11. Other Relevant Regulatory Issues

L294: ## 2. Background
L295: Granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) is a hematopoietic cytokine and an endogenous growth factor that regulates the proliferation and differentiation of progenitor cells within the bone marrow and the release of mature neutrophils into the peripheral blood. G-CSF is a positive regulator of granulopoiesis, acting at different stages of myeloid cell development. G-CSF enhances the effector functions of normal mature neutrophils, including chemotaxis, phagocytosis and oxidative metabolism. It exerts its effects via a high-affinity G-CSF-specific receptor mechanism, accounting for its selective action as compared with many other cytokines. The endogenous, human form of G-CSF is a glycoprotein composed of a single polypeptide chain of (b) (4) amino acids. A bacterially synthesized, non-glycosylated commercial recombinant methionyl human G-CSF (r-metHuG-CSF) was approved by FDA in 1991 under the proper name filgrastim (Neupogen ®). Neupogen is indicated to decrease the incidence of infection, as manifested by febrile neutropenia (FN), in patients with nonmyeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive chemotherapy (CTX), and for reducing the time to neutrophil recovery and the duration of fever following induction or consolidation chemotherapy treatment of adults with AML. Neupogen is also indicated for reducing the duration of neutropenia in patients undergoing myeloablative therapy followed by bone marrow transplantation, and for the mobilization of autologous peripheral blood stem cells as monotherapy or after myelosuppressive CTX. Finally, Neupogen is indicated in long-term treatment of severe congenital, cyclical or idiopathic neutropenia.
L296: Intensive cytotoxic chemotherapy for the treatment of cancer often results in profound neutropenia, which may lead to hospitalization for treatment of fever or to potentially fatal infections. The most widely accepted definition of neutropenia is an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) &lt;500 cells/\muL or &lt;1,000 cells/\muL with a predicted nadir of &lt;500 cells/\muL. There is an increased risk of occult infection in patients with an ANC &lt;1,000 cells/\muL, and the risk of infection increases as the ANC decreases. Fever in a neutropenic patient is usually defined as a single temperature of &gt;38.3°C (101°F), or a sustained temperature &gt;38°C (100.4°F) for more than one hour. Patients who are neutropenic may not exhibit fever as their presenting symptom despite the presence of infection. This occurs more often in elderly patients or patients receiving corticosteroids or other anti-pyretics.
L297: Factors contributing to the pathogenesis of febrile neutropenia include the direct effects of chemotherapy on mucosal barriers and immune deficits related to the underlying malignancy. Chemotherapy-induced mucositis followed by seeding of the blood stream with endogenous flora from the entire GI tract is believed to explain a majority of febrile neutropenic episodes. Obstruction of the lymphatics, biliary tract, bronchial, gastrointestinal or urinary systems by tumors, or as a result of surgical procedures, or infections related to indwelling catheters are also common causes of infections. Immune defects related to underlying malignancies also put patients at higher risk for infections.
L298: Colony Stimulating Factors (CSFs) have been evaluated for prophylactic use following the administration of chemotherapy when neutropenia is anticipated ("primary prophylaxis"), during retreatment after a previous cycle of chemotherapy causing febrile neutropenia ("secondary prophylaxis"), and to shorten the duration of severe chemotherapy-induced neutropenia without fever ("afebrile neutropenia"). CSFs have also been evaluated as an adjunct to other therapies in patients with febrile neutropenia. The likelihood of developing febrile neutropenia is the primary factor determining whether or not prophylactic CSFs are indicated. The incidence of febrile neutropenia following treatment is influenced by the intensity of chemotherapy, the degree of injury to the gastrointestinal mucosa, the presence of underlying damage to the patient's hematopoietic stem cells, the concurrent use of radiation, and the overall clinical status of the patient. Current American Society of Clinical Oncology guidelines recommend prophylactic treatment with CSFs when the expected incidence of febrile neutropenia is over 20 percent. Treatment is suggested to reduce the need for hospitalizations for antibiotic therapy. If the incidence of febrile neutropenia is expected to be less than 10 percent following chemotherapy, CSFs are not recommended for routine primary prophylaxis. CSFs can also be used to maintain dose-dense or dose-intense chemotherapy strategies having survival benefits, or in settings where reductions in chemotherapy dose-intensity or dose-density are known to be associated with a poorer prognosis. When the risk of febrile neutropenia would prevent the administration of full doses of potentially curative chemotherapy, administration of CSFs is recommended for secondary prophylaxis in lieu of dose reduction. In patients with established afebrile neutropenia, CSFs are not recommended.
L299: In the United States, in addition to filgrastim (Neupogen), the other currently approved G-CSF product is pegfilgrastim (Neulasta). Pegfilgrastim received regular approval in 2002. Current labeled indications are to decrease the incidence of infection, as manifested by febrile
L300: neutropenia, in patients with non-myeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anti-cancer drugs associated with a clinically significant incidence of febrile neutropenia.

L331: ## 4. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology
L332: The following excerpts and summary, revised for this review, are based on the review provided by Mary Jane Masson Hinrichs, Ph.D., Division of Biologic Oncology Products.
L333: Among the regulatory requirements to support approval of a BLA under section 351 (a) of the PHS Act are assessments of the nonclinical developmental and reproductive risks associated with the product. In general, an embryo-fetal toxicology study in a single species is required. However, class effects of certain agents that are well characterized with regard to their effect on nonclinical developmental and reproductive risks have been relied upon to support risk communication in labeling in the past. These effects have been demonstrated by an assessment of the scientific literature. The Applicant in this case has not performed an embryo-fetal toxicology study, but instead has provided a literature assessment of the developmental and reproductive risks. During the review cycle, the Division was advised by the Office of Regulatory Policy (ORP), the Office of Chief Counsel (OCC), the Biosimilar Review Committee, and the Biosimilar Implementation Committee that a literature assessment of the potential reproductive toxicity of XM02 does not fulfill the regulatory requirements for assessing nonclinical developmental and reproductive toxicity under section 351(a) of the PHS Act. An application filed under this section may not rely on published literature describing studies of other biological products, including licensed products, to fulfill the requirement. As a result, the nonclinical team recommends that this application receive a CR Letter which notifies the Applicant that a full battery of reproductive toxicology studies is required.
L334: Nonclinical safety of XM02 was assessed in two Good Laboratory Practices (GLP) 26-week repeat dose toxicity studies with 4-week recovery periods in rats (doses of 0, 5, 50, and 500 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day}) and monkeys (doses of 0, 5, 25, and 125 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day}), as well as a standard battery of GLP safety pharmacology studies. From the repeat dose toxicity studies, the rat was determined to be the most sensitive species because of a significant number of animal mortality (20%) in the high dose group. As a result, 50 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day} was determined to be the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of XM02 in the rat. In contrast, there were no deaths or adverse clinical signs in the monkey repeat dose toxicity study at doses of XM02 up to 125 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day}. The major toxicities associated with XM02 treatment in both species, i.e., bone marrow hypercellularity, enlarged spleens, increased alkaline phosphatase (ALP), extramedullary hematopoiesis (rat), and granulocyte infiltrations (monkey), were dose-dependent, related to the exaggerated pharmacology of G-CSF, and reversible in recovery animals. Similarly, the bone (joint) findings of painful, swollen hind limbs that led to the premature euthanization of a number of the animals in the high dose group in the rat repeat dose toxicity study, were dose-dependent, reversible, and also likely related to the exaggerated pharmacology of G-CSF since there were histological findings of hyperostosis and granulocytic infiltrates in articular surfaces and surrounding soft tissue of the bone. There were no detectable effects on the respiratory or central nervous system in rats or the cardiovascular system in dogs after a single dose of 3500 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} XM02 in the GLP safety pharmacology studies.
L335: There were no specific genotoxicity or carcinogenicity studies performed with XM02 since genotoxicity studies are not appropriate for large molecular weight proteins and a carcinogenicity assessment is not warranted for the intended patient population.
L336: There were no developmental and reproductive toxicology studies conducted with XM02. The Applicant's proposed justification for the absence of these studies was based on the clinical indication, which limits use of XM02 to cancer patients receiving concomitant therapy with
L337: anti-cancer agents with known reproductive and developmental risks. Upon review of this justification by the nonclinical review team, it was determined that it would be unethical to not communicate the potential reproductive and developmental risks associated with XM02 to patients and prescribers since there are currently two US-licensed recombinant G-CSF products that are labeled with this information. As a result, the Applicant was requested to provide an assessment of the potential reproductive toxicity of XM02 as an amendment to the BLA. In response to this request, the Applicant submitted a literature-based assessment of the reproductive toxicities associated with other G-CSF receptor agonists to support a 'class-labeling' approach. Upon review of the submitted literature, it was determined by the Pharmacology/Toxicology primary and secondary reviewers that the developmental and reproductive toxicities associated with recombinant G-CSF products, primarily an abortifacient effect, were well-characterized and consistent within the scientific class. A class labeling approach to communicate the developmental and reproductive risks of XM02 was deemed acceptable from the nonclinical perspective. However, as noted previously, during the review cycle the Division was advised by ORP, OCC, the Biosimilar Review Committee, and the Biosimilar Implementation Committee that a literature assessment of the potential reproductive toxicity of XM02 does not fulfill the regulatory requirements for assessing nonclinical developmental and reproductive toxicity under section 351(a) of the PHS Act. As a result, the nonclinical team has amended their review to recommend that this application receive a CR Letter which notifies the Applicant that a full battery of reproductive toxicology studies is required.

L339: *The following excerpt, revised for this review, is based on the review provided by Sarah Schrieber, Pharm.D., Division of Clinical Pharmacology 5*
L340: XM02, Neutroval, is a recombinant methionyl human granulocyte colony-stimulating factor (G-CSF) produced by recombinant DNA technology using the bacterial strain *Escherichia coli* K802. It has a molecular weight of 18,799 Dalton and is composed of 175 amino acids. Neutroval is not glycosylated and contains a methionine residue at its amino (\text{NH}_2)-terminal end that is not present on endogenous human G-CSF. Neutroval is a sterile, clear, colorless, preservative-free solution containing [REDACTED](b)(4), glacial acetic acid, sorbitol, polysorbate 80, and Water for Injection.
L341: The selection of dose and dose regimen for the efficacy and safety trials were based on data obtained in the phase 1 trials and historical clinical use with an FDA-approved product, Neupogen® (Amgen). To support this application, two phase 1 PK/PD trials in healthy volunteers (N=176) and three phase 3 trials in patients with breast cancer, lung cancer, or non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) (N=348, 240, and 92, respectively) were conducted.
L342: The pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamics (PD) of XM02 were studied in healthy subjects and patients with cancer.
L343: ## **Pharmacokinetics Findings:**
L344: **Healthy Subjects:** Subjects in the phase 1 trials were assigned to receive single 5 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} or 10 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} IV or SC doses of Neutroval or European Neupogen (EU Neupogen). The absolute bioavailability of these doses were 33% and 45%, respectively. After single dose SC
L345: administration of 5 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} Neutroval (N=33), the geometric mean (CV%) of serum C_{\text{max}} was 18 ng/mL (41%) and of \text{AUC}_{0-48\text{h}} was 158 ng\cdoth/mL (37%). The median T_{\text{max}} was 6 hours and the median half-life (t_{1/2}) was 8.9 hours. Increasing the dose of Neutroval from 5 to 10 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} resulted in an approximately 3-fold increase in both C_{\text{max}} and \text{AUC}_{0-48\text{h}}.
L346: **Cancer Patients:** PK data were obtained from patients with breast cancer, lung cancer and NHL (N=12 per group) who received SC Neutroval 5 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day}. Following the 1st Neutroval dose in cycle 1, the geometric mean (CV%) of serum C_{\text{max}} and \text{AUC}_{0-24\text{h}} were 36 ng/mL (41%) and 305 ng\cdoth/mL (35%) in breast cancer, 25 ng/mL (60%) and 273 ng\cdoth/mL in lung cancer (61%), and 20 ng/mL (24%) and 184 ng\cdoth/mL (23%) in NHL, respectively. For the 3 groups combined, the median T_{\text{max}} was between 4 to 6 hours and the median t_{1/2} was between 3.2 to 3.8 hours. The terminal half-life was calculated from serum levels measured up to 24 hours as compared to up to 48 hours in the healthy subjects. Accumulation after repeated dosing was not observed. No dose adjustment based on cancer type is warranted.
L347: No gender-related differences were observed in the PK of Neutroval following SC administration. The PK profile in patients with renal or hepatic impairment has not been assessed.
L348: ## **Pharmacodynamic Findings:**
L349: **Healthy Subjects:** In healthy subjects who received a single SC Neutroval dose of 5 or 10 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}, a transient decrease in the ANC occurred during the first hour, followed by an increase in ANC. Following subcutaneous 5 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} (N=61), the mean maximum ANC was 22 x 10^9/\text{L}; the median time to peak ANC was 12 hours. After subcutaneous 10 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} (N=58), the mean maximum ANC was 26 x 10^9/\text{L}; the median time to peak ANC was between 14 to 18 hours. The ANC returned to baseline by 4 days post-dose.
L350: **Cancer Patients:** In cancer patients receiving various chemotherapies (N=361), SC Neutroval 5 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} was administered daily starting 24 hours after the completion of chemotherapy until an ANC of 10 x 10^9/\text{L} after a nadir was reached or after 14 days of Neutroval dosing, whichever occurred first. During cycle 1, the time to maximum mean ANC was between 3 to 5 days, and then ANC decreased to a nadir by 7 to 11 days following chemotherapy. Mean ANC values returned to baseline by 21 days following completion of chemotherapy. The ANC profile was similar in subsequent chemotherapy cycles.

L393: There were a total of 27 deaths reported from all three trials, with the greatest number (22) reported in study XM02-03, which was conducted in patients with lung cancer. L465: Approval of this biologic license application (BLA) for XM02 (NeutrovalTM) is not recommended for reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia and the incidence of febrile neutropenia in patients with non-myeloid malignancies receiving myelosuppressive anti-cancer drugs associated with a clinically significant incidence of febrile neutropenia. XM02 demonstrated efficacy in decreasing the incidence of severe neutropenia compared to placebo, and has a safety profile that closely resembles a similar product, Neupogen® (EU-Neupogen) that is marketed in the European Union. The efficacy of XM02 is based on the results of a single, 3 arm, randomized (2:2:1) double-blind, active- and placebo-controlled trial of 348 patients with breast cancer receiving myelosuppressive chemotherapy (doxorubicin 60 mg/m2 and docetaxel 75 mg/m2 administered every 21 days for up to 4 cycles). The major efficacy outcome measure was the duration of severe neutropenia in cycle 1, defined as the number of days with an ANC &lt; 0.5 x 109/L. XM02, an active G-CSF comparator (EU-Neupogen), and a placebo were administered at a dose of 5 mcg/kg subcutaneously daily starting 24 hours after the completion of chemotherapy until an ANC of 10 x 109/L occurred following a nadir or after 14 days of dosing, whichever occurred first.
L466: The mean duration of severe neutropenia in cycle 1 was highly statistically significant, 3.8 and 1.1 days (p &lt; 0.0001, \chi^2 test) when the XM02 and placebo arms, respectively, were compared with a difference of -2.7 days (95% confidence interval -3.2 days, -2.2 days). The incidence of febrile neutropenia was lower in the XM02 arm compared to the placebo arm (12.1% versus 36.1%). Patients in the EU-Neupogen arm had a mean duration of severe neutropenia of 1.1 days. The difference in mean duration of severe neutropenia between the XM02 and EU-Neupogen arms was 0.03 days (95% confidence interval -0.26 days, +0.33 days).
L467: The safety database in cancer patients includes 677 patients who received at least one dose of XM02 following myelosuppressive chemotherapy in three clinical trials. Study XM02-02 was a placebo-controlled and active-controlled, randomized clinical trial in patients with breast cancer (N = 348) and Study XM02-03 and Study XM02-04 were active-controlled, randomized clinical trials conducted in patients with lung cancer (N = 237) and patients with lymphoma (N = 92). Both XM02 and EU-Neupogen were administered at a dose of 5 mcg/kg per day. In Study XM02-02, the most frequent treatment-emergent adverse reaction that occurred at a higher incidence in patients treated with Neutroval compared with patients treated with placebo was bone pain (3.4% vs. 1.4% in cycle 1). In Study XM02-02 and Study XM02-03, Adverse Events occurred at similar frequencies and intensities in the XM02 and EU-Neupogen arms.

L485: ## Neutropenic Fever in the Patient Receiving Chemotherapy
L486: Intensive cytotoxic chemotherapy for the treatment of cancer often causes profound neutropenia, which may result in hospitalization for treatment of fever or cause potentially fatal infections. The definition of neutropenia varies from institution to institution but is usually defined as an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) &lt; 500 cells/mcL or &lt; 1,000 cells/mcL with a predicted nadir of &lt; 500 cells/mcL (Robbins, 2009; Hughes, 2002). There is an increased risk of an occult infection in patients with an ANC &lt; 1,000 cells/\muL, and the risk of infection increases as the ANC decreases. Fever in a neutropenic patient is usually defined as a single temperature of &gt; 38.3°C (101°F), or a sustained temperature &gt; 38°C (100.4°F) for more than one hour. Occasionally a neutropenic patient may not present with fever despite the presence of infection. This occurs more often in elderly patients or patients receiving corticosteroids or other antipyretics.
L487: Contributory factors to the pathogenesis of febrile neutropenia include the direct effects of chemotherapy on mucosal barriers and immune deficits related to the underlying malignancy (Mermel, 2002). Chemotherapy-induced mucositis followed by seeding of the blood stream with endogenous flora from the entire GI tract is believed to explain a majority of febrile neutropenic episodes. Obstruction of the lymphatics, biliary tract, bronchial, gastrointestinal or urinary systems by tumors, or as a result of surgical procedures, or infections related to indwelling catheters are also common causes of infections. Immune defects related to underlying hematologic disorders, in addition to the immunosuppressive effects of chemotherapy also place patients at higher risk for infections.
L488: ## Colony Stimulating Factors
L489: Colony Stimulating Factors (CSFs) have been evaluated for prophylactic use following the administration of chemotherapy when neutropenia is anticipated ("primary prophylaxis"), during retreatment after a previous cycle of chemotherapy that caused febrile neutropenia ("secondary prophylaxis"), and to shorten the duration of severe chemotherapy-induced neutropenia without fever ("afebrile neutropenia") (Larson, 2009). The use of CSFs has also been evaluated as an adjunct to other therapies in patients with febrile neutropenia. The likelihood of developing febrile neutropenia is the primary factor that determines whether or not prophylactic CSFs are indicated. The incidence of febrile neutropenia following treatment is also influenced by the intensity of chemotherapy, the degree of injury to the gastrointestinal mucosa, the presence of underlying damage to the patient's hematopoietic stem cells, the concurrent use of radiation, and the overall clinical status of the patient.
L490: ## Current Clinical Practice
L491: For primary prophylaxis (Larson, 2009), ASCO guidelines (Smith, 2006) recommend that if the incidence of febrile neutropenia is expected to be less than 10 percent following chemotherapy, CSFs should not be routinely administered for primary prophylaxis. When the expected incidence of febrile neutropenia is over 20 percent, prophylactic CSFs are suggested to reduce the need for hospitalization for antibiotic therapy. CSFs may also be used to maintain dose-dense or dose-intense chemotherapy strategies that have survival benefits or in settings where reductions in chemotherapy dose-intensity or dose-density are known to be associated with a poorer prognosis. If the estimated risk of febrile neutropenia is between 10 and 20 percent, the decision to use hematopoietic growth factor support should be individualized. Patients who may be at risk for increased complications from prolonged neutropenia for whom primary prophylaxis might be justified are patients who are age 65 and older, have a poor performance status, have had prior episodes of febrile neutropenia, large radiation portals, or receiving combined chemoradiotherapy, cytopenias due to marrow involvement, poor nutritional status, open wounds or active infection, advanced cancer or other serious comorbidities.
L492: For secondary prophylaxis (Larson, 2009), patients who had an episode of febrile neutropenia after an earlier cycle of palliative chemotherapy, a dose reduction or delay should be the primary therapeutic option and CSFs should not be administered routinely. However, when the risk of febrile neutropenia would prevent the administration of full doses of potentially curative chemotherapy, we suggest the use of CSFs for secondary prophylaxis rather than dose reduction (Smith, 2006). In patients with afebrile neutropenia CSFs should not be used (Larson, 2009).

L502: ## 2.4 Important Safety Issues With Consideration to Related Drugs
L503: ## Allergic Reactions
L504: Allergic-type reactions occurring on initial or subsequent treatment have been reported in &lt; 1 in 4000 patients treated with filgrastim (Neupogen). These have generally been characterized by systemic symptoms involving at least 2 body systems, most often skin (rash, urticaria, facial edema), respiratory (wheezing, dyspnea), and cardiovascular (hypotension, tachycardia). Some reactions occurred on initial exposure. Reactions tended to occur within the first 30 minutes after administration and appeared to occur more frequently in patients receiving Neupogen by intravenous infusion. Rapid resolution of symptoms occurred in most cases after administration of antihistamines, steroids, bronchodilators, and/or epinephrine. Symptoms recurred in more than half the patients who were rechallenged.
L505: ## Splenic Rupture
L506: Splenic Rupture, including fatal cases, has been reported following the administration of Neupogen.
L507: ## Acute Respiratory Distress Syndrome (ARDS)
L508: ARDS has been reported in patients receiving Neupogen, and is postulated to be secondary to an influx of neutrophils to sites of inflammation in the lungs.
L509: ## Alveolar Hemorrhage and Hemoptysis
L510: Alveolar hemorrhage manifesting as pulmonary infiltrates and hemoptysis requiring hospitalization has been reported in healthy donors undergoing peripheral blood progenitor cell (PBPC) mobilization with Neupogen. Hemoptysis resolved with discontinuation of Neupogen. The use of Neupogen for PBPC mobilization in healthy donors is not an approved indication.
L511: ## Sickle Cell Disorders
L512: Severe sickle cell crises, in some cases resulting in death, have been associated with the use of Neupogen in patients with sickle cell disorders. Only physicians qualified by specialized training or experience in the treatment of patients with sickle cell disorders should prescribe Neupogen for such patients, and only after careful consideration of the potential risks and benefits.
L513: ## Patients With Severe Chronic Neutropenia
L514: The safety and efficacy of Neupogen in the treatment of neutropenia due to other hematopoietic disorders (eg, myelodysplastic syndrome) has not been established. Care should be taken to confirm the diagnosis of SCN before initiating Neupogen therapy.
L515: MDS and acute myelogenous leukemia (AML) have been reported to occur in the natural history of congenital neutropenia without cytokine therapy. Cytogenetic abnormalities, transformation to MDS, and AML have also been observed in patients treated with Neupogen for SCN. Based on available data including a postmarketing surveillance study, the risk of developing MDS and AML appears to be confined to the subset of patients with congenital neutropenia. Abnormal cytogenetics and MDS have been associated with the eventual development of myeloid leukemia. The effect of Neupogen on the development of abnormal cytogenetics and the effect of continued administration in patients with abnormal cytogenetics or MDS are unknown.

L548: ## 4.3 Preclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology (excerpted from Non-clinical review)
L549: The nonclinical safety of XM02 was assessed in two GLP 26-week repeat dose toxicity studies with 4-week recovery periods in rats (0, 5, 50, and 500 mcg/kg/day) and monkeys (0, 5, 25, and 125 mcg/kg/day), as well as a standard battery of GLP safety pharmacology studies. From the repeat dose toxicity studies, the rat was determined to be the most sensitive species due to a significant number of mortalities (12/60; 20%) in the high dose group attributable to severe pain caused by enlarged, swollen joints in hind paws and limbs. As a result of these mortalities, 50 µg/kg/day was determined as the maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of XM02 in the rat. In contrast, there were no mortalities or adverse clinical signs in the monkey repeat dose toxicity study at doses of XM02 up to 125 mcg/kg/day. The major toxicities associated with XM02 treatment in both species, were bone marrow hypercellularity, enlarged spleens, increased alkaline phosphatase (ALP), and extramedullary hematopoiesis (rat)/granulocyte infiltrations (monkey), were dose-dependent, related to the exaggerated pharmacology of G-CSF, and reversible in recovery animals. Similarly, the bone (joint) findings of painful, swollen hind limbs that led to the premature euthanization of several high dose animals in the rat GLP repeat dose toxicity study, were dose-dependent, reversible, and also likely related to the exaggerated pharmacology of G-CSF as there were histological findings of hyperostosis and granulocytic infiltrates in articular surfaces and surrounding soft tissue of the bone. There were no detectable effects on the respiratory or central nervous system in rats or the cardiovascular system in dogs after a single dose of 3500 mcg/kg XM02 in the GLP safety pharmacology studies.

L555: ## 4.4.1 Mechanism of Action
L556: XM02 is a glycoprotein growth factor, produced by recombinant DNA technology that regulates the proliferation and differentiation of myeloid progenitor cells and results in an increase in the number of mature functional neutrophils in the peripheral blood. XM02 exerts its effects through a specific, high-affinity receptor on the surface of myeloid cells. XM02 binding to this receptor results in an increase in the number of neutrophils in the periphery.
L557: ## 4.4.2 Pharmacodynamics
L558: In healthy subjects who received a single, subcutaneous dose of 5 or 10 mcg/kg of XM02, a transient decrease in the absolute neutrophil count (ANC) occurred during the first hour, followed by an increase in ANC. Following a subcutaneous 5 mcg/kg dose (N = 61), the mean maximum ANC was 22 \times 10^9/L; the median time to peak ANC was 12 hours. After a subcutaneous 10 mcg/kg dose (N = 58), the mean maximum ANC was 26 \times 10^9/L; the median time to peak ANC was between 14 to 18 hours. The ANC returned to baseline by 4 days post-dose.
L559: In cancer patients receiving various chemotherapies (N = 361), a subcutaneous 5 mcg/kg dose of XM02 was administered daily starting 24 hours after the completion of chemotherapy until an ANC of 10 \times 10^9/L after a nadir was reached or after 14 days of XM02 dosing, whichever occurred first. During cycle 1, the time to maximum mean ANC was between 3 to 5 days, and then ANC decreased to a nadir by 7 to 11 days following
L560: chemotherapy. Mean ANC values returned to baseline by 21 days following completion of chemotherapy. The ANC profile was similar in subsequent chemotherapy cycles.
L561: The potential effects of XM02 on the QTc interval were not adequately evaluated in clinical trials included in the BLA since ECGs were monitored at times when XM02 was cleared from systemic circulation. In a study by Guneysel (Guneysel, 2009) there were no statistically significant differences between baseline and any QTc interval measurements in a population of 102 neutropenic patients, treated for 2 days with Neupogen (Roche, Basel, Switzerland) at a dose of 5 mcg/kg/day SC were observed (Clin Drug Invest. 2009;29:551-55). Teva provided a summary of its XM02 European September 2008 approval post-marketing safety reports to support the lack of QTc prolongation signal. The post-marketing safety data collected up to March 31, 2010 includes (b) (4) daily XM02 doses and only 1 case of syncope was reported and an ECG taken during the syncopal episode was reported to be normal. No sudden death was reported in the post marketing period. Based on all these data together, the potential risk of XM02 to prolong the QTc interval appears to minimal and no further evaluation is warranted at this time.
L562: ## 4.4.3 Pharmacokinetics
L563: In healthy subjects, the absolute bioavailability of a 5 mcg/kg subcutaneous dose of XM02 was 33%. After single dose subcutaneous administration of 5 mcg/kg of XM02 (N = 33), the geometric mean (CV%) of serum Cmax was 18 ng/mL (41%) and of AUC 0-48 h was 158 ng•h/mL (37%). The median Tmax was 6 hours and the median half-life (t_{1/2}) was 8.9 hours. Increasing the dose of XM02 from 5 to 10 mcg/kg resulted in an approximately 3-fold increase in both Cmax and AUC 0-48h.
L564: Pharmacokinetic data were also obtained from patients with breast cancer, lung cancer and NHL (N = 12 per group) who received subcutaneous XM02 5 mcg/kg/day. Following the first XM02 dose in cycle 1, the geometric mean (CV%) of serum Cmax and AUC0-24h were 36 ng/mL (41%) and 305 ng•h/mL (35%) in breast cancer, 25 ng/mL (60%) and 273 ng•h/mL in lung cancer (61%), and 20 ng/mL (24%) and 184 ng•h/mL (23%) in NHL, respectively. For the 3 groups combined, the median Tmax was between 4 to 6 hours and the median t_{1/2} was between 3.2 to 3.8 hours. The terminal half-life was calculated from serum levels measured up to 24 hours as compared to up to 48 hours in the healthy subjects. Accumulation after repeated dosing was not observed. No dose adjustment based on cancer type is warranted.
L565: No gender-related differences were observed in the pharmacokinetics of XM02 following subcutaneous administration. The pharmacokinetic profile in patients with renal or hepatic impairment has not been assessed.

L587: ## Trial Design
L588: This was a randomized double blinded trial comparing XM02 to EU-Neupogen and placebo in patients receiving myelosuppressive chemotherapy for the treatment of breast cancer. Patients were randomized to treatment with either XM02, EU-Neupogen, or placebo (2:2:1). Patients in the placebo group were switched to treatment with XM02 after completion of cycle 1 (Figure 1). The patients underwent a maximum of 4 chemotherapy cycles (3 weeks per cycle), each cycle beginning with a day of chemotherapy. On Day 2 of each cycle, the patients received daily subcutaneous (sc) injections with XM02, EU-Neupogen, or placebo (in the first cycle only) for a minimum of 5 days and a maximum of 14 days, or until an absolute neutrophil count (ANC) \ge 10 \times 10^9/L after nadir was documented.
L589: In cycle 1, blood samples to determine ANC were taken within 24 hours before chemotherapy and then daily until Day 15, or longer until ANC reached \ge 2.0 \times 10^9/L. In cycles 2, 3, and 4, ANC was assessed within 24 hours before chemotherapy and then daily starting on Day 5 until Day 15, or longer until ANC reached \ge 2.0 \times 10^9/L. In each cycle, body temperature (axillary) was measured daily until Day 15, or longer until ANC reached \ge 2.0 \times 10^9/L. Safety assessments (laboratory parameters, antibody sampling, physical examination, vital signs) were performed at screening, within 24 hours before chemotherapy in each cycle (Day 1), and at the end of study visit. Patients were monitored for adverse events (AEs) and concomitant medication throughout the study.
L590: Three weeks after the last chemotherapy infusion (Day 85 \pm 1), end of study assessments were performed. On Day 180 \pm 5, there was an additional assessment for determination of antibodies against XM 02 or EU-Neupogen.

L723: ## 6 Review of Efficacy
L724: ## Efficacy Summary
L725: The trial data submitted to support efficacy, Study XM02-02 demonstrated that XM02 was superior to placebo in the reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia (DSN) with a statistically significant reduction in DSN (2.7 days reduction in DSN, p &lt; 0.0001), and XM02 was equivalent to EU-Neupogen with a 95% confidence interval (-0.26, 0.33) in DSN difference lying within the 1 day margin. However, a claim that XM02 is equivalent to EU-Neupogen is not established due to the lack of comparative results between US-licensed Neupogen and EU-Neupogen.
L726: Study XM02-02 was a multicenter, randomized, placebo and active controlled clinical trial in patients with breast cancer. The primary objectives were to demonstrate the equivalence of XM02 and EU-Neupogen in DSN (95% CI within (-1, 1) day range and superiority of XM02 to placebo. The study utilized a non-inferiority design comparing XM02, EU-Neupogen and placebo with DSN as the primary endpoint. A total of 350 patients with no prior exposure to chemotherapy for high-risk stage II, stage III or IV breast cancer and requiring chemotherapy were randomized. There were 348 patients included in the full analysis set as two patients were erroneously randomized with screen failures. Randomization was 2:2:1 to XM02, EU-Neupogen or placebo stratified by country and adjuvant vs. metastatic therapy. There was a maximum of 4 cycles of treatment. On the day after chemotherapy, XM02, EU-Neupogen, or placebo were given for at least 5 days and up to a maximum of 14 days. The dose was 5 mcg/kg/day. The ANCs were evaluated within 24 hours before chemotherapy and then daily from Day 2 until Day 15, or longer until ANC reached 2.0 \times 10^9/L. Placebo patients switched to XM02 after completion of cycle 1.
L727: The primary efficacy endpoint was DSN in cycle 1, defined as the number of days of grade 4 neutropenia with an ANC &lt;0.5 \times 10^9/L. The secondary efficacy endpoints were the incidence of observed febrile neutropenia, DSN in cycles 2 to 4, depth of ANC nadir in cycles 1 to 4, times to ANC recovery in cycles 1 to 4, and mortality. The secondary endpoints were exploratory.
L728: ## 6.1 Indication
L729: The sponsor proposed the following indication for this submission: Neutroval for the reduction in the duration of severe neutropenia and the incidence of febrile neutropenia in patients treated with established myelosuppressive chemotherapy for cancer.
L730: ## 6.1.1 Methods
L731: The efficacy review contains results from Study XM02-02. XM02 vs. placebo analyses were performed using ANCOVA with factors treatment, country, type of chemo, and baseline ANC value as covariates. There was a 1-sided alpha 0.025. The full analysis population was all patients randomized excluding two screening failures who received random numbers erroneously. XM02 vs. EU-Neupogen analyses were performed using ANCOVA for 95% CI and in the PP population. A sample size of 140:140:70 has 90% power to show equivalence between XM02 and EU-Neupogen (95% CI within (-1, 1) day) and 90% power to show superiority of XM02 to placebo with 1-sided alpha 0.025. Missing values were imputed by interpolation of non-missing ANC's before and after the missing value. For patients with placebo receiving therapeutic G-CSF treatment, the DSN values in cycle 1 were replaced with the median DSN value of patients with placebo who received no G-CSF treatment.
L732: The statistical reviewer noted the sponsor's method in data replacement and data imputation. In the sponsor's primary analysis for XM02 vs. placebo, the DNS value of placebo patients who received therapeutic G-CSF treatment were replaced with the median DSN value of placebo patients who received no G-CSF treatment. This approach could underestimate the variability of DSN in the placebo arm, and exaggerate the statistical significance of the difference in DSN. Missing ANCs were imputed by linear interpolation of the ANC values immediately before and after the missing value. The validity of this method is questionable because it assumes? an ANC value to be in-between the ANC values before and after.
L733: To address the concerns above and to evaluate the robustness of the sponsor's results, the statistical reviewer performed the following sensitivity analyses:
L734: - DSN results were based on intent-to-treat (ITT) data without replacing DSN values for placebo patients receiving therapeutic G-CSF treatment. The missing ANCs were imputed primarily by linear interpolation. (For full details see the Appendix of the Statistics Review)
- DSN results were based on ITT data without replacing DSN values for placebo patients receiving therapeutic G-CSF treatment. Missing ANCs were treated as &lt; 0.5 \times 10^9/L (non-response).
- DSN results were based on ITT data without replacing DSN values for placebo patients receiving therapeutic G-CSF treatment. Missing ANCs were treated as &lt; 0.5 \times 10^9/L in XM02 arm, but as \ge 0.5 \times 10^9/L in placebo arm. This is the worst case analysis.
L735: The results of the sensitivity analyses performed by FDA are consistent with that of the sponsor's analysis.

L986: Figure 1 is a timeline diagram illustrating the study design of Study XM02-02, comparing Placebo, XM 02, and Filgrastim groups across four cycles of chemotherapy.
The timeline spans from Day 1 (D1) to Day 180 (D180). Key dates marked are D1, D22, D43, D64, D85 (End of Study), and D180 (Antibody Test).
The study is divided into a Run-In period (D1 to D22) and four cycles (Cycle 1: D22 to D43, Cycle 2: D43 to D64, Cycle 3: D64 to D85, Cycle 4: D85 to D180).

Filgrastim Group (n = 140): Receives Filgrastim from D2 to D15 (Run-In). Receives Filgrastim from D23 to D36, D44 to D57, and D65 to D78 during Cycles 1-4.
The diagram visually represents the treatment schedule for all three groups across the study duration.

L988: Figure 3 illustrates the study design of Study XM02-04, showing the timeline and patient allocation.
The study begins with a Run-In period, followed by a screening phase involving XM 02 (n=60) and Filgrastim (n=30). Both groups receive D2-D15 treatment.
Following the screening phase, the study proceeds through six cycles (Cycle 1 to Cycle 6), each consisting of a 2-week chemotherapy (Chemo) period (D1, D22, D43, D64, D85, D106) followed by a 15-day rest period (D2-15).
The timeline marks key dates: D1 (Chemo), D22 (Chemo), D43 (Chemo), D64 (Chemo), D85 (Chemo), D106 (Chemo), D127 (End of Study), and D180 (Antibody-Test).
