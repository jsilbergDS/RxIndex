L29: 21. Other Drug Development Considerations
L30: 21.1. Clinical Outcome Assessment
L31: In both phase 3 trials, motion-induced vomiting and nausea symptoms were assessed using the VA and NA instruments, respectively. The VA is a single-item patient-reported questionnaire that assesses the incidence of vomiting on a Yes/No response scale. The NA is a single-item patient-reported questionnaire that assesses nausea severity at its worst on a 5-point Likert scale, with score ranging from 0 to 4, where 0 = none, 1 = mild, 2 = moderate, 3 = severe, and 4 = very
L32: severe. Subjects completed the VA and NA approximately every 30 minutes throughout the boat travel.
L33: The VA and NA questionnaires are simplistic measures that appear straightforward for patients to complete and comprehend. Based on the intrinsic construction of the instruments, the VA and NA questionnaires are deemed appropriate for assessing motion-induced vomiting presence/absence and nausea severity, respectively, in the target patient population.
L34: The primary endpoint for both phase 3 trials is constructed as an event-driven measure based on a “Yes” response on the VA instrument. It indicates the presence or absence of vomiting and inherently represents a clinically meaningful outcome for patients. In addition, the nausea component of the secondary endpoint in Trial 3404, defined as worst nausea \geq 3 on the NA instrument, represents severe to very severe symptoms and appears to be clinically meaningful. Anchor-based analysis is not deemed necessary to interpret the meaningfulness of the primary endpoint results as this endpoint indicates the presence or absence of vomiting and is inherently clinically important to patients.
L35: 21.2. Abuse Potential
L36: The Applicant provided an Abuse Liability Justification Report and the Controlled Substance Staff (CSS) reviewed this report to evaluate the abuse potential of tradipitant. Tradipitant was evaluated for its primary pharmacology in vitro using receptor binding assays and determined to be a potent, selective, NK-1 receptor inhibitor. Tradipitant was also determined to have no significant activity (\geq 50\% inhibition) in a broad panel of receptors, transporters, ion channel receptors, and enzymes associated with abuse. Based on these in vitro findings, additional studies including nonclinical abuse potential assessments and a human abuse potential study were not requested. In addition, AEs associated with abuse from the clinical development program were reviewed. This analysis indicated that the most prevalent AEs observed in clinical trials were somnolence, headache, and fatigue. CSS notes that these AEs are not indicative of abuse in the absence of other events such as euphoria, and feeling high, among others. There were no concerning reports of AEs that suggest that tradipitant has a potential for abuse or physical dependence. Based on these data, CSS provides the following recommendations:
L37: • Tradipitant does not appear to have a potential for abuse and does not require scheduling under the Controlled Substances Act.• Tradipitant does not require a Section 9 (Drug Abuse and Dependence section) in its label, should tradipitant be approved.

L152: The inhibitory potential of tradipitant and metabolites M2, M3, M4, and M8 were evaluated in a series of in vitro binding assays. Tradipitant, M2, M3, M4, and M8 all inhibited binding to the human tachykinin NK-1 receptor by \geq 99.0\% at 10 \mu\text{M}. Tradipitant inhibited binding to the NK-1 receptor with an \text{IC}_{50} of 3.3 nM, while M8 inhibited binding to the receptor with an \text{IC}_{50} of 0.49 nM. The Applicant reported similar \text{IC}_{50} values for the other metabolites, although these were not included in the provided study reports.
L153: In functional assays, tradipitant and M8 inhibited substance P-mediated calcium release with \text{IC}_{50} values of 0.63 and 6.3 nM, respectively. These data confirm that M8 is pharmacologically active at the NK-1 receptor. Functional assays were not conducted
L154: with M2, M3, or M4. Based on data from binding assays and their structural similarity to tradipitant, M2, M3, and M4 are presumed to be active metabolites as well.
L155: Tradipitant significantly inhibited the rat benzothiazepine and dihydropyridine L-type calcium channels (65% and 63%, respectively) and rat GABAA t-butyl bicyclo-orthobenzoate (TBOB) chloride channel (62%). M2 also significantly inhibited the rat benzothiazepine L-type calcium channel (52%), human estrogen receptor (ER) alpha (64%), and rat GABAA TBOB chloride channel (53%). No significant off-target effects were observed with M3, M4, or M8.
L156: Safety Pharmacology
L157: Key Findings:
L158: The Applicant conducted four non-GLP safety pharmacology studies using cultured 3D human hepatocyte spheroids and one non-GLP safety pharmacology study using an in vitro human liver co-culture microphysiological system (MPS) with the objective of quantifying the in vitro hepatotoxic potential of tradipitant and its metabolites and to evaluate their underlying toxicity mechanisms. High variability of the positive controls, between donors, and between studies was observed. The rationale for the chosen positive controls was not provided, and the biological and/or clinical context for chosen endpoints and observed effects was not established. No primary mechanism was identified by which tradipitant or its metabolites may induce liver injury. In light of the limitations stated above, the clinical risk of liver injury with tradipitant administration remains poorly defined; however, these studies support that the risk of drug-induced liver injury is not an animal-specific finding and is relevant to humans.
L159: PK/ADME/TK
L160: Key Findings:
L161: Tradipitant and its metabolites M2, M3, and M4 were highly bound in vitro using plasma samples from rat, mouse, and human. Protein binding of M8 was not assessed. There was no clear effect of concentration on plasma binding of tradipitant or its metabolites in any species.
L162: Using recombinant human enzymes, UGT1A4 and UGT2B7 were identified as UDP-glucuronosyltransferases capable of converting tradipitant to the metabolite M8.
L163: In studies using human liver microsomes, tradipitant competitively inhibited CYP3A activity with a K_i of 0.92 \mu\text{M} and non-competitively inhibited CYP3A activity with a K_i of 11 \mu\text{M}. Tradipitant competitively inhibited both CYP2C19 and CYP2C9 activity with K_i values of 9.0 \mu\text{M} and 4.7 \mu\text{M}, respectively. Tradipitant also non-competitively inhibited CYP2C8 activity with a K_i value of 17 \mu\text{M}. No significant effects were observed on CYP1A2, CYP2D6, CYP2E1, or CYP2B6 activity.
L164: In a study using primary human hepatocytes, tradipitant was considered a potential inducer of CYP3A4 activity in vitro. No biologically significant effects on CYP1A2 activity were observed.
L165: Toxicology
L166: Genotoxicity
L167: Key Findings:
L168: Tradipitant metabolites M2, M3, and M4 were negative for genotoxicity in the in vitro bacterial reverse mutation assay and the in vitro mammalian chromosome aberration assay using human lymphocytes. M8 was not tested for genotoxic potential.
L169: Special Toxicology Studies
L170: Key Findings:
L171: The Applicant conducted two studies using a “four-organ” MPS comprised of primary human hepatocytes, induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC)-derived cardiomyocytes, primary human renal proximal tubular epithelial cells, and iPSC-derived cortical neurons with the objective of evaluating the safety of tradipitant. Although the Applicant positioned these studies as pivotal to determining the safety of tradipitant, these studies were not conducted in compliance with US FDA GLP regulations; given the absence of adequate details regarding deviations from GLP and the potential impact of any deviations on the study validity, this was considered a limitation of these studies. Other limitations were identified that precluded use of these studies to support regulatory decision-making in this application, including the narrow scope of toxicity evaluations, unjustified biological relevance of the system and chosen endpoints, use of a single donor/cell source per organ, and short duration of the studies (7 or 14 days). Ultimately, due to these limitations, these studies do not substantively contribute to the safety profile of tradipitant.
L172: Other Toxicology Studies
L173: Key Findings:
L174: An in silico analysis of tradipitant and metabolites M2, M3, M4, and M8 using various tools and databases was conducted in 2015 and submitted to the current NDA.
L175: Potential toxicities were identified; however, in light of the completed in vitro and in vivo toxicity studies with tradipitant and/or its metabolites, the findings in this report are not considered toxicologically relevant.
L176: Updated in silico analyses were conducted by the Applicant to support the safety of metabolites M2, M3, and M4 for tradipitant for the prevention of vomiting induced by motion. These analyses were intended to assess if and how structural differences in the metabolites might affect their toxicity profile compared to tradipitant. Briefly, these in silico analyses were conducted using a machine learning-assisted workflow developed by xAI's Grok system, an advanced large language model trained on scientific datasets and further validated via the Applicant's in-house Python scripts. Notably, the Applicant's comparative structural prediction data were inferred based on RDKit-computed descriptors and qualitative thresholds aligned with best practices from consensus classifiers, rather than direct execution of these models. It is not clear why the Applicant chose to design a workflow using a large language model and a custom set of molecular descriptors and use information from existing classifier techniques to 'infer' predictions, rather than directly using existing (Q)SAR modeling software. Although insufficient detail was provided on the consensus classifiers used in the analyses to fully define the approach, enough information was provided to facilitate
L177: review of the findings. Overall, the updated in silico analyses predicted that metabolites M2, M3, and M4 were unlikely to present additional toxicity concerns compared with tradipitant.
L178: Nonclinical Assessment and Recommendation
L179: The NDA submission included reports of 24 new nonclinical studies that had not been previously submitted to IND 141315 or cross-referenced from IND (b) (4). These included reports of primary pharmacology, safety pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, genetic toxicology, special toxicology, and other toxicology studies with tradipitant and/or its metabolites. In addition to review of the individual studies, this document contains an assessment of the available nonclinical data with tradipitant with regard to two major issues:
L180: 1. If the available nonclinical information, including additional in vitro assays, adequately support the safety of unrestricted use of tradipitant over a lifetime for the prevention of vomiting induced by motion; and2. How the available nonclinical information addresses the safety of the four major human metabolites of tradipitant, M2, M3, M4, and M8.
L181: Adequacy of Nonclinical Data to Support the Safety of Unrestricted Use
L182: As previously documented, the Applicant has not conducted a chronic toxicity study in a nonrodent species. The available nonclinical data with tradipitant, including repeat-dose toxicity studies of up to 6 months in rats and up to 3 months in dogs, do not support the safety of unrestricted, repeated intermittent use of tradipitant over a lifetime for the prevention of vomiting induced by motion. While the duration of the nonclinical toxicity studies supports the safety of tradipitant itself for repeat intermittent dosing for a limited duration, it does not support unrestricted use over a lifetime.
L183: As an alternative to conducting a chronic toxicity study in a nonrodent species, the Applicant submitted a total of seven additional in vitro assays to support the long-term safety of tradipitant.
L184: Four studies using cultured 3D human hepatocyte spheroids and one study using an in vitro human liver co-culture MPS were conducted with tradipitant and/or its major metabolites with the objective of validating/quantifying the drug's hepatotoxic potential and to evaluate its underlying toxicity mechanisms. Treatment with tradipitant and all four major metabolites resulted in decreased hepatocyte viability that was deemed biologically relevant by the Applicant. Despite their limitations, these studies support the risk for tradipitant to cause liver injury, initially identified in Han Wistar rats, and that the potential for liver injury is not animal-specific, as these studies used human cells.
L185: It should be noted that, while adaptive liver changes were observed in all animal toxicity studies with tradipitant and adverse liver findings were only observed in the 3-month Han Wistar rat study, the metabolites M2, M4, and M8 were not present in the rat studies. Therefore, the toxicological impact of these compounds, including the potential for hepatotoxicity, has not been adequately elucidated in animal studies.
L186: Two additional in vitro studies using a “four-organ” MPS comprised of human hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, renal proximal tubule epithelial cells, and cortical neurons were conducted to evaluate the safety of tradipant. Limitations in the design and conduct of the studies were identified, including the narrow scope of toxicity evaluations, unjustified biological relevance of the system and chosen endpoints, lack of compliance with US FDA GLP regulations, and short duration of treatment (up to 14 days) relative to the intended clinical use.
L187: Ultimately, the submitted alternative in vitro assays do not substantially contribute to the safety profile of tradipant, and a chronic toxicity study in a nonrodent species is still scientifically warranted to support unrestricted use over a lifetime. Moreover, the in vitro liver safety pharmacology studies support a human-relevant risk of hepatotoxicity with tradipant use that was initially identified in the animal studies, though the clinical risk of liver injury with tradipant administration remains poorly defined.
L188: Overall, there continues to be insufficient nonclinical safety information to support the safety of unrestricted, repeated intermittent use of tradipant over a lifetime in humans, and this is an approvability issue for the current application.
L189: Nonclinical Qualification of Major Human Metabolites
L190: The Applicant has identified four major metabolites of tradipant in human plasma: M2, M3, M4, and M8. These metabolites have been investigated in a series of nonclinical studies, and their plasma levels were measured in select toxicity studies conducted in rats and mice. Metabolite levels were not measured in any toxicity study conducted in dogs. Based on available data, these metabolites are considered to be pharmacologically active, toxicologically relevant, and present at significant levels compared to the parent drug, tradipant. These factors support that the safety of levels of these metabolites should be qualified based on nonclinical data. Among these metabolites, only M3 was present in nonclinical studies in sufficient levels to be qualified for safety; M2, M4, and M8 are disproportionate human metabolites, and their safety was not adequately addressed with in vivo animal studies.
L191: The Applicant proposed, and the review team considered, a Weight of Evidence (WoE) analysis to support that metabolites M2, M4, and M8 were not expected to be toxicologically different from tradipant itself, thereby allowing for qualification based on total tradipant drug-related exposure rather than on an individual basis. (Q)SAR analyses of the di-aryl alcohol and/or N-oxide moieties present on M2, M3, and M4 compared to tradipant were conducted by FDA/CDER, and comparative computational prediction analyses were conducted in parallel by the Applicant. These analyses supported that the structural differences would not result in new or exaggerated toxicities with the metabolites compared to tradipant itself. Analysis of the recently provided in vitro pharmacology, pharmacokinetic, and genotoxicity data (reviewed in this document) further supported the toxicological similarity of tradipant and all four major metabolites. Therefore, the WoE supported that qualification of the disproportionate human metabolites (M2, M4, and M8) using total drug-related exposure, rather than on an individual basis, was scientifically appropriate.
L192: Comparison of the total tradipitant drug-related exposure (i.e., tradipitant plus all measured active metabolites) between animals and humans demonstrated sufficient exposure margins such that metabolites M2, M4, and M8 were considered qualified based on the available nonclinical data. Thus, there is no safety concern with the levels of the major human metabolites.
L193: Notably, it is not generally expected that such a WoE approach will be appropriate for the qualification of metabolites in other development programs, as each situation must be evaluated on its own merits based on the totality of data and information. Although in silico analyses are highly valuable in certain situations, they are unlikely to routinely address safety concerns in lieu of typical in vivo toxicity studies.

L243: Methods:
L244: As stated in the study report, the objective of this non-GLP study4 was to quantify the in vitro hepatotoxic potential of tradipitant and to evaluate its underlying toxicity mechanisms. Tradipitant was evaluated using 3D spheroid cultures of primary human hepatocytes as previously reported by Bell et al.5 In each experiment, primary human hepatocytes from a single human donor were seeded into ultra-low attachment plates and cultured for seven days until spontaneous self-aggregation was observed. Cells were then cultured overnight in serum free medium prior to treatment with tradipitant (Lot # DVVE18001M6) at approximately 3.5, 7.0, 10.5, 17.5, or 35 µg/mL (i.e., approximately 10, 20, 30, 50, or 100x the Applicant's reported clinical Cmax of 350 ng/mL).
L245: Reviewer note: Steady state PK data for subjects who experience vomiting induced by motion are derived from Trial VP-VLY-686-1102, in which healthy volunteers were administered tradipitant at 85 mg twice daily (BID) for 12 days (assumed but not
L246: (b) (4)
L247: confirmed to represent steady state) under fasted conditions. In this Trial, the C_{max} for tradipitant was reported as 327 ng/mL. Thus, the concentrations used in this study represent tradipitant exposure approximately 1.1-, 3.2-, 11-, 21-, 32-, or 107-fold the C_{max} respectively, in this population. It should be noted that the Applicant did not conduct in vitro to in vivo extrapolation (IVIVE) analyses to determine how the in vitro concentration relates to that observed in humans. In fact, a direct comparison of C_{max} and in vitro concentrations is scientifically inappropriate as such an “approach oversimplifies the complex relationship between in vitro and in vivo drug behavior and can lead to inaccurate conclusions.”7 Thus, the stated effect margins should be interpreted with caution, and all observed effects are discussed in terms of absolute concentration.

L307: Methods:
L308: As stated in the study report, the objective of this non-GLP study4 was to quantify the in vitro hepatotoxic potential of tradipitant and to evaluate its underlying toxicity mechanisms. Tradipitant was evaluated using 3D spheroid cultures of primary human hepatocytes as previously reported by Bell et al.5 In each experiment, primary human hepatocytes from a single human donor were seeded into ultra-low attachment plates and cultured for 7 days until spontaneous self-aggregation was observed. Cells were then cultured overnight in serum free medium prior to treatment with tradipitant (Lot # DVVE18001M6) at approximately 0.35, 1.05, 3.5, 7.0, 10.5, 17.5, or 35 \mu\text{g/mL} (i.e., approximately 1, 3, 10, 20, 30, 50, or 100x the Applicant's reported clinical C_{\text{max}} of 350 ng/mL); this concentration range was selected by the Applicant based on the findings in Study HPTOX-3550.
L309: Reviewer note: As stated previously, the concentrations used in this study represent tradipitant exposure at approximately 11-, 21-, 32-, 54-, or 107-fold the clinical C_{\text{max}} of 327 ng/mL, respectively, as determined in healthy volunteers following 12 days of
L310: dosing at 85 mg BID. The Applicant did not conduct IVIVE analyses to determine how the in vitro concentration relates to that observed in humans. In fact, a direct comparison of Cmax and in vitro concentrations is scientifically inappropriate as such an "approach oversimplifies the complex relationship between in vitro and in vivo drug behavior and can lead to inaccurate conclusions."7 Thus, the stated effect margins should be interpreted with caution, and all observed effects are discussed in terms of absolute concentration.
L311: DMSO was used as the negative control in all experiments. Drug-containing medium was replaced every 48-72 hours for a total of 2 or 4 weeks.
L312: A total of five donors were used in this study; the cells from each donor were not pooled. Demographic and health information were not provided for the donors. Eight spheroids per donor per endpoint were evaluated.
L313: Cell viability was determined via quantification of ATP using a commercial kit following treatment with tradipitant alone and following co-culture of spheroids with pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF\alpha, INF\gamma, and IL-6; each individually and in combination at 0.1, 1, and 10 ng/mL). Treatments were considered hepatotoxic if cell viability was reduced to &lt; 80% of control levels.9
L314: Results:
L315: Reviewer note: The Applicant's interpretation of these results does not clearly report or identify effects that are synergistic versus those that are additive or sub-additive. As no raw data were provided and the presentation of the data did not allow for potential synergistic effects to be directly determined, the results reported here should be interpreted with caution. Synergistic effects were identified by the reviewer as those being greater or equal to 1.1 times the estimated additive effect (i.e., the sum of the tradipitant only effect and the cytokines only effect, as estimated from the bar graph). Synergistic effects were only analyzed for the cytokine mixtures, not for the individual cytokines, due to the large number of conditions tested. Notably, the Applicant did not provide any rationale to explain how the cytokine conditions are relevant to the indication of vomiting induced by motion, as these subjects are generally considered healthy in the absence of the motion stimulus.
L316: Summary of Findings
L317: Tradipitant induced hepatotoxicity in 4/5 donors after 2 or 4 weeks of treatment, evidenced by decreased cell viability of the hepatocyte spheroids compared to controls. The concentration(s) at which biologically significant toxicity occurred were highly donor-dependent.
L318: Overall, no clear synergistic effects of tradipitant with pro-inflammatory cytokines (TNF\alpha, INF\gamma, IL-6, or the combination) were observed on the viability of hepatocyte spheroids. However, interpretation of these data was severely limited by the lack of raw data provided, the Applicant's method of analysis, the high variability of data generated for
L319: each individual donor, and the frequently large effects observed with tradipitant or cytokines alone.
L320: Reviewer note: In this study, treatment with tradipitant at 3.5 µg/mL (or less) for 2 or 4 weeks resulted in biologically significant decreases in cell viability in 4/5 donors. This is a lower concentration than that observed in Study HPTOX-3550 (7.0 µg/mL). A possible explanation for the increased sensitivity could not be determined, and no explanation was provided.
L321: Individual Donor Findings
L322: For Donor 1, biologically significant, dose-dependent hepatotoxicity was observed at tradipitant concentrations \geq 3.5 µg/mL following treatment for both 2 and 4 weeks.
L323: None of the cytokines (TNF\alpha, INF\gamma, IL-6) alone significantly impacted cell viability; however, treatment with the cytokine mixture resulted in decreased cell viability at 10 ng/mL at 2 weeks and at \geq 1 ng/mL at 4 weeks.
L324: No combined effects of tradipitant and individual cytokines were reported for this donor. Sporadic instances of synergistic effects of tradipitant and the cytokine mixture on cell viability were identified in this review, but no consistent or concentration-dependent synergy was observed in this donor following 2 weeks of treatment. Synergistic effects were identified with tradipitant at 0.35 µg/mL only using all concentrations of the cytokine mixture following 4 weeks of treatment, but not with other combinations.

L381: Reviewer note: Based on nonclinical and clinical data submitted by the Applicant, CYP3A4 has a role in the metabolism of tradipitant, but it is not the predominant or only pathway responsible (see NDA Integrated Review, Sections 13 and 14, for review of nonclinical and clinical data, respectively). Tradipitant is metabolized to M2 by non-CYP-mediated pathways; to M3 by CYP3A4 (major), CYP2C19 (minor), and CYP2C8 (minor); and to M8 by glucuronidation. M2 and M3 are further metabolized to M4 by CYP-mediated and non-specified pathways, respectively. According to the current study report, previous work (Study HSTOX-2008.001, not submitted to the Agency) demonstrated that the M2 metabolite is “spontaneously generated” and not CYP-dependent. Induction of CYP enzymes was observed following repeated dosing in rats and dogs, and clinically relevant induction of CYP3A is possible but was not definitively demonstrated. Apparent clearance of tradipitant was 33% lower in subjects with the heterozygous CYP3A4*22 allele compared to wild type (CYP3A4*1), and the Applicant suggests that CYP3A4*22 subjects may be at higher risk of adverse events; however, the impact of this finding on human PK was of unclear clinical significance. Additionally, no scientific justification or biological rationale was provided for the criteria used to define a low, moderate, or high CYP3A4 donor, and it is not clear that these criteria were pre-specified. Overall, the available data do not clearly demonstrate that endogenous/baseline CYP3A4 activity has a significant impact on tradipitant metabolism, and the clinical relevance of using donors with three different levels of CYP3A4 activity in this study has not been justified.

L402: Reviewer note: As discussed for Study HSTOX-7619, the drug and metabolite pharmacokinetic profiles measured in this in vitro study are not consistent with human clinical or in vivo animal study data. Following a single dose of 25 mg radiolabeled tradipitant in healthy human volunteers, drug-related exposure measured over 24 hours was 9% parent drug, 4% M2, 5% M3, and 8% M4 (i.e., the parent drug and major identified human metabolites accounted for only 26% of drug-related exposure; see Study H8R-MC-HKAK and 2.4 Nonclinical Overview, pg. 20). In healthy volunteers administered 85 mg tradipitant BID for 12 days, drug-related exposure measured over 24 hours was composed of 50% parent drug, 17% M2, 16% M3, and 17% M4 (see Study VP-VLY-686-1102, pgs. 60-63). Additionally, although data are limited, M2 represented &lt; 1% of drug-related exposure in nonclinical toxicology studies. Due to the differences in in vitro and in vivo metabolism, any findings related to toxicity in this assay may not be biologically relevant to humans. Finally, these results call into question the role and importance of CYP3A4 in the metabolism of tradipitant. As stated
L403: above, CYP3A4 has been identified as the major pathway of metabolism of tradipitant to M3, with little or no additional effects; however, the production of M3 was not highest in the high metabolizer hepatocytes, as would be expected. An alternative explanation could be that increased production of M3 led to further conversion of M3 to M4 (by non-CYP-mediated pathways); however, again, levels of M4 were lowest in the high metabolizer hepatocytes, contrary to that expected. While it is clear that differences in metabolism were present among the three donors in this study, it is not clear what the biologically significant differences were (e.g., differences in baseline or induced activities of other CYP enzymes, differences in non-CYP pathways) or how such differences may be clinically relevant.

L613: 12.2.6 Conclusions and Recommendation
L614: As discussed, the Applicant has identified four major human metabolites of tradipitant: M2, M3, M4, and M8. Prior to submission of NDA 220152, because of limited clinical pharmacology data, it could not be determined if these metabolites individually represent \geq 10\% of total drug-related exposure at steady state, although tradipitant itself did not seem to meet this threshold.121 However, based on all data available at the time of this review, it was determined that adequate nonclinical assessment of these metabolites is warranted to support the safety of tradipitant for its intended clinical use. Based on the available data, three of the four major human metabolites (i.e., M2, M4, and M8) are present at disproportionately higher levels in humans compared to species used in the nonclinical toxicology studies and therefore had not been qualified based on in vivo data. However, using a WoE approach for M2, M4, and M8, taking into consideration data from a (Q)SAR analysis as well as primary pharmacology, secondary pharmacology, pharmacokinetic, general toxicity, special toxicity, and genotoxicity studies with each metabolite and/or tradipitant, the review team has determined that each metabolite is expected to have a similar toxicological profile to that of tradipitant, and no potential for increased or novel toxicities was identified. Therefore, it was considered acceptable to qualify the safety of these metabolites based on total drug-related exposure rather than on an individual basis.

L651: 2. Pharmacometrics Assessment
L652: 2.1 Executive Summary
L653: The Applicant's initial population pharmacokinetic (PPK) analysis (submitted on December 30, 2024) included no PK data from subjects with motion sickness. An information request (IR) was sent on April 2, 2025, requesting the Applicant conduct additional PPK modeling and simulation analysis incorporating the PK data for tradipitant and its metabolites (M2, M3, M4, and M8) in subjects with motion sickness obtained from Trial 3404 and 3403 (if available). In response to the IR, an updated PPK report was submitted on July 22, 2025, based on pooled tradipitant and metabolite concentration data from 7 trials (1002, 1102, 1301, 2301, 3301, 3403, and 3404). Key highlights of the PPK models are described below:
L654: • The Applicant's NONMEM executions finished with unsuccessful minimization, probably due to the over-parameterization of the models. However, the PPK analysis generally captured the concentration data reasonably well and the predicted tradipitant and metabolite exposures appear generally reasonable.• The observed PK exposures of tradipitant and metabolites of Trial 3403 are higher than other trials (including Trial 3404, another trial in motion sickness). Per the Applicant, these differences may reflect the small number of postdose samples obtained in each trial and the short interval of these samples relative to dosing.• In addition, each dosing amount was derived via averaging the total dosing micrograms over a period of time for subjects with poor compliance (as low as 46.4%) added uncertainty to the PPK modeling and simulation analysis. It might be rational to exclude PK data from subjects with confirmed low-compliance in the PPK analysis. The FDA reviewer's independent PPK analysis of tradipitant suggested one-compartmental model could also be used to describe the PK data (with low-compliance subject and Trial 1002 excluded). However, this model also under-predicted the elevated concentrations observed in Trial 3403.

L739: 2.2.5 Methods
L740: A linear two-compartment PK model with elimination from the central compartment was adopted. To accommodate large secondary peaks in the fasted sessions of the food effect study,
L741: the model included two absorption compartments for that session with the dose apportioned between the two. Parameters were added to the model to accommodate differences in PK characteristics between studies. Post hoc fits for each study showed minimal bias. Post hoc parameters were then used to simulate concentration profiles for each subject. Exposure metrics in two trials (Trials 3403, Trials 3404) were calculated for each subject, then summarized by trial and presented graphically and in tables and text. First order conditional estimation with Eta-Sigma interaction was performed using the nonlinear mixed-effects modeling program NONMEM, Version 7.5.1 or later (ICON Development Solutions, Ellicott City, MD).
L742: [REDACTED] was used for file management and creation of graphics. Estimates for population mean parameters (typical values), between-subject variability, residual variability and, if possible, standard errors of the parameters were obtained.
L743: Using NONMEM's ADVAN5, a model was developed for Trial 1002 that addressed the secondary peaks as well as differences between the fasted and fed sessions (Figure 7). For the fasted condition, the dose is partitioned between the proximal absorption (F1) and distant absorption (F2) with F1 + F2 equals 1. For the fed sessions, the entire dose, scaled by F_{\text{fed}}, is delivered to the central compartment. Distant absorption exists but is ignored because bioavailability to that compartment (F2) equals 0.
L744: The lower limit of quantitation (LOQ) was 1.00 ng/mL for tradipitant and 0.500 ng/mL for metabolites. Predose samples with concentrations below the LOQ (BQL) were assigned a value of 0 and excluded from the analysis. Postdose BQL samples &gt;15 days after the final dose were also excluded from the analysis. The remaining postdose BQL values were assigned a concentration of LOQ/2 and included in the analysis.
