L163: 2. Describe the particular review issue and the goal of the study.
L164: In the original submission, the applicant included in-process specification for % assay at (b)(4)% of the (b)(4) volume with limits between (b)(4)% of (b)(4) mg/mL. The in-process assay test is conducted (b)(4) to ensure that the drug concentration (b)(4) is within acceptable limits. However, in an email communication to RBPM dated 03/27/2017, the applicant asked if provisional acceptance criteria for in-process assay limits can be set at (b)(4)% until completion of the process validation batches. If the process validation data supports the tighter limits, then the limits will be revised and submitted as CBE-30. In the meantime, the final release specifications for the product will remain unchanged.
L165: Initial method suitability for sterility testing was performed by the applicant using only 2 vials of drug product, which did not comply with the USP &lt;71&gt; requirement for the number of vials to be tested based on the production batch size. An information request was issued advising the applicant that sterility testing method suitability should be repeated to conform with USP &lt;71&gt; requirements. The applicant's response to the IR was unclear, so a 15 February 2017 teleconference was scheduled to clarify how they intended to perform method suitability. At the conclusion of the teleconference, the applicant agreed to submit a revised sterility testing protocol to satisfy USP &lt;71&gt; (submitted 24 February 2017). However, method suitability could not be performed until new batches of drug product were produced; the applicant has committed to performing sterility testing method suitability per the submitted protocol once drug product is produced (b)(4). The goal of the PMC study is to establish the suitability of the proposed sterility testing method for sterility testing of the drug product in compliance with USP &lt;71&gt;.

L409: ## II. BACKGROUND
L410: The Applicant submitted the NDAs to support the use of delafloxacin intravenous (IV) infusion or oral tablet in the treatment of acute bacterial skin and skin structure infections (ABSSSI) caused by designated susceptible bacteria in adults.
L411: The following two pivotal study protocols were inspected in support of this application:
L412: ***Study RX-3341-302:*** A Phase 3, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Active-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Delafloxacin Compared with Vancomycin + Aztreonam in Patients with Acute Bacterial Skin and Skin Structure Infections
L413: This was a randomized, double-blind, multicenter study, to assess the clinical efficacy of delafloxacin compared with vancomycin + aztreonam in patients with ABSSSIs at 48 to 72 hours after initiation of treatment. Patients who met the criteria were randomly assigned to 1 of 2 treatment arms in a 1:1 ratio to receive either delafloxacin 300 mg IV every 12 hours or vancomycin 15 mg/kg IV every 12 hours based on actual body weight for a total of 10-28 doses based on the investigator's judgment. The primary efficacy endpoint was a \ge 20\% reduction in lesion erythema area at 48 to 72 hours after initiation of treatment compared to baseline as determined by digital measurements of the leading edge of the affected site.
L414: The study was conducted at 34 clinical sites in 7 countries including the US between 4/25/2013 and 6/6/2014. A total of 660 patients were enrolled and 547 patients completed the study.
L415: Two clinical sites were selected for inspection:
L416: Dr. Overcash's site was selected for inspection mainly because of high enrollment, and high site efficacy effect; in addition, the site shared the same address with Dr. Green's site for Study 303.
L417: Dr. Farley's site was selected for inspection mainly because of high site efficacy effect, and high events of serious adverse events (SAE), with four SAEs in the study drug arm.
L418: ***Study RX-3341-303:*** A Phase 3, Multicenter, Randomized, Double-Blind, Active-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of IV and Oral Delafloxacin Compared with Vancomycin + Aztreonam in Patients with Acute Bacterial Skin and Skin Structure Infections
L419: The study was identical to Study 302 in study design and methodologies. However, Study 303 used the IV formulation for 6 doses, then all patients in the delafloxacin group had a mandatory switch to delafloxacin 450 mg orally Q12h. Study 303 also included patients with severe renal impairment (CrCl of 15 to 29 mL/min) and used a fixed dose of delafloxacin 200 mg IV Q12h in these patients.
L420: The study was conducted at 76 clinical sites in 15 countries including the US between 5/2/2014 and 1/29/2016. A total of 850 patients were enrolled and 734 patients completed the study.
L421: Two clinical sites were selected for inspection:
L422: Dr. Hansen's site was selected for inspection mainly because of high number of SAEs, with three SAEs in the study drug arm.
L423: Dr. Green's site was selected for inspection mainly because of high enrollment, high site efficacy effect, and high number of SAEs, with four SAEs in study drug arm; in addition, the site shared the same address with Dr. Overcash's site for Study 302.
L424: According to the sponsor, in the primary efficacy analysis, the proportion of patients who were responders (\ge 20\% reduction of erythema area) at 48 to 72 hours after initiation of study drug was similar in the 2 treatment groups in both Studies 302 and 303.
