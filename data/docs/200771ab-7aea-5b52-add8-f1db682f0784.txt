L13: ## B. Clinical Background
L14: Kuvan is an immediate-release oral formulation of sapropterin dihydrochloride (sapropterin), a synthetic form of BH4. BH4 is an endogenous molecule that is a required cofactor for a variety of enzymes, including the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase (PAH). Kuvan is being proposed as a treatment to reduce blood phenylalanine (Phe) levels in patients with hyperphenylalaninemia (HPA) due to BH4-responsive phenylketonuria (PKU). The proposed mechanism of action for BH4 in the treatment of PKU is that it stimulates the residual activity of PAH. PAH is altered (mutated) in patients with PKU.
L15: BH_4 is an essential cofactor in the hydroxylation of Phe to tyrosine, which is catalyzed by the enzyme PAH. The hydroxylation of Phe to tyrosine is the first and rate-limiting step in Phe oxidation that eventually results in the complete oxidation of Phe to CO_2 and water. In this reaction, BH_4 binds with PAH and then reacts with oxygen to form an active intermediate that hydroxylates Phe to produce tyrosine. BH_4 is converted to q-dihydrobiopterin (qBH_2) in this reaction. Normally, BH_4 is then recycled by the NADH-dependent enzyme dihydropteridine reductase (DHPR), which converts qBH_2 to active BH_4. The hydroxylation of Phe to tyrosine is represented graphically in the following figure:
L16: **Figure 1: Hydroxylation of Phenylalanine to Tyrosine**
L17: BH_4 also serves as a natural cofactor for the hydroxylation of aromatic amino acids (e.g., tyrosine and tryptophan), and for nitric oxide synthase and glyceryl-ether monooxygenase.
L18: PKU (classic PKU) is a rare autosomal recessive inherited disorder. Approximately one of every 15,000 infants in the United States (US) is born with PKU. Affected patients have deleterious mutations in the *PAH* gene that lead to deficient hepatic PAH enzymatic activity, which results in elevated levels of blood Phe (hyperphenylalaninemia, HPA). HPA is toxic to the central nervous system (CNS), and prolonged elevations in blood Phe lead to neurocognitive impairment. HPA is thought to inhibit the transport of large neutral amino acids (LNAA) into the brain. Reduction of LNAA in the brain is thought to cause inhibition of protein synthesis and neurotransmitter synthesis, leading to deficient serotonin and dopamine levels. When untreated, this can result in mental retardation, microcephaly, delayed speech, seizures, behavioral abnormalities, and other symptoms. More than 400 different *PAH* mutations have been identified, and most patients are compound heterozygotes. Consequently, PKU has a broad spectrum of phenotypes. In patients with BH_4-responsive PKU, BH_4 appears to stimulate residual PAH activity, promoting the hydroxylation of Phe to tyrosine, and resulting in lower blood Phe levels; however, no clear association with the type of PAH mutation and BH_4-responsiveness has been found to date.
L19: HPA can also be a consequence of primary BH_4 deficiency. Patients with primary BH_4 deficiency have normal activity of PAH; however, they have mutations in one of several genes coding for the enzymes involved in BH_4 synthesis or recycling, resulting in HPA. Primary BH_4 deficiency is found in about 1% of patients with HPA.
L20: Phe is an essential amino acid that is obtained from the diet. Phe is necessary to meet requirements for endogenous protein syntheses. The nutritional requirement for Phe is
L21: difficult to estimate in normal subjects, but estimates in treated PKU patients indicate that the minimum requirement to support protein synthesis is approximately 200 to 500 mg/day in infants and young children, and probably not more than 1.5 times greater than that in older children and adults.
L22: Newborn screening for PKU began in the US in the 1960s, and all states in the US currently screen infants for PKU. Newborn screening has been very successful in preventing severe mental retardation that can result from PKU. When diagnosed early in the newborn period and treated by diet to achieve good metabolic control, infants with PKU have normal health and development.
L23: The National Institutes of Health (NIH) held a Consensus Conference on the screening and management of PKU in October, 2000. For this conference, an extensive literature search on PKU (Medline searched from January 1980 through July 2000) was performed, and the findings and recommendations of the conference were published in 2001.1 Important findings from this publication are summarized as follows:
L24: - Implementing a Phe-restricted diet early in life can significantly reduce mental deficiencies associated with PKU. Age at treatment initiation and levels of metabolic control have been clearly shown to influence neurocognitive outcomes; patients with poorer metabolic control (i.e., elevated Phe levels) show significantly lower scores on measures of IQ, attention, and reaction time.
- Metabolic control is necessary across the life span of individuals with PKU. Dietary discontinuation before eight years of age is associated with poorer performance on IQ measures. The effects of dietary discontinuation at older ages (\ge 12 years of age) are less clear. Adults did not show decreasing IQ if they were not on restricted diets; however, these adults manifest poorer performance on measures of attention and speed of processing.
- Infants with blood Phe levels &gt;600 \mu\text{mol/l} (10 mg/dL) should start treatment within a week after birth. Most physicians treat newborns with persistent levels between 420 and 600 \mu\text{mol/l}.
- There is no consensus concerning optimal levels of blood Phe, either across different countries or among treatment centers in the US, and formally recommended guidelines do not exist in the US. The most commonly reported blood Phe recommendations in US clinics are 120 and 360 \mu\text{mol/l} (2 to 6 mg/dL) for neonates through age 12 years, and between 120 and 900 \mu\text{mol/l} (2 to 10 mg/dL) for ages greater than 12 years. The Conference stated that maintenance of Phe levels between 120 and 360 \mu\text{mol/l} (2 to 6 mg/dL) for patients \le 12 years of age seems to be medically necessary for ensuring optimal outcome. The conference noted that blood Phe levels related to cognitive function in adolescents and adults,

L27: The current treatment for HPA due to PKU is to restrict Phe in the diet and to supplement the diet with low-Phe medical foods. Compliance with diet, especially in older children, adolescents, and adults, is difficult, and non-compliance at older ages is almost universal. Barriers to adherence include economic, psychosocial, and health care system issues. Thus, new treatments for PKU are clearly needed.
L28: ## C. Regulatory History of Sapropterin
L29: The Applicant's (BioMarin's) initial Investigational New Drug (IND) application for the sapropterin clinical development program for the treatment of PKU was opened on 16-August-2004, under IND #69,708. The initial IND protocol was for a Phase 2, multi-dose, safety and efficacy study of sapropterin administration to approximately 400 patients with PKU.
L30: Prior to the opening of this IND in 2004, sapropterin dihydrochloride (tetrahydrobiopten; BH4) as Biopten 2.5% granules was approved for use in Japan in 1992 for the treatment of atypical HPA due to primary BH4 deficiency due to mutations in GTP cyclohydrolase 1,6-pyruvoyl-tetrahydropterin synthase or dihydropteridine reductase (DHPR) (licensed by Daiichi Suntory Pharm Co [Daiichi Suntory]). The labeled dosage for the treatment of primary BH4 deficiency is 2 to 5 mg/kg/day (of sapropterin) orally in one to three divided doses. BioMarin acquired the rights to sapropterin from Daiichi Suntory to develop and commercialize sapropterin in countries outside of Japan.
L31: As a part of the agreement between BioMarin and Daiichi Suntory, BioMarin was given access to nonclinical pharmacology and toxicology data, and clinical safety and pharmacokinetic (PK) data for sapropterin used in support of the Japanese license. A summary of this information was submitted as part of the initial IND. This supportive clinical information included previous human experience with sapropterin that had been obtained in approximately 600 patients in 15 clinical studies with sapropterin administration conducted by Daiichi Suntory. These studies included single-dose and multiple-dose PK studies in healthy adult male volunteers (24 patients), and efficacy and safety studies in infants and children with autism (approximately 450 patients in seven studies), patients with primary BH4 deficiency (16 patients), and patients with Machado-Joseph disease (spinocerebellar ataxia type 3; approximately 120 patients in four studies). The information also included published literature on the use of BH4 in patients with PKU (from four publications involving 14 patients), and post-marketing safety surveillance data in 30 Japanese patients (27 primary BH4 deficiency and three PKU patients) treated with BH4 for up to ten years.

L57: ## III. Nonclinical Pharmacology and Toxicology
L58: Nonclinical pharmacotoxicology data have been reviewed in detail by the Animal Pharmacotoxicology Reviewer (Fang Cai, Ph.D.); please refer to this review for more detailed information. The Pharmacotoxicology Reviewer's overall assessment based on the review of the pharmacotoxicology (pharmtox) data submitted in this NDA is that the application should be approved. Notable findings from Dr. Cai's review include:
L59: - Sapropterin tested positive in the bacterial reverse mutation and chromosome aberration test, but was not mutagenic when assessed in the mouse micronucleus tests. Sapropterin may have mutagenic and/or clastogenic potential.
- The carcinogenic potential of sapropterin was assessed in a 104-week oral carcinogenicity study in rats, and in a 78-week oral carcinogenicity study in mice. There was a dose-related increase in the incidence of benign adrenal pheochromocytoma in males, which was significantly increased in high-dose (250 mg/kg/day) male rats relative to the control group. In female mice, a slight but non-significant increase in the incidence of pulmonary adenoma was observed at the mid-dose (80 mg/kg/day) and high-dose (250 mg/kg/day).
- Pregnancy Category C was recommended for sapropterin labeling. In nonclinical studies in rats and rabbits:Sapropterin was not teratogenic in rats at doses up to 400 mg/kg/day; however, there was a significant reduction in the numbers and in the body weights of live fetuses from F1 dams treated at the 4,000 mg/kg/day dose.
- In rabbits treated at oral doses up to 600 mg/kg/day, there was a significant increase in the incidence of holoprosencephaly at the 600 mg/kg/day dose compared to controls.
- In the post-natal and peri-natal developmental rat study with sapropterin, the average number of stillbirths was increased in 40 and 400 mg/kg/day-treated dams, and birth rate was decreased in 400 mg/kg/day dams. An increased incidence of newborn pups (F2) with thymic cervical residue was noted in all treated groups.
- Sapropterin did not produce any effects on fertility and reproductive functions of male and female rats at an oral dose up to 400 mg/kg/day.
- Sapropterin is excreted in the milk of intravenously, but not orally treated lactating rats. It is not known whether sapropterin is excreted in human milk. The Pharmacotoxicology Reviewer recommended that labeling include wording that because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in nursing infants from sapropterin, and because of the potential for tumorigenicity shown for sapropterin in the rat carcinogenicity study, a decision should be made whether to discontinue the drug taking into account the importance of the drug to the mother.
- Sapropterin had no antigenic potential under the conditions of any of these studies.
L60: Overall, the Pharmacotoxicology Reviewer assessed that the Applicant had conducted adequate preclinical studies with sapropterin to determine the safety of the drug; however, relevant findings of the preclinical studies should be included in the labeling. There were no additional recommendations for nonclinical studies for this NDA.

L68: Notable findings from Dr. Ahn's review include:
L69: - PKU-005 and PKU-009 evaluated the PK of Kuvan in healthy adults. In these studies, T_{max} and T_{1/2} were each approximately four hours regardless of mode of administration or delivery (swallowed as intact tablets or dissolved in water or juice, or given fed or fasted).
- Multiple-dose PK studies were not conducted with the Kuvan TBMP in healthy volunteers; however, steady-state PK was evaluated using a population PK approach in Study PKU-004 sub-study 02, which was the only PK study conducted in the target patient population with the TBMP. The results showed that the PK of sapropterin was described by a two-compartment model with a short lag time (16.5 minutes) prior to first-order absorption, followed by biphasic elimination. Within-subject variability was 22%. Total body weight was the only significant patient variable identified to be a predictor of PK variability; gender and age did not significantly affect the PK variability. Other intrinsic factors, such as renal insufficiency, hepatic insufficiency, and ethnicity have not been studied.
- Extrinsic factors, such as drugs, herbal products, smoking, and alcohol, have not been studied. The Applicant states that there are no known clinically relevant pharmacodynamic (PD) interactions with other medicinal products or substances; however, patients who required concomitant treatment with any drug known to inhibit folate synthesis (e.g., methotrexate) were excluded from study. Folate synthesis inhibitors are known to inhibit DHPR *in vivo* and *in vitro*. After conversion of BH4 to qBH2 during the hydroxylation of Phe to tyrosine, BH4 is normally recycled back to active BH4 by DHPR. Thus, folate synthesis inhibitors have the potential to decrease levels of BH4.
- The ability of Kuvan to maintain stable blood Phe levels during a 24-hour period when given as a single dose was evaluated in two sub-studies: PKU-001 sub-study 01 (n=11) and PKU-004 sub-study 01 (n=12). In both sub-studies, mean blood Phe levels remained stable over the course of the 24-hour period after administration of Kuvan 10 mg/kg/day. The results were notable for large inter-subject variability in blood Phe levels, and there were many missing data points; however, intra-subject variations in blood Phe levels were relatively small, and there seemed to be no spikes of Phe levels in individual data. Thus, the once-daily dosing interval of Kuvan is based on the PD properties of sapropterin on blood Phe reduction, rather than the known PK properties of sapropterin in humans, which can be justified by the PD results in the two sub-studies. It is noted that all of the PKU clinical efficacy and safety studies conducted in the Kuvan clinical development program were conducted using a once daily dose of Kuvan.
- In Study PKU-005, under fasted conditions, administration of Kuvan dissolved in orange juice has equivalent bioavailability (BA) to Kuvan dissolved in water. Under fed conditions (high-fat, high-calorie meal), Kuvan dissolved in orange juice has 25% less BA than Kuvan dissolved in water. Since orange juice contains a
L70: significant amount of Phe, the Applicant proposed that the drug be dissolved in water or apple juice. The BA of Kuvan in apple juice has not been studied; however, the stability data on Kuvan dissolved in apple juice and water were comparable (per CMC Reviewer). Therefore, the Clinical Pharmacology Reviewer assessed that the Applicant's proposal that Kuvan be administered dissolved in water or apple juice is acceptable. It is additionally noted that Kuvan was administered dissolved in water, apple juice, or orange juice in the clinical safety and efficacy studies, where the efficacy (by PD effect on blood Phe level) of Kuvan has been demonstrated. It was not feasible to detect differences in efficacy or safety between vehicles in the clinical studies because no records stating which patients used which vehicle were provided.
L71: - In Study PKU-009, Kuvan administered as an intact tablet was 20% more bioavailable than a dissolved tablet under fasted conditions. However, the Kuvan tablets used in this study were manufactured by [REDACTED] and the TBMP is produced by [REDACTED]. The manufacturing change from [REDACTED] that requires a bioequivalence (BE) study before the two formulations could be assessed as comparable. Therefore, since clinical safety and efficacy trials were conducted with the [REDACTED] clinical BA studies were conducted with the [REDACTED] tablets, and there were no data available to assess the comparability of the two formulations, the Clinical Pharmacology Reviewer recommended that Kuvan tablets be labeled for administration to patients only after dissolution in water or apple juice, and not as intact tablets, since dissolution in solution of the tablet diminished the differences between the formulations manufactured by different processes.

- Food effect on the BA of Kuvan was studied in PKU-009 and PKU-005. The two studies indicated that food (high fat, high calorie meals) increased the BA of Kuvan, especially when the drug was administered as solution dissolved in water (an 87% increase compared to fasting conditions). The pivotal clinical studies were conducted regardless of relationship to meals, and no information on the PD effect of Kuvan in these trials in relation to food is available. Therefore, Dr. Ahn recommended that the drug be taken with food in order to optimize the BA of Kuvan.

- The Applicant proposed that the starting dose of Kuvan be 20 mg/kg/day, based on the results of Study PKU-006, and that doses of Kuvan may be adjusted in the range of 5 to 20 mg/kg/day. However, a starting dose of 10 mg/kg/day was used in Study PKU-001 (n=490), with an approximately 20% response rate seen in this study. Thus, the 10 mg/kg/day dose has been demonstrated to be efficacious as a starting dose, and sapropterin 10 mg/kg/day is recommended as the starting dose. Patients may then be titrated up or down from the 10 mg/kg/day dose based on response.

- A clear dose-response relationship with Kuvan was demonstrated during the forced dose-titration period in Study PKU-004. Efficacy was seen in this clinical study in
L72: some patients with doses as low 5 mg/kg/day. The highest dose administered in clinical studies was 20 mg/kg/day, and doses of 20 mg/kg/day have not been assessed. Thus, a dose range of 5 to 20 mg/kg/day is acceptable.

L76: ## A. Clinical Studies
L77: The most important clinical efficacy information submitted in the NDA is from four BioMarin-sponsored, short-term (up to 30 weeks), clinical efficacy (and safety) studies conducted with Kuvan (sapropterin) in a total of 579 patients with PKU. The data contained in these four studies were amenable to substantive review, and are supportive of the clinical effectiveness of Kuvan in the treatment of BH4-responsive PKU. These four studies are:
L78: - PKU-001 (Enrichment Study): a non-randomized, open-label (OL), uncontrolled, safety and efficacy study of sapropterin 10 mg/kg/day administered once a day for up to eight days to 489 patients with PKU, ages eight years and older. The purpose of the study was to identify “responders” to sapropterin treatment, defined as patients who had a \ge 30\% decrease in blood Phe from Baseline (pre-treatment) at Day 8. Responders were then eligible for enrollment in PKU-003 (Efficacy Study).
- PKU-003 (Efficacy Study): a randomized (1:1, sapropterin or placebo), double-blind (DB), placebo-controlled (PC), safety and efficacy study of sapropterin 10 mg/kg/day administered once a day for six weeks to 89 patients with PKU, who were responders in PKU-001. The primary efficacy endpoint was mean change in blood Phe from Baseline (pre-treatment) at Week 6 in each treatment group.
- PKU-004 (Extension Study): an OL, uncontrolled, extension study of 80 patients who completed PKU-003. The total duration of sapropterin treatment in PKU-004 was 22 weeks. PKU-004 was conducted in two parts:Part 1 was a six-week, OL, forced dose-titration period in which patients were consecutively treated with three different doses of sapropterin (5, then 20, then 10 mg/kg/day) for two weeks at each dosage level. The objective of Part 1 was to evaluate the dose-response of three different dosage levels
L79: of sapropterin. After completion of the forced dose-titration period, patients were then maintained on sapropterin 10 mg/kg/day for an additional four weeks (Week 6 to Week 10) while each patient's optimal dose of sapropterin was determined from the forced dose-titration period (with the objective of maintaining blood Phe level &lt;600 \mu\text{mol/l}, or a maximum dose of 20 mg/kg/day).
L80: - Part 2 was a 12-week, OL, fixed-dose treatment period, in which each patient's daily sapropterin dose was 5, 10, or 20 mg/kg/day based on the patient's blood Phe level results from Part 1. The objective of Part 2 was to evaluate the longer-term (12-week) safety and efficacy of sapropterin.
- PKU-006 (Diet Study) was conducted in two parts:Part 1 was a non-randomized, OL, uncontrolled, safety and efficacy treatment period in which 90 patients with PKU, ages four to 12 years, were administered sapropterin 20 mg/kg/day for up to eight days. The purpose of Part 1 was to identify responders to sapropterin treatment (defined as patients who had a \ge 30\% decrease in blood Phe from Baseline at Day 8) for continuation in Part 2 of the study.
- Part 2 was a randomized (3:1, sapropterin or placebo), DB, PC, dietary Phe-supplementation, safety and efficacy treatment period in which 45 responders from Part 1 were randomized to DB study drug treatment (sapropterin n=33, placebo n=12). Patients received sapropterin 20 mg/kg/day without dietary Phe supplementation from Week 0 (entry into Part 2) to Week 3. Beginning at Week 3, all patients' dietary Phe intake was supplemented by 5 mg/kg/day for two weeks. Dietary Phe supplementation could then be adjusted at Weeks 5, 7, and 9 based on blood Phe level results at Weeks 4, 6, and 8, while maintaining blood Phe control (i.e., blood Phe &lt;360 \mu\text{mol/l}). The primary endpoint for Part 2 (and for PKU-006) was the mean amount of dietary Phe supplement tolerated after ten weeks of DB treatment in each treatment group.

L85: ## B. Efficacy Results
L86: The efficacy of sapropterin in the treatment of HPA due to PKU was assessed in four short-term, clinical efficacy (and safety) studies, with the total length of sapropterin administration to individual patients ranging from one day to up to 30 weeks, at dosages of sapropterin 5 to 20 mg/kg/day. The efficacy variable of interest in the four efficacy studies was change in blood Phe level from no-treatment to treatment during the studies. In all four studies, sapropterin (or placebo) was administered as a single daily dose with the tablets dissolved in water, apple juice, or orange juice. Diet was controlled only in the PKU-006 study. In the remaining studies (PKU-001, -003, and -004), patients were instructed not to alter their diet and dietary Phe intake was monitored by patient-reported diaries, but no formal diet-control measures were undertaken during these studies.
L87: The conduct and results of the four short-term efficacy (and safety) studies submitted in this NDA are summarized as follows:
L88: ## 1. PKU-001: Enrichment Study
L89: PKU-001 (Enrichment Study) was a multi-center, multi-national (US and Europe), non-randomized, OL, uncontrolled, safety and efficacy study of sapropterin administration for up to eight days to 489 patients with PKU. All patients in the study received treatment with sapropterin 10 mg/kg/day once daily. The purpose of the study was to identify “responders” to sapropterin treatment, defined as patients who had a \ge 30\% decrease in blood Phe from Baseline at Day 8 (primary outcome variable). Patients who were identified as responders in this study were eligible for enrollment and treatment in the DB, PC study PKU-003. L90: Patients were eligible for treatment in this study if they were ages eight years or older, had a diagnosis of PKU, and had a Baseline blood Phe \ge 600 \mu\text{mol/l} (criterion in original protocol; n=432 patients entered) or \ge 450 \mu\text{mol/l} (after protocol Amendment 2; n=57). Patients were excluded from the study if they had a diagnosis of primary BH4 deficiency, or had liver enzyme elevations (ALT &gt;5 times the upper limit of normal [ULN]), among others. L92: Analysis of the primary outcome variable showed that 96 of 485 patients (20%) were responders to sapropterin treatment (had a decrease in blood Phe \ge 30\% from Baseline at Day 8). L93: For the efficacy analysis population (ITT) overall, blood Phe decreased by a mean of 10%. Patients responded across a range of changes in blood Phe from Baseline, with an approximately normal distribution ranging from about -500 to +300 \mu\text{mol/L}. L127: ## 4. PKU-006: Diet Study
L128: PKU-006 (Diet Study) was a multi-center, multi-national study conducted in two parts:
L129: Part 1 was the non-randomized, OL, uncontrolled, short-term, safety and efficacy period of the study, in which 90 patients with PKU, ages four to 12 years, were administered sapropterin 20 mg/kg/day for up to eight days. Responders were defined as patients who had a decrease in blood Phe of \ge 30\% from Baseline (Day 1, pre-treatment) at Day 8 and had a blood Phe level \le 300 \mu\text{mol/l} on Day 8 (efficacy endpoint for Part 1). Responders were eligible for continuation in Part 2 of the study. Non-responders were discontinued from the study.
L130: Part 2 was the randomized, DB, PC, dietary Phe-supplementation, safety and efficacy period of the study. Patients who were identified as responders in Part 1 and agreed to participate in Part 2 were randomized to treatment in Part 2 after a sapropterin washout period of at least one week. Patients were randomized 3:1 to sapropterin 20 mg/kg/day or
L131: placebo beginning at Week 0 (prior to dosing in Part 2) for 10 weeks of treatment (through Week 10). Blood Phe was evaluated weekly, and no changes to the diet were made for the first three weeks of the study (Week 0 through Week 3 study visit). Beginning at Week 3, all patients' dietary Phe intake was increased (with no-fat dried milk or dry egg powder) as follows:
L132: - At Week 3, dietary Phe was increased by 5 mg/kg/day for a two-week period if blood Phe \le 300 \mu\text{mol/l}.
- Dietary Phe could be modified at Weeks 5, 7, and 9 based on blood Phe levels at Weeks 4, 6, and 8, respectively. Dietary Phe could be increased by an additional 5 mg/kg/day for blood Phe 241 to \le 300 \mu\text{mol/l}, by 10 mg/kg/day for blood Phe 181 to \le 240 \mu\text{mol/l}, and by 15 mg/kg/day for blood Phe 0 to \le 180 \mu\text{mol/l}. For the secondary endpoint of comparison between the treatment groups of the mean amount of additional
L137: Phe supplement tolerated at the end of the study (Week 10), the amount tolerated by the sapropterin group was significantly greater than the placebo group (p&lt;0.001).

Responders were eligible for entry into Part 2 of PKU-006. Non-responders were discontinued from treatment with sapropterin.

L142: ## 5. Efficacy Conclusions
L143: The efficacy findings from the four Kuvan short-term efficacy and safety studies show that:
L144: - Twenty percent (20%) of patients with PKU, ages eight years and older, who were not under dietary-control with a Phe-restricted diet at study entry, responded to treatment with sapropterin 10 mg/kg/day once daily, as evidenced by a \ge 30\% decrease in blood Phe levels from a no-treatment Baseline at Day 8 of treatment (PKU-001).
- Patients with BH4-responsive PKU (from PKU-001) had statistically significant and clinically meaningful decreases in blood Phe levels with sapropterin treatment as compared to placebo treatment. Blood Phe levels decreased after one week of treatment (first measurement during DB treatment), and the decreases were sustained throughout the six-week treatment period of the study (PKU-003).
L145: - There was a clear dose-response effect of sapropterin treatment on blood Phe levels in patients with BH4-responsive PKU (from PKU-001 and -003), with statistically significant differences in mean change in blood Phe levels seen between the different dosage levels of sapropterin 5, 10, and 20 mg/kg/day. Blood Phe level decreases were sustained throughout the 22-week treatment period of this study (PKU-004).

This amount was significantly greater than the pre-treatment amount of zero Phe supplement at Baseline, and was significantly greater than the amount tolerated by the placebo group of 3 mg/kg/day.
- safety of this outcome measure on the long-term neurocognitive development of pediatric patients with PKU have been demonstrated (PKU-006 Part 2).
- Patients with BH4-responsive PKU (from PKU-006 Part 1) who were treated with sapropterin 20 mg/kg/day had a statistically significant and clinically meaningful mean decrease in blood Phe level after three weeks of treatment (prior to dietary Phe supplementation) of 149 \mu\text{mol/l} (PKU-006 Part 2).
L146: The overall assessment of the efficacy findings by the Clinical Reviewer is that Kuvan should be approved for the treatment of HPA due to BH4-responsive PKU. The overall assessment of the Statistical Reviewer was that there are no statistical issues in this application, the design and analysis of the studies are adequate, and the data support the conclusion that Kuvan is efficacious in lowering blood Phe levels in BH4-responsive PKU patients. Both Reviewers also found that there is insufficient data to indicate a change in diet for PKU patients receiving Kuvan treatment.
L147: This Reviewer is in agreement that sapropterin treatment resulted in clinically meaningful decreases in blood Phe levels in patients with BH4-responsive PKU, and that Kuvan should be approved for the treatment of HPA due to BH4-responsive PKU.
L148: ## C. Safety Results
L149: The safety experience with sapropterin includes exposure to sapropterin to a total of about 1,400 subjects. L183: ## 3. Additional Safety Information
L184: A consultation from the QT-Interdisciplinary Review Team (QT-IRT) was requested by the Division during the review cycle. The consultation was requested because sapropterin is a new molecular entity (NME), and a clinical cardiac safety evaluation was not performed as part of the clinical development program for sapropterin in the treatment of PKU. Specifically, no routine electrocardiograph (ECG) monitoring was performed as part of the safety procedures in the clinical studies in PKU patients, and a thorough QT (TQT) study has not been performed.
L185: To date, the Applicant has performed nonclinical hERG channel and cardiovascular safety studies with sapropterin, which showed the following:
L186: - An hERG channel assay study (0162-06-005) was negative.
- An electrophysiologic/telemetry study (Study 0166-06-015) in eight conscious dogs at doses of 20, 50 or 100 mg/kg on Days 1, 4, 8 and 11 did not show abnormal findings by the cardiovascular parameters of ECG analysis, hemodynamic data (diastolic, systolic, and mean arterial pressures), inotropic state, and heart rate (NOEL 100 mg/kg).
- A second cardiovascular safety study (Study 0166-06-020) was performed in eight beagle dogs. Oral sapropterin (at doses up to 100 mg/kg) was administered in combination with sildenafil citrate. The results showed that sildenafil alone or in combination with sapropterin decreased diastolic pressure. No effect of sapropterin alone was noted on any of the cardiovascular parameters, but there was an indication the sapropterin may attenuate or augment the observed sildenafil-induced increase in pulse pressure.
L187: Sapropterin also underwent evaluation in single-dose or short-term PK and BA studies. The ECG data collected in these studies are summarized as follows:
L188: - PKU-009 and PKU-005 were two Phase 1 PK and BA studies in healthy adult volunteers, in which subjects received three or four, respectively, single doses of sapropterin 10 mg/kg/day, with each dose administered at least seven days apart. ECGs were performed at Baseline prior to treatment and one week after the last dose. Since the half-life of sapropterin is approximately four hours and serum levels of sapropterin at the time these ECGs were obtained were likely negligible, the ECGs collected in these studies are not useful for determining the effect of sapropterin on ECG parameters, such as QT interval.
- P1501 was a Phase 1 study in healthy adults with single- (n=6) and multiple-dose (n=6) administration of sapropterin. In patients treated with single-dose sapropterin on Day 1 and a second dose on Day 7, ECGs were performed at pre-dose, two hours post-dose, and 24 hours after the Day 7 dose. In patients treated for seven days with sapropterin, ECGs were performed at pre-dose, two hours after each morning dose, and 24 hours after the final dose. The results showed that no abnormalities in ECGs were observed, and no AEs were reported in the Cardiovascular System Organ Class (SOC).
L189: - FB1602 was a Phase 1, multi-dose, placebo-controlled study in healthy adult males (n=6 sapropterin; n=2 placebo). Study subjects received their daily study drug treatment as three divided doses for seven days. ECGs were performed prior to each daily dose, and two hours after each daily dose, and 24 hours after the final dose. The results showed that no abnormalities in ECGs were observed, and no AEs were reported in the Cardiovascular SOC.
- FB1701 was a Phase 1, multi-dose study in six healthy adult volunteers. Study subjects received their daily study drug treatment as three divided doses for seven days. ECGs were performed on Day 7 after completion of study. The study report does not clearly indicate if Baseline ECGs were performed. The results showed that no abnormalities in ECGs were observed, but the study report did not provide the ECG findings.
L190: Study HTN-001, a Phase 2, randomized, DB, PC, parallel-group study (n=39 placebo; n=77 sapropterin) was performed to evaluate the effects of sapropterin on blood pressure in patients with poorly controlled systemic hypertension. Sapropterin was administered as a 10 mg/kg/day dose, divided into two 5 mg/kg doses, for eight weeks. ECGs were performed at Screening, prior to dosing at the Week 8 visit, and at the Week 12 visit (which was four weeks after the final dose). The Applicant reports that the mean values for ECG parameters, including QT interval, were similar in the placebo- and sapropterin-treated groups. Mean changes in ECGs from Baseline were negligible in sapropterin-treated patients, and there were no AE reports that were suggestive of pro-arrhythmogenic effects of sapropterin.
L191: Upon review of the ECG and cardiac safety information provided in the NDA, the QT-IRT Consultant (Christine Garnett, Pharm.D.) recommended the following (please see completed consultation for more detailed information):
L192: - ICH E14 recommends that new drugs having systemic bioavailability undergo clinical ECG evaluation during clinical development; therefore, a TQT study with sapropterin is recommended.
- The Applicant should perform the TQT study consistent with guidelines per ICH E14. A single-dose, positive- and placebo-controlled, cross-over study in healthy volunteers may be sufficient to characterize the effects of administering sapropterin on the QT interval. The dose selected for the TQT study should give plasma concentrations to cover the expected high clinical exposure scenario in patients with PKU without compromising subject safety.
L193: Thus, the Division will request that a TQT study with sapropterin be conducted. The study should be designed per ICH E14 recommendations, e.g., as a single-dose, positive- and placebo-controlled, cross-over study in normals volunteers. In the opinion of this Reviewer, the TQT study can be conducted in the post-approval period, and will be required as a condition of approval as a post-marketing commitment (PMC). The post-
L194: approval conduct of the TQT study can be justified based on the facts that: 1) no noteworthy cardiac or other safety signal has been identified to date in clinical studies for PKU or in the clinical experience with other disease indications, such as hypertension; 2) non-clinical data (hERG channel assay and cardiovascular safety studies in dogs) did not show any cardiac safety concerns; 3) PKU patients are not at higher-risk of cardiovascular problems than the non-PKU population; and 4) sapropterin appears to have a favorable risk-benefit profile in BH4-responsive PKU patients, and appears to address an unmet medical need. Thus, delaying approval to conduct the TQT study does not appear to be warranted.
L195: The Applicant will be asked to submit the proposed study protocol for the TQT study for review by the QT-IRT prior to initiating the study.
L196: ## 4. Safety Conclusions
L197: Overall, sapropterin appears to have a favorable risk-benefit profile in the treatment of BH4-responsive PKU. Sapropterin appeared to be well-tolerated by patients with PKU during clinical trials. No deaths were reported, and few serious or clinically significant safety findings were noted. One safety signal, neutropenia, was noted to be temporally associated with the use of sapropterin during clinical safety and efficacy studies in PKU. Due to the design of the studies (OL, uncontrolled), and the limited safety population available for study, a definitive association of sapropterin administration and the development of neutropenia could not be established; however, neutropenia should appear in the Adverse Reactions section of the product labeling. Common AEs reported during DB clinical trials included headache, URI, rhinorrhea, pharyngolaryngeal pain, diarrhea, and vomiting. There were no meaningful differences in incidence rates for these common AEs between the sapropterin- and placebo-treated, groups, these AEs tended to be mild to moderate in intensity, and tended to resolve without intervention.
L198: A clinical cardiac safety evaluation was not performed as part of the sapropterin clinical development program. Since sapropterin is a new drug with systemic bioavailability, the QT-IRT recommended that sapropterin undergo clinical ECG evaluation. Therefore, the performance of a TQT study with sapropterin will be required of the Applicant as a condition of approval (i.e., as a PMC).
L199: ## D. Clinical Conclusions and Recommendations
L200: This Reviewer recommends that Kuvan be approved as a treatment of HPA due to BH4-responsive PKU.
L201: The Clinical and Statistical Reviewers assessed Kuvan as efficacious in the treatment of HPA due to BH4-responsive PKU. The Statistical Reviewer found that there were no statistical issues in this application, the design and analysis of the studies are adequate, and the data support the conclusion that Kuvan is efficacious in lowering blood Phe levels in PKU patients. Both Reviewers also found that there is insufficient data to indicate a change in diet for PKU patients receiving Kuvan treatment.
L202: For safety, sapropterin appears to be well-tolerated by patients with PKU, with few serious or clinically significant safety findings reported during clinical safety and efficacy studies. Given the potential benefits of sapropterin administration to patients with BH4-responsive PKU, sapropterin appears to have a favorable risk-benefit profile. Labeling for sapropterin should include the SAEs noted during sapropterin administration, the association of sapropterin administration and neutropenia, and the common AEs seen in DB, PC trials.
L203: Thus, this Reviewer is in agreement with the Clinical and Statistical Reviewers that sapropterin treatment resulted in clinically meaningful decreases in blood Phe levels in patients with BH4-responsive PKU, and that Kuvan should be approved for the treatment of HPA due to BH4-responsive PKU.

L225: - Since PKU is a chronic disease that will require management throughout the lifetime of patients, and since it is anticipated that Kuvan will be administered to patients for many years, thirty weeks of exposure to Kuvan is an insufficient length of time to adequately assess the long-term safety profile of Kuvan. This is especially problematic given that the goal of treatment in PKU is to prevent long-term neurocognitive impairment (over years) due to HPA, and because Kuvan will be administered to pediatric patients during a time of tremendous physical and neurologic growth and development. Thus, longer-term, post-approval evaluation of Kuvan in the treatment of pediatric patients will be required of the Applicant as a condition of approval. These requirements as PMCs will include:A long-term (i.e., at least five years) study to assess growth and neurocognitive development in patients less than or equal to eight years of age at study entry.
- Completion of the OL extension study PKU-008 for a minimum of two years of total treatment with Kuvan.
- A long-term (i.e., 15 years) voluntary registry to collect clinical status information, including neurocognitive status, growth, and development in patients with PKU treated with Kuvan.
- Patients less than four years of age were not included in the Kuvan clinical development program. evaluation of these
L226: patients in a short-term, safety, efficacy (by blood Phe levels), and PK study will be required of the Applicant as a condition of approval as a PMC.
L227: ## X. Regulatory Conclusions
L228: In the opinion of this Reviewer, the data in this NDA support the approval of Kuvan for the treatment of HPA due to BH_4-responsive PKU at a dosage of 5 to 20 mg/kg/day administered once daily. These data also provide the information needed for product labeling that are important for the safe and effective use of Kuvan. Important issues that should be identified in the product labeling for Kuvan are as follows:
L229: For efficacy, labeling should identify the results of the short-term (up to 30 weeks) clinical efficacy and safety studies conducted in patients with PKU, ages four years and older, that show evidence of the effectiveness of Kuvan in lowering blood Phe levels in a subgroup of patients with PKU.
L230: For safety, the Warnings and Precautions section of the labeling should reflect that:
L231: - The long-term effects of Kuvan treatment on neurocognitive outcome in patients with PKU have not been determined.
- Not all patients respond to Kuvan.
- Dietary control with a Phe-restricted diet and close monitoring of blood Phe levels by medical professionals familiar with the treatment of PKU are necessary for all patients receiving treatment with Kuvan.
- Kuvan should be used with caution in patients with hepatic insufficiency, and with co-administration of drugs known to inhibit folate metabolism, drugs known to affect nitric oxide-mediated vasorelaxation, and with levodopa.
L232: For the Use in Specific Populations section of the labeling, Kuvan should be listed as Pregnancy Category C, and labeling should state that patients with PKU who are less than four years of age have not been studied in clinical trials with Kuvan.
L233: BioMarin has agreed to a number of Post-Marketing Commitments (PMCs) for Kuvan that are needed to adequately define the safety and appropriate use of Kuvan post-approval. These PMCs are:
L234: - A short-term (six-month) study to evaluate the safety, efficacy (by blood Phe levels), and PK parameters of Kuvan administration to pediatric patients less than four years of age.
- A long-term study (i.e., at least five years) with Kuvan to assess growth and neurocognitive development in patients who are less than or equal to eight years of age at study entry.
L235: - Completion of the long-term (minimum of two years of total Kuvan treatment) extension study PKU-008.
- Implementation of a voluntary registry of patients with PKU being treated with Kuvan that will obtain long-term (i.e., 15 years) clinical status information on these patients.
- Conduct of a TQT study with Kuvan in normal volunteers.
- Analysis of blood samples for *PAH* gene mutations that were collected during PKU-001, for the purpose of determining whether patients with PKU with specific *PAH* mutations are likely to be responders to treatment with Kuvan.
