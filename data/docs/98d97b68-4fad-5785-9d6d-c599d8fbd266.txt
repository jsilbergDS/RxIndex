L20: ## Classic Congenital Adrenal Hyperplasia (CAH)
L21: Classic CAH is a rare, autosomal recessive disorder, affecting approximately one in every 10,000 livebirths.5 The most common cause of classic CAH is 21-hydroxylase deficiency (21OHD) due to mutations in the *CYP21A2* gene.5,6 In classic CAH, conversion of 17-hydroxyprogesterone (17OHP) to 11-deoxycortisol is defected, leading to decreased cortisol synthesis, loss of negative feedback, and therefore increased corticotropin secretion. The resulting adrenal stimulation creates an increased production of adrenal-derived androgens and a variable degree of aldosterone deficiency.6 Patients with classic CAH need lifelong hydrocortisone and/or long-acting glucocorticoids plus mineralocorticoids as clinically indicated.7

L23: ## Preclinical data

L27: (2.9-fold the MRHD based on AUC).a In rabbits treated with crinecerfont at approximately 2-fold the MRHD, three incidences of poly-malformations (craniofacial defects) and slightly lower mean fetal weights were observed. In rabbits exposed to 1000 mg/kg/day, slightly decreased fetal weights were observed.
L28: The review team of Division of General Endocrinology (DGE) and Division of Pediatrics and Maternal Health (DPMH) considers the nonclinical findings with crinecerfont were inconclusive as these findings occurred in a small number of rabbits. The team concludes that the nonclinical findings do not rise to the level of a Contraindication or a Warning and Precaution for embryofetal toxicity, and do not preclude conducting a postmarketing pregnancy safety study in pregnant individuals. Please refer to the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling review (PLLR) by Dr. Katherine Kratz of DPMH for details.8
L29: ## Clinical Experience
L30: According to the PLLR,8 there were three pregnancies reported in the clinical development program.
L31: Two pregnancies occurred in healthy volunteers who participated in the phase 1 study of QT/QTc evaluations.
L32: - A 22-year-old female subject received a single dose of 300 mg crinecerfont and a single dose of moxifloxacin. She became pregnant during the study based on urine and serum pregnancy test. The estimated date of conception was approximately 10 days after receiving the single dose of crinecerfont and approximately one month after receiving moxifloxacin. Study treatment was discontinued. The subject was subsequently lost to follow-up. There was no available information for additional laboratory assessment and pregnancy outcome.
- A female of unreported age was reported to be pregnant based on positive urine and serum pregnancy tests; however, ultrasound showed no evidence of pregnancy. The subject subsequently had decreasing beta human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) levels, consistent with a spontaneous abortion.
L33: One pregnancy was reported in a 32-year-old female subject with classic CAH during the open-label period in phase 3 study CAH3003. Crinecerfont was discontinued on the day of pregnancy diagnosed. This subject underwent an uncomplicated C-section and delivered a healthy infant at 38 weeks of gestation.
L34: With only three pregnancies and no pattern to the outcomes, the PLLR concludes that conclusions cannot be drawn regarding the impact of crinecerfont on pregnancy outcomes.
L35: ## Lactation
L36: In nonclinical study, crinecerfont was present in rat milk. Toxicity was not observed in rats exposed to crinecerfont via milk through lactation day 20. According to the PLLR, when a drug is present in animal milk, it is likely that the drug will be present in human milk.8 The DPMH review team is concerned that “healthy neonates exposed to crinecerfont via breast milk could develop adrenal insufficiency” and recommends “monitoring infants exposed to crinecerfont through breast milk for symptoms of adrenal insufficiency, such as weakness, decreased feeding and weight loss.”8

L51: ## Animal Data
L52: *Crinecerfont was administered orally to pregnant rabbits at doses of 100, 500, and 1000 mg/kg/day, and to pregnant rats at doses of 150, 500, and 2000 mg/kg/day during the period of organogenesis. No crinecerfont-related malformations were observed in rats at 4-fold higher than human exposure at the MRHD based on AUC. L53: *In a pre- and postnatal developmental toxicity study, crinecerfont was administered orally to pregnant rats at doses of 15, 50, and 250 mg/kg/day during the period of organogenesis and lactation through Day 20 postpartum. No changes in pup mortality, growth, sexual maturation, behavior, mating and fertility, or ovarian and uterine parameters were observed. The exposure in dams at the No Observed Adverse Effect Level (NOAEL) of 250 mg/kg/day was approximately 4-fold higher than human exposure at the MRHD based on AUC.*

L237: ## I. SUMMARY
L238: ## 1. Background
L239: This memorandum is in response to a consult request dated June 7, 2024 from the Division of General Endocrinology (DGE) pertaining to NDA 218808 and NDA 218820 for crinecerfont softgelatin capsules and crinecerfont oral solution, respectively. The Sponsor, Neurocrine Biosciences, Inc., is developing crinecerfont, a selective corticotropin releasing factor-1 (CRF_1) receptor antagonist, (b) (4)
L240: Crinecerfont was granted Orphan Drug designation; Fast Track designation; Rare Pediatric Disease Designation (RPDD); and Breakthrough Therapy designation. DGE requested that the Controlled Substance Staff (CSS) review the Applicant's NDA from an abuse potential perspective. CSS has not previously reviewed crinecerfont.
L241: Crinecerfont is a new molecular entity (NME) currently under investigation in pediatric and adult patients with classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia but has also been evaluated for the treatment of major depressive disorder (MDD) and generalized anxiety disorder under IND (b) (4) (Division of Psychiatry Products (DPP)). IND (b) (4) was withdrawn (b) (4). Neurocrine Biosciences acquired the development of this product from Sanofi (formerly Sanofi-Synthelabo, Inc. and then Sanofi Aventis US Inc.) in May 2015.
L242: CAH is a rare disorder in which deficiency of 21-hydroxylase results in glucocorticoid (cortisol) and mineralocorticoid (aldosterone) deficiencies. These deficiencies result in loss of negative feedback on the hypothalamus and pituitary, leading to increased corticotropin-releasing factor (CRF) and adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) secretion and consequently excess adrenal androgen production. Current standard of care therapies for CAH include glucocorticoid (e.g., hydrocortisone) and mineralocorticoid (e.g., fludrocortisone) replacement, and salt supplementation (in infants). However, supraphysiological doses of glucocorticoids are often required, creating a clinical management challenge of balancing disease control (by normalizing adrenal androgen levels) while avoiding Cushing's syndrome (due to excess glucocorticoid exposure).
L243: Crinecerfont, a selective corticotropin releasing factor-1 (CRF_1) receptor antagonist, reduces pituitary ACTH production and thereby helps to improve control of adrenal androgen levels and reduce the total daily glucocorticoid dose required to manage the disease. The Sponsor hypothesizes that inhibition of ACTH release in patients with CAH will decrease adrenal androgen production sufficiently to allow lower, more physiologic doses of glucocorticoids, thus minimizing the complications associated with glucocorticoid therapy.
L244: According to the Sponsor, crinecerfont exhibits limited distribution to the brain at concentrations demonstrated to engage pituitary CRF_1 and there were no observed CNS effects associated with abuse in pharmacology and toxicology studies. Crinecerfont was also determined to have no significant activity in a broad panel of receptors, transporters, enzymes, and ion channels, including those associated with abuse. Therefore, additional studies including non-clinical abuse potential assessments and a human abuse potential study were not conducted.
L245: Overall, CSS has not identified any abuse- or dependence-related concerns with crinecerfont and did not identify any evidence to suggest that crinecerfont poses risks of addiction liability in humans. CSS concludes that crinecerfont is unlikely to be abused and therefore should not be controlled under the Controlled Substance Act (CSA). The proposed drug product, if approved under this NDA will not require Section 9 (Drug Abuse and Dependence section) in its label.
L246: ## 2. Conclusions
L247: - The receptor binding and activity data indicate that crinecerfont is a selective corticotropin releasing factor-1 (CRF1) receptor antagonist. Receptor binding studies demonstrated that crinecerfont did not bind to any receptors, transporters, or ion channels typically associated with drugs having a potential for abuse.
- Additional studies including non-clinical abuse potential assessments and a human abuse potential study were not requested.
- A review of TEAEs in clinical studies did not reveal any evidence to suggest that crinecerfont has a potential for abuse or physical dependence. The most prevalent AEs observed in clinical trials were fatigue, headache, and pyrexia.
- The proposed drug product, if approved under this NDA, will not require a Drug Abuse and Dependence section in its label.
L248: ## 3. Recommendations (to the Division)
L249: - Based on crinecerfont low permeability, limited brain penetration and the lack of CNS activity, and the lack of abuse related AEs, crinecerfont does not appear to have abuse potential and should not be controlled under the Controlled Substances Act (CSA).
- Crinecerfont does not require a section 9 (Drug Abuse and Dependence section) in its label.

L260: ## 2. Nonclinical Pharmacology
L261: ## 2.1 Receptor Binding and Functional Assays
L262: The Sponsor conducted several in vitro studies to assess the binding and functional activity of crinecerfont. The receptor binding assay included receptors, transporters, and ion channels associated with abuse as well as various individualized studies conducted to determine
L263: crinecerfont's mechanism of action. The data, summarized below, indicate that crinecerfont is a selective CRF1 antagonist that inhibits CRF-stimulated ACTH secretion.
L264: Studies EVT0106 and EVT0147 were binding assays to determine the binding affinity of crinecerfont to receptors, ion channels, enzymes, and transporters, including those associated with abuse potential. The results of the studies indicate that crinecerfont at a fixed concentration of 10 µM (4830 ng/mL) demonstrated ≤50% inhibition of ligand binding or enzyme activity for all receptors and enzymes tested, including those potentially associated with abuse. The targets for which crinecerfont demonstrated &gt;50% inhibition of ligand binding included: 88% inhibition of acetylcholinesterase activity, 54% inhibition of sigma-1 receptor binding, and 66% inhibition of the serotonin transporter. Follow-up assays on the serotonin transporter were conducted across a range of concentrations, with the result indicating that secondary activity was not clinically relevant. These off-target effects are considered negligible compared to the nanomolar affinity of crinecerfont for CRF1 receptor.
L265: A series of studies (Table 2) were conducted to characterize the affinity and selectivity of crinecerfont for CRF1. These studies demonstrated that crinecerfont selectively binds to CRF1 with IC50 values between 0.6 and 2.5 nM (0.290 and 1.21 ng/mL) in human cell lines, mouse, rat, and gerbil brain tissue. There were no apparent species differences in its affinity. In Chinese Hamster Ovary (CHO) cells transfected with hCRF1, the IC50 value was 3.99 nM (1.93 ng/mL). Crinecerfont showed no affinity for hCRF2a or hCRF-BP.
L266: In addition, the binding affinity of NBI-756179, a minor human metabolite (representing approximately 6% of crinecerfont in human steady state), was characterized for its affinity for CRF1 (Study 23-74788-001-PH). According to the Sponsor, given the low abundance of NBI-756179, it is not expected to significantly contribute to the clinical CRF1 receptor antagonist activity following administration of crinecerfont. Therefore, all of the pharmacodynamic activity is assumed to be through the parent drug.

L270: ## 2.2 Animal Behavioral Studies
L271: ## *Safety Pharmacology*
L272: The Sponsor evaluated CNS/behavioral, and pulmonary effects of crinecerfont in a battery of safety pharmacology studies. The effect of crinecerfont on CNS function was assessed in mouse after single oral administration at doses up to 1000 mg/kg in 3 different studies: a modified Irwin test (Study: SNX0237), a barbital-induced sleeping time study (Study: SNX0240), and a PTZ-induced convulsive activity and mortality study (Study: SNX0239). The effect of crinecerfont on the CNS was also assessed in Sprague Dawley rats after single oral administration at doses up to 1230 mg/kg in a rotarod test (Study: SNX0241) and a locomotor and rearing study (Study: SNX0734).
L273: ## CNS effects:
L274: - Study: SNX0237 - The Sponsor conducted a modified Irwin study to evaluate crinecerfont tosylate for its CNS mediated behavioral effects and effects on body temperature. Male Swiss mice (N = 5/group) were administered crinecerfont tosylate at a dose of 10, 100, or 1000 mg/kg. Additional groups of mice were also treated with 10 mg/kg haloperidol as a positive control, along with its vehicle. According to the sponsor, all animals who received haloperidol decreased spontaneous motor activity and showed slowed irregular breathing within 30 minutes of administration, demonstrating the validity of the test. In contrast, all animals who received crinecerfont at doses of 10, 100, or 1000 mg/kg exhibited normal behavior and normal spontaneous motor activity. There were also no effects on body temperature at any dose observed up to 24 hours after administration.
- Study: SNX0240 - The effect of crinecerfont on barbital-induced sleeping time was assessed following single oral doses of 10, 100, or 1000 mg/kg in male mice (n=10 animals/group). A group of mice was also treated with caffeine (8 mg/kg) or diazepam (8 mg/kg) as positive controls. Barbital (200 mg/kg) was administered intraperitoneally 1 hour after the crinecerfont dose, and the latency to fall asleep and sleeping time were evaluated. According to the Sponsor, crinecerfont at doses up to 1000 mg/kg po did not significantly increase or decrease latency to fall asleep or sleeping time in barbital-treated male mice, indicating that crinecerfont has no sedative or stimulant effect. Under the same conditions, caffeine increased the latency to fall asleep and statistically significantly decreased the sleeping time, while diazepam statistically significantly decreased the latency to fall asleep and statistically significantly increased the sleeping time relative to vehicle control.
L275: - Study: SNX0241 - The purpose of this study was to evaluate the potential effects of a single oral dose of crinecerfont on motor coordination and muscle tone in male Sprague Dawley rats using the rotarod test. Sprague Dawley rats (N = 8/group) were administered crinecerfont tosylate at a dose of 10, 100, or 1230 mg/kg. A group of rats was also treated with diazepam (12 mg/kg) as a positive control. At 1-hour post-dose, the time the animals successfully remained on a rod rotating at a constant speed of 12 rpm was recorded for a maximum of 180 seconds. According to the Sponsor, crinecerfont did not induce any significant modification of the performance time on the rotarod test in male rats, indicating that crinecerfont did not impair motor performance.
- Study: SNX0734 - The purpose of this study was to evaluate the potential effects of a single oral dose of crinecerfont on locomotor and rearing activities in Sprague Dawley rats. Rats (n=8/group) were administered crinecerfont free base at 15, 250, or 1000 mg/kg. A group of rats was also treated with haloperidol (3 mg/kg) as a positive control. At approximately 4 hours post-dose, rats were individually placed in actimeters equipped with infrared light beams to monitor their spontaneous activity for 24 hours. According to the Sponsor, haloperidol significantly decreased exploratory locomotor and rearing during the first 30 minutes of recording. In contrast, single po administration of crinecerfont at doses of 15, 250, and 1000 mg/kg did not change exploratory behavior of male rats and did not change overall spontaneous locomotor activity up to 28 hours post-dose. Thus, crinecerfont did not have psychostimulant or depressant effects at doses up to 1000 mg/kg.
L276: ## Pulmonary effects:
L277: - The cardiovascular and respiratory effects of crinecerfont were tested (studies CVE0009 and RCV0031). In the cardiovascular study, beagle dogs were administered crinecerfont tosylate orally at 10, 100, or 1000 mg/kg in a dose-escalating design. Parameters evaluated included morbidity, mortality, clinical observations, body weight, body temperature, and cardiovascular assessment (arterial blood pressure, heart rate, and ECG parameters). There were no observed CNS-related clinical signs; clinical observations were restricted to intermittent vomiting in some animals. In the respiratory study, crinecerfont was administered orally at single doses of 10, 100, or 1000 mg/kg in male guinea pigs (n=8 animals/group). According to the Sponsor, crinecerfont at 10 and 100 mg/kg free base had no significant effects on any of the respiratory parameters measured. At 1000 mg/kg free base, crinecerfont induced long-lasting changes in respiratory parameters suggestive of a moderate respiratory depressant effect (increase in inspiratory, expiratory and relaxation times associated with an increase in peak expiratory flow and in tidal volume). However, these signs were likely associated with a poor clinical tolerance of the compound in guinea pigs, and not a CNS-depressant effect. Overall, the results of both studies indicate that, at these doses, crinecerfont does not appear to produce significant cardiovascular or respiratory effects.

L286: ## **3.2 Adverse Event Profile Through all Phases of Development**
L287: The Sponsor's analysis of abuse-related AEs (e.g., CNS-related, drug abuse related, etc.) is based on data from nineteen Phase 1 studies, three Phase 2 studies (2 in subjects with CAH and 1 in subjects with major depressive disorder), and two Phase 3 studies (subjects with CAH).
L288: ## Phase 1 Studies
L289: Based on the Sponsor's analysis of AEs captured in Phase 1 studies, very few healthy volunteers reported potential abuse-related AEs at single crinecerfont doses up to 800 mg (8-fold higher than a therapeutic single dose) or with multiple doses up to 400 mg/day. Crinecerfont produced sedative-type effects in several open-label trials, most notably somnolence and hypersomnia. However, the incidence rate of these events was similar to, or less than that of placebo in placebo-controlled trials. Additionally, there were no events of particular concern, such as euphoric mood, feeling drunk, hallucinations, or AEs related to actual abuse, misuse, dependence, or withdrawal involving crinecerfont.
L290: ## Phase 3 Studies
L291: The primary evaluation of the safety and efficacy of crinecerfont for the treatment of CAH comes from the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter Phase 3 studies in adult subjects with CAH (Study CAH3003) and pediatric subjects with CAH (Study CAH2006).
L292: - Study CAH3003 - was a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of crinecerfont 100 mg bid (fed) in adult female and male subjects with classic CAH due to 21-hydroxylase deficiency, followed by open-label treatment. The study was conducted at 54 sites in the United States (US), Canada, Europe, and Israel with 182 subjects enrolled. The most common neurological TEAEs (\ge 10\% in either treatment group) were fatigue (24.6% crinecerfont; 15.3% placebo), headache (15.6%; 15.3%), and pyrexia (5.7%; 10.2%). The incidence of potentially abuse-related TEAEs (consisting primarily of mood effects) in the double-blind period was similar or lower with crinecerfont compared to placebo. None of the TEAEs were of particular interest for abuse and are commonly observed in subjects with CAH.
- Study CAH2006 - was a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of crinecerfont in subjects 2 to 17 years of age with classic CAH due to 21-hydroxylase deficiency. The study was conducted at approximately 39 sites in the United States, Canada, Europe, and United Kingdom. A total of 103 subjects (69 crinecerfont and 34 placebo) were randomized into the study, of whom 102 subjects (69 crinecerfont and 33 placebo) received at least 1 dose of crinecerfont. In this study four crinecerfont-treated subjects (5.8%) experienced potential abuse-related TEAEs in the double-blind period of this study compared to one (3.0%) placebo-treated subject. However, most of these events were not considered related to study treatment. One subject experienced TEAEs of depression, anxiety, and suicidal ideation that were considered possibly related to study treatment, however, these are unlikely to contribute to abuse potential.

L294: Adverse events from multiple clinical studies indicate that crinecerfont does not produce pharmacodynamic effects that are typically associated with abuse. These data support the in vitro data indicating that crinecerfont does not have abuse potential.

L397: ## CAH and Pregnancy5,6
L398: Congenital adrenal hyperplasias (CAHs) are rare, autosomal recessive disorders. In approximately 95 percent of cases, CAH is characterized by deficiency of 21-hydroxylase (21-OHD) due to mutations in the CYP21A2 gene, which is known as classic CAH. The estimated prevalence of classic CAH in the U.S. is 1/10,000.7 46,XX infants with 21-OHD are typically born with atypical genitalia characterized by clitoral enlargement, labial fusion, and formation of a urogenital sinus caused by the effects of in-utero androgen excess on the development of the external genitalia. Rarely, virilization may be so profound that genital atypia is unrecognized, and male sex assignment with undescended testes is made at birth in a 46,XX patient. Signs of classic CAH include adrenal insufficiency and salt wasting in the neonatal period or virilization in the first few years of life. Classic CAH is diagnosed at birth through newborn screening with a high serum concentration of 17-hydroxyprogesterone. In patients with classic CAH, sexual function and fertility are impaired; however, in individuals who receive proper treatment and have regular intercourse, pregnancy rates exceed 90 percent. In pregnancy, treatment with hydrocortisone/prednisolone and fludrocortisone therapy is recommended, with dosage adjustments if symptoms and signs of glucocorticoid insufficiency occur.8

L472: ## III. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS
L473: ## Pregnancy
L474: Although craniofacial abnormalities were seen in rabbits in the nonclinical embryofetal development studies, the relationship of these findings to crinecerfont is uncertain. The animal data should be conveyed in subsection 8.1 of labeling under the “Risk Summary” and “Animal Data.” Clinical data related to crinecerfont use during pregnancy are insufficient to inform labeling, which should be explained in subsection 8.1 of labeling.
L475: DPMH recommends issuing a post-marketing requirement (PMR) for a descriptive pregnancy safety study (DPSS) for the following reasons:
L476: - Three pregnancies occurred during the drug development program, indicating that females of reproductive potential with classic CAH may take crinecerfont. Although there were pregnancies that occurred during drug development, there are no data from these pregnancies to inform the use of crinecerfont during pregnancy. Thus, a DPSS would provide necessary pregnancy safety data.
- Given that classic CAH is a rare disease, the number of pregnant patients who use crinecerfont is likely to be small. With a small number of pregnant patients using crinecerfont, a pregnancy exposure registry would be unlikely to adequately enroll patients; therefore, a DPSS is preferred.
- Crinecerfont is systemically absorbed and will cross the placenta. Crinecerfont has the potential to inhibit ACTH secretion in the developing fetus thereby reducing cortisol levels in the fetus, which may result in fetal growth restriction, preterm delivery or demise.
L477: ## Lactation
L478: Nonclinical data demonstrated that crinecerfont was excreted in milk of lactating rats. Although there are no clinical data related to crinecerfont use during human lactation, when a drug is present in animal milk, it is likely that it will be present in human milk. This information should be included in the labeling of subsection 8.2.
L479: Rats exposed to crinecerfont via milk did not experience adverse events; however, the nonclinical data are available until lactation day 20 only. This timeframe may not be sufficient to observe adrenal insufficiency in rats. Serious adverse events associated with crinecerfont include adrenal insufficiency or crisis, which is life-threatening. With anticipated use in lactating females and the potential for the severe adverse events of adrenal insufficiency or crisis in healthy neonates and infants exposed to crinecerfont via breast milk, DPMH recommends the inclusion of text in labeling about monitoring infants exposed through breast milk for signs and symptoms of adrenal insufficiency.

L555: The sponsor conducted C-QTc analysis using 43 subjects contributing 823 time matched \Delta\Delta QTc observations in the TQT study. Before modeling, the sponsor conducted exploratory analyses to examine the assumptions of the white paper model (i.e., linear mixed effect model). Crinecerfont caused small dose dependent increase in HR. Although the increase was &lt; 10 bpm (beats per minute) the sponsor assessed different HR correction methods for QT using drug free baseline data. According to the sponsor, population based, study-specific correction (QTcP) most effectively reduced correlations between QTc and RR. Therefore \Delta\Delta QTcP for assessment of QTc hysteresis and C-QTc modeling. Although a clockwise hysteresis was observed, indicating delayed QTc effect, the sponsor's final model did not account for delayed QTc effects. Since the LOESS line of the exploratory \Delta\Delta QTcP vs concentration graph indicated a sigmoid relationship, the sponsor developed an Emax model to describe crinecerfont C-QTc relationship (Figure 1). The estimates of INT, Emax, and EC50 were 1.95 msec, -6.95 msec and 874 ng/mL respectively.
L556: ## Figure 1: C-QTc model equation
L557: \Delta\Delta QTc_{ij} = INT_i + \frac{Emax_i \cdot Conc_{ij}}{EC50_i + Conc_{ij}} + \epsilon_{ij}
L558: where:
L559: - \Delta\Delta QTc_{ij} is the placebo-corrected change in QTc for subject i at time j;
- INT_i is the individual intercept in the absence of a treatment effect in subject i;
- Emax_i is the maximum drug effect in subject i;
- EC50_i is the drug concentration that results in half the maximal effect in subject i;
- Conc_{ij} is the observed concentration for subject i at time j;
- \epsilon_{ij} is the residual error for subject i at time j (assumed independent normal with mean 0 and variance \sigma^2).
L560: Source: Sponsor's Pharmacometrics Report, Page 26
L561: At the observed C_{max} following a 300 mg and 800 mg dose with high-fat meal, the mean model-predicted \Delta\Delta QTcP (90% CI) was -4.21 (-6.54 to -1.92) and -4.48 msec (-6.82 to -2.11), respectively.
L562: At the observed C_{max ss} in Study NBI-74788-CAH2001 for 100 mg BID with standard meal, the mean model-predicted \Delta\Delta QTcP (90% CI) was -3.83 msec (-6.12 to -1.56).
