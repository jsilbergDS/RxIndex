L89: ## 5.2.2.3 Epidemiology95
L90: Malignant melanoma involves cancerous changes to melanin producing cells found primarily on the skin. About 70% of melanomas appear on previously normal skin, and 30% occur in moles, nevi or other lesions.
L91: Superficial spreading melanoma is the earliest form of melanoma. This represents 70% of the cases at the time of diagnosis. Nodular melanoma accounts for 15% of cases, and frequently presents as an ulceration that does not heal in adults 20 to 60 years of age. Acral lentiginous melanoma occurs in about 10% of the cases and generally appears on the palms, soles and mucosal surfaces in the elderly. Lentigo maligna melanoma occurs in 5% of the cases, generally in sun damaged skin of the head, neck, and arms in the elderly.
L92: Melanoma accounts for 1 to 3% of all cancers within the United States, and about 5% of all skin cancers. Mortality due to melanoma is related to the depth of skin involvement. Superficial lesions &lt;0.76 mm are virtually always curable with adequate excision, whereas lesions with depths &gt;1.6 mm have only a 25% five-year survival rate.

L119: ## 5.4.1.1 SEER Introduction
L120: According to its website (http://www.seer.cancer.gov/), the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) program of the National Cancer Institute was founded in 1973, and currently collects data on cancer incidence and survival from 11 population-based and 3 supplemental registries. The registries (which include statewide registries in Connecticut, Hawaii, Iowa, New Mexico and Utah, in addition to metropolitan registries in Atlanta, Detroit, Los Angeles, San Francisco-Oakland, San Jose-Monterey, and Seattle) are estimated to encompass approximately 14 % of the United States population. In 2001, the SEER Program expanded coverage to include Kentucky and Greater California, while New Jersey and Louisiana resumed participation.
L121: While cases of melanoma in situ are collected,102 SEER calculates melanoma rates for invasive lesions only. Although SEER employs stringent quality control measures and is recognized worldwide as a source of United States cancer statistics (http://seer.cancer.gov/about/quality.html), the sponsor notes that for melanoma in particular there are challenges to complete ascertainment.
L122: SEER age-adjusted incidence of invasive melanoma for white males and females was 23.8 and 16.2 per 100,000, respectively, in 2000.103

L236: Teva notes that melanoma rates are rising faster than for any other cancer worldwide (ISS Section 11.2), and the incidence in the United States has increased by more than 3% annually since 1981.131 132 In 1990, the Centers for Disease Control (CDC) estimated that 1 person in 105 would develop melanoma during their lifetime, compared to 1 in 75 in 2000, and 1 in 50 by 2010 (NDA Appendix 18.3, pg. 13). The SEER registry has also reflected this increase. This is demonstrated in a study by Hall et al.,133 which reviewed SEER incidence data from 1973 to 1994, and found an increase in invasive melanoma incidence of about 150% in white males and about 90% in white females.
L237: Teva further notes that dysplastic nevi can be difficult to distinguish from melanoma in situ, and may be misclassified as such.
L238: **Reviewer comment:** Although there is some discussion in the literature on the causality behind the rising melanoma rates (including overdiagnosis versus a true increase in incidence)134, the rate increase both within the United States and worldwide is well documented. This factor will be addressed in the analysis of melanoma within the rasagiline trials by selecting a comparator group as closely temporally matched to the time periods covered by the rasagiline studies as possible.

L325: ## 5.6 Conclusions
L326: The analyses and other considerations of the occurrence of melanoma in the rasagiline development program yielded mixed results. While most aspects were not supportive of a causal relationship between rasagiline treatment and the development of melanomas, other analyses did suggest an increased risk with rasagiline exposure. The specific aspects which were not supportive of a causal association between rasagiline and melanoma included the following:
L327: - The rate of melanomas observed in the rasagiline development program was within the range of rates seen in five other Parkinson's disease therapy programs, and was exceeded by the rate of one of the four (tolcapone).
- Although some cases occurred after more extended rasagiline treatment, the latency between rasagiline treatment and detection of the melanoma was generally too short for biological plausibility (range: 2.5 and 55 months, median 13 months, mean 21 months).
- The rate of melanoma within the rasagiline development trials, and within the other Parkinson's disease therapy development programs for which datasets were submitted, was strongly associated with the study location of the clinical trials. Specifically, studies conducted within North America had higher rates of melanoma than those conducted outside North America.
- There has been a history of case reports associating melanoma with Parkinson's disease and in particular with levodopa treatment. Although the literature on the subject is limited to case reports, my assessment is that the examination of this issue in the literature has suggested that confounding factors, rather than medication, were responsible for the association.
- Melanoma rates are rising rapidly worldwide, making the upper limit of the expected number of cases difficult to ascertain. In addition, the rate of melanoma identification in the rasagiline development program increased considerably following the commencement of dermatologic screening for melanoma, which also complicates the determination of the number of expected cases.
L328: Elements of data that are suggestive of an elevated risk include the following:

L332: ## 5.6.1 Phase IV Randomized Controlled Trial
L333: While the data accumulated during the rasagiline development program is generally not supportive of a causal association between rasagiline and melanoma, the analysis is limited by the lack of a demographically equivalent, parallel control group. It is therefore recommended that Teva fully address this question through a Phase IV agreement by conducting a randomized controlled trial in rasagiline-exposed and unexposed Parkinson's disease patients to measure melanoma incidence.
L334: A randomized controlled trial is suggested as this design has the greatest likelihood of producing treatment and control groups that are equivalent except for the exposure of interest. Parkinson's disease patients (both newly diagnosed and those already on levodopa therapy) could be recruited through their outpatient providers. To control for geographic variation in background rates, it is recommended that subjects either be drawn from a single general location, or preferably that equivalent numbers of rasagiline exposed and unexposed subjects be drawn from various geographic areas. Given the much lower risk of melanoma among persons with increased melanin content in their skin, subject recruitment could be restricted to Caucasian subjects only.
L335: Baseline information for cohort members would include demographic information, information on melanoma risk factors and past diagnosis of skin cancer, as well as information on current and past Parkinson's disease therapies. Subjects would then be monitored through questionnaires (yearly or twice a year) inquiring about interim changes in Parkinson's disease therapies. Active screening for melanoma (once every three months) is recommended, as this will enhance the likelihood of complete melanoma detection and minimize the potential confounder of differing access to medical care. Upon study entry, subjects should also be given basic information on skin cancers and self-examination for suspicious lesions upon study entry.

L432: ## *Outstanding Issues:*
L433: - The occurrence of 15 cases of melanoma in rasagiline-treated subjects during the development program was investigated by analysis with various reference populations. This is discussed in Section 5.0 of this review. L473: ## 7. Discussion
L474: ## *Adverse Event Monitoring*
L475: Teva adequately captured and described safety data during the rasagiline development program. The three Phase III trials (TEMPO, LARGO and PRESTO) appeared to have been appropriately designed to capture treatment emergent adverse events and other safety data. The coding of adverse events was generally acceptable, although the FDA did request that the sponsor make several adjustments in how certain classes of adverse events were coded. The most notable of these adjustments was that fall-related verbatim adverse events be coded to both a new preferred term (PT) "FALLS" and, when fall-related verbatim terms included two or more events, to a reasonable PT for the precipitant or resultant verbatim term. Following these revisions, the coding process provided an accurate depiction of rasagiline's adverse event profile.
L476: ## *Exposure*
L477: The overall number of subjects exposed to rasagiline at any dose (including 1537 Parkinson's disease patients) just exceeded ICH guidelines. The three phase III trials each included a rasagiline 1 mg/day treatment arm, which is the dosage that Teva intends
L478: to recommend for the treatment of Parkinson's disease. The number of subjects exposed to this indicated dose at six months and one year also met ICH recommendations. As with most NDA safety databases, due to the relatively small number of persons exposed, there was limited power to detect infrequent drug-related events.
L479: The age distribution of subjects in the different treatment groups within the two placebo-controlled cohorts (monotherapy and levodopa adjunct) was similar. When the age distribution of the two placebo-controlled cohorts were compared to one another, subjects in Cohort 2 were slightly older (mean age 64.2 years) than those in Cohort 1 (mean age 61.3 years) which could be expected given that subjects in Cohort 2 (levodopa adjunct treatment) had more advanced Parkinson's disease. The age mean, median and range were likewise equivalent between treatment groups within the three Phase III trials. When adverse event risk was stratified by age greater or less than 65 years, no meaningful differences in risk were observed.
L480: For the 1537 Parkinson's disease patients exposed to rasagiline (Cohort 9), there was an overall male predominance (64.4%). A roughly equivalent percentage (~33%) of rasagiline and placebo group subjects in Cohort 1 and Cohort 2 were female. No significant difference in adverse event risk was associated with a particular gender.
L481: For Cohort 2 (placebo-controlled levodopa adjunct), Teva also stratified data by concomitant medications, including dopamine agonists, amantadine, entacapone and antidepressants. The use of the latter (antidepressants) was significantly restricted by the study protocol, due to serious adverse events associated with concomitant use with other MAO inhibitors. The most notable finding upon stratification was that rash (n= 10), skin disorders (n=5), tenosynovitis (n=5) and extrapyramidal syndrome (n= 10) were reported in the rasagiline treatment group only in participants receiving concomitant dopamine agonists. In addition, hallucinations and peripheral edema were seen primarily with combined dopamine agonist and rasagiline treatment.
L482: There were no cases of pregnancy or rasagiline overdose in patients receiving rasagiline during the development program.
L483: ## *Deaths*
L484: A total of twenty-one rasagiline-treated subjects, five entacapone-treated subjects and six placebo subjects died during the rasagiline development program. The reported causes of death were those expected in an older population. Six rasagiline-treated subjects, compared to no placebo or entacapone-treated subjects, died of cerebrovascular accidents. However, rates of non-fatal cerebrovascular accidents were similar in the two placebo-controlled cohorts. The rasagiline development database included none of the following causes of death: acute hepatic failure, acute renal failure, serious skin reactions, rhabdomyolysis, pancreatitis or hematological dyscrasias.
L485: ## *Serious Adverse Events (SAEs)*
L486: In general, rasagiline and placebo subjects had similar SAE risks. No specific SAEs were reported for more than 2% of participants in Cohort 9 (all Parkinson's disease patients
L487: exposed to rasagiline). SAEs reported by at least 1% of Cohort 9 subjects were accidental injury (1.9%), skin melanoma (1%) and angina pectoris (1%). In the primary data trials, there were no SAEs of liver failure, acute renal failure, aplastic anemia, serious skin reactions, or pancreatitis. Two SAEs of rhabdomyolysis were reported in ongoing studies, both following a fall and prolonged immobilization. One case lacked laboratory values (creatinine phosphokinase) confirming rhabdomyolysis.
L488: ## SAE: Melanoma
L489: Sixteen cases of melanoma were diagnosed among fifteen rasagiline-treated subjects, and one placebo-treated subject.150 An additional three melanomas were identified prior to treatment initiation. A number of factors complicated the analysis of melanoma within the rasagiline development program. The most significant of these was that most of the cases were diagnosed in extension studies without parallel controls, and that the majority (14 of 20) were identified following the implementation of active skin examinations to screen for melanomas in all rasagiline trials. Other confounding factors include the presence of concomitant Parkinson's disease and other medications (especially levodopa), the variable melanoma risk factors among patients, the different background rates for the geographic locations of the study sites, and the rapidly increasing rates of melanoma worldwide.
L490: When the number of cases of invasive melanoma (seven cases) in North American rasagiline-treated subjects was compared to the number of invasive melanomas expected by age-adjusted rates with the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) registry of the U.S. National Cancer Institute, the number of cases observed was significantly higher than expected. This remained true even when the number of cases expected by SEER rates was tripled to adjust for any potential under-reporting (Incidence Density Ratio: 3.0, 95% CI 1.3, 5.8).
L491: To address the effect of the active screening for melanoma on the number of melanoma cases uncovered, comparison was also made with another population undergoing melanoma screening through the American Academy of Dermatology (AAD) skin cancer screening program. Although the number of *invasive* melanomas did not differ significantly from those expected by age-adjusted rates from the AAD screening program (Incidence Density Ratio: 2.6, 95% Confidence Interval: 0.72, 6.74), the number of *in situ* melanomas was significantly increased (Incidence Density Ratio: 10.2, 95% Confidence Interval: 3.7, 22.1).
L492: The rate of melanomas within the rasagiline development program fell within the range of rates for five other Parkinson's disease therapy programs. It was exceeded by melanoma rate within in the tolcapone development program.
L493: While some analyses and other considerations of the data accumulated during the rasagiline development program are not supportive of a causal association between rasagiline and melanoma, others do suggest an elevated risk. However, these analyses are limited by the lack of a parallel control group with comparable demographics and

L495: Parkinson's disease history. It is therefore recommended that, as part of a Phase IV commitment, Teva conduct a randomized controlled trial to assess melanoma rates in Parkinson's disease patients who are exposed or not exposed to rasagiline.
L496: ## *Potential SAE: Tyramine Reaction*
L497: The co-administration of MAO inhibitors and tyramine (which is contained in a variety of foods such as aged cheese and red wine) may result in a hypertensive crisis (also known as a "cheese effect.") The sponsor studies specifically investigating the potential for hypertensive crisis resulting from interaction of rasagiline with tyramine administration is addressed by Dr. Len Kapcala in his review.
L498: Safety data was generally not suggestive of the presence of tyramine reactions with the dosages of rasagiline used in the Phase III studies. Specifically, no deaths were attributed to hypertensive reactions. In the placebo-controlled cohorts, no excess of discontinuations due to hypertension was seen among the rasagiline-treated subjects compared to placebo-treated subjects. Vital sign monitoring likewise did not register hypertension more frequently in the rasagiline-treated subjects. In Cohort 9, hypertension was reported as a serious adverse event in only 0.5% of subjects (n=7).
L499: A possible exception to this lack of evidence for tyramine reactions is a previously normotensive151 Phase I/II subject who experienced an unexplained hypertensive crisis that resulted in her discontinuation from the study.152 No mention was made of the tyramine content of the subject's recent diet in the study report or in the case report form, and a tyramine reaction cannot be excluded.
