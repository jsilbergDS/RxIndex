L76: ## II. BACKGROUND
L77: Amgen Inc. submitted BLA761344 seeking approval for tarlatamab for the above indication based on the efficacy and safety results from Study DeLLphi-301, a 3-part, phase 2, open-label, international trial in patients with advanced SCLC with disease progression after receiving previous platinum-based chemotherapy.
L78: Study DeLLphi-301 was conducted in 3 parts. Part 1 evaluated 2 dose levels of tarlatamab in which 176 subjects were enrolled and randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive either 10 mg or 100 mg of tarlatamab. Tarlatamab 1 mg step dose was given on cycle 1 day 1. The full dose of tarlatamab was randomized between 10 mg or 100 mg and was given on day 8 and 15 of cycle 1, and every 2 weeks (Q2W) thereafter. Administration of tarlatamab was an IV infusion for 60 minutes followed by a flush.
L79: Part 2 was a dose expansion phase at the selected target dose based on an interim analysis of Part 1. A total of 100 subjects (from Part 1 and Part 2 combined) were enrolled at the selected target dose level of 10 mg. Part 3 was initiated after completing enrollment of Part 1 and Part 2, to enroll additional subjects at the target dose level, with modified cycle 1 monitoring criteria.
L80: Eligible patients must have histologically or cytologically confirmed relapsed or refractory SCLC and had progressed or recurred following on platinum-based regimen and at least 1 other prior line of therapy, an ECOG performance status of 0 or 1 at baseline, and adequate organ function as specified in the protocol.
L81: The primary objectives of the Part 1 of the study were to evaluate the safety, efficacy and pharmacokinetics of tarlatamab at 10 mg and 100 mg administered orally, daily. The primary objective and endpoint of the Part 2 was to evaluate the anti-tumor activity of tarlatamab at 10 mg, as determined by Objective Response Rate. The primary objective of Part 3 was to evaluate safety of reduced monitoring period in cycle 1 at 10 mg dose of tarlatamab. Key secondary objectives/endpoints (all Parts) were to determine, pharmacokinetics and immunogenicity of tarlatamab.
L82: Eligible patients were to sign the informed consent before undergoing any protocol-specific procedures. Baseline clinical, laboratory and imaging scans should be performed within 21 days from the time of consent to enrollment. Tumor assessments were to be performed at Screening (within 21 days prior to enrollment), every 6 weeks from weeks 1 thru 48, then every 12 weeks thereafter until confirmed disease progression per RECIST 1.1, withdrawal of consent or start of new anticancer therapy. All scans were to be submitted to [REDACTED] (b) (4) the imaging CRO for central review.
L83: At the time of the data cut-off date (June 27, 2023), a total of 222 subjects had been enrolled across the 3 arms (176 subjects in Part 1, 12 in Part 2 and 34 in Part 3). The efficacy population to support the proposed indication consists of data from 99 subjects enrolled in Part 1 and 2 of the study, who received at least one dose of tarlatamab at the target dose of 10 mg. The safety population consists of 220 subjects enrolled in the study who received at least one dose of tarlatamab.

L92: ## 1. Subjects enrolled despite not meeting Eligibility Criteria
L93: - Subject [REDACTED] (b) (6) did not meet protocol inclusion criterion 103, which requires for subject to have “*histologically or cytologically confirmed relapsed/refractory SCLC*”. The subject was a 65-year-old male who was referred from another hospital with the diagnosis of small cell carcinoma, and had progressed following prior therapy with platinum, etoposide and atezolizumab for SCLC. Since an archived tumor sample was not available for confirmation, a biopsy was conducted at the site which revealed adenocarcinoma. The subject was enrolled into Part 1 of the study and received tarlatamab 100mg from [REDACTED] (b) (6). Treatment was discontinued per investigator’s decision and subject died to disease progression on [REDACTED] (b) (6).
L94: *Reviewer's comment: With the biopsy report of adenocarcinoma, the subject should not have been enrolled in the investigational trial with tarlatamab. Dr. Ahn claimed that “it would be rare for a small cell carcinoma to transform into non-small cell carcinoma/adenocarcinoma and that in her “clinical judgement, the treatment (with tarlatamab) would be their best option because it was progressing rapidly”. It is noted that no effort was made by the site to confirm the diagnosis of adenocarcinoma, to obtain genomic tumor markers or to consider a second primary, either from lung or another organ sites (e.g., breast or colon cancer), which could potentially offer alternative standard of care therapy for this subject.*
L95: *In her response to FDA's form 483 dated February 27, 2024, Dr. Ahn acknowledged the protocol deviation and the need to consult with the Sponsor concerning this subject's eligibility assessment.*
L96: *OSI recommends excluding data from this subject from all efficacy analysis.*
L97: - Subject (b)(6) did not meet protocol exclusion criterion 207, which excludes subjects with “history of arterial thrombosis...within 12 months of first dose of tarlatamab”. Baseline CT scan of the abdomen (07/15/22) showed the presence of an abdominal aneurysm with thrombosis, which was confirmed by subsequent follow-up scans measuring a maximum diameter of 4.1cm on 12/12/2022. The subject was enrolled into Part 1 of the study and received tarlatamab 10 mg from August 2, 2022, thru June 21, 2023. No apparent adverse events were reported.
L98: *Reviewer's comment: Reports from repeated CT scans of the abdomen indicate that the subject had an aortic aneurysm with thrombosis. She further stated that this was a case of dissection without thrombosis and was not sure the patient should be excluded. Where the radiology report and the sub-investigator's conclusion differed, it was not contemporaneously documented, nor was there a discussion with the medical monitor regarding eligibility.*
L99: *In her response to FDA's form 483 dated February 27, 2024, a follow-up CT angiography on 2/2/24 to assess the current status of the aortic aneurysm and identified no change in size of any complication such as thrombosis. Corrective and preventive actions to be taken include retraining relating to ALCOA principles and ensure that where questions arise from the available objective documentations, any subject's eligibility assessment will be fully documented by the investigator.*

L188: ## 2.2 DISCUSSION OF METHODOLOGY
L189: Per the Applicant's use-related risk analysis (URRA), pharmacy technicians are intended users of the proposed product; however, they were not included in the HF validation study. The Applicant states,
L190: *Although both pharmacists and pharmacy technicians are involved with preparing the drug product solution to the prescribed dose ... pharmacists have the sole responsibility of determining how their pharmacy would prepare a drug based off their interpretation of the drug product PI. The pharmacy technician is under the pharmacist's supervision and follows instructions ... that are developed by pharmacists and does not perform the task of interpreting information from the PI into steps that need to be followed.*
L191: We considered the Applicant's justification for not including pharmacy technicians in the HF validation study and we disagree because we consider pharmacists and pharmacy technicians to be distinct user groups due to differences in professional experience, knowledge, and expertise. However, this methodology limitation does not preclude our review of the HF validation study results in this particular instance.

L257: ## 3 BACKGROUND
L258: ## 3.1 Product Information
L259: The sponsor, Amgen is developing Tarlatamab (AMG 757, brand name Imdeltra; molecular weight of about 105 kilodaltons) as an intravenous injection indicated for the treatment of adult patients with advanced small cell lung cancer (SCLC) with disease progression on or after platinum-based chemotherapy. This indication is approved under accelerated approval based on the overall response rate and duration of response. Continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in a confirmatory trial(s).
L260: Tarlatamab is a bispecific T-cell engager (BiTE®) molecule that targets the tumor-associated antigen delta-like ligand 3 (DLL3). Tarlatamab consists of 2 single-chain variable fragment binding domains, specific for DLL3 and the T-cell receptor (TCR)-associated protein cluster of differentiation 3 (CD3), fused to a silenced fragment crystallizable (Fc) immunoglobulin domain to extend serum half-life. Tarlatamab is designed to simultaneously bind to DLL3 that is expressed on the surface of cancer cells and CD3 expressed on the surface of T cells.
L261: The mechanism of action of tarlatamab is T cell-dependent lysis of tumor cells. The pharmacological effect of tarlatamab is mediated by specific redirection of T cells to kill DLL3-expressing tumor cells. The bispecific binding of tarlatamab to T-cells and DLL3-positive tumor cells triggers T-cell activation, production of inflammatory cytokines, release of cytotoxic proteins, which results in redirected lysis of tumor cells. The pharmacodynamic response was characterized by transient cytokine elevation and immune related biomarkers.
L262: Tarlatamab has been developed as an injection with (1) 1 mg of lyophilized powder in a single-dose vial for reconstitution and (2) 10 mg of lyophilized powder in a single-dose vial for reconstitution. It is produced using recombinant DNA technology in Chinese hamster ovary cells. It consists of 982 amino acids and has a molecular weight of approximately 105 kilodaltons. It is for injection and is supplied as a sterile, single dose, preservative free, white to slightly yellow, lyophilized powder for reconstitution.
L263: Each 1 mg vial contains tarlatamab (1 mg), sucrose (37.1 mg) (b) (4) glutamic acid (0.72 mg), polysorbate 80 (0.04 mg), and sodium hydroxide to adjust pH to 4.2. After reconstitution with 1.3 mL of Sterile Water for Injection the resulting concentration is 0.9 mg/mL of tarlatamab. Each 10 mg vial contains tarlatamab (10 mg), sucrose (194.4 mg), (b) (4) glutamic acid (3.7 mg), polysorbate 80 (0.2 mg), and sodium hydroxide to adjust pH to 4.2. After reconstitution with 4.4 mL of Sterile Water for Injection the resulting concentration is 2.4 mg/mL of tarlatamab.
L264: The recommended dosage is an initial dose of 1 mg on Day 1 followed by 10 mg on Days 8, 15, and every 2 weeks thereafter. It is administered as an intravenous infusion over 1 hour.
L265: ## 3.2 Sponsor's position related to the question
L266: The sponsor posits that, as a therapeutic protein, tarlatamab is not expected to inhibit hERG channel function directly. Nevertheless, the sponsor has conducted concentration-QTc analysis of time matched PK/ECG data collected from studies 20160323 and 20200491.
L267: Study 20160323 was a phase 1, open label, multiple ascending dose study evaluating tarlatamab monotherapy and in combination with anti-PD-1 in subjects with SCLC. Only data from monotherapy part was used for C-QTc assessment. The planned doses in the ascending monotherapy phase were: 0.003, 0.01, 0.03, 0.1, 0.3, 1, 3, 10, 30, and 100 mg. Doses between 0.003 – 0.1 were to be administered to one subject per dose. Doses between 3 – 100 were to be administered in steps. Different step procedures were tested e.g., 1 mg on day 1 followed by step dose on day 4 or 8 and target dose on day 15 and Q2W thereafter. Triplicate 12-lead ECGs were collected at screening, pre-dose, and at end of infusion on C1D1, C1D4, C1D8, C1D15, C2D1, C2D15, C3D1, C4D1, C4D8, and C4D15. PK samples were collected at several time points including at the end of infusion.
L268: Study 20200491 was a phase 2, open label, registration study to evaluate the efficacy, safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetics of tarlatamab in subjects with recurrent SCLC. The study had 3 parts. Part 1 randomized subjects in 1:1 ratio to 10 mg or 100 mg Q2W. Part 2 was dose expansion at the selected target dose of 10 mg. Triplicate 12-lead ECGs were collected in first 50 subjects in part 1 (~25 subjects per dose level) with time matched PK at pre-infusion C1D1 and end of infusion C2D1 and C2D15.
L269: The sponsor's C-QTc analysis included 231 subjects with baseline QTcF and post-infusion time-matched PK/ECG data.
L270: The relationship between tarlatamab concentration and the absolute change from baseline in QTcF was evaluated by a linear mixed-effect regression analysis. Baseline QTcF was evaluated as a covariate and subject was evaluated as a random effect for intercept and/or slope in the model. The plots of model predicted tarlatamab concentrations and associated mean \DeltaQTcF (90% CI) were used to evaluate the concentration-response relationship.
L271: According to the sponsor a linear mixed effect model with inter-subject variability on intercept and slope best described the relationship between tarlatamab exposure and \DeltaQTcF. The sponsor's final model is presented in Equation 1.
L272: \Delta QTcF_{i,j} \sim (\theta_0 + \eta_{0,i}) + (\theta_1 + \eta_{1,i}) \cdot C_{i,j} + \theta_2 \cdot (QTcF_{bs,i} - QTcF_{bs,all})
L273: ## **Equation 1. Sponsor's final linear mixed effect model to describe C-QTc relationship for tarlatamab**
L274: Where \Delta QTcF_{i,j} represents the absolute change in QTcF from baseline for subject i at time j; \theta_0 is the population mean intercept and \eta_{0,i} is the random effect on intercept for subject i; \theta_1 is the population mean slope of the assumed linear association between concentration and \Delta QTcF_{i,j}, and \eta_{1,i} is the random effect on the slope for subject i; C_{i,j} is the concentration for subject i at time j; \theta_2 is the fixed effect associated with the individual baseline value, QTcF_{bs,i}, relative to overall mean baseline QTcF value, QTcF_{bs,all}. The model assumed that the random effects and residual error were normally distributed with mean of zero.
L275: Based on the sponsor's findings, a shallow but statistically significant slope was observed (Slope = 0.0000283, p-value = 0.0131). However, the model predicted mean \Delta QTcF at the model estimated steady state Cmax of 3580 ng/ml following the proposed clinical dosing regimen of 10 mg Q2W was 0.25 msec (90%CI: 0.085 – 0.42).
L276: The sponsor's proposed label does not include any QT related labelling.
L277: **Reviewer's Comment 1:** Although the sponsor's analysis determines a statistically significant positive C-QTc relationship, this relationship is unlikely to be due to interaction with hERG channel since tarlatamab is a large protein therapeutic molecule consisting of 982 amino acids and having a molecular weight of about 105 kilodaltons. Notably, the relationship is very shallow and not clinically significant. Nonclinical ECG data from toxicology studies in Cynomolgus Monkeys receiving up to 4.5 mg/Kg (Day 85 trough concentration = 315000 ng/mL) do not show ECG abnormalities after treatment with tarlatamab (See Section 3.4). According to the ICH E14 Q&amp;A 6.3 (R3), "Large targeted proteins and monoclonal antibodies have a low likelihood of direct ion channel interactions and a thorough QT/QTc study is not necessary, unless the potential for proarrhythmic risk is suggested by mechanistic considerations or data from clinical or nonclinical studies". The data presented by the sponsor do not suggest a mechanism for QT prolongation by tarlatamab.

L292: ## 3.4 Nonclinical Cardiac Safety
L293: Electrographic assessments were included in the 28 day and 3-month toxicological studies in cynomolgus monkeys.
L294: In the 28-day GLP study, monkeys received slow bolus infusion of either 0, 50, 500, or 4500 \mug/Kg QW for 28 days. No clinically significant ECG abnormalities were observed up mean trough concentration of 95100 ng/ml in 6 monkeys receiving 4500 \mug/Kg. However, on day 2 of
L295: this study, there were slight tarlatamab-related increases in heart rate at \ge 500 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg} that were associated with decreased PR, RR, and QT intervals. Throughout the 24-hour collection period, the mean change in heart rate was less than 10% higher than the control group (individual changes up to 23% were recorded). These changes were not evident in any animal regardless of anti-drug antibody (ADA) status on day 23, the next point at which they were measured. Given the low magnitude of the heart rate increase and the absence of an effect at the end of study despite continued dosing, this finding was not considered adverse. There were no tarlatamab-related neurological observations or changes in body temperature or respiratory rate in the 28-day toxicology study.
L296: In the 3-month toxicology study, monkeys received slow bolus injection of either 0, 50, or 4500 \mu\text{g}/\text{Kg}. Up to mean trough concentration of 315000 ng/ml of day 85 in monkeys receiving 4500 \mu\text{g}/\text{Kg} there were no tarlatamab-related changes in the following parameters: electrocardiograms, body temperature, respiration rates, and neurological examinations. Furthermore, no treatment-related macroscopic or microscopic changes were detected in the heart.
