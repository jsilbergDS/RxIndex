L70: ## 5 WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
L71: ## 5.1 Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis
L72: Gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCAs) increase the risk for nephrogenic systemic fibrosis (NSF) among patients with impaired elimination of the drugs. Avoid use of GBCAs among these patients unless the diagnostic information is essential and not available with non-contrast MRI or other modalities. The GBCA-associated NSF risk appears highest for patients with chronic, severe kidney disease (GFR &lt; 30 mL/min/1.73m^2) as well as patients with acute kidney injury. The risk appears lower for patients with chronic, moderate kidney disease (GFR 30 - 59 mL/min/1.73m^2) and little, if any, for patients with chronic, mild kidney disease (GFR 60 - 89 mL/min/1.73m^2). NSF may result in fatal or debilitating fibrosis affecting the skin, muscle and internal organs. Report any diagnosis of NSF following DOTAREM administration to Guerbet LLC (1-877-729-6679) or FDA (1-800-FDA-1088 or www.fda.gov/medwatch).
L73: Screen patients for acute kidney injury and other conditions that may reduce renal function. Features of acute kidney injury consist of rapid (over hours to days) and usually reversible decrease in kidney function, commonly in the setting of surgery, severe infection, injury or drug-induced kidney toxicity. Serum creatinine levels and estimated GFR may not reliably assess renal function in the setting of acute kidney injury. For patients at risk for chronically reduced renal function (e.g., age &gt; 60 years, diabetes mellitus or chronic hypertension), estimate the GFR through laboratory testing.
L74: Among the factors that may increase the risk for NSF are repeated or higher than recommended doses of a GBCA and the degree of renal impairment at the time of exposure. Record the specific GBCA and the dose administered to a patient. For patients at highest risk for NSF, do not exceed the recommended DOTAREM dose and allow a sufficient period of time for elimination of the drug prior to re-administration. For patients receiving hemodialysis, physicians may consider the prompt initiation of hemodialysis following the administration of a GBCA in order to enhance the contrast agent's elimination. The usefulness of hemodialysis in the prevention of NSF is unknown [*see Dosage and Administration (2) and Clinical Pharmacology (12)*].
L75: ## 5.2 Hypersensitivity Reactions
L76: Anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions have been reported with DOTAREM, involving cardiovascular, respiratory, and/or cutaneous manifestations. Some patients experienced circulatory collapse and died. In most
L77: cases, initial symptoms occurred within minutes of DOTAREM administration and resolved with prompt emergency treatment [see *Adverse Reactions* (6)].
L78: - Before DOTAREM administration, assess all patients for any history of a reaction to contrast media, bronchial asthma and/or allergic disorders. These patients may have an increased risk for a hypersensitivity reaction to DOTAREM.
- Administer DOTAREM only in situations where trained personnel and therapies are promptly available for the treatment of hypersensitivity reactions, including personnel trained in resuscitation.
- During and following DOTAREM administration, observe patients for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions.
L79: ## 5.3 Acute Kidney Injury
L80: In patients with chronically reduced renal function, acute kidney injury requiring dialysis has occurred with the use of GBCAs. The risk of acute kidney injury may increase with increasing dose of the contrast agent; administer the lowest dose necessary for adequate imaging. Screen all patients for renal impairment by obtaining a history and/or laboratory tests. Consider follow-up renal function assessments for patients with a history of renal dysfunction.
L81: ## 5.4 Extravasation and Injection Site Reactions
L82: Ensure catheter and venous patency before the injection of DOTAREM. Extravasation into tissues during DOTAREM administration may result in tissue irritation [see *Nonclinical Toxicology* (13.2)].
L83: ## 6 ADVERSE REACTIONS
L84: GBCAs have been associated with a risk for NSF [see *Warnings and Precautions* (5.1)]. NSF has not been reported in patients with a clear history of exposure to DOTAREM alone.
L85: Hypersensitivity reactions and acute kidney injury are described in other sections of the labeling [see *Warnings and Precautions* (5.2) and (5.3)].

L102: ## 8 USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS
L103: ## 8.1 Pregnancy
L104: *Pregnancy Category C*
L105: *Risk Summary*
L106: There are no adequate and well-controlled studies with DOTAREM conducted in pregnant women. Limited published human data on exposure to other GBCAs during pregnancy did not show adverse effects in exposed neonates. No effects on embryo/fetal development were observed in rats or rabbits at doses up to 10 mmol/kg/day in rats or 3 mmol/kg/day in rabbits. The doses in rats and rabbits were respectively 16 and 10 times the recommended human dose based on body surface area. DOTAREM should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
L107: *Human Data*
L108: While it is unknown if DOTAREM crosses the human placenta, other GBCAs do cross the placenta in humans and result in fetal exposure.
L109: *Animal Data*
L110: Reproductive and developmental toxicity studies were conducted with gadotere meglumine in rats and rabbits. Gadotere meglumine was administered intravenously in doses of 0, 2, 4 and 10 mmol/kg/day (or 3.2, 6.5 and 16.2 times the recommended human dose based on body surface area) to female rats for 14 days before mating throughout the mating period and until gestation day (GD) 17. Pregnant rabbits were intravenously administered gadotere meglumine at the dose levels of 0, 1, 3 and 7 mmol/kg/day (or 3.3, 10 and 23 times the human doses based on body surface area) from GD6 to GD19. No effects on embryo/fetal development were observed in rats or rabbits at doses up to 10 mmol/kg/day in rats or 3 mmol/kg/day in rabbits. Maternal toxicity was observed in rats at 10 mmol/kg/day (or 16 times the human dose based on body surface area) and in rabbits at 7 mmol/kg/day (23 times the human dose based on body surface area).
L111: ## 8.3 Nursing Mothers
L112: It is not known whether DOTAREM is excreted in human milk. Limited case reports on use of GBCAs in nursing mothers indicate that 0.01 to 0.04% of the maternal gadolinium dose is excreted in human breast milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, exercise caution when DOTAREM is administered to a nursing woman. Nonclinical data show that gadotere meglumine is excreted into breast milk in very small amounts (&lt; 0.1% of the dose intravenously administered) and absorption via the gastrointestinal tract is poor.
L113: ## 8.4 Pediatric Use
L114: The safety and efficacy of DOTAREM at a single dose of 0.1 mmol/kg have been established in pediatric patients from 2 to 17 years of age. No dosage adjustment according to age is necessary in this population [See *Dosage and Administration (2.1) and Clinical Studies (14)*]. The safety and efficacy of DOTAREM have not been established in pediatric patients below 2 years of age. GFR does not reach adult levels until 1 year of age [see *Warnings and Precautions (5.1)*].
L115: ## 8.5 Geriatric Use
L116: In clinical studies of DOTAREM, 900 patients were 65 years of age and over, and 312 patients were 75 years of age and over. No overall differences in safety or efficacy were observed between these subjects and younger subjects. In general, use of DOTAREM in elderly patients should be cautious, reflecting the greater frequency of impaired renal function and concomitant disease or other drug therapy. No age-related dosage adjustment is necessary.
L117: ## 8.6 Renal Impairment
L118: No DOTAREM dosage adjustment is recommended for patients with renal impairment. Gadoterate meglumine can be removed from the body by hemodialysis [see *Warnings and Precautions* (5.1) and *Clinical Pharmacology* (12.3)].
L119: ## 10 OVERDOSAGE
L120: DOTAREM administered to healthy volunteers and to patients at cumulative doses up to 0.3 mmol/kg was tolerated in a manner similar to lower doses. Adverse reactions to overdose with DOTAREM have not been reported. Gadoterate meglumine can be removed from the body by hemodialysis [See *Clinical Pharmacology* (12.3)].

L130: ## 12 CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY
L131: ## 12.1 Mechanism of Action
L132: Gadoterate is a paramagnetic molecule that develops a magnetic moment when placed in a magnetic field. The magnetic moment enhances the relaxation rates of water protons in its vicinity, leading to an increase in signal intensity (brightness) of tissues.
L133: In magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), visualization of normal and pathological tissue depends in part on variations in the radiofrequency signal intensity that occurs with:
L134: - differences in proton density
- differences of the spin-lattice or longitudinal relaxation times (T1)
- differences in the spin-spin or transverse relaxation time (T2)
L135: When placed in a magnetic field, gadoterate shortens the T1 and T2 relaxation times in target tissues. At recommended doses, the effect is observed with greatest sensitivity in the T1-weighted sequences.
L136: ## 12.2 Pharmacodynamics
L137: Gadoterate affects proton relaxation times and consequently the MR signal, and the contrast obtained is characterized by the relaxivity of the gadoterate molecule. The relaxivity values for gadoterate are similar across the spectrum of magnetic field strengths used in clinical MRI (0.2-1.5 T).
L138: Gadoterate does not cross the intact blood-brain barrier and, therefore, does not enhance normal brain or lesions that have a normal blood-brain barrier, e.g. cysts, mature post-operative scars. However, disruption of the blood-brain barrier or abnormal vascularity allows distribution of gadoterate in lesions such as neoplasms, abscesses, and infarcts.
L139: ## 12.3 Pharmacokinetics
L140: The pharmacokinetics of total gadolinium following an intravenously administered 0.1 mmol/kg dose of DOTAREM in normal subjects conform to a one-compartment open-model with a mean elimination half-life (reported as mean \pm SD) of about 1.4 \pm 0.2 hr and 2.0 \pm 0.7 hr in female and male subjects, respectively. Similar pharmacokinetic profile and elimination half-life values were observed after intravenous injection of 0.1 mmol/kg of DOTAREM followed 20 minutes later by a second injection of 0.2 mmol/kg (1.7 \pm 0.3 hr and 1.9 \pm 0.2 hr in female and male subjects, respectively).
L141: ## Distribution
L142: The volume of distribution at steady state of total gadolinium in normal subjects is 179 \pm 26 and 211 \pm 35 mL/kg in female and male subjects respectively, roughly equivalent to that of extracellular water.
L143: Gadoterate does not undergo protein binding in vitro. The extent of blood cell partitioning of gadoterate is not known.
L144: ## Metabolism
L145: Gadoterate is not known to be metabolized.
L146: ## Elimination
L147: Following a 0.1 mmol/kg dose of DOTAREM, total gadolinium is excreted primarily in the urine with 72.9 \pm 17.0\% and 85.4 \pm 9.7\% (mean \pm SD) eliminated within 48 hours, in female and male subjects,
L148: respectively. Similar values were achieved after a cumulative dose of 0.3 mmol/kg (0.1 + 0.2 mmol/kg, 20 minutes later), with 85.5 \pm 13.2\% and 92.0 \pm 12.0\% recovered in urine within 48 hrs in female and male subjects respectively.
L149: In healthy subjects, the renal and total clearance rates of total gadolinium are comparable (1.27 \pm 0.32 and 1.74 \pm 0.12 mL/min/kg in females; and 1.40 \pm 0.31 and 1.64 \pm 0.35 mL/min/kg in males, respectively) indicating that the drug is primarily cleared through the kidneys. Within the studied dose range (0.1 to 0.3 mmol/kg), the kinetics of total gadolinium appear to be linear.

L155: ## 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY
L156: ## 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility
L157: Long-term animal studies have not been performed to evaluate the carcinogenic potential of gadoterate meglumine.
L158: Gadoterate meglumine did not demonstrate mutagenic potential in *in vitro* bacterial reverse mutation assays (Ames test) using Salmonella typhimurium, in an *in vitro* chromosome aberration assay in Chinese hamster ovary cells, in an *in vitro* gene mutation assay in Chinese hamster lung cells, nor in an *in vivo* mouse micronucleus assay.
L159: No impairment of male or female fertility and reproductive performance was observed in rats after intravenous administration of gadoterate meglumine at the maximum tested dose of 10 mmol/kg/day (16 times the maximum human dose based on surface area), given during more than 9 weeks in males and more than 4 weeks in females. Sperm counts and sperm motility were not adversely affected by treatment with the drug.
L160: ## 13.2 Animal Toxicology and/or Pharmacology
L161: Local intolerance reactions, including moderate irritation associated with infiltration of inflammatory cells were observed after periveneous injection in rabbits suggesting the possibility of local irritation if the contrast medium leaks around the veins in a clinical setting (see *Warnings and Precautions* (5.4)).
L162: ## 14 CLINICAL STUDIES
L163: Efficacy and safety of DOTAREM were evaluated in a multi-center clinical trial (Study A) that enrolled 364 adult and 38 pediatric patients (aged \ge 2 years) with known or suspected CNS lesions. Adults were randomized 2 to 1 to receive either DOTAREM or gadopentetate dimeglumine, each administered at a dose of 0.1 mmol/kg. All pediatric patients received DOTAREM, also at a dose of 0.1 mmol/kg. In the trial, patients first underwent a baseline (pre-contrast) MRI examination followed by the assigned GBCA administration and a post-contrast MR examination. The images (pre-contrast, post-contrast and "paired pre- and post-contrast") were interpreted by three independent off-site readers blinded to clinical information. The primary efficacy analysis compared three patient-level visualization scores (paired images) to baseline MRI (pre-contrast images) for adults who received DOTAREM. The three primary visualization components were: contrast enhancement, border delineation and internal morphology. For each of these components there was a pre-defined scoring scale. Lesion counting (up to five per patient) was also reflected within each component's patient-level visualization score.
L164: Among the adult patients, 245 received DOTAREM and their data comprised the primary efficacy population. There were 114 (47%) men and 131 (53%) women with a mean age of 53 years (range 18 to 85 years), the racial and ethnic representations were 84% Caucasian, 11% Asian, 4% Black, and 1% other.

L266: ## 5 WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
L267: ## 5.1 Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis
L268: Gadolinium-based contrast agents (GBCAs) increase the risk for nephrogenic systemic fibrosis (NSF) among patients with impaired elimination of the drugs. Avoid use of GBCAs among these patients unless the diagnostic information is essential and not available with non-contrast MRI or other modalities. The GBCA-associated NSF risk appears highest for patients with chronic, severe kidney disease (GFR &lt; 30 mL/min/1.73m2) as well as patients with acute kidney injury. The risk appears lower for patients with chronic, moderate kidney disease (GFR 30 - 59 mL/min/1.73m2) and little, if any, for patients with chronic, mild kidney disease (GFR 60 - 89 mL/min/1.73m2). NSF may result in fatal or debilitating fibrosis affecting the skin, muscle and internal organs. Report any diagnosis of NSF following DOTAREM administration to Guerbet LLC (1-877-729-6679) or FDA (1-800-FDA-1088 or www.fda.gov/medwatch).
L269: Screen patients for acute kidney injury and other conditions that may reduce renal function. Features of acute kidney injury consist of rapid (over hours to days) and usually reversible decrease in kidney function, commonly in the setting of surgery, severe infection, injury or drug-induced kidney toxicity. Serum creatinine levels and
L270: estimated GFR may not reliably assess renal function in the setting of acute kidney injury. For patients at risk for chronically reduced renal function (e.g., age &gt; 60 years, diabetes mellitus or chronic hypertension), estimate the GFR through laboratory testing.
L271: Among the factors that may increase the risk for NSF are repeated or higher than recommended doses of a GBCA and the degree of renal impairment at the time of exposure. Record the specific GBCA and the dose administered to a patient. For patients at highest risk for NSF, do not exceed the recommended DOTAREM dose and allow a sufficient period of time for elimination of the drug prior to re-administration. For patients receiving hemodialysis, physicians may consider the prompt initiation of hemodialysis following the administration of a GBCA in order to enhance the contrast agent's elimination. The usefulness of hemodialysis in the prevention of NSF is unknown [*see Dosage and Administration (2) and Clinical Pharmacology (12)*].
L272: ## 5.2 Hypersensitivity Reactions
L273: Anaphylactic and anaphylactoid reactions have been reported with DOTAREM, involving cardiovascular, respiratory, and/or cutaneous manifestations. Some patients experienced circulatory collapse and died. In most cases, initial symptoms occurred within minutes of DOTAREM administration and resolved with prompt emergency treatment [*see Adverse Reactions (6)*].
L274: - Before DOTAREM administration, assess all patients for any history of a reaction to contrast media, bronchial asthma and/or allergic disorders. These patients may have an increased risk for a hypersensitivity reaction to DOTAREM.
- Administer DOTAREM only in situations where trained personnel and therapies are promptly available for the treatment of hypersensitivity reactions, including personnel trained in resuscitation.
- During and following DOTAREM administration, observe patients for signs and symptoms of hypersensitivity reactions.
L275: ## 5.3 Acute Kidney Injury
L276: In patients with chronically reduced renal function, acute kidney injury requiring dialysis has occurred with the use of GBCAs. The risk of acute kidney injury may increase with increasing dose of the contrast agent; administer the lowest dose necessary for adequate imaging. Screen all patients for renal impairment by obtaining a history and/or laboratory tests. Consider follow-up renal function assessments for patients with a history of renal dysfunction.

L329: ## 12 CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY
L330: ## 12.1 Mechanism of Action
L331: Gadoterate is a paramagnetic molecule that develops a magnetic moment when placed in a magnetic field. The magnetic moment enhances the relaxation rates of water protons in its vicinity, leading to an increase in signal intensity (brightness) of tissues.
L332: In magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), visualization of normal and pathological tissue depends in part on variations in the radiofrequency signal intensity that occurs with:
L333: - differences in proton density
- differences of the spin-lattice or longitudinal relaxation times (T1)
- differences in the spin-spin or transverse relaxation time (T2)
L334: When placed in a magnetic field, gadoterate shortens the T1 and T2 relaxation times in target tissues. At recommended doses, the effect is observed with greatest sensitivity in the T1-weighted sequences.
L335: ## 12.2 Pharmacodynamics
L336: Gadoterate affects proton relaxation times and consequently the MR signal, and the contrast obtained is characterized by the relaxivity of the gadoterate molecule. The relaxivity values for gadoterate are similar across the spectrum of magnetic field strengths used in clinical MRI (0.2-1.5 T).
L337: Gadoterate does not cross the intact blood-brain barrier and, therefore, does not enhance normal brain or lesions that have a normal blood-brain barrier, e.g. cysts, mature post-operative scars. However, disruption of the blood-brain barrier or abnormal vascularity allows distribution of gadoterate in lesions such as neoplasms, abscesses, and infarcts.
L338: ## 12.3 Pharmacokinetics
L339: The pharmacokinetics of total gadolinium following an intravenously administered 0.1 mmol/kg dose of DOTAREM in normal subjects conform to a one-compartment open-model with a mean elimination half-life (reported as mean \pm SD) of about 1.4 \pm 0.2 hr and 2.0 \pm 0.7 hr in female and male subjects, respectively. Similar pharmacokinetic profile and elimination half-life values were observed after intravenous injection of 0.1 mmol/kg of DOTAREM followed 20 minutes later by a second injection of 0.2 mmol/kg (1.7 \pm 0.3 hr and 1.9 \pm 0.2 hr in female and male subjects, respectively).
L340: ## Distribution
L341: The volume of distribution at steady state of total gadolinium in normal subjects is 179 \pm 26 and 211 \pm 35 mL/kg in female and male subjects respectively, roughly equivalent to that of extracellular water.
L342: Gadotere does not undergo protein binding in vitro. The extent of blood cell partitioning of gadotere is not known.
L343: ## Metabolism
L344: Gadotere is not known to be metabolized.
L345: ## Elimination
L346: Following a 0.1 mmol/kg dose of DOTAREM, total gadolinium is excreted primarily in the urine with 72.9 \pm 17.0\% and 85.4 \pm 9.7\% (mean \pm SD) eliminated within 48 hours, in female and male subjects, respectively. Similar values were achieved after a cumulative dose of 0.3 \text{ mmol/kg} (0.1 + 0.2 \text{ mmol/kg}, 20 minutes later), with 85.5 \pm 13.2\% and 92.0 \pm 12.0\% recovered in urine within 48 hrs in female and male subjects respectively.
L347: In healthy subjects, the renal and total clearance rates of total gadolinium are comparable (1.27 \pm 0.32 and 1.74 \pm 0.12 \text{ mL/min/kg} in females; and 1.40 \pm 0.31 and 1.64 \pm 0.35 \text{ mL/min/kg} in males, respectively) indicating that the drug is primarily cleared through the kidneys. Within the studied dose range (0.1 to 0.3 \text{ mmol/kg}), the kinetics of total gadolinium appear to be linear.
L348: ## Special Populations
L349: ## Renal Impairment
L350: A single intravenous dose of 0.1 \text{ mmol/kg} of DOTAREM was administered to 8 patients (5 men and 3 women) with impaired renal function (mean serum creatinine of 498 \pm 98 \mu\text{mol/L} in the 10-30 \text{ mL/min} creatinine clearance group and 192 \pm 62 \mu\text{mol/L} in the 30-60 \text{ mL/min} creatinine clearance group). Renal impairment delayed the elimination of total gadolinium. Total clearance decreased as a function of the degree of renal impairment. The distribution volume was unaffected by the severity of renal impairment (Table 5). No changes in renal function test parameters were observed after DOTAREM injection. The mean cumulative urinary excretion of total gadolinium was approximately 76.9 \pm 4.5\% in 48 hrs in patients with moderate renal impairment, 68.4 \pm 3.5\% in 72 hrs in patients with severe renal impairment and 93.3 \pm 4.7\% in 24 hrs for subjects with normal renal function.

L353: ## 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY
L354: ## 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility
L355: Long-term animal studies have not been performed to evaluate the carcinogenic potential of gadotere meglumine.
L356: Gadotere meglumine did not demonstrate mutagenic potential in *in vitro* bacterial reverse mutation assays (Ames test) using Salmonella typhimurium, in an *in vitro* chromosome aberration assay in Chinese hamster ovary cells, in an *in vitro* gene mutation assay in Chinese hamster lung cells, nor in an *in vivo* mouse micronucleus assay.
L357: No impairment of male or female fertility and reproductive performance was observed in rats after intravenous administration of gadotere meglumine at the maximum tested dose of 10 mmol/kg/day (16 times the maximum human dose based on surface area), given during more than 9 weeks in males and more than 4 weeks in females. Sperm counts and sperm motility were not adversely affected by treatment with the drug.
L358: ## 13.2 Animal Toxicology and/or Pharmacology
L359: Local intolerance reactions, including moderate irritation associated with infiltration of inflammatory cells were observed after perivenous injection in rabbits suggesting the possibility of local irritation if the contrast medium leaks around the veins in a clinical setting (see *Warnings and Precautions* (5.4)).
L360: ## 14 CLINICAL STUDIES
L361: Efficacy and safety of DOTAREM were evaluated in a multi-center clinical trial (Study A) that enrolled 364 adult and 38 pediatric patients (aged \ge 2 years) with known or suspected CNS lesions. Adults were randomized 2 to 1 to receive either DOTAREM or gadopentetate dimeglumine, each administered at a dose of 0.1 mmol/kg. All pediatric patients received DOTAREM, also at a dose of 0.1 mmol/kg. In the trial, patients first underwent a baseline (pre-contrast) MRI examination followed by the assigned GBCA administration and a post-contrast MR examination. The images (pre-contrast, post-contrast and "paired pre- and post-contrast") were interpreted by three independent off-site readers blinded to clinical information. The primary efficacy analysis compared three patient-level visualization scores (paired images) to baseline MRI (pre-contrast images) for adults who received DOTAREM. The three primary visualization components were: contrast enhancement, border delineation and internal morphology. For each of these components there was a pre-defined scoring scale. Lesion counting (up to five per patient) was also reflected within each component's patient-level visualization score.
L362: Among the adult patients, 245 received DOTAREM and their data comprised the primary efficacy population. There were 114 (47%) men and 131 (53%) women with a mean age of 53 years (range 18 to 85 years), the racial and ethnic representations were 84% Caucasian, 11% Asian, 4% Black, and 1% other.

Across the three readers 56% to 94% of patients had improved lesion visualization for paired images compared to pre-contrast images. DOTAREM provided a statistically significant improvement for all three primary visualization components. More lesions were seen on the paired images than the pre-contrast images.

L382: ## 17 PATIENT COUNSELING INFORMATION
L383: ## 17.1 Nephrogenic Systemic Fibrosis
L384: Instruct patients to inform their healthcare provider if they:
L385: - have a history of kidney disease, or
- have recently received a GBCA.
L386: GBCAs increase the risk for NSF among patients with impaired elimination of the drugs. To counsel patients at risk for NSF:
L387: - Describe the clinical manifestations of NSF.
- Describe procedures to screen for the detection of renal impairment.
L388: Instruct the patients to contact their physician if they develop signs or symptoms of NSF following DOTAREM administration, such as burning, itching, swelling, scaling, hardening and tightening of the skin; red or dark patches on the skin; stiffness in joints with trouble moving, bending or straightening the arms, hands, legs or feet; pain in the hip bones or ribs; or muscle weakness.
L389: ## 17.2 Common Adverse Reactions
L390: Inform patients that they may experience:
L391: - Reactions along the venous injection site, such as mild and transient burning or pain or feeling of warmth or coldness at the injection site.
- Side effects of headache, nausea, abnormal taste and feeling hot.
L392: ## 17.3 General Precautions
L393: Instruct patients receiving DOTAREM to inform their physician if they:
L394: - Are pregnant or breastfeeding.
- Have a history of allergic reaction to contrast media, bronchial asthma or allergy.
- Are taking any medications.
