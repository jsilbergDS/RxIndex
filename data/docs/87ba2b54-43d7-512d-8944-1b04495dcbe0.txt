L51: ## 3 OVERALL ASSESSMENT OF THE MATERIALS REVIEWED
L52: Bracco Diagnostics Inc. (Bracco) submitted a 505(b)(2) application to obtain marketing approval of Iomervu (iomeprol injection) under NDA 216016 and NDA 216017 relying on medical literature. We performed a risk assessment of the proposed PI, container labels and carton labeling for Iomervu to determine whether there are deficiencies that may lead to medication errors and other areas of improvement.
L53: Our evaluation of the proposed PI, container label, and carton labeling for Iomervu identified areas of vulnerability that may lead to medication errors. We note in the request for proprietary name review (PNR) for Iomervu Bracco states
L54: The request for PNR was for "Iomervu" (b) (4), as such, we did not consider the (b) (4) as part of the PNR or this review. We consulted the Division of Imaging and Radiation Medicine (DIRM) regarding the name presentation with the
L55: use of the [REDACTED](b)(4). Ultimately, it was determined to present the proprietary name as "Iomervu" throughout the labels and labeling without the [REDACTED](b)(4) and presenting the individual strengths as "250 mg Iodine/mL", "300 mg Iodine/mL", "350 mg Iodine/mL" and "400 mg Iodine/mL". We include our recommendations below for the presentation of the proprietary name and individual product strengths. In addition, for the PI we recommend including the appropriate units after all numeric values, removing the strength presentations for Iomervu in terms of [REDACTED](b)(4) from Sections 3 and 16, removing trailing zeros, and replacing the term "saline" with the official USP name "0.9% Sodium Chloride Injection". For the container labels and carton labeling, we recommend removing the strengths in terms of [REDACTED](b)(4), including the appropriate units after all numeric values, presenting the established name in parentheses, and revising the storage information statement to align with the storage information in the PI. We provide our recommendations below.

L77: ## A. General Comments (Container Labels and Carton Labeling)
L78: - The proposed name, lomervu, was found conditionally acceptable on August 7, 2024. Please revise all labels and labeling to replace [REDACTED] (b) (4) with the proprietary name "lomervu".
- As currently presented, the proposed container labels and carton labeling include the following strength statements: "XX% organically bound iodine", "lomeprol Injection XX%", and the milligrams of iodine per mL strength prominently displayed in a colored box. We recommend removing the strengths in terms of percentages to avoid confusion, and only presenting the strength in terms of milligrams of iodine per mL on the container labels which aligns with the dosing in the prescribing information (PI). Additionally, we recommend including the units "mg iodine/mL" next to the strength. For example, revise "250" to read "250 mg iodine/mL".
- We note the established name and dosage form are presented below the proposed proprietary name, but are not presented inside parentheses. This is inconsistent with the PI which presents this information inside parentheses. Revise the established name and dosage form on the proposed container labels and carton labeling to be presented within parentheses as "(lomeprol injection)".
- We note the statement "For intravascular use only" is presented in a red box at the bottom of the proposed container labels and carton labeling. We recommend revising this statement to read "For intravenous or intraarterial use only" to align with the more specific routes of administration described in the PI.
- We note use of the hyphen symbol "-" to represent the word "to" in numeric value ranges. We recommend replacing the hyphen symbol with its intended meaning of "to" to prevent misinterpretation. Additionally, we note there are numeric values presented on the proposed container label that are not immediately followed by the appropriate units. We recommend including the appropriate units after all numeric values presented on the proposed container label. For example, revise the storage information statement to read "Store at room temperature between 20° to 25°C (68° to 77°F) [see USP Controlled Room Temperature]". If space is limited, "[see USP Controlled Room Temperature]" can be omitted.
- We note the storage information presented on the container labels and carton labeling lacks the statement "do not freeze". We recommend including the statement "Do not freeze" to align with the full storage information presented in the PI.
- We note the proposed container labels and carton labeling include the statement [REDACTED] (b) (4) This terminology
L79: is inconsistent with the terminology in the PI. Therefore, we recommend revising this statement to read "Recommended Dosage: See Prescribing Information".
L80: 8. As currently presented, the proposed container labels and carton labeling include a placeholder for the expiration information but the proposed format for the expiration is not provided. We are unable to assess the proposed expiration date format from a medication safety perspective. FDA recommends that the human-readable expiration date on the drug package label include a year, month, and non-zero day. FDA recommends that the expiration date appear in YYYY-MM-DD format if only numerical characters are used or in YYYY-MMM-DD if alphabetical characters are used to represent the month. If there are space limitations on the drug package, the human-readable text may include only a year and month, to be expressed as: YYYY-MM if only numerical characters are used or YYYY-MMM if alphabetical characters are used to represent the month. FDA recommends that a hyphen or forward slash be used to separate the portions of the expiration date.

L130: This review evaluates the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database, published medical literature, VigiBase, and Applicant submissions for adverse events (AEs) reported with iomeprol. The Division of Imaging and Radiation Medicine (DIRM) requested this review on January 26, 2024, to help inform the evaluation of New Drug Applications (NDAs) for iomeprol injection (NDA 216016 for intraarterial administration; NDA 216017 for intravenous administration) and potential eventual product labeling.

L132: - Gathered event-level safety data from postmarketing sources.
- Screened reported events to identify those not represented in applicant-proposed iomeprol labeling.

L133: ## 1.1 BACKGROUND
L134: Iomeprol is a drug in the class of iodinated contrast media (ICM) which are widely used in conjunction with X-ray and computed tomography for imaging modalities including angiography, arteriography, venography, and urography. ICM may be subcategorized based on certain properties, such as osmolarity, ionicity, and the number of tri-iodinated rings in the compound (monomeric versus dimeric) (Spampinato et al. 2017). Iomeprol is a low-osmolar nonionic monomer which are characteristics also shared by several approved ICM: iohexol, iopamidol, iopromide, and ioversol (Bracco 2023a; Spampinato et al. 2017).
L135: Iomeprol has never been approved for marketing in the United States, although FDA granted temporary importation of iomeprol in 2022 to address ICM shortages (ACR 2022). In contrast, iomeprol's presence in the foreign marketplace spans more than three decades. Iomeprol was first approved by the United Kingdom in 1992 and is currently approved for marketing (under the tradename Iomeron) in 50 countries (Bracco 2023a). The Applicant estimates that more than 160 million patients have been exposed to iomeprol (Bracco 2023a), of which an estimated 0.8% was pediatric patient exposure (&lt; 18 years of age) (Bracco 2024a).

L248: ## 7.2 APPENDIX B. FDA ADVERSE EVENT REPORTING SYSTEM
L249: ## FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS)
L250: FAERS is a database that contains information on adverse event and medication error reports submitted to FDA. The database is designed to support FDA's postmarketing safety surveillance program for drug and therapeutic biological products. The informatic structure of the database adheres to the international safety reporting guidance issued by the International Council on Harmonisation. Adverse events and medication errors are coded to terms in the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities terminology. The suspect products are coded to valid tradenames or active ingredients in the FAERS Product Dictionary.
L251: FAERS data have limitations. First, there is no certainty that the reported event was actually due to the product. FDA does not require that a causal relationship between a product and event be proven, and reports do not always contain enough detail to properly evaluate an event. Further, FDA does not receive reports for every adverse event or medication error that occurs with a product. Many factors can influence whether or not an event will be reported, such as the time a product has been marketed and publicity about an event. Therefore, FAERS data cannot be used to calculate the incidence of an adverse event or medication error in the U.S. population.
L252: ## Data Mining of FAERS Using Empirica Signal
L253: Empirica Signal refers to the software that the Office of Surveillance and Epidemiology (OSE) uses to perform data mining analyses while using the Multi-item Gamma Poisson Shrinker (MGPS) data mining algorithm. "Data mining" refers to the use of computer algorithms to identify patterns of associations or unexpected occurrences (i.e., "potential signals") in large databases. These potential signals can then be evaluated for intervention as appropriate. In OSE, the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS) database is utilized for data mining. MGPS analyzes the records in FAERS and then quantifies reported drug-event associations by producing a set of values or scores that indicate varying strengths of reporting relationships between drugs and events. These scores, denoted as Empirical Bayes Geometric Mean (EBGM) values, provide a stable estimate of the relative reporting of an event for a particular drug relative to all other drugs and events in FAERS. MGPS also calculates lower and upper 90% confidence limits for EBGM values, denoted EB05 and EB95, respectively. Because EBGM scores are based on FAERS data, limitations relating to FAERS data also apply to data mining-derived data. Further, drug and event causality cannot be inferred from EBGM scores.
L254: ## 7.3 APPENDIX C. VigiBase DATABASE DESCRIPTION
L255: VigiBase is a global database of individual case safety reports (ICSRs) received by the Uppsala Monitoring Centre (UMC) in its role as the World Health Organization (WHO) Collaborating Centre for International Drug Monitoring. VigiLyze is a tool used to search and analyze VigiBase. VigiBase includes ICSRs submitted by approximately 150 countries, including the U.S., for allopathic medicines, traditional medicines (herbals), and biological medicines, including vaccines. The FDA does not have access to case narratives in VigiBase but may request them from the regulatory authorities that submitted the ICSRs. Some cases in VigiBase may also be in the FDA Adverse Event Reporting System (FAERS). The limitations and qualifications that apply to VigiBase information and its use include:
L256: ## **Tentative and variable nature of the data**
L257: *Uncertainty:* The reports submitted to UMC generally describe no more than suspicions which have arisen from observation of an unexpected or unwanted event. In most instances it cannot be proven that a specific medicinal product is the cause of an event, rather than, for example, underlying illness or other concomitant medication.
L258: *Variability of source:* Reports submitted to national centers come from both regulated and voluntary sources. Practice varies: some national centers accept reports only from medical practitioners; others from a broader range of reporters, including patients, some include reports from pharmaceutical companies.
L259: *Contingent influences:* The volume of reports for a particular medicinal product may be influenced by the extent of use of the product, publicity, the nature of the adverse effects and other factors.
L260: *No prevalence data:* No information is provided on the number of patients exposed to the product, and only a small part of the reactions occurring are reported.
L261: *Time to VigiBase:* Some national centers make an assessment of the likelihood that a medicinal product caused the suspected reaction, while others do not. Time from receipt of an ICSR by a national center until submission to UMC varies from country to country. Information obtained from UMC may therefore differ from that obtained directly from national centers.
L262: **For these reasons, interpretations of adverse effect data, and particularly those based on comparisons between medicinal products, may be misleading. The data comes from a variety of sources and the likelihood of a causal relationship varies across reports. Any use of VigiBase data must take these significant variables into account.**

L364: *In the case report described above, the timing of the transient neonatal hypothyroidism development and administration of iodinated contrast to the lactating mother raise concern about the effects of iodine in the preterm neonate. L396: ## ***DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSIONS***
L397: ## Pregnancy
L398: DPMH reviewed published literature and applicant's pharmacovigilance case reports regarding iomeprol use during pregnancy. There is over 30 years of post-marketing experience outside of the US and nine pregnancy cases with known outcomes reported in pharmacovigilance, which are insufficient to evaluate the potential for congenital malformation or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes. Post-marketing data provided by the applicant are limited but the available published information does not identify adverse effects in the fetus following maternal exposure to iomeprol during pregnancy. In addition to the limited quantity of the data, a majority of postmarketing case reports by the applicant included unknown pregnancy outcomes.
L399: Both ESUR and ACR do not recommend withholding use of iodinated contrast agents for diagnostic purposes in pregnancy. ACOG acknowledges the theoretical fetal risk of neonatal
L400: 22 TERIS database, Truven Health Analytics, Micromedex Solutions. Accessed 4/23/24.
L401: hypothyroidism but, due to the lack of known harm, advises iodinated contrast agents should be used only if necessary for diagnostic purposes during pregnancy. Therefore, DPMH recommends including a statement in subsection 8.1 Pregnancy labeling based on the available ex-US data from postmarketing reports that iomeprol use in pregnant females from decades of use have not identified a drug associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or other adverse maternal or fetal outcomes.
L402: Regarding postmarketing requirements (PMRs), iomeprol is anticipated to be widely used in females of reproductive potential, including pregnant women. Given the available human pregnancy data from outside the US over several decades of use of iomeprol, DPMH does not recommend issuing a PMR for a pregnancy safety study but would recommend routine pharmacovigilance to report on any pregnancy exposures.
L403: ## Lactation
L404: There are no data on the presence of iomeprol in human milk following maternal IV or IA administration. Iomeprol is present in animal milk. There are no data on the effects of iomeprol on the breastfed infant or milk production. Although iomeprol is not currently available in the US, ACOG notes that other iodinated contrast agents are excreted unchanged in human milk in low amounts and are poorly absorbed from breast milk by the gastrointestinal tract of the nursing infant.23 Clinical guidelines and position papers do not recommend withholding breastfeeding after use of other iodinated contrast agents.
L405: DPMH recommends including the standard benefit/risk statement in Subsection 8.2 labeling. In addition, for consistency with PLLR labeling for other iodinated contrast media, DPMH recommends adding the following under Clinical Consideration specific to breastfeeding recommendations:
L406: “Interruption of breastfeeding after exposure to iodinated contrast agents is not necessary because the potential exposure of the breastfed infant to iodine is small. However, a lactating woman may consider interrupting breastfeeding and pumping and discarding breast milk for 10 hours (approximately 5 elimination half-lives) after (b) (4) administration in order to minimize drug exposure to a breast fed infant.”
L407: DPMH does not recommend issuing a PMR for a clinical lactation study but would recommend routine pharmacovigilance to report on any lactational exposures.
L408: ## Females and Males of Reproductive Potential
L409: There were no adequately designed and conducted reproductive toxicity studies in animals to evaluate the effects of iomeprol on fertility. There are no available human data to suggest iomeprol adversely effects male or female fertility. Therefore, DPMH recommends omitting labeling subsection 8.3 Females and Males of Reproduction Potential.

L460: ## 3.4 Nonclinical Cardiac Safety
L461: An in vitro study showed that the inhibitory effect of Iomeprol 400 mg/mL on HERG tail current was equivalent to that of OMNIPAQUE 350, with changes attributed to increased osmolarity (Study No. DGMH1007). In a separate study, no significant effects were detected on dog Isolated Purkinje fibers in the range of Iomeprol concentrations from 5 to 40 mg/mL. The changes seen were almost superimposable to those caused by the addition of equiosmolar mannitol (Study No. DGMH1008).
L462: **Reviewer's Comment:** The GLP hERG study report (DGMH1007; link) describes the effects of Iomeprol on the hERG current in HEK293 cells, in comparison with Omnipaque. The hERG current was assessed at room temperature, using a step-step voltage protocol (from a holding potential of -80 mV to a depolarizing pulse of +20 mV for 4.8 second, followed by a repolarizing pulse to -50 mV for 5 seconds) that is different from the recommended hERG current protocol by the FDA (link). The full hERG blocker (E-4031 at 0.1 µM) inhibited hERG potassium currents by 95.7%. No drug concentration verifications were performed in the assay.
L463: Iomeprol inhibited (corrected with control) the hERG currents by -2.2%, 15.2%, 16.4%, and 37.1% at 1, 4, 10, 40 mg/mL, respectively. The Applicant also observed that Iomeprol caused concentration-dependent increase in the osmolarity of the external solution. Further study showed that mannitol (to increase the osmolarity of the external solution) decreased the hERG current by -3.9%, 6.5%, 15.5% and 26.4%, at 1.7, 6.8, 17, and 68 mM, respectively. The osmolarities of the external solution at 1.7, 6.8, 17, and 68 mM mannitol approximately matched the osmolarities of the external solution of Iomeprol at 1, 4, 10 and 40 mg/mL, respectively. Therefore, the osmolarity-corrected inhibition of hERG current by Iomeprol are 1.7%, 8.7%, 0.9%, and 10.7%, at 1, 4, 10 and 40 mg/mL, respectively. The estimated IC50 of Iomeprol inhibiting the hERG current is expected to be &gt; 40 mg/mL (11% inhibition). The estimated hERG safety margin over free (protein binding 0%) clinical exposure is &gt; 6-fold (11% inhibition). Similar to Iomeprol 400, Omnipaque 350 inhibited (corrected with osmolarity) the hERG currents by 0.1%, 4.8%, 0.5%, and 12.7% at 1, 4, 10, 40 mg/mL, respectively.
L464: In summary, the hERG assay showed deviations (e.g. room temperature; no drug concentration verification; no positive control drug) from the best practice recommendation from the S7B Q&amp;As 2.1. The results showed that Iomeprol inhibited the hERG current by 11% at 40 mg/mL (safety margin &gt; 6), suggesting the direct interaction of Iomeprol on hERG channel cannot be ruled out.
L465: In an in vivo study, intravenous administration of 4, 6 and 8 gI/kg of Iomeprol did not show evident effects on pressure parameters, heart rate and ECG intervals in conscious dogs (Study No. 6030).
L466: **Reviewer's comments:** In the in vivo study 6030 (link), Iomeprol given at 4, 6, 8 gI/kg in conscious instrumented dogs induces minor changes in systolic and diastolic blood pressures. Iomeprol as well as mannitol and OMNIPAQUE induces a transient increase in heart rate during the IV infusion period up to 15 min after infusion. Heart rates were increased by 23 bpm,
L467: 29 bpm and 36 bpm at 5 min post-IV at dose levels of 4, 6 and 8 gI/kg, respectively. QTcF were increased by 19 ms, 25 ms and 24 ms at dose levels of 4, 6, and 8 gI/kg, at 5 min post-IV. Similar to Iomeprol, Omnipaqué at 8 gI/kg also increased the heart rate (+22 bpm) and prolonged the QTcF (+20 ms) at 5 min post-IV. PK data was not collected in this study. No positive control was included in the study.
L468: QTcF prolongations were observed in the in vivo dog study at all dose levels. However, significant increases in heart rate were also observed in all dose levels.
