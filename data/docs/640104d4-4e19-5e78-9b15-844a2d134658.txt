L9: 1 INTRODUCTION
L10: The proposed proprietary name, Exondys 51, was reviewed by DMEPA on October 29, 2014 under IND 077429 and was found conditionally acceptable.1 We note that the product characteristics are the same. This memorandum is to communicate that DMEPA maintains the proposed proprietary name, Exondys 51, is acceptable from both a misbranding and safety perspective under the NDA 206488.
L11: 2 METHODS AND DISCUSSION
L12: For re-assessment of the proposed proprietary name, DMEPA searched the POCA databases to identify new names with orthographic and phonetic similarity not identified in the previous OSE proprietary name review. Additionally, we searched the USAN stem list to determine if the name contains any USAN stems as of the last USAN update.2
L13: Our re-assessment identified one new name that represents a potential source of drug name confusion. However, this name does not pose a risk for confusion as described in Appendices A. Finally, the September 1, 2015, search of the USAN stems did not find any USAN stems in the proposed proprietary name.
L14: 2.1 MISBRANDING ASSESSMENT
L15: The Office of Prescription Drug Promotion (OPDP) determined that the proposed name would not misbrand the proposed product. DMEPA and the Division of Neurology Products (DNP) concurred with the findings of OPDP's assessment of the proposed name.
L16: 3 CONCLUSIONS
L17: The proposed proprietary name is acceptable.

L27: 2. Phonetic and Orthographic Computer Analysis (POCA)
L28: POCA is a system that FDA designed. As part of the name similarity assessment, POCA is used to evaluate proposed names via a phonetic and orthographic algorithm. The proposed proprietary name is converted into its phonemic representation before it runs through the phonetic algorithm. Likewise, an orthographic algorithm exists that operates in a similar fashion. POCA is publicly accessible.
