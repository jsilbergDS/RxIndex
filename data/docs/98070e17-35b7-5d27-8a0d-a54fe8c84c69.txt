L28: 1 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L29: The applicant (AbbVie) submitted a New Drug Application (NDA) 209394 for a New Molecular Entity Mayvret™ (GLE/PIB), which is a fixed two-drug combination tablet comprising 100 mg of glecaprevir (GLE) and 40 mg of pibrentasvir (PIB). Both active products are direct-acting antiviral (DAA) agents and specifically, GLE is a NS3/4A protease inhibitor and PIB is a NS5A inhibitor, each targeting multiple steps involved in the hepatitis C viral replication cycle. The applicant is seeking approval for treatment of chronic hepatitis C genotypes (GT) 1-6 in adults, including patients with compensated liver disease (with or without cirrhosis) and with or without renal impairment to include patients receiving dialysis. Further, the proposed dosing and administration includes an 8- and 12-week GLE/PIB regimen for which duration is driven by HCV GT, cirrhotic status and prior treatment experience. The proposed daily dose is three tablets of GLE/PIB to deliver 300 mg GLE and 120 mg PIB administered once-daily.
L30: Evidence supporting this NDA comes from 4 Phase 3 trials (ENDURANCE-1, ENDURANCE-2, ENDURANCE-3 and EXPEDITION-4) that enrolled 974 HCV-infected adult subjects, of which 759 received GLE/PIB 300 mg/120 mg. ENDURANCE-1 evaluated both 8- and 12-week GLE/PIB regimens in a 1:1 randomized design in GT1, non-cirrhotic (NC) subjects with or without HIV co-infection and either treatment-naïve (TN) or treatment-experienced with PegIFN/ribavirin/sofosbuvir (TE-PRS). ENDURANCE-2 evaluated a 12-week GLE/PIB regimen in GT2, NC, TN or TE-PRS HCV-infected subjects in a double-blind, placebo-controlled design. ENDURANCE-3 evaluated 8- and 12-week GLE/PIB regimens in GT3, NC, TN HCV-infected subjects in an open-label design against a sofosbuvir/daclatasvir active-control. EXPEDITION-4 evaluated a 12-week GLE/PIB regimen in 104 GTs 1-6 infected subjects who were TN or TE with or without cirrhosis with severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease, among which 85 were on dialysis at time of enrollment. ENDURANCE-1 and ENDURANCE-2 were designed to demonstrate superiority against a pre-specified SVR12 clinical threshold. ENDURANCE-3 was designed to demonstrate non-inferiority of the 12-week GLE/PIB regimen to the active control SOF/DCV. EXPEDITION-4 was a single-arm trial with no testable hypothesis.
L31: In addition, supportive data were reviewed from 5 Phase 2 trials (SURVEYOR-1, SURVEYOR-2, EXPEDITION-1, MAGELLAN-1, and ENDURANCE-4) enrolling a total of 1273 HCV-infected adult subjects, of which, 970 received the proposed GLE/PIB 300 mg/120 mg dosage. SURVEYOR-1 evaluated 8- and 12-week GLE/PIB regimens in GT1, 4-6 infected subjects, TN or TE-PRS with or without cirrhosis. SURVEYOR-2 was a multi-arm, dose and duration finding trial evaluating 8- 12- and 16-week regimens of GLE/PIB in GT2-6 infected, NC or cirrhotic (C), TN or TE-PRS adult subjects. EXPEDITION-1 was a single-arm trial evaluating a 12-week GLE/PIB regimen in GT1, 2, 4-6 infected adults who were cirrhotic and either TN or TE-PRS. MAGELLAN-1 was a multi-part trial of 12- and 16-week GLE/PIB regimens in GT1, GT4-6, NS5A and/or NS3/4A PI-experienced, NC or C subjects. Finally, ENDURANCE-4 was a single arm trial evaluating GLE/PIB 12 weeks in less-prevalent GT4-6, NC subjects. All nine trials were designed around the same primary efficacy endpoint of sustained virologic response, defined as proportion of subjects achieving a HCV RNA value below the lower limit of quantification, at 12 weeks following the end-of-treatment (SVR12).

L34: Evidence supporting the 8-week regimen in GT1, NC subjects largely comes from ENDURANCE-1 achieving a SVR12 (95% CI) of 99% (97, 100) demonstrating superiority against a 91% pre-specified clinical threshold. Supportive data from an additional 29 GT1, NC subjects comes from SURVEYOR-1 in which all but one subject achieved SVR12. Evidence for the 8-week regimen in GT2 and GT4, NC subjects comes from SURVEYOR-2 for which the SVR12 (95% CI) was 99% (96, 100) for GT2 and 92% (80, 97) for GT4.
L35: Evidence supporting treatment for GT3, TN, NC subjects largely comes from ENDURANCE-3 for which the SVR12 (95% CI) in the 8-week and 12-week arms were 95% (90, 97) and 95% (92, 97), respectively. The trial was successful in demonstrating efficacy of the 12-week regimen based on showing non-inferiority (NI) to the active-control. The 8-week arm was non-randomized and therefore comparisons of NI are statistically inappropriate. Instead, the regimen was compared against the pre-specified 92% clinical threshold and findings suggest that the arm failed to achieve superiority against this rate. Supportive data for the 8-week regimen in 29 GT3, TN, NC subjects come from SURVEYOR-2 yielding a SVR12 (95% CI) of 96% (83, 99).
L36: The available data are too limited and do not support an 8-week GLE/PIB regimen in TN, GT5 (n=2 studied) or GT6 (n=8 studied) infected non-cirrhotic subjects. However, more data exist in subjects treated with the 12-week regimen; 22 GT5 and 28 for GT6 specifically, for these less-prevalent genotypes and the overall SVR12 was 100%.
L37: EXPEDITION-1 provides evidence to support a 12-week GLE/PIB regimen in TN and TE-PRS, cirrhotic subjects infected with GT 1, 2, 4-6. SVR12 (95% CI) rates were 98.9% (94, 100) for GT1 and 100% for GT2 (89, 100), GT4 (81, 100), GT5 (34, 100), and GT6 (65, 100) with one post-treatment relapse in a GT1-infected subject.
L38: Evidence supporting a 16-week GLE/PIB regimen in GT3, TE subjects with and without cirrhosis comes from SURVEYOR-2 from which the pooled SVR12 (95% CI) was 94.5% (87, 98).
L39: Data supporting treatment in HCV-infected patients with CKD disease come from EXPEDITION-4, which enrolled 104 subjects of which 53%, 15%, 11%, 19%, 1% and 1% were GT1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6; respectively. Subjects in the trial achieved a SVR12 (95% CI) of 98.1% (93, 99) with two non-virologic failures in two GT1-infected subjects. Refer to the clinical review for a deeper assessment of this trial and the underlying level of kidney disease among the enrolled subjects.

L60: 2 INTRODUCTION
L61: 2.1. Overview
L62: Hepatitis C is a complex, rapidly mutating enveloped RNA virus classified into eleven major genotypes (GT) (1-11), several subtypes (a, b, c, etc.). Globally, subgenotypes 1a and 1b are the most common accounting for about 60% of all GTs, followed by GT2. GT3 is most common in parts of Southeast Asia whereas GT4 is often found in part of the Middle East, Egypt, and central Africa. GT5 occurs almost exclusively in South Africa and GTs 6-11 in Asia (WHO Global Alert and Response Report).
L63: The World Health Organization estimates that about 3% of the world's population is presently infected with the virus and is therefore viewed as a global public health issue. Data from the Centers for Disease Control and Prevention's National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey (NHANES 2003-2010) suggest that an estimated 3.6 million people possess antibodies to the hepatitis C virus and of these; 2.7 million are infected in the United States (Denniston et al., 2014). The distribution of HCV by genotype is approximately 70% GT1 (of which the majority is of 1a), 20% GT2, 3, and the remainder 4, 5, 6. GT4 accounts for only about 2% of all HCV cases the US. Given that the NHANES is a population-based
L64: survey that excludes institutionalized and homeless persons-both high at-risk for HCV infection populations-the true prevalence is arguably higher in the United States.
L65: HCV is typically spread via infected needles, blood, and sexual contact leading to an attack of liver cells and subsequent inflammation and multiple chronic conditions in the infected host. Most infected persons fail to clear the virus and therefore become chronic carriers. The definition of chronic hepatitis C (CHC) is continued demonstration of HCV RNA in the blood for at least six months after onset of infection. CHC leads to increased risk of cirrhosis (current estimates of 25% among HCV-infected persons in the United States), hepatocellular carcinoma and end-stage liver disease (about 10-20%) and a slowly progressing lifelong infection leading to need for liver transplantation or death. Among persons with advanced chronic kidney disease (defined as stages 4 or 5); HCV infection is a strong predictor of mortality and also can limit a patient's eligibility for kidney transplantation.
L66: The universal goal in treating HCV infection is to reduce the associated complications and prolong survival. However given the long period required to evaluate the effect of treatment on reducing end stage liver disease or death. An acceptable surrogate endpoint of sustained virologic response (SVR) is used when testing new treatments. The SVR endpoint is typically defined as unquantifiable or undetectable HCV RNA in serum at 24 weeks following the end of treatment (SVR24). More recently SVR at 12 weeks following the end of treatment (SVR12) has been adopted for use as a reliable surrogate endpoint in CHC clinical trial as it has been shown to be highly correlated with SVR24.
L67: There are several FDA approved therapies to treat CHC by specific genotype (Table 3). Specifically, treatment options for GT1 CHC infection includes IFN-sparing oral DAAs, which are preferred over regimens requiring IFN as these latter regimens are associated with various serious and life-threatening toxicities including bone marrow suppression induced neutropenia and infections. Among the current IFN-sparing regimens, the overall SVR12 rates range from 93-99%. For treatment of GT4 CHC infection, fewer options exist including sofosbuvir plus IFN or a regimen of ombitasvir, paritaprevir, and ritonavir (Technivie®) given with RBV for 12 weeks in TN and TE GT4 patients without cirrhosis. Thus, needs remain for other IFN-sparing regimens to treat GT4 subgenotype CHC subjects particularly those with cirrhosis of the liver. In addition, there are few approved DAA or IFN-free regimens to treat HCV GT6 infection or HCV GT 1, 4, or 6 subgenotype infections in patients receiving hemodialysis.

L113: On-treatment virologic failure (OTVF) was defined as a confirmed increase of &gt;1 \log_{10} IU/mL above nadir during treatment, confirmed HCV RNA \geq 100 IU/mL after HCV RNA &lt; \text{LLOQ} during treatment, or HCV RNA \geq \text{LLOQ} at the end of treatment with at least 6 weeks of treatment.
L114: Post-Treatment relapse was defined as a subject with a confirmed HCV RNA \geq \text{LLOQ} [TD (q)] following end of all study therapy, after becoming undetectable (TND) at end of treatment. Confirmation of relapse was based on two blood draws within two weeks with an HCV RNA \geq \text{LLOQ}. Relapse was measured in subjects who completed planned treatment and therefore omits subjects who prematurely discontinued treatment.
L115: Handling of Missing Values
L116: A backward imputation approach was used for missing assessment for SVR, if possible. This approach used the nearest HCV RNA value after the SVR window. If this value was undetectable or unquantifiable, then it was used to impute the HCV RNA value in the SVR window. If after performing backward imputation, the subject is missing an HCV RNA value in the appropriate SVR window, then this value will be imputed using an HCV RNA value obtained from a local laboratory, if present and otherwise imputed as missing. Missing is treated as a failure in the primary analyses of SVR12. If a subject begins an alternative treatment for their HCV infection, then all HCV RNA values measured on or after initiation of new treatment were excluded from any analyses and the subject treated as a failure in the primary efficacy analysis.
L117: Regarding values for virologic failure, if HCV RNA values from the central laboratory were missing but a local laboratory value is available that value was used to assess post-treatment relapse and OTVF.
L118: Calculation of Confidence Intervals
L119: In most instances, the applicant used a normal approximation to the binomial method (Wald method) to calculate confidence intervals. A 95% CI is expected to have a 95% coverage rate, defined as the proportion of times the interval contains the true value of interest. However, when using an asymptotic method, such as the Wald method, there is a decreased coverage rate when the response rate (e.g. SVR12) is close to 0% or 100%. Alternative methods, such as the Wilson score method provide adequate coverage levels closer to the nominal level of 95% for a 95% CI without being overly conservative like exact methods (e.g. Clopper-Pearson) when the response rate is close to 100% (Wilson, 1927). Therefore, unless otherwise specified, the reviewers' confidence intervals were calculated using the Wilson method.

L296: Statistical Methods
L297: ENDURANCE-1 was designed to compare the 12-week GLE/PIB regimen to a pre-specified SVR12 of 97% using a 6% margin, i.e. achieve a lower bound of the 95% CI around the 12-week GLE/PIB SVR12 of 91% or greater. The justification for the 97% pre-specified clinical threshold was based on data from a Phase 3 trial among GT1 HCV DAA-naïve subjects who were non-cirrhotic receiving ombitasvir/paritaprevir/ritonavir+dasabuvir+/-RBV. Also, SVR12 was based on findings from previous trials of SOF/LDV (ION-1, ION-2, and ION-3), which demonstrated a high 97% SVR12. The 6% NI margin was chosen to preserve at least 68% of the treatment effect of the historical control.

L339: Statistical Methods
L340: The primary analysis compared the SVR12 of the GLE/PIB arm to a SVR12 reference value of 95% using a 6% NI margin (i.e. lower bound around the GEL/PIB SVR12 must be greater than 89% to be deemed NI) in the ITT population (all randomized who received at least one dose of study drug), excluding prior SOF+RBV+/-pegIFN failures. The target sample size of the trial was 291-321 subjects in a 2:1 ratio of GLE/PIB:placebo based on the premise that with 180 subjects in the GLE/PIB arm and while assuming a 96% SVR12 that the study would have at least 90% power to demonstrate non-inferiority to the pre-specified margin of 89%. This power was derived using a normal approximation around the binomial calculation method. Using a fixed sequence testing strategy, if non-inferiority was demonstrated, then the analysis included a second test for superiority against the same 95% reference rate without adjustment.

Similarly, the overall SVR12 in subjects treated for 8 weeks was 95.2% (177/186) but there were slightly more relapses (n=5) and one OTVF. Some concern exists over the high number of treatment relapses on the 8 week regimen; however, the overall SVR12 is acceptable, this regimen is only proposed for use in TN, NC subjects and the relapse rate is lower than observed in Harvoni®, which was 5% among GT1-infected, TN subjects treated for 8-weeks. Further, it was concluded by the review team that there are other treatment options available for TN, GT3-infected subjects (SOF+RBV or SOF+P/R) that could be utilized for the few patients who might suffer relapse on an 8-week GLE/PIB regimen.
L435: Among GT4-infected, TN, NC subjects, a subtype with a lower prevalence (approximately 2% worldwide), the SVR12 was 100% among 79 subjects treated for 12 weeks and slightly lower at 92.3% among only 39 subjects treated with the shorter, 8-week duration. Among the failures, all were non-virologic related. Therefore, these data suggest that either an 8- or 12-week regimen is likely to confer acceptable SVR12 among GT4-infected subjects. The evidence supporting the proposed 12-week GLE/PIB regimen for treating GT4-infected, C, TN subjects is based on data in only 16 subjects; however, all achieved SVR12.
L436: Evidence supporting treatment in TN, NC, GT5 and GT6 subjects is less adequate compared to that for the other genotypes discussed above. Specifically, 22 and 28 TN, NC GT5 and GT6 subjects respectively were treated with a 12 week course of GLE/PIB resulting in a 100% SVR12. Only 2 and 8 TN, NC GT5 and GT6 subjects, respectively, were treated for 8 weeks with GLE/PIB. While all achieved SVR12, these data are too small to provide sufficient evidence in support of the 8-week duration for treating TN, GT5 and GT6-infection in NC subjects. Note also that overall only 10 GT6 subjects were studied on the 8-week GLE/PIB regimen and all but two were TN. Similarly, few cirrhotic, TN, GT5 (n=2) or GT6 (n=6) subjects were evaluated in the GLE/PIB 12-week program though all achieved SVR12. In summary, the review team concluded that the 12-week GLE/PIB regimen should be the recommended duration for all TN, GT5 and GT6 subjects regardless of cirrhotic status.

L525: 5 SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
L526: 5.1. Statistical Issues
L527: The primary efficacy endpoint for the trials was the proportion of subjects who achieve SVR12, which is the clinical endpoint most predictive of long-term HCV cure. For the Phase 2 trials and the single-arm Phase 3 trials, the primary efficacy analysis was to summarize SVR12 via the 95% CI. While the analysis is fairly straightforward for most trials, there are some statistical issues present in the analyses for this application, particularly for the pooled analyses.
L528: 1. Lack of Comparative Trials
L529: The NDA included only two randomized comparative trials from which to enable head-to-head comparisons. ENDURANCE-2 was a placebo-controlled trial allowing for comparative safety only. ENDURANCE-3 included a SOF/DCV active-control for purposes of comparative efficacy; however, it was limited in scope to only TN, GT3-infected subjects. There are no comparative trials for GT4, GT5, or GT6, due to a lower prevalence of these subtypes. Nevertheless, a lack of a control arm for the majority of the trials obscures the evidence of efficacy. The lack of control arms can lead to unbalanced values of measured and unmeasured confounders that could affect the treatment estimates in the trials. While some of the trials compared the SVR12 to a pre-specified clinical threshold SVR12, it is likely that the populations studied previously differ in some characteristics to those studied in these new clinical trials.
L530: 2. Large Proportion of Data from Exploratory Phase 2 Trials
L531: In addition, approximately 44% of the overall pool of subjects comes from trials that lacked formal hypothesis testing, and are therefore considered proof-of-concept. As a result, the reviewers are unable to make decisions or draw statistical conclusions from the individual trials lacking formal hypothesis testing.
L532: 3. Pooling of Data across Trials
L533: Pooling of the results across trials allows for larger sample sizes for subgroup analyses, particularly for determining the proper treatment duration for subpopulations of the HCV infected. However, caution must be taken when interpreting the results of the pooled analyses. Only four of the nine clinical trials were randomized, which is not accounted for in the un-weighted pooling.
L534: 4. Calculation of Confidence Intervals
L535: For some of the individual trial analyses, the applicant used a normal approximation to the binomial distribution to calculate the 95% CI for SVR12. The approximation improves as the sample size increases and performs best when the probability of interest is away from boundary values, 0 or 1. Given that the SVR12 rates across trials are fairly high, mostly above 90%, the coverage probability of the CIs is estimated to be less than 95%, the expected coverage of a 95% CI. The applicant sometimes utilized the Wilson score method to calculate the 95% CI, which typically has a higher coverage than the normal approximation (Wilson, 1927). Therefore, the Wilson score method is also presented by the reviewers when the applicant utilized the normal approximation.
L536: 5. Limited Data in Various Subgroups
L537: Some genotypes and subgroups were limited in sample size. Among all the trials, there were a total of 31 GT5 subjects and 47 GT6 subjects. Of the GT5 and GT6 subjects:
L538: • Two GT5 subjects received the 8 week treatment duration, both of whom were treatment-naïve• 30 GT5 subjects received the 12 week treatment duration, 24 of whom were treatment-naïve• 10 GT6 subjects received the 8 week treatment duration, eight of whom were treatment-naïve• 38 GT6 subjects received the 12 week treatment duration, 33 of whom were treatment-naïve
L539: It is expected that the sample sizes for GT5 and GT6 are smaller than the sample sizes for the other genotypes due to their low prevalence worldwide. However, the small sample sizes contribute to less evidence of efficacy for GT5 and GT6, particularly for the 8-week treatment duration. Under such circumstances, it can become necessary to extrapolate knowledge from the other genotypes regarding the efficacy of GLE/PIB.
L540: In addition, there are limited data supporting the efficacy of GLE/PIB among NS5A and/or NS3/4A PI-experienced subjects. MAGELLAN-1 was the only trial that investigated the safety and efficacy of GLE/PIB in NS5A and/or NS3/4A PI-experienced subjects. In MAGELLAN-1, 113 subjects received the recommended dosage of GLE/PIB. Of the 113 subjects enrolled, 38 subjects were NS5A-experienced, 38 subjects were NS3/4A PI-experienced, 37 subjects were both NS5A and NS3/4A PI-experienced and all but four were infected with GT1.
L541: A thorough efficacy evaluation of the proposed GLE/PIB durations requires assessing the treatment effect among race, gender, age, geographic region and relevant baseline factors germane to HCV subgroups.
L542: Given the overall high SVR12 and small numbers enrolled for 4-6 genotypes, overall subgroup analyses are not informative.
L543: 5.2. Collective Evidence
L544: Among TN, NC subjects with GT1-6 CHC infection, the applicant originally proposed GLE/PIB for 8 weeks, and for TN, C subjects with GT1-6 CHC infection a 12-week regimen of GLE/PIB was proposed. During the mid-cycle review, it was communicated to the applicant that data do not support the 8-week duration for the GT5-6 subgenotypes. (b) (4)
L545: The collective evidence supporting this NME NDA comes from nine clinical trials, four of which are viewed as Phase 3 confirmatory trials. The data covers treatment received for all six genotypes, those with and without compensated cirrhosis, a subgroup with renal impairment and data from subject who were prior treatment-failures with DAAs. Across the five Phase 2 clinical trials, 1273 subjects received the study drug of which 970 received GLE/PIB 300 mg/150 mg for either 8- 12- or 16-weeks. Across the four Phase 3 clinical trials, there were a total of 1614 subjects received treatment, of which 1399 received GLE/PIB 300 mg/120 mg for either 8- or 12-week in duration. The remaining subjects received placebo (n=100) or SOF/DCV for 12 weeks (n=115). The overall pertinent findings by trials are summarized below.
L546: ENDURANCE-1 was a randomized, open-label trial in GT1, NC subjects with or without HIV co-infection (n=33 total co-infected) who were either TN or TE-PRS to compare 8- and 12-week duration of GLE/PIB 300 mg/150 mg. The 8- and 12-week SVR12 (95% CI) rates were 99.1 (97.5, 99.7) and 99.7 (98.4, 100), respectively in the ITT leading to a difference between groups (8-12 weeks) of -0.6%, (-2.2, 0.8). The trial primary objectives to demonstrate superiority of the 12-week regimen against a 91% target historical control followed by a second objective to demonstrate non-inferiority of the 8-week regimen to the 12-week using a 5% margin were achieved.
L547: ENDURANCE-2 was a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (delayed treatment) multicenter study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of GLE/PIB in HCV GT2-infected adult subjects without cirrhosis and where either TN or TE-PRS. The overall SVR12 (95% CI) was 99.5% (97.2, 100) with one failure due to missing the SVR12 assessment. The trial objective to achieve superiority of the GLE/PIB 12-week regimen versus a target historical control of 89% was achieved as the lower CI was well above the target. One subjects had on-treatment virologic failure in the 8-week arm and the remaining failures were due to missing assessments at the SVR12 time point.
L548: ENDURANCE-3 was a Phase 3, randomized, open-label, active-controlled (SOF/DCV), multicenter, non-inferiority trial to assess the efficacy and safety of GLE/PIB in GT3-infected, TN, non-cirrhotic adult subjects. This trial included three treatment arms, GLE/PIB for 12 weeks, SOF/DCV for 12 weeks and GLE/PIB for 8 weeks. The 8-week GLE/PIB arm was non-randomized. The SVR12 (95% CI, Wilson's method) was 95.3% (91.7, 97.3), 96.5% (91.4, 98.6) and 94.9% (90.3, 97.4). The primary objective was to demonstrate non-inferiority of the 12-week GLE/PIB regimen to SOF/DCV based on a -6% margin. The difference (GLE/PIB 12w-SOF/DCV) was -1.2% with a resulting 95% CI of -5.6% to 3.1%, which
L549: excludes -6%. Since the 8-week arm was non-randomized, no direct comparison of the 8-week arm to the one of the two randomized arms was done by the reviewer. Instead, the reviewer assessed the SVR12 of the 8-week arm against the pre-specified 92% pre-specified clinical threshold using an adjusted 97.5% CI of -7.7% to 5.1%. This CI suggests that the SVR12 of the GLE/PIB 8-week regimen fails to demonstrate superiority over the clinical threshold. It is important to note that the lower SVR12 in the 8-week arm was driven by more relapses compared to the 12-week arm. Specifically, failure to achieve SVR12 was due to one OTVF and 5 relapses in the 8-week arm compared to one OTVF and 3 relapses in the 12-week arm. No OTVFs were reported for the SOF/DCV arm and only one relapse.
L550: EXPEDITION-4 was a single-arm Phase 3 trial that evaluates the safety and efficacy of ABT-493/ABT-530 in adults with HCV GT 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6 infection who are TN or TE with or without cirrhosis and who have severe renal impairment or end-stage renal disease: stage 4 without dialysis, stage 5 without dialysis, or dialysis. SVR12 was 98.1% (102/104) with two subjects failing due to premature treatment discontinuation and one death deemed unrelated to study drug.
L551: EXPEDITION-1 was a Phase 2, single-arm, open-label, multicenter study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of a 12-week regimen of GLE/PIB for treating GT 1, 2, 4, 5 or 6 HCV-infected adult subjects with compensated cirrhosis who are either TN or TE-PRS. All but one subject achieved SVR12 and all subjects achieved SVR4 leading to a SVR12 of 99.3% (145/146). The single failure was a relapse in a treatment-experienced (P/R experienced), GT1a-infected subject.
L552: ENDURANCE-4 was a single-arm, open label Phase 3 trial that evaluated the safety and efficacy of GLE/PIB in adults with HCV GT 4, 5, or 6 infection without cirrhosis who were TN or TE-PRS. The SVR12 was 99.2% (120/121) with one subject failing due to premature discontinuation.
L553: MAGELLAN-1 was a multi-arm, two-part, open label Phase 2 trial that evaluated the safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetics of GLE/PIB, co-administered or combined, with or without RBV in adults with HCV GT 1, 4, 5, and 6 infection who failed a prior anti-HCV DAA-containing regimen. Out of 141 subjects enrolled in the trial, all but four subjects were GT1. GT1, NS3/4A PI-experienced subjects that received the proposed dosage for 12 weeks achieved a SVR12 rate of 92.0% (23/25; 2 missing SVR12) across two treatment arms with equivalent dosages and treatment durations. Those who received 16 weeks of the proposed dosage achieved a SVR12 of 100% (12/12). GT1, NS5A-experienced subjects that received the proposed dosage achieved a combined SVR12 rate of 90.0% (18/20) in a 12 week treatment regimen with one on-treatment VF and one post-treatment relapse and a SVR12 rate of 94.1% in a 16 week treatment regimen with one on-treatment VF. GT1 subjects who were both NS3/4A PI- and NS5A-experienced achieved SVR12 rates of 80.0% in both 12 week (16/20; 1 on-treatment VF and 3 post-treatment relapses) and 16 week (12/15; 3 on-treatment VF) treatment durations.
L554: SURVEYOR-1 was a multi-arm, multi-part, open label Phase 2 trial that evaluated the safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetics of GLE/PIB in adults with HCV GT 1, 4, 5, and 6 infection who were TN or TE-PRS, with or without compensated cirrhosis. GT1 subjects without cirrhosis received the proposed dosage for 8 weeks and achieved an SVR12 rate of 97.1% (33/34) with one subject failing due to premature discontinuation. GT 4, 5, and 6 subjects without cirrhosis received the proposed dosage for 12 weeks and achieved an SVR12 rate of 100% (34/34).
L555: SURVEYOR-2 was a multi-arm, multi-part, open label Phase 2 trial that evaluated the safety and efficacy of GLE/PIB, co-administered or combined, with or without ribavirin (RBV) in adults with HCV GT 2, 3, 4, 5, or 6 infection who were TN or TE-PRS with or without cirrhosis.
L556: 5.3. Conclusions and Recommendations
L557: Considering the total amount of evidence from nine clinical trials evaluating GLE/PIB 300 mg/120 mg for the treatment of CHC infection, the conclusions of the statistical reviewers are as follows:
L558: 1. GLE/PIB 8-weeks for TN, NC GT 1, 2 and 4: A regimen of GLE/PIB 300 mg/120 mg once daily for 8 weeks achieved a clinically acceptable SVR12 rate among treatment-naive CHC subjects with GT 1, 2, and 4. Among TN, NC subjects treated with an 8-week GLE/PIB regimen, the SVR12 were 98.8% (245/248), 98.8% (172/174), and 92.3% (36/39) in the GT1, GT2, GT4 pooled groups, respectively. None of the failures were due to virologic failure or relapse.2. GLE/PIB 8-weeks for TN and TE-PRS, NC, GT5 and 6: Too few TN and TE-PRS, NC GT5- and GT6- subjects, 2 and 8 respectively, were studied on the 8-week GLE/PIB regimen. However, among subjects treated with the 12-week GLE/PIB regimen, the SVR12 was 100% for GT5 (22/22) and for GT6 (28/28), providing evidence to support the 12-week regimen for these less-common genotypes.3. GLE/PIB 8-weeks for TN, NC GT 3: A regimen of 8-week GLE/PIB for GT3-infected, NC, TN subjects resulted in an SVR12 of 95% (90, 97) with one OTVF and five relapses from the ENDURANCE-3 trial suggesting an inferior rate of SVR12 versus a 92% historical control on the basis of the statistical target alone. However, the SVR12 is high and a 3% relapses is not viewed by the review team as problematic therefore an 8-week regimen for this subpopulation is viewed as acceptable.4. GLE/PIB 12-weeks for TN, C, GT 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, and 6: Subjects with cirrhosis with GT1, 2, 4, 5, or 6 CHC infection received GLE/PIB 300 mg/120 mg for 12 weeks. The sample sizes for subjects with cirrhosis are limited. Nevertheless, SVR12 rates were 100% in all GTs with the exception of the GT3, which was 98.4%. (NOTE: Please refer to the Clinical review of GLE/PIB for greater exploration of overall severity of cirrhosis.)5. GLE/PIB 16-week for PRS-Experienced, GT3: The data support a 16-week regimen of GLE/PIB regardless of cirrhotic status (pooled SVR12 (95% CI) of 95% (87, 98) over a 12-week regimen that conferred lower a SVR12 of 88%.6. GLE/PIB 8-weeks for DAA- and PRS-experienced, NC GT 1, 2 and 4: An 8 week treatment regimen achieved a clinically acceptable SVR12 rate among DAA- and PRS-experienced CHC subjects with GT 1, 2, and 4 and without cirrhosis. The SVR12 rates among DAA- and PRS-experienced, NC subjects treated for 8 weeks are 99.3%, 91.3%, and 100% for GT 1, 2, and 4, respectively. The sample sizes for PRS-experienced CHC subjects with GT 2 and 4 and without cirrhosis that received 8 weeks of GLE/PIB are limited. However, the SVR12 rates observed are similar (although no testing was done) from the SVR12 rates among subjects of the same subgroup that received a 12-week
L559: treatment duration. In addition, there were only two virologic failures in DAA- and PRS-experienced, GT2-infected subjects without cirrhosis that received the 8 week treatment duration.
L560: 7. GLE/PIB 16-weeks for DAA- and PRS-Experienced, NC, GT3: A 16 week treatment regimen achieved a clinically acceptable SVR12 rate of 95.5% among DAA- and PRS-experienced, GT3-infected subjects without cirrhosis that were pooled across trials. In contrast, DAA- and PRS-experienced, GT3-infected subjects without cirrhosis that received GLE/PIB for 12 weeks achieved a SVR12 rate of 89.8%, only slightly lower than 90%. However, there were four post-treatment relapses and one on-treatment virologic failure among those that received the 12 week treatment duration, compared to one post-treatment relapse among those that received the 16 week treatment duration.8. GLE/PIB 16-weeks for NS3/4A PI- and/or NS5A-Experienced, non-GT1: In the one trial that assessed efficacy of GLE/PIB in NS3/4A PI- and/or NS5A-experienced subjects, all but four subjects were GT1-infected, with the other four being GT4-infected. As a result, the reviewers only considered indications for GT1-infected patients with NS3/4A PI and/or NS5A experience.9. GLE/PIB 16-weeks for NS5A-Naïve, NS3/4A PI-Experienced, GT1: Sample sizes are relatively small for the relevant sub-populations of subjects with prior HCV treatment experience. Nevertheless, the SVR12 rate for 12 weeks of treatment in GT1, NS3/4A PI-experienced subjects was 92.0% and no such subjects experienced virologic failure, providing evidence to support a 12-week indication.10. GLE/PIB 16-weeks for NS5A-Experienced, NS3/4A PI-Naïve, GT1: The SVR12 rate for 12 weeks of treatment in GT1, NS5A-experienced subjects that received 12 weeks of treatment was 90.0%. However, there were two virologic failures in such subjects, one of whom experienced a post-treatment relapse. In contrast, NS5A-experienced subjects that received 16 weeks of treatment achieved a SVR12 of 94.1% with no post-treatment relapses. Therefore, the evidence supports a 16 week treatment duration for GT1-infected, NS5A-experienced subjects.11. GLE/PIB 16-weeks for NS3/4A PI- and NS5A-Experienced, GT1: For subjects that are both NS3/4A PI- and NS5A-experienced, the SVR12 rate was 80% for both the 12 and 16 week treatment durations. In addition, there were three post-treatment relapses and one on-treatment virologic failure in the 12 week treatment arm and three on-treatment virologic failures in the 16 week treatment arm. The lower SVR12 rate and higher number of virologic failures indicated that GLE/PIB 300 mg/120 mg did not achieve an acceptable threshold of efficacy among GT1-infected subjects who are both NS3/4A PI- and NS5A-experienced.12. GLE/PIB 12 weeks in CHC subjects with renal impairment: Subjects with renal impairment, including dialysis, received only GLE/PIB for 12 weeks. Nevertheless, all but two subjects (102/104, 98.1%) achieved SVR12 with no virologic failures. While the applicant did not evaluate an 8-week GLE/PIB treatment regimen in subjects with renal impairment, the evidence does not suggest the need for separate indications for CHC-infected patients with renal impairment.
