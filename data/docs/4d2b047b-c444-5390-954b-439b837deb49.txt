L48: ## 6 ADVERSE REACTIONS
L49: ## 6.1 Clinical Trials Experience
L50: Because clinical trials are conducted under widely varying conditions, adverse reaction rates observed in the clinical trials of a drug cannot be directly compared to rates in the clinical trials of another drug and may not reflect the rates observed in clinical practice.
L51: A total of 1346 subjects were treated with at least one dose of HETLIOZ, of which 139 were treated for &gt; 26 weeks and 93 were treated for &gt; 1 year.
L52: A 26-week, parallel-arm placebo-controlled study (Study 1) evaluated HETLIOZ (n=42) compared to placebo (n=42) in patients with Non-24. A randomized-withdrawal, placebo-controlled study of 8 weeks duration (Study 2) also evaluated HETLIOZ (n=10), compared to placebo (n=10), in patients with Non-24.
L53: In placebo-controlled studies, 6% of patients exposed to HETLIOZ discontinued treatment due to an adverse event, compared with 4% of patients who received placebo.

L58: ## 7 DRUG INTERACTIONS
L59: ## 7.1 Strong CYP1A2 Inhibitors (e.g., fluvoxamine)
L60: Avoid use of HETLIOZ in combination with fluvoxamine or other strong CYP1A2 inhibitors because of a potentially large increase in tasimelteon exposure and greater risk of adverse reactions [see *Clinical Pharmacology* (12.3)].
L61: ## 7.2 Strong CYP3A4 Inducers (e.g., rifampin)
L62: Avoid use of HETLIOZ in combination with rifampin or other CYP3A4 inducers because of a potentially large decrease in tasimelteon exposure with reduced efficacy [see *Clinical Pharmacology* (12.3)].
L63: ## 8 USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS
L64: ## 8.1 Pregnancy
L65: ## *Pregnancy Category C*
L66: There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of HETLIOZ in pregnant women. In animal studies, administration of tasimelteon during pregnancy resulted in developmental toxicity (embryofetal mortality, neurobehavioral impairment, and decreased growth and development in offspring) at doses greater than those used clinically. HETLIOZ should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risks.
L67: In pregnant rats administered tasimelteon at oral doses of 5, 50, or 500 mg/kg/day during the period of organogenesis, there were no effects on embryofetal development. The highest dose tested is approximately 240 times the recommended human dose (RHD) of 20 mg/day, on a \text{mg}/\text{m}^2 basis.
L68: In pregnant rabbits administered tasimelteon at oral doses of 5, 30, or 200 mg/kg/day during the period of organogenesis, embryo lethality and embryofetal toxicity (reduced fetal body weight and delayed ossification) were observed at the highest dose tested. The highest dose not associated with adverse effects (30 mg/kg/day) is approximately 30 times the RHD on a \text{mg}/\text{m}^2 basis.
L69: Oral administration of tasimelteon (50, 150, or 450 mg/kg/day) to rats throughout organogenesis and lactation resulted in persistent reductions in body weight, delayed sexual maturation and physical development, and neurobehavioral impairment in offspring at the highest dose tested. Reduced body weight in offspring was also observed at the mid-dose. The no effect dose (50 mg/kg/day) is approximately 25 times the RHD on a \text{mg}/\text{m}^2 basis.
L70: ## 8.3 Nursing Mothers
L71: It is not known whether this drug is excreted in human milk. Because many drugs are excreted in human milk, caution should be exercised when HETLIOZ is administered to a nursing woman.
L72: ## 8.4 Pediatric Use
L73: Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients have not been established.
L74: ## 8.5 Geriatric Use
L75: The risk of adverse reactions may be greater in elderly (&gt;65 years) patients than younger patients because exposure to tasimelteon is increased by approximately 2-fold compared with younger patients.
L76: ## 8.6 Hepatic Impairment
L77: Dose adjustment is not necessary in patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment. HETLIOZ has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C). Therefore, HETLIOZ is not recommended for use in patients with severe hepatic impairment [see *Clinical Pharmacology* (12.3)].
L78: ## 8.7 Smokers
L79: Smoking causes induction of CYP1A2 levels. The exposure of tasimelteon in smokers was lower than in non-smokers and therefore the efficacy of HETLIOZ may be reduced in smokers [see *Clinical pharmacology* (12.3)].
L80: ## 9 DRUG ABUSE AND DEPENDENCE
L81: ## 9.1 Controlled Substance
L82: Tasimelteon is not a controlled substance under the Controlled Substances Act.
L83: ## 9.2 Abuse
L84: Tasimelteon did not produce any abuse-related signals in animal behavioral studies. Rats did not self-administer tasimelteon, suggesting that the drug does not have rewarding properties. There were also no signs or symptoms indicative of abuse potential in clinical studies with HETLIOZ.
L85: ## 9.3 Dependence
L86: Discontinuation of HETLIOZ in humans following chronic administration did not produce withdrawal signs. HETLIOZ does not appear to produce physical dependence.
L87: ## 10 OVERDOSAGE
L88: There is limited premarketing clinical experience with the effects of an overdose of HETLIOZ.
L89: As with the management of any overdose, general symptomatic and supportive measures should be used, along with immediate gastric lavage where appropriate. Intravenous fluids should be administered as needed. Respiration, pulse, blood pressure, and other appropriate vital signs should be monitored, and general supportive measures employed.
L90: While hemodialysis was effective at clearing HETLIOZ and the majority of its major metabolites in patients with renal impairment, it is not known if hemodialysis will effectively reduce exposure in the case of overdose.
L91: As with the management of any overdose, the possibility of multiple drug ingestion should be considered. Contact a poison control center for current information on the management of overdose.

L97: ## 12 CLINICAL PHARMACOLOGY
L98: ## 12.1 Mechanism of Action
L99: The precise mechanism by which tasimelteon exerts its therapeutic effect in patients with Non-24 is not known. Tasimelteon is an agonist at melatonin MT_1 and MT_2 receptors. These receptors are thought to be involved in the control of circadian rhythms.
L100: ## 12.2 Pharmacodynamics
L101: HETLIOZ is an agonist at MT_1 and MT_2 receptors. HETLIOZ exhibits a greater affinity for the MT_2 as compared to the MT_1 receptor. The most abundant metabolites of HETLIOZ have less than one-tenth of the binding affinity of the parent molecule for both the MT_1 and MT_2 receptors.
L102: ## 12.3 Pharmacokinetics
L103: The pharmacokinetics of HETLIOZ is linear over doses ranging from 3 to 300 mg (0.15 to 15 times the recommended daily dosage). The pharmacokinetics of HETLIOZ and its metabolites did not change with repeated daily dosing.
L104: ## Absorption
L105: The peak concentration (T_{max}) of tasimelteon occurred approximately 0.5 to 3 hours after fasted oral administration.
L106: When administered with a high-fat meal, the C_{max} of tasimelteon was 44% lower than when given in a fasted state, and the median T_{max} was delayed by approximately 1.75 hours. Therefore, HETLIOZ should be taken without food.
L107: ## Distribution
L108: The apparent oral volume of distribution of tasimelteon at steady state in young healthy subjects is approximately 56 - 126 L. At therapeutic concentrations, tasimelteon is about 90% bound to proteins.
L109: ## Metabolism
L110: Tasimelteon is extensively metabolized. Metabolism of tasimelteon consists primarily of oxidation at multiple sites and oxidative dealkylation resulting in opening of the dihydrofuran ring followed by further oxidation to give a carboxylic acid. CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 are the major isozymes involved in the metabolism of tasimelteon.
L111: Phenolic glucuronidation is the major phase II metabolic route.
L112: Major metabolites had 13-fold or less activity at melatonin receptors compared to tasimelteon.
L113: ## Elimination
L114: Following oral administration of radiolabeled tasimelteon, 80% of total radioactivity was excreted in urine and approximately 4% in feces, resulting in a mean recovery of 84%. Less than 1% of the dose was excreted in urine as the parent compound.
L115: The observed mean elimination half-life for tasimelteon is 1.3 \pm 0.4 hours. The mean terminal elimination half-life \pm standard deviation of the main metabolites ranges from 1.3 \pm 0.5 to 3.7 \pm 2.2.
L116: Repeated once daily dosing with HETLIOZ does not result in changes in pharmacokinetic parameters or significant accumulation of tasimelteon.
L117: ## Studies in Specific Populations
L118: ## *Elderly*
L119: In elderly subjects, tasimelteon exposure increased by approximately two-fold compared with non-elderly adults.
L120: ## *Gender*
L121: The mean overall exposure of tasimelteon was approximately 20-30% greater in female than in male subjects.
L122: ## *Race*
L123: The effect of race on exposure of HETLIOZ was not evaluated.
L124: ## *Hepatic Impairment*
L125: The pharmacokinetic profile of a 20 mg dose of HETLIOZ was compared among eight subjects with mild hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Score \ge 5 and \le 6 points), eight subjects with moderate hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Score \ge 7 and \le 9 points), and 13 healthy matched controls. Tasimelteon exposure was increased less than two-fold in subjects with moderate hepatic impairment. Therefore, no dose adjustment is needed in patients with mild or moderate hepatic impairment. HETLIOZ has not been studied in patients with severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh Class C) and is not recommended in these patients.
L126: ## *Renal Impairment*
L127: The pharmacokinetic profile of a 20 mg dose of HETLIOZ was compared among eight subjects with severe renal impairment (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] \le 29 mL/min/1.73\text{m}^2), eight subjects with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) (GFR &lt; 15 mL/min/1.73\text{m}^2) requiring hemodialysis, and sixteen healthy matched controls. There was no apparent relationship between tasimelteon CL/F and renal function, as measured by either estimated creatinine clearance or eGFR. Subjects with severe renal impairment had a 30% lower clearance, and clearance in subjects with ESRD was comparable to that of healthy subjects. No dose adjustment is necessary for patients with renal impairment.
L128: ## *Smokers (smoking is a moderate CYP1A2 inducer)*
L129: Tasimelteon exposure decreased by approximately 40% in smokers, compared to non-smokers [see Use in Specific Populations (8.7)].
L130: ## Drug Interaction Studies
L131: No potential drug interactions were identified in *in vitro* studies with CYP inducers or inhibitors of CYP1A1, CYP1A2, CYP2B6, CYP2C9/2C19, CYP2E1, CYP2D6 and transporters including P-glycoprotein, OATP1B1, OATP1B3, OCT2, OAT1 and OAT3.
L132: ## *Effect of Other Drugs on HETLIOZ*
L133: Drugs that inhibit CYP1A2 and CYP3A4 are expected to alter the metabolism of tazimelteon.
L134: *Fluvoxamine (strong CYP1A2 inhibitor)*: the AUC0-inf and Cmax of tazimelteon increased by 7-fold and 2-fold, respectively, when co-administered with fluvoxamine 50 mg (after 6 days of fluvoxamine 50 mg per day) [see Drug Interactions (7.1)].
L135: *Ketoconazole (strong CYP3A4 inhibitor)*: tazimelteon exposure increased by approximately 50% when co-administered with ketoconazole 400 mg (after 5 days of ketoconazole 400 mg per day) [see Drug Interactions (7.2)].
L136: *Rifampin (strong CYP3A4 and moderate CYP2C19 inducer)*: the exposure of tazimelteon decreased by approximately 90% when co-administered with rifampin 600 mg (after 11 days of rifampin 600 mg per day). Efficacy may be reduced when HETLIOZ is used in combination with strong CYP3A4 inducers, such as rifampin [see Drug Interactions (7.2)].
L137: ## *Effect of HETLIOZ on Other Drugs*
L138: *Midazolam (CYP3A4 substrate)*: Administration of HETLIOZ 20 mg once a day for 14 days did not produce any significant changes in the Tmax, Cmax, or AUC of midazolam or 1-OH midazolam. This indicates there is no induction of CYP3A4 by tazimelteon at this dose.
L139: *Rosiglitazone (CYP2C8 substrate)*: Administration of HETLIOZ 20 mg once a day for 16 days did not produce any clinically significant changes in the Tmax, Cmax, or AUC of rosiglitazone after oral administration of 4 mg. This indicates that there is no induction of CYP2C8 by tazimelteon at this dose.
L140: ## *Effect of Alcohol on HETLIOZ*
L141: In a study of 28 healthy volunteers, a single dose of ethanol (0.6 g/kg for women and 0.7 g/kg for men) was co-administered with a 20 mg dose of HETLIOZ. There was a trend for an additive effect of HETLIOZ and ethanol on some psychomotor tests.
L142: ## 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY
L143: ## 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility
L144: ## Carcinogenesis
L145: Tasimelteon was administered orally for up to two years to mice (30, 100, and 300 mg/kg/day) and rats (20, 100, and 250 mg/kg/day). No evidence of carcinogenic potential was observed in mice; the highest dose tested is approximately 75 times the recommended human dose (RHD) of 20 mg/day, on a mg/m2 basis. In rats, the incidence of liver tumors was increased in males (adenoma and carcinoma) and females (adenoma) at 100 and 250 mg/kg/day; the incidence of tumors of the uterus (endometrial adenocarcinoma) and uterus and cervix (squamous cell carcinoma) were increased at 250 mg/kg/day. There was no increase in tumors at the lowest dose tested in rats, which is approximately 10 times the RHD on a mg/m2 basis.
L146: ## *Mutagenesis*
L147: Tasimelteon was negative in an *in vitro* bacterial reverse mutation (Ames) assay, an *in vitro* cytogenetics assay in primary human lymphocytes, and an *in vivo* micronucleus assay in rats.
L148: ## *Impairment of Fertility*
L149: When male and female rats were given tasimelteon at oral doses of 5, 50, or 500 mg/kg/day prior to and throughout mating and continuing in females to gestation day 7, estrus cycle disruption and decreased fertility were observed at all but the lowest dose tested. The no-effect dose for effects on female reproduction (5 mg/kg/day) is approximately 2 times the RHD on a mg/m2 basis.
L150: ## 14 CLINICAL STUDIES
L151: The effectiveness of HETLIOZ in the treatment of Non-24-Hour Sleep-Wake Disorder (Non-24) was established in two randomized double-masked, placebo-controlled, multicenter, parallel-group studies (Studies 1 and 2) in totally blind patients with Non-24.
L152: In study 1, 84 patients with Non-24 (median age 54 years) were randomized to receive HETLIOZ 20 mg or placebo, one hour prior to bedtime, at the same time every night for up to 6 months.
L153: Study 2 was a randomized withdrawal trial in 20 patients with Non-24 (median age 55 years) that was designed to evaluate the maintenance of efficacy of HETLIOZ after 12-weeks. Patients were treated for approximately 12 weeks with HETLIOZ 20 mg one hour prior to bedtime, at the same time every night. Patients in whom the calculated time of peak melatonin level (melatonin acrophase) occurred at approximately the same time of day (in contrast to the expected daily delay) during the run-in phase were randomized to receive placebo or continue treatment with HETLIOZ 20 mg for 8 weeks.
L154: Study 1 and Study 2 evaluated the duration and timing of nighttime sleep and daytime naps via patient-recorded diaries. During Study 1, patient diaries were recorded for an average of 88 days during screening, and 133 days during randomization. During Study 2, patient diaries were recorded for an average of 57 days during the run-in phase, and 59 days during the randomized-withdrawal phase.
L155: Because symptoms of nighttime sleep disruption and daytime sleepiness are cyclical in patients with Non-24, with severity varying according to the state of alignment of the individual patient's circadian rhythm with the 24-hour day (least severe when fully aligned, most severe when 12 hours out of alignment), efficacy endpoints for nighttime total sleep time and daytime nap
L156: duration were based on the 25% of nights with the least nighttime sleep, and the 25% of days with the most daytime nap time. In Study 1, patients in the HETLIOZ group had, at baseline, an average 195 minutes of nighttime sleep and 137 minutes of daytime nap time on the 25% of most symptomatic nights and days, respectively. Treatment with HETLIOZ resulted in a significant improvement, compared with placebo, for both of these endpoints in Study 1 and Study 2 (see Table 2).

L160: The efficacy of HETLIOZ in treating Non-24 may be reduced in subjects with concomitant administration of beta adrenergic receptor antagonists.
