L10: ## 1.1 Introduction
L11: Omaveloxolone (RTA 408) has been developed by Reata Pharmaceuticals, Inc. for the treatment of Friedreich's Ataxia in adults and in adolescents at least 16 years of age.
L12: ## 1.2 Brief Discussion of Nonclinical Findings
L13: RTA 408, an activator of the Nrf2 pathway, is for the treatment of Friedreich's Ataxia (FA). The recommended human dose (RHD) is 150 mg/day. There are no safety concerns regarding excipients or impurities.
L14: The ability of RTA 408 to activate Nrf2 has been demonstrated in ARE-luciferase reporter assays and a variety of CNS cell types. RTA 408 restored mitochondrial function in myoblasts under oxidative stress, suppressed IFN\gamma-induced nitric oxide (NO) production in macrophages, and inhibited proinflammatory gene expression in LPS-stimulated microglial cells. In *in vitro* model of FA, RTA 408 induced Nrf2 target genes in hFXNI154F and FA patient-derived cells, as well as increased GSH levels and mitochondrial function in hFXNI154F cells. Daily oral administration of RTA 408 in mouse, rat, and monkey increased Nrf2 target gene expression and antioxidant enzyme activity in various tissues including brain. RTA 408 increased the expression of *Fth1* (Ferritin Heavy Chain 1) in mouse myoblasts and dose-dependently increased the expression of *FTH1* in monkey liver after oral dosing.
L15: The major circulating human metabolites are M17 and M22. Following a single oral administration of [14C]RTA 408, M17 was present in monkeys at higher levels than humans; however, M22 was not observed in monkey plasma. Additional mixed-matrix normalization analyses were used to assess the metabolite profiles for M17 and M22 after repeated oral administration in monkey, rat, Tg.rasH2 mouse, CD1 mouse, and patients with FA. M17 showed adequate safety coverage (&gt;50% peak area ratios in plasma) in all tested species compared with humans; however, M22 was 25.6%, 16.6%, and 5% in monkey, male rat, and female rat, respectively, of levels in FA patients. Therefore, M22 was not adequately tested in the general toxicology studies in rat and monkey or in any reproductive and developmental toxicology studies in rat. M22 showed minimal pharmacological activity and was negative in *in silico* analysis and in a non-GLP Ames assay.
L16: Oral toxicology studies of up to 26 and 39 weeks' duration were conducted in Sprague Dawley (SD) rat and cynomolgus monkey, respectively. The primary toxicities in rats were body weight loss, increased liver weight, panlobular hepatocellular hypertrophy, bile duct hyperplasia, and kidney tubular degeneration/regeneration. Findings in the liver were associated with increases in AST and ALT. Based on the liver (bile duct hyperplasia) and kidney (tubular degeneration/regeneration) findings at all doses (0, 0.3, 1, or 3 mg/kg) in the 26-week study, a NOAEL was not established in rats. L80: ## 4 Pharmacology
L81: ## 4.1 Primary Pharmacology
L82: The ability of RTA 408 to activate Nrf2 was demonstrated in two ARE-luciferase reporter assays (human NQO1-ARE and rat GSTA2-ARE), with activity observed at concentrations as low as 3.9 nM. RTA 408 dose-dependently increased mRNA levels of Nrf2 target genes (*NQO1*, *HMOX1*, *GCLC*, or *GCLM*) in CNS cells (neurons, astrocytes, and microglia). RTA 408 restored mitochondrial function (including basal, ATP-linked, and maximal respiration, and spare respiratory capacity) in myoblasts under oxidative stress (treatment with sulfoximine (BSO) and ferric ammonium citrate (FAC)). Additionally, RTA 408 suppressed IFN\gamma-induced nitric oxide (NO) production in mouse macrophages with an IC50 of 3.8 nM, and concentration-dependently inhibited LPS-induced tumor necrosis factor (*Tnf*), interleukin-6 (*IL-6*), and *Ccl2* gene expression in mouse microglial cells. In *in vitro* model of Friedreich's Ataxia (FA), RTA 408 concentration-dependently increased mRNA levels of Nrf2 target genes in hFXNI154F MEFs (*Nqo1*, *Gclm*, *Gclc*, *Txnrd1*, and *Ho-1*) and in FA patient-derived fibroblasts and lymphoblastoid cells (*NQO1* and *GCLM*). RTA 408 significantly increased GSH levels and mitochondrial function (including basal, ATP-linked, and maximal respiration, and spare respiratory capacity) in hFXNI154F MEFs. Daily oral administration of 10, 30, and/or 100 mg/kg RTA 408 in mouse, rat, and monkey for 14 days resulted in an increased expression of various Nrf2 target genes and activity of the antioxidant enzymes (including NQO1, GST, and/or GSR) in a generally dose-dependent manner in various tissues (mouse and rat: liver; monkey: liver, lung, and brain). RTA 408 was 62 to &gt;1422 times more potent than its metabolites (including M22, M17, M28, M14, M15, and M23) in suppressing IFN\gamma-induced NO production in mouse macrophages.
L83: ## 4.2 Secondary Pharmacology
L84: RTA 408 was evaluated for off-target binding against an *in vitro* panel of 38 targets comprised of receptors, ion channels, and transporters, as well as 6 enzymes. RTA 408 did not significantly interact with any target.
L85: RTA 408 differentially modulated the expression of aminotransferase genes (including *ALT1*, *ALT2*, *AST1*, and *AST2*) in different cell lines originating from different types of tissues. Among four aminotransferase genes tested, RTA 408 consistently reduced the mRNA levels of *AST2* in all tested cells.

L94: ## 5 Pharmacokinetics/ADME/Toxicokinetics
L95: ## 5.1 PK/ADME
L96: Standard PK parameters were assessed following oral administration of RTA 408 in mouse, rat, rabbit, and monkey, with t_{1/2} ranging from 6 to 30 h. Following oral administration to monkeys, [^{14}C]RTA 408 was approximately 3.41% bioavailable and was widely distributed to most tissues, including brain, between 8 and 30 h, with the highest concentrations observed in the gastrointestinal tract. RTA 408 was between 92.7 and 99.6% protein bound in plasma from mouse, rat, dog, rabbit, minipig, and monkey, and was 96.9% protein bound in human plasma. The major circulating human metabolites were M17 and M22 that accounted for 10.9% and 18.6% of total plasma radioactivity, respectively. Following a single oral dose of [^{14}C]RTA 408, M17 was observed at higher plasma levels in monkeys (13.9%) compared to human (10.9%); however, M22 was not observed in monkey plasma. Additional mixed-matrix normalization analytical methodologies were used to assess the metabolite profiles for M17 and M22 after repeated oral administration in monkey, rat, Tg.rasH2 mouse, CD1 mouse, and patients with FA. M17 showed adequate safety coverage (75.1 to 67700% peak area ratios in plasma) in all tested species compared with humans. The ratios for M22 in Tg.rasH2 mouse plasma and CD1 mouse plasma at HD were similar to patients with FA; however, M22 was 25.6%, 16.6%, and 5% in monkey, male rat, and female rat, respectively, of levels in patients. The hepatobiliary route was the primary route of elimination in monkey after oral administration of [^{14}C]RTA 408.

L105: ## **Rat**
L106: *(28-Day, GLP; RTA408-P-1103; from nonclinical review of 122349 by Chun-Ting (David) Lee):*
L107: Male and female SD rats (15/sex/group) were administered doses of 0 (sesame oil), 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg RTA 408 by daily oral gavage for 28 days, with 28 days recovery. A dose-dependent decrease in body weight gain was observed in males at all doses (LD 10%, MD 16%, and HD 44%) with incomplete recovery, which correlated with decreases in food consumption. Increases in AST (up to 2.83-fold) and ALT (up to 6.88-fold) were observed at all doses except the LD, relative to controls, which resolved over the recovery period. Dose-dependent increases in liver weights were observed at all doses, while dose-dependent increases in kidney weights were observed at the MD and HD in females, which did not fully recover during the recovery period. Dose-dependent minimal to severe panlobular hepatocellular hypertrophy was observed at all doses, which was associated with minimal individual hepatocyte necrosis at the MD and HD. Minimal to moderate focal necrosis was observed in all dose groups except LDM. Additionally, minimal to mild bile duct hyperplasia was observed at the MD. All these liver findings showed incomplete recovery. Dose-dependent minimal to mild kidney tubular degeneration/regeneration was observed at the MD and HD with incomplete recovery. TK parameters were evaluated on Days 1 and 28. Increases in Cmax and AUC were generally dose proportional. The NOAEL of RTA 408 in rats through 28 days could not be defined as the liver findings of focal necrosis and bile duct hyperplasia were observed at the LD at the end of the recovery period.

L259: ## 11 Integrated Summary and Safety Evaluation
L260: ## Introduction
L261: RTA 408 is a Nrf2 activator developed by Reata Pharmaceuticals Inc. for the treatment of Friedreich's Ataxia. The recommended human dose (RHD) is 150 mg/day.
L262: ## Pharmacology
L263: ## Primary Pharmacology
L264: The ability of RTA 408 to activate Nrf2 has been demonstrated in ARE-luciferase reporter assays and a variety of CNS cell types including neurons, astrocytes, and microglia, with activity observed at concentrations in the low nM range. RTA 408 restored mitochondrial function in myoblasts under oxidative stress, suppressed IFN\gamma-induced nitric oxide (NO) production in mouse macrophages, and inhibited proinflammatory gene expression in LPS-stimulated mouse microglial cells. In *in vitro* model of Friedreich's Ataxia (FA), RTA 408 increased mRNA levels of Nrf2 target genes in hFXNI154F MEFs and in FA patient-derived fibroblasts and lymphoblastoid cells. RTA 408 significantly increased GSH levels and mitochondrial function in hFXNI154F MEFs. Daily oral administration of RTA 408 in mouse, rat, and monkey for 14 days increased Nrf2 target gene expression and antioxidant enzyme activity in various tissues including liver, lung, and/or brain. RTA 408 was more potent than its metabolites (including M22, M17, M28, M14, M15, and M23) in suppressing IFN\gamma-induced NO production in mouse macrophages.
L265: ## Secondary Pharmacology
L266: RTA 408 was evaluated for off-target binding against an *in vitro* panel of 38 targets as well as 6 enzymes. RTA 408 did not significantly interact with any target.
L267: RTA 408 differently modulated the expression of aminotransferase genes (including *ALT1*, *ALT2*, *AST1*, and *AST2*) in different types of cells, increased the expression of *Fth1* (Ferritin Heavy Chain 1) in mouse myoblasts, and dose-dependently increased the expression of *FTH1* in monkey liver after oral dosing.
L268: ## Safety Pharmacology
L269: Dedicated single dose oral studies in rat (CNS and respiratory) and monkey (cardiovascular) did not indicate drug effects on safety pharmacology endpoints, and there was no significant effect on QT prolongation in HEK293 cell cultures exposed to up to 1.2 \muM RTA 408.
L270: ## PK/ADME
L271: Plasma half-life for RTA 408 was approximately 6 to 30 h in mouse, rat, rabbit, and monkey. A single oral dose of [14C]RTA 408 in monkeys showed that radioactivity was distributed to tissues, including brain, with the highest concentrations observed in the gastrointestinal tract. The major circulating human metabolites were M17 and M22. Following a single oral administration of [14C]RTA 408, M17 was present in monkeys at higher levels (13.9%) than humans (10.9%); however, M22 was not observed in monkey plasma after a single dose. Additional mixed-matrix normalization analyses were used to assess the metabolite profiles for M17 and M22 after repeated oral administration in
L272: monkey, rat, Tg.rash2 mouse, CD1 mouse, and patients with FA. M17 showed adequate safety coverage (75.1 to 67700% peak area ratios in plasma) in all tested species compared with humans; therefore, was of less for the toxicities of concern. The ratios for M22 in Tg.rash2 mouse plasma and CD1 mouse plasma at HD were similar to patients with FA; however, M22 was 25.6%, 16.6%, and 5% in monkey, male rat, and female rat, respectively, of levels in patients. M22 was, therefore, not adequately tested in the general toxicology studies in rat and monkey or in any reproductive and developmental toxicology studies in rat. M22 was &gt;10000 times less potent than RTA 408 in suppressing IFN\gamma-induced NO production in mouse macrophages; therefore, showed minimal pharmacological activity relative to parent. Additionally, M22 (TX304579) was negative in an ICH M7-compliant *in silico* analysis (Leadscope Genetox Statistical Models and Derek Nexus) and in a non-GLP Ames assay. However, issues in this Ames assay were noted, including only 4 strains used, higher maximum test concentration and longer incubation time than recommended by the OECD guideline, respectively.
L273: ## Toxicology
L274: ## General Toxicology
L275: The general toxicology of RTA 408 was assessed in GLP studies in SD rat (28-day and 26-week) and cynomolgus monkey (28-day and 39-week). In rats, drug-related effects included decreases in body weight gain in males (28-day) and absolute body weight in males (39-week), increases in liver weight, panlobular hepatocellular hypertrophy, bile duct hyperplasia, and kidney tubular degeneration/regeneration. Findings in the liver were associated with increases in AST and ALT. Based on the liver (bile duct hyperplasia) and kidney (tubular degeneration/regeneration) findings at all doses in the 26-week study, a NOAEL was not established in rats. In monkeys, drug-related effects included decreases in absolute body weight, prolongations in APTT, decreases in red cell mass (39-week), increases in liver weight, panlobular hepatocellular hypertrophy (28-day), kidney vacuolation (28-day), kidney tubular degeneration, decreased cellularity of bone marrow (28-day), and bile duct hyperplasia (39-week). Based on the prolongations in APTT and bile duct hyperplasia at \ge MD, the NOAEL for daily-administration of RTA 408 for 39-week in monkey was 10 mg/kg/day.
L276: ## Genetic Toxicology
L277: An OECD-compliant genetic toxicology battery was conducted to support development of RTA 408. RTA 408 was negative in Ames and *in vivo* rat bone marrow micronucleus assays. RTA 408 was positive in an *in vitro* chromosomal aberration assay in human lymphocytes but was negative in an *in vivo* rat liver comet assay. Given the findings of the *in vivo* studies, concern over the positive result in the *in vitro* chromosome aberration assay is reduced.
L278: ## Reproductive and Developmental Toxicity
L279: A fertility study in SD rats administered 0, 1, 3, or 10 mg/kg/day RTA 408 by oral gavage resulted in a reduction in absolute body weight in HDM relative to controls from Day 36 through the end of the study. Additionally, increases in pre- and post-implantation losses and resorptions (early and late), as well as decreases in the number of implantation sites, the number of viable embryos, and uterus with cervix weight were
L280: observed in HDF. Based on body weight loss in HDM and fertility and embryonic development findings at HD, the NOAEL was the MD of 3 mg/kg.
L281: Embryofetal development was evaluated in SD rats (0, 1, 3, or 10 mg/kg/day) and NZW rabbits (0, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg/day). In rats, no drug-related findings on maternal and fetal parameters were observed, the maternal and embryofetal NOAEL was the HD of 10 mg/kg/day. In rabbits, 2 MDF and 3 HDF aborted and were euthanized between GD 25 and 27. Drug-related clinical signs at all doses included few/absent/tan discolored feces, thin body condition, and red material in the pan. Early delivery was observed at all doses. Body weight gain and food consumption in HDF were reduced relative to controls from GD 7 to 19. Additionally, drug-related adverse effects at the HD included increases in post-implantation loss, number of late resorptions, resorptions (early and late), and the incidence of unilateral full rib, as well as a decrease in fetal body weight. Based on these findings, the maternal NOAEL was the LD of 3 mg/kg/day, and the embryofetal NOAEL was the MD of 10 mg/kg/day.
L282: Effects on pre- and postnatal development were evaluated in pregnant SD rats administered 0, 1, 3, or 10 mg/kg/day RTA 408 from GD 6 to Lactation Day (LD) 20. The drug effects on F0 dams at HD included decreases in body weight gain, food intake, and lactation food consumption, as well as an increase in enlarged livers. Additionally, a decrease in the number of liveborn pups/litter at birth and during the lactation periods, as well as increases in the number of stillborn pups/litter at birth and percent of stillborn pups/litter were observed at HD. The drug effects on F1 animals at HD included decreases in F1 pup survival to LD 21, decreased mean body weight in F1 pups (pre-weaning; MD and HD), changes in sexual maturation (increased age of attaining preputial separation in males and decreased body weights of females at the time of vaginal opening), and reduced mean numbers of corpora lutea and impanation sites. Mean F1 body weight (post-weaning) was reduced in MDM, HDM, and HDF. Additionally, F1 males exhibited an increase in the basic movement count and total distance at 0-5 min at HD. F1 females exhibited an increase in the basic movement count and total distance at 0-5 min at all doses, as well as in the total distance at 5-10 min at LD. There were no drug effects on the passive avoidance testing; however, the learning and memory task consisted of passive avoidance which lacks the sensitivity of a more complex learning and memory assessment, such as the Morris or Cincinnati water maze. Based on these findings, the NOAEL for dams was the MD of 3 mg/kg/day, and the NOAEL for pups was not established.
L283: ## Carcinogenicity
L284: Carcinogenicity studies (26-week study in Tg.rasH2 mice and 2-year study in SD rats) are to be conducted as post-marketing requirements.
L285: ## Summary and Recommendation
L286: The sponsor's general toxicology studies indicated a risk for drug-related liver and kidney injury based on findings in rats and monkeys, but such effects are likely monitorable in a clinical setting. Carcinogenicity was not evaluated but is to be assessed as a post-marketing requirement.
L287: The nonclinical package provided in the New Drug Application for omaveloxolone (RTA 408) is considered to be adequate to support approval, with one caveat. Since M22 did not show adequate safety coverage in monkey, male rat, and female rat compared with humans, and was formed in the Tg.rasH2 and CD-1 mice at levels similar to those in humans, it is recommended that the sponsor assess the general and developmental toxicity of this metabolite in CD1 mouse and in an embryofetal development study in one species (M22 levels similar to humans), respectively.

L296: Subject: NDA 216718 (Skyclarys, omaveloxolone)
L297: The sponsor (Reata Pharmaceuticals) submitted NDA 216718 on March 30, 2022, for omaveloxolone for the treatment of Friedreich's ataxia in adults and in adolescents 16 years of age and older. Friedreich's ataxia is a rare neurodegenerative disease caused, in most patients, by a homozygous guanine-adenine-adenine (GAA) trinucleotide repeat expansion in the frataxin (FXN) gene, resulting in decreases in frataxin, a protein involved in cellular iron homeostasis.
L298: For the NDA, the sponsor conducted the battery of GLP-compliant nonclinical studies agreed to by the division (Pre-NDA meeting minutes, September 28, 2021), including toxicity studies of up to 6 and 9 months in duration in rat and monkey, respectively, reproductive and developmental toxicology studies, and genetic toxicology studies of omaveloxolone.
L299: The nonclinical studies have been reviewed by Dr. Lee (Review and Evaluation of NDA 216718, Chung-Ting (David) Lee, February 28, 2023) for the NDA. Many of the nonclinical studies were previously reviewed during clinical development; Dr. Lee references these reviews. Based on his review, Dr. Lee has concluded that the nonclinical data support approval of the NDA.
L300: This memo summarizes selected findings from the nonclinical studies.
L301: ## Pharmacology
L302: The pharmacological activity of omaveloxolone was characterized in a series of in vitro and in vivo assays. In vitro assays in rodent (e.g., mouse myoblasts and microglial) and human (e.g., mesangial, primary astrocytes, RPE) cell lines and
L303: in vivo studies in animals (non-GLP 14-day dose range-finding studies in mouse, rat, and monkey) demonstrated activation of the nuclear factor erythroid 2-related factor 2 (NrF2) pathway, which is involved in the cellular response to oxidative stress.
L304: The sponsor conducted no studies of omaveloxolone in an animal model of Friedreich's ataxia, noting that "well-established animal models of FA are not commercially available."
L305: ## PK/ADME
L306: The PK/ADME of omaveloxolone was assessed in multiple animal species (mouse, rat, rabbit, monkey) and in human.
L307: Oral bioavailability was low (3-12%) in monkey and not assessed in other animal species.
L308: Distribution of omaveloxolone into selected tissues at selected time points post-dose was assessed in 14-day toxicity studies in mouse, rat, and monkey. Brain levels were similar or higher than plasma levels in all three species (brain:plasma ratios: 0.877-1.40, 1.40-1.51, and 3.94-5.27, respectively, at 4 (mouse, rat) or 24 (monkey) hours after the last dose.
L309: In vivo metabolism studies in humans indicated the presence of two major circulating metabolites, M22 (1,2-dihydro-30-COOH omaveloxolone) and M17 (1,2-dihydro-29-OH omaveloxolone), which accounted for 18.6 and 10.9% of total drug-related material in plasma, respectively. Neither of these metabolites was quantitated in the GLP-compliant nonclinical studies; however, the sponsor conducted analyses on pooled plasma samples from 7-day pharmacokinetic studies in Tg.ratH2 mouse (100 mg/kg/day), rat (10 mg/kg/day, the high dose in the embryofetal development study), and monkey (100 mg/kg/day, the high dose in the 39-week oral toxicity study). A mixed-matrix method was used "Because validated bioanalytical methods were not available."
L310: The results of these analyses indicated that plasma levels (relative peak area) of M22 in Tg.rasH2 mouse were 102-88.3% of that in Friedreich's ataxia patients at steady state; however, plasma levels in monkey and rat were only 25.6% and 5-16%, respectively, of that in Friedreich's ataxia patients at steady state, indicating that the safety of M22 was not adequately tested in the completed nonclinical studies. Plasma levels (relative peak area) of M17 in mouse, rat, and monkey were similar or higher than that in patients in all three species (M-F: 530-1720, 75-91, and 6770%, respectively). No metabolite data were provided for rabbit.
L311: Metabolites M17 and M22 were tested for pharmacological activity only in one (IFN\gamma-induced nitric oxide) assay in RAW264.7 mouse macrophages. Both metabolites were reported to have substantially less activity compared to
L312: omaveloxolone, suggesting that neither metabolite contributes to Nrf2-mediated effects in vivo.
L313: ## Toxicology
L314: GLP-compliant general oral (gavage) toxicity studies of omaveloxolone were conducted in Sprague Dawley rat and cynomolgus monkey, with durations up to 26 and 39 weeks, respectively. Liver and kidney were target organs in both species, with rat being the more sensitive species.
L315: **Rat:** in an initial 14-day non-GLP oral toxicity study, omaveloxolone (0, 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg/day) resulted in deaths (by Day 6) at the high dose, with cause of death identified as renal toxicity (tubular degeneration/necrosis and regeneration). Liver findings included panlobular hepatocellular hypertrophy, correlated with increases in GGT, ALT, and AST.
L316: In a GLP-compliant 28-day (+ 28-day recovery) oral toxicity study (0, 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg/day), there were no drug-related deaths; however, kidney toxicity (tubular degeneration/regeneration) was observed at all but the low dose, and liver toxicity (bile duct hyperplasia/hypertrophy, panlobular hepatocyte hypertrophy, focal single cell necrosis) was observed at all doses. Clinical pathology findings included increases in total bilirubin, GGT, AST, ALT, SDH, BUN, and creatinine.
L317: In a non-GLP 13-week oral toxicity study (0, 1, 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg/day), deaths occurred at 10 (Day 54) and 30 (Days 54-87) mg/kg/day, considered the result of multiple organ (liver, kidney, and cardiac myofiber) degeneration/necrosis. Liver findings correlated with increases in total bilirubin, GGT, ALT, and AST.
L318: Additional (non-GLP) studies were conducted in male rat (14-day: 0, 3, and 10 mg/kg/day; 14-day: 0 and 30 mg/kg/day) to further characterize omaveloxolone-induced liver (only up to 10 mg/kg/day) and kidney toxicity. Liver changes (hepatocellular vacuolation, hypertrophy of bile duct epithelium) were correlated with increases in ALT and GLDH and ultrastructural findings (increased glycogen and smooth endoplasmic reticulum but no evidence of peroxisomal proliferation) at 3 and 10 mg/kg/day. Renal tubular degeneration/regeneration correlated with increases in urinary NGAL at 10 mg/kg/day and in urinary Kim-1 and NGAL at 30 mg/kg/day.
L319: In the GLP-compliant 26-week (+ 4-week recovery) oral toxicity study (0, 0.3, 1, and 3 mg/kg/day), there were no drug-related deaths; however, liver (bile duct hyperplasia/hypertrophy) and renal degeneration/regeneration) were observed at all doses. Liver findings were correlated with clinical pathology changes, including increases in total bilirubin, GGT, AST, and ALT. Total, LDL, and HDL cholesterol were increased all doses.
L320: As noted by the sponsor, doses associated with toxicity decreased with increased duration of dosing; in the 26-week study, no NOAEL was identified.
L321: **Monkey:** in an initial 14-day non-GLP oral toxicity study, omaveloxolone (0, 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg/day) did not cause premature death but was associated with a dose-related decrease in food consumption and watery feces; liver weight was increased at all doses, but no microscopic findings were detected.
L322: In the GLP-compliant 28-day (+ 28-day recovery) oral toxicity study (0, 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg/day), there were no drug-related deaths; however, clinical signs (distended abdomen, inappetence, unkempt and thin appearance) were observed at the high dose, and watery feces were observed in a dose-related manner. (The sponsor attributed fecal changes to omaveloxolone-induced exacerbation of vehicle [sesame oil] effects.) Microscopic findings were observed in liver (panlobular hepatic hypertrophy), gallbladder (hypertrophy/hyperplasia), and kidney (vacuolation), which persisted in recovery.
L323: In the GLP-compliant 39-week (+ 28-day recovery) oral toxicity study (0, 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg/day), there were 8 premature deaths (2 control males, 2 mid-dose males, 3 high-dose males and 1 high-dose female); all (except in the control male) occurred during the first 6 weeks of dosing and were attributed to “dosing injury.” (This suggests some personnel issue.) Clinical signs (hunched posture, unkempt appearance, decreased activity, emesis, watery/discolored feces) were attributed to omaveloxolone-induced exacerbation of vehicle effects. APTT prolongation was observed at all doses. Microscopic changes in kidney (focal tubular epithelial degeneration/regeneration) were observed at the high dose; liver (bile duct hyperplasia) and gallbladder (mucosal epithelial hypertrophy) changes were observed at all doses.
L324: ## Reproductive and developmental toxicology
L325: A standard battery of reproductive and developmental toxicology studies was conducted for omaveloxolone.
L326: To assess effects on fertility and early embryonic development, omaveloxolone (0, 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg/day) was orally administered to male and female Sprague Dawley rats prior to and during mating and continuing in females to gestation day 7. At the highest dose tested, there was an increase in pre- and post-implantation loss and resorptions, resulting in a decrease in viable embryos at the highest dose tested. The no-effect dose (3 mg/kg/day) for adverse effects on fertility and reproductive function was associated with plasma exposures (AUC) approximately 2 times that in humans (1340-1440 ng*hr/mL) at the recommended human dose of 150 mg/day.
L327: The potential for adverse effects on embryofetal development (EFD) was assessed in embryofetal development studies in pregnant Sprague Dawley rat and New Zealand White rabbit.
L328: Doses for the GLP-compliant EFD study in rat were selected based on a dose range finding study in Sprague Dawley rat at oral doses of 0, 1, 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg/day), administered from gestation days (GD) 6 through GD 17. At 30 mg/kg/day, omaveloxolone produced post-implantation loss and resorptions, resulting in a decrease in viable fetuses, and reduced fetal weight.
L329: In the pivotal study in rat, oral administration of omaveloxolone (0, 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg/day) throughout organogenesis (GDs 6 to 17) resulted in no adverse effects on embryofetal development. At 10 mg/kg/day, plasma exposure (AUC) was approximately 5 times that in humans at the recommended human dose (RHD) of 150 mg/day.
L330: In the GLP-compliant EFD study in rabbit, oral administration of omaveloxolone (0, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg/day) throughout organogenesis (GDs 7 to 19) resulted in increased embryofetal mortality and skeletal variations and reduced fetal weight at the highest dose tested, which was associated with maternal toxicity. At the no-effect dose for adverse effects on embryofetal development (10 mg/kg/day), plasma exposure was less than that in humans at the RHD.
L331: Potential effects of omaveloxolone on pre- and postnatal development were tested in Sprague Dawley rat; omaveloxolone (0, 1, 3, or 10 mg/kg/day) was orally administered throughout pregnancy and lactation (GD 6 through lactation day 20). Omaveloxolone produced an increase in stillbirths at all doses, reduced body weight in offspring at all but the lowest dose tested, and delayed sexual maturation (males), increased postnatal mortality, and impaired reproductive performance in offspring at the highest dose tested.
L332: The study was notable for the minimal evaluation of potential effects on neurobehavioral function; no complex learning and memory task (e.g., Morris Water maze) or startle reflex with habituation testing was included. However, as assessed, adverse effects on neurobehavioral function were observed in offspring at all doses. Evaluation of locomotor activity (postnatal day [PND] 35) indicated increases in basic movement counts and total distance at the beginning of the monitoring period in males at the high dose and at all doses in females. Effects on learning and memory were evaluated using the step-through passive avoidance test (between PNDs 70 and 85). Latency to step into the dark compartment was decreased in males at all doses on testing days 2 and 3.
L333: A no-effect dose for adverse effects on pre- and postnatal development in rat was not identified. Plasma exposure (AUC) at the lowest dose tested was less than that in humans at the RHD.
L334: ## Genetic toxicology
L335: A full battery of genetic toxicology studies was conducted for omaveloxolone.
L336: Omaveloxolone was negative in a bacterial reverse mutation (Ames) assay, and positive in a chromosomal aberration assay in human peripheral blood lymphocytes but negative in in vivo (micronucleus and comet in rat) assays.
L337: ## Carcinogenicity
L338: Carcinogenicity studies of omaveloxolone have not been conducted. The division previously agreed that carcinogenicity studies in mouse and rat may to be submitted post-approval.
L339: The sponsor submitted protocols under SPA for a 26-week carcinogenicity study in Tg.rasH2 mouse and a 2-year carcinogenicity study in Sprague Dawley rat. These were reviewed by the division and the Executive CAC (Executive CAC Minutes, June 4, 2021, and April 14, 2022, respectively) under IND 122349.
L340: Both carcinogenicity studies are ongoing.
L341: ## Conclusions and Recommendations
L342: The nonclinical studies of omaveloxolone submitted to the NDA have identified clear target organs (primarily liver and kidney) toxicity, as well as adverse effects on reproductive and developmental endpoints, at clinically relevant plasma exposures. Carcinogenicity studies have not been completed, as previously agreed to by the division because of the seriousness of the indication and data from an adequate battery of genetic toxicology studies indicating, overall, a lack of mutagenic or clastogenic potential.
L343: There are two major circulating metabolites in humans, M17 and M22. Quantitation of these metabolites was not included in the GLP-compliant toxicity studies. To estimate metabolite exposures, the sponsor conducted analyses on samples from non-GLP 7-day pharmacokinetic studies using a mixed-matrix approach. These analyses indicated that exposure to M17 was similar or higher at the highest doses tested in the embryofetal development study in rat and the 39-week oral toxicity study in monkey, compared to that in humans at the recommended human dose of 150 mg/day; however, plasma exposures to metabolite M22 were substantially lower at those doses than in humans at the RHD.
L344: Mixed-matrix analysis of samples from a 7-day oral dose range-finding study in Tg.rasH2 mouse indicated similar levels of M22 in male (100%) and females
L345: (88.3%) at 100 mg/kg/day compared to that in humans at the RHD; therefore, it is expected that M22 will be adequately tested in the ongoing 26-week carcinogenicity study in Tg.rasH2 mouse. However, the chronic toxicity and reproductive and developmental effects of M22 have not been assessed in animals. To address these concerns, a post-marketing requirement for a chronic toxicity study and an embryofetal development study of M22, each in one species, is recommended.
L346: From a nonclinical standpoint, there is no objection to the approval of omaveloxolone for the proposed indication, with appropriate labeling and the following post-marketing requirements:
L347: - A 26-week carcinogenicity study of omaveloxolone in Tg.rasH2 mouse
- A 2-year carcinogenicity study of omaveloxolone in rat
- A 26-week toxicity study of metabolite M22 (a major circulating metabolite in humans) in rat, based on the lack of adequate exposure to M22 in the completed 26- and 39-week toxicity studies in rat and monkey, respectively.
- An embryofetal development (EFD) study of metabolite M22 in one species, based on the lack of adequate exposure to M22 in the completed EFD studies of omaveloxolone in rat or rabbit.
