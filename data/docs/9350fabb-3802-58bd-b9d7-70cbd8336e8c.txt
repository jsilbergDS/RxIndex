L38: 1 Executive Summary
L39: 1.1. Product Introduction
L40: Merck, Sharp, &amp; Dohme, Corp. is seeking the approval of a New Molecular Entity (NME). This NME is a monoclonal antibody directed at Toxin B of Clostridium difficile and has been designated as MK-6072. The applicant is seeking the indication of prevention of Clostridium difficile Infection (CDI) recurrence in patients 18 years or older receiving antibiotic therapy for CDI.
L41: 1.2. Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
L42: This reviewer recommends that MK-6072 be approved for the prevention of recurrence of CDI in subjects 18 years and older. The drug's effect on preventing recurrence appears variably persuasive, both statistically and clinically, in studies P001 and P002. Though, a potentially harmful effect of MK-6072 on initial clinical cure cannot be ruled out given the inconsistent findings in study P001 and P002, further analysis appears to diminish the clinical significance of several of those deemed failures of initial treatment. Still, the potential negative findings relative to clinical cure should be made clear in labelling, and clinicians should use clinical judgment in deciding the best candidates for this therapy.

L49: Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
L50: MK-6072 is an antibody directed at Toxin B of Clostridium difficile. It is meant to be given during a subject's CDI episode along with standard of care (SOC) in order to prevent recurrence of CDI. In two phase 3 trials, studies P001 and P002, the drug demonstrated a significant effect on prevention of recurrence. However, it also appeared to have an uncertain effect on the initial CDI episode. In study P001, it appeared to have a possible harmful effect on the clinical cure of the initial CDI. In study P002, no harmful effect on the clinical cure of the initial CDI infection was noted. A major amendment allowed for further analysis of both the severity/frequency of recurrences as well as the clinical variables associated with clinical failures. After this analysis, this reviewer felt there was reasonable comfort that potential negative effects on initial clinical cure were minor and that the benefit of reducing the frequency/severity of recurrences outweighed possible negative effects on initial clinical cure.

L58: 2 Therapeutic Context
L59: 2.1. Analysis of Condition
L60: Clostridium difficile infection is a well-recognized cause of colitis associated with morbidity and mortality, particularly in hospitalized patients and patients in long-term care facilities. It is implicated in 20-30% of patients with antibiotic-associated diarrhea, and 50-75% of those with antibiotic-associated colitis. It is a gram positive, spore forming, anaerobic rod-shaped bacterium which colonizes subjects through the fecal-oral route. Toxin-producing C. difficile may expand and cause infection in the colon, particularly in those persons with recent disruptions to their colonic flora by antimicrobial or chemotherapeutic exposure. It mediates its disease activity through toxin production, particularly through toxins A and B (though other unknown mechanisms of action/toxin actions are likely at play as well). These toxins are thought to have harmful effects on the colonic epithelium and induce deleterious secretory, inflammatory, and apoptotic processes. The toxins appear to disrupt cytoskeletal regulation eventually resulting in cell rounding in death. Toxin A mediates fluid secretion and an inflammatory response. Toxin B's mechanism of action is less well-understood but appears to be more potent than Toxin A. CDI can be distinguished by strain, particularly by ribotyping of the spacer region 16S and 23S rDNA. Notably, particular strains such as NAP1/027 have been associated with epidemic disease.
L61: Subjects suffering from infection may range from having mild diarrhea, to significant colitis leading to colectomy and, possibly, death. Symptoms are often associated with fever and leukocytosis. Occasionally, subjects may not have diarrhea but may instead present with a toxic megacolon, a potentially life threatening complication. Diagnosis of the disease is most often made with a combination of clinical suspicion (due to diarrhea in a hospitalized elderly patient, etc.) and a positive ELISA for Toxin A or B or both. These ELISA tests have variable sensitivity but are commonly used due to their ease of testing and cheap expense. Increasingly, PCR-based tests that assess for the toxin-producing genes are also being used.
L62: The initial infection episode is often successfully managed by fluid replacement, discontinuation of the offending agent such as an antimicrobial (if possible), and oral/IV antimicrobial therapy with oral metronidazole, vancomycin, or fidaxomicin (and occasionally IV metronidazole or rectally delivered vancomycin or metronidazole). Second-line agents used very infrequently include nitazoxanide and rifamycin as well as cytotoxin binding agents such as cholestyramine or colestipol. However, a significant proportion (estimates are wide-ranging from a quarter to almost half of patients) of treated subjects will experience a recurrence of CDI, including elderly subjects, subjects with previous episodes of diarrhea or underlying morbidity, and subjects infected with particular strains. There are no approved therapies for the treatment of recurrences.
L63: 2.2. Analysis of Current Treatment Options
L64: Though treatment of an initial episode of CDI is relatively straightforward, generally involving 10-14 day courses of oral vancomycin, oral fidaxomicin, or oral metronidazole (sometimes combined with intravenous metronidazole in severe cases), there is no approved or well-studied definitive treatment for prevention of recurrences. As to the treatment of recurrent episodes of CDI, a repeat of the initial regimen is commonly given and is often successful for the first recurrence. In cases of repeat recurrences, prolonged/tapering oral vancomycin regimens, pulsed oral vancomycin therapy, administration of immunoglobulins (particularly in cases of hypogammaglobulinemia), probiotic administration and fecal transplant have all been advocated. While fecal transplant also shows promise for prevention of recurrences, this therapy has not been rigorously studied or received regulatory approval. Also, its production has yet to be streamlined.

L68: 3.2. Summary of Presubmission/Submission Regulatory Activity
L69: The initial investigational new drug application (IND) for human monoclonal antibodies against C. difficile toxin A and toxin B was submitted on November 25, 2005. Early in product development, the applicant hypothesized that an anti-toxin A antibody or the combination of anti-toxin A and B antibodies would be the most effective in prevention of CDI recurrence.
L70: The first Phase 2 trial evaluated the efficacy and safety of a single infusion of an anti-toxin A antibody versus placebo. The trial was conducted in 2005-2006 and was terminated early after enrolling 46 subjects because emerging non-clinical data suggested that the combination of antibodies against toxin A and B would be more effective for the prevention of CDI recurrence.
L71: A Phase 2 study comparing a combination of C. difficile toxin A and B antibodies with placebo in patients receiving antibacterial therapy for CDI was conducted in 2006-2008. The study enrolled 101 patients in the combination arm and 99 patients in the placebo arm. The recurrence rate was lower among patients treated with the combination therapy (7% vs. 25%; p &lt; 0.001). No Phase 2 studies evaluated bezlotoxumab alone.

L80: On December 21, 2010 a Special Protocol Assessment (SPA) agreement was reached on the design of study P001, a Phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, adaptive-design study of the efficacy in the prevention of CDI recurrence of a single infusion of human monoclonal antibody to C. difficile toxin A, human monoclonal antibody to C. difficile toxin B, and the combination of human monoclonal antibodies to C. difficile toxin A and toxin B in patients receiving antibacterial therapy for C. difficile infection. The primary endpoint in the protocol was CDI recurrence following clinical cure of baseline CDI episode. The proportion was calculated as the number of subjects with CDI recurrence divided by the total number of subjects in the analysis population regardless of whether or not they had a successful resolution of the initial episode of CDI.
L81: However, upon review of Protocol P002 FDA recommended changing the primary endpoint from CDI recurrence to global cure. The reason for the recommendation was the concern that in the calculation of the CDI recurrence rate, subjects who failed treatment of the initial CDI episode would be counted as not having a recurrence. For this binary endpoint, a successful outcome would group those who were initially cured and did not have a recurrence with those who failed initial treatment. Subsequently a greater number of initial cure failures in a treatment group would result in a smaller number of subjects who could potentially develop recurrence. Therefore, an imbalance between treatment groups in the number of subjects who failed treatment of the initial CDI episode could confound the assessment of recurrence rates. This would be most concerning if the monoclonal antibody arm had a lower initial cure rate than placebo. The endpoint of global cure rate avoids this problem by defining treatment success as clinical cure of the initial CDI episode AND the absence of CDI recurrence. In August 2012 FDA conveyed their recommendations to the applicant.
L82: The applicant disagreed with changing the primary endpoint. Global Cure remained a key secondary endpoint in both trials. FDA acknowledged that the Phase 3 trials would preserve the use of CDI recurrence rate as the primary efficacy analysis but noted that interpretation of the results of these trials would depend on the number of subjects with clinical cure of the initial infection.
L83: In November 2015 the applicant submitted a BLA with the results of two Phase 3 pivotal trials to support approval of bezlotoxumab for the prevention of C. difficile infection recurrence.

L106: 4.4. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology
L107: Single and repeat intermittent-dose toxicology studies in mice with IV bezlotoxumab administered for 14 days (5 doses/3 day interval) and 21 days (2 doses/14 day interval) were conducted. L110: 4.5.1. Mechanism of Action
L111: MK-6072 binds to toxin B secreted by toxin producing C. difficile during acute infection. Theoretically, such binding might limit further epithelial and inflammatory damage incited by the toxin, however exactly how binding of the toxin during initial infection prevents recurrence is unclear.
L112: 4.5.2. Pharmacodynamics
L113: As bezlotoxumab is a monoclonal antibody to an exogenous toxin, it has no intrinsic antimicrobial activity. It binds toxin B with an affinity equilibrium dissociation constant (K_d) of &lt;1 \times 10^{-9} M. In Vero cells 200 mcg/mL of bezlotoxumab could block binding of 50 ng/mL toxin B. Similarly, in Caco-2 cells the addition of bezlotoxumab (in concentrations 0-100mcg/ml) resulted in a dose-dependent neutralizing effect of 100ng/ml of toxin B as measured by transepithelial resistance.

L167: Overview and Objective
L168: P001: The purpose of the MODIFY I trial was to determine whether the infusion of the combined monoclonal antibodies to C. difficile toxins A and B (MK-3415A) in conjunction with standard of care was superior to the infusion of monoclonal antibody to toxin A only (MK-3415) plus standard of care , the infusion of monoclonal antibody to toxin B only (MK-6072) plus standard of care , and placebo plus standard of care in preventing the recurrence of C. difficile infection over a period of 12 weeks. The trial was also designed to potentially show the superiority of MK-3415 plus standard of care or MK-6072 plus standard of care over placebo plus standard of care in preventing recurrence.
L169: P002: Similar to that of P001 except that there was no antitoxin A alone arm (MK-3415).
L170: Trial Design
L171: P001
L172: This was a randomized, double-blind, placebo controlled, multicenter, adaptive design study evaluating the recurrence of C. difficile infection (CDI) in subjects receiving a single intravenous infusion of monoclonal antibodies to both toxin A and toxin B, individual monoclonal antibodies to toxin A or toxin B alone, or placebo along with standard of care antimicrobial therapy . The study was designed to evaluate recurrence over a 12 week period post infusion. The trial involved 184 sites worldwide including sites in North and South America, Europe, Africa, and Australia; there were 75 sites in the United States. 1600 subjects were planned for enrollment.
L173: Appropriately, the study was placebo controlled/blinded as there is no currently approved drug to prevent recurrence of C. difficile infection (CDI). The study was also designed to meet the combination rule (in this case demonstrating the superiority of the combination antibody arm over the individual antibody arms in preventing recurrence). The follow up time of 12 weeks would be expected to capture a significant portion of recurrences though CDI recurrences have been known to occur after even longer intervals. All subjects received standard of care antimicrobial therapy, which appropriately mimicked the likely adjunct use of the study drug that would occur in a clinical setting.
L174: A subject could be included in the trial if he/she had CDI as defined by 3 or more loose stools in the prior 24 hours and a positive stool test for toxigenic C. difficile in the seven days prior to infusion. This is similar to the diagnostic criteria used in the recent approval for fidaxomicin, although in those studies the toxin test had to have been positive in the previous 48 hours.
L175: The inclusion and exclusion criteria were appropriately narrow. Elderly subjects and subjects with significant illness (such as renal or hepatic dysfunction) were not necessarily excluded however there were some exclusions based on the use of concomitant medications that might

Thus, after the interim analysis randomization could have occurred into one of two or three arms. Subjects were stratified by SOC and by inpatient vs outpatient status

L188: The blinding in this study was generally adequate. This is important as some the study endpoints were somewhat subjective and could be influenced by poor study blinding. For example, recurrence was based on loose stool values recorded in a subject's daily stool log; these loose stool assessments were to be based on the Bristol Stool chart. All subjects, study investigators, study site personnel, and the sponsor were to be blinded until the study was completed and data had been screened for completeness and accuracy.

L507: Durability of Response
L508: As has already been discussed, both studies P001 and P002 assessed for recurrences over a period of 12 weeks. However, in P002, a subset of 300 subjects were followed for an additional nine months (the extension cohort). 293 of these subjects were a part of the FAS (112 subjects in the MK-3415A arm, 99 in the MK-6072 arm, and 82 in the placebo arm). When looking at global cures over the 12 month period in the extension cohort, the MK-6072 arm did considerably better than placebo (69.7% global cures vs. 40.2%, respectively). However, the extension cohort appeared to differ significantly from the full P002 FAS. In fact the rate of recurrence over the 12 week study period in the full FAS set in the placebo arm was 25.7% while it was 41.5% over the same period for the placebo arm of the extension cohort (using the sponsor definition for recurrence). Thus, it is difficult to draw any real conclusions regarding durability given the atypical characteristics of the extension cohort.

Currently there is no approved therapy to treat the recurrence of CDI. Instead, clinicians have applied a heterogeneous group of unapproved therapies to treat recurrences. L542: Secondly, it is important to consider how clinicians might use any therapy directed toward preventing recurrence. In an ideal scenario, any adjunct therapy to prevent recurrence would be given at the time of/right after initial clinical cure. This would spare the clinician and patient the risk and cost associated with administering the adjunct therapy early in the course of the initial CDAD episode. Such risk would include administering adjunct therapy to a subject that was never cured initially (in which case preventing recurrence becomes moot) or administering therapy that might somehow actually interfere with the SOC treatment of the initial episode.
L543: Bearing the above considerations in mind, one can now evaluate the data submitted in this application.
L544: In regards to recurrences, no matter what definitions of recurrence or clinical cure was used (the original applicant definitions of recurrence and cure, the reviewer expanded definition of recurrence, or the applicant expanded definition of clinical cure), bezlotoxumab appeared to either have a statistically significant effect or a trend toward lessening recurrences.
L545: Using the reviewer expanded definition of recurrence that captures subjects not tested for toxin upon recurrence of diarrhea as well as those who used therapies that could essentially treat CDI, MK-6072 appears to perform well relative to placebo in P001 and marginally better in study P002. In P001, MK-6072 had an absolute reduction in recurrence rate of 13.6%. Moreover, MK-6072 appeared to do better than placebo in preventing recurrence in almost all subgroups tested. In study P002, MK-6072 had absolute reduction in recurrence rate of 5.3%. Though this reduction was modest, the MK-6072 arm had lower recurrence rates in almost every subgroup (except, importantly, in males).
L546: It is interesting to note that in P001 administration of SOC for \leq 3 days before study drug infusion was associated with “less” favorable outcomes, and in P002, administration of SOC for 5-6 days before study drug infusion was associated with “more” favorable outcomes. Whether this is reflective of an overall improved activity of MK-6072 on recurrence when given later in the initial course of SOC is unclear, particularly given that the opposite effect was seen in subjects who had received &gt;6 days of SOC before receiving study drug infusion (though the sample size in this subgroup is quite small). It should be noted that the original recurrence definition did not distinguish between those requiring treatment and those who did not. However, there appeared to be a trend toward less need for treatment in the bezlotoxumab arm; this was in conjunction with other exploratory analyses that seemed to suggest some decreased severity of recurrence with bezlotoxumab. Overall, there appears to be a modest to moderate effect of MK-6072 on recurrence.

L556: 8 Review of Safety
L557: 8.1. Safety Review Approach
L558: This safety review focuses on data from the safety population enrolled in the two Phase 3 randomized, placebo-controlled trials, study P001 and P002, supporting this application. These two studies evaluated the efficacy, safety and tolerability of a single infusion of bezlotoxumab (bezo) alone and in combination with actoxumab (acto) for prevention of Clostridium difficile infection (CDI) in patients receiving standard of care antibacterial therapy (oral vancomycin, fidaxomicin, oral or intravenous metronidazole). Both bezlo and acto are fully human monoclonal antibodies: bezlo is an anti- type B Clostridium difficile toxin antibody and acto is an anti-type A Clostridium difficile toxin antibody.
L559: The two studies are similar in design with the following exceptions: the first study, study P001, followed an adaptive design. It also had four arms study arms: subjects that received acto infusion only (acto arm), subjects that received bezlo infusion alone (bezo arm), subjects that received combination of acto and bezlo infusions (acto+bezo arm), and subjects that received normal saline infusion (placebo arm). In addition, study P001 had a planned interim analysis at 40% enrollment to evaluate each study arm and to drop one of the single therapy arms if it did not demonstrate efficacy more than the combination arm. Based on the interim analysis, which showed worsening outcome in the acto arm as compared to all three arms, the acto arm was dropped for the post-interim analysis study period. Study P002 did not have planned interim analysis and was designed with only 3 arms: bezlo arm, acto+bezo arm and placebo arm. In addition, study P002 had an extended safety cohort of 300 participants that were followed for additional 9 months after the 12-week study period. Given the similarity in study design, including adverse event (AE) reporting period, pooled data from study P001 and P002 are presented.
L560: The safety population included subjects in Study P001 and P002 that received partial or full infusion of bezlo, acto+bezo or placebo. Of note, subjects that received acto alone were excluded from the safety population. However, data from the acto arm is presented separately for completeness of the review since the safety population includes subjects that received the combined acto+bezo infusion. Also, data from a phase 2 study that compared the combination of acto and bezlo with placebo, study P017, is not pooled with the phase 3 studies mentioned above due to difference in AE reporting time frame The safety data for this study is presented separately.
L561: Since bezlo is a monoclonal antibody, special attention was given to adverse events related to immunogenicity, and infusion-related adverse reactions.
L562: For the integrated safety analysis, MAED version 18.0, JMP version 12.0 and JReview 9.2 analytic software were utilized.

L1019: 8.8.2. Human Reproduction and Pregnancy
L1020: There was one pregnancy reported in study P002, and the narrative is included below. It occurred in a 23 year old, multi-racial (African American and white) subject with a negative result on a serum pregnancy test prior to receiving infusion with acto+bezlo. On day 10, she presented to an emergency room with dizziness, abdominal cramping, nausea, and occasional retching. She was discharged from the hospital with a diagnosis of moderate, persistent CDI. She subsequently had positive urine and a confirmatory serum pregnancy test. On day 258, the subject had a spontaneous, live, vaginal birth of a healthy, female baby with normal anthropometric measurements.

L1029: 8.9.2. Expectations on Safety in the Postmarket Setting
L1030: Since the mechanism of action for this monoclonal antibody is not fully elucidated, it is difficult to anticipate all the potential off-target effects. Hence, postmarketing pharmacovigilance is recommended.
L1031: In addition, there are only 29 subjects that received more than one dose of bezlo in a Phase 1 study of healthy volunteers. In the post-marketing setting, it is possible that patients may receive more than one dose of bezlo, especially patients who attend multiple health care facilities.

L1035: 8.11. Integrated Assessment of Safety
L1036: Summary
L1037: Bezlotoxumab, given as a single IV infusion, had an overall favorable safety profile with the rates of treatment emergent adverse events (TEAE), serious adverse events (SAE) and deaths similar to placebo. No increase in adverse events (AEs) related to potential immune-mediated reactions was observed in the bezlotoxumab- treated subjects as compared to placebo- treated subjects. A safety finding identified during the review of data from the two Phase 3 trials is the numerically higher numbers of serious adverse events and deaths in bezlotoxumab-treated subjects with baseline congestive heart failure as compared to placebo.
L1038: Discussion
L1039: There was adequate number of subjects exposed to bezlo at the proposed dose during the drug development program. There were 1790 subjects exposed to bezlo either alone or in combination with acto; 126 were healthy subjects in Phase 1 studies (30 subjects received bezlo alone and 96 received acto + bezlo), and 1664 were subjects in Phase 2 and 3 (786 received bezlotoxumab alone and 878 received acto+bezlo). Of these, 1741 subjects were exposed to bezlo dose at 10mg/kg. With the exception of subjects in study P004 who received two doses of acto+bezlo (n=30), all subjects were exposed to a single dose of bezlo or acto+bezlo.
L1040: The safety population is comprised of all subjects in the two Phase 3 studies, studies P001 and P002), that received partial or full infusion of the study medication. For the safety analysis, subjects were grouped in the treatment arm they received (as treated), as opposed to the group they were randomized to. Throughout the two pivotal studies, P001 and P002, there were 212 deaths. Among these, 166 occurred in the safety population because of AEs reported during the 12-week study period. There was no significant difference in the mortality rate of the bezlo (7.1%) and acto+bezlo (6.6%) arm as compared to placebo (7.1%).
L1041: There is no major imbalance in the frequency of serious adverse events among the three arms: 29.4%, 27.3% and 32.7% of subjects in the bezlo, acto+bezlo and placebo arm, respectively, experienced at least one SAE in the 12-week study period. In all three arms, the infection, infestation, and gastrointestinal MedDRA System Organ Classes accounted for the majority of the SAEs. A detailed look of the SAEs revealed cardiac failure, urinary tract infection, diarrhea, abdominal pain to have occurred more frequently in the bezlo arm as compared to the placebo arm. From these, cardiac failure showed the largest risk difference, with 17 subjects (2.2%) in the bezlo arm as compared to placebo (n=7, 0.009%). Among subjects with baseline diagnosis of congestive heart failure, more subjects in the bezlo died (23/119 vs. 13/104).

The subgroup analysis of adverse events by age, race, sex and body weight did not reveal significant differences by the abovementioned variables.
