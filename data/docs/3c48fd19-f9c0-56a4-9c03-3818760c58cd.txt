L6: ## 1 Introduction
L7: From the clinical safety review, there was an observed imbalance in mortality from Phase 3 Trial AACI in BLA 761248. Given the complexities in the design and conduct of Trial AACI, DBVII was consulted to provide further analytical assessment of mortality in the donanemab development program. A statistical memorandum was signed into DARRTS on March 4, 2024. Subsequent to the memorandum, an information request was sent to the Sponsor to provide an updated analysis of mortality and obtain updated data on mortality and study discontinuation. This addendum is to update the statistical memorandum based on the responses and data submitted (SDN165, SDN161) to this information request.
L8: Also of note is that the sponsor submitted additional vital status information (SDN 168, 169) for the cohorts who were lost to follow-up to respond to an informal request by the clinical team on February 27, 2024. Data from these participants is not included in the comparative analyses, but summarized in Section 4.3.
L9: The tables and figures in this document are numbered to match with the original memorandum. For example, Table 1A is to update Table 1 in the original memorandum.
L10: ## 2 Approach to Analysis
L11: The methods for analyzing mortality rely on an on-study approach to analysis that includes all deaths that occur by week 76 (or week 76 + 57 days) regardless of discontinuing randomized treatment – see original memorandum for detailed descriptions of the analysis approach. This addendum includes an additional sensitivity analysis to further assess the impact of missing vital status information on the assessment of mortality risk. The sensitivity analysis was conducted by assuming each of those participants who discontinued Trial AACI due to severe adverse events to be dead at random time point
L12: after the discontinuation but within the 76 week visit. The sensitivity analysis is provided in Section 5 of this addendum.

L49: ## 4.3 Additional Observed Deaths in Trial AACI
L50: The on-study analysis of mortality at 76 weeks, was based on including any death that occurred prior to or at week 76 regardless of discontinuation of randomized treatment. There were several other notable deaths observed in Trial AACI summarized below (see also Table A.1A in the Appendix). These deaths were not included in the 76 week mortality analysis results in Table 5A.
L51: - Donanemab additional deathsSubject (b) (6) died 4 days after week 76 with reason listed as "Death."
- Subject (b) (6) died due to cerebrovascular accident 52 days after the week 76 visit with a fatal adverse event recorded prior to week 76.
L52: - Placebo additional deathsSubject (b)(6) died 2 days after week 76 of a malignant lung neoplasm with a fatal adverse event recorded prior to week 76.
L53: Responding to an informal discussion with the clinical review team on February 27, 2024, the sponsor established a process to retrieve vital status information from for the cohort of subjects who discontinued from the trial due to a reason of lost to follow-up. From the original memorandum, this represents 22 of 291 subjects that discontinued from the trial prior to week 76. As of April 23, 2024, the sponsor was able to retrieve vital status information from 18 of the 22 participants that were lost to follow up (SDN 168, 169). Of these 18 participants, four were identified to have died (2 on donanemab and 2 on placebo). Three of the deaths occurred after what would have been the week 76 visit, and one death on Placebo (ID (b)(6)) would have occurred prior to the week 76 visit. Causes of death and electronic data on these participants has not been submitted.
L54: ## 5. Sensitivity Analysis on Missing Mortality Data
L55: Given that there is high percentage of missing vital status information at week 76 (26% for donanemab and 20% for placebo), a sensitivity analysis was conducted to assess the impact of missing data on the risk assessment. Among the participants who discontinued before week 76, there are 26 participants discontinued due to severe adverse events (16 in donanumab arm and 10 in placebo arm). Assuming each of these participants died within the 76 weeks at random time after the discontinuation, the risk differences and 95% confidence intervals can be re-calculated. Table A.2A in the Appendix presents the re-calculated risk differences and 95% confidence intervals, and it shows that the lower bound of 95% confidence intervals could go beyond zero only if there were two or three more additional death in the denonemab arm than the placebo arm among these 26 participants who discontinued due to severe adverse events.
L56: ## 6. Summary and Conclusions
L57: The assessment of mortality, primarily based on the placebo-controlled portion of Trial AACI, resulted in an estimated risk difference of 1.0% for death at week 76 with 95% CI of (-0.3%, 2.3%). This means that if 1000 patients from the population were treated with donanemab rather than placebo for 76 weeks, 10 additional patients would be expected to die. Estimates for mortality at 76 weeks + 57 days resulted in slightly increased estimate of the RD (1.1%). An integrated analysis that incorporates 76 week data from the Phase 2 trial, Trial AACG, resulted in similar findings: IRD = 0.61 with 95% CI (-0.21, 1.44).
L58: The main conclusions and recommendations remain the same. Overall, the mortality analyses identified a potential safety signal of an increased risk of death - the point estimate from the analysis of Trial AACI suggests an increase of 1.0% in the risk of death on donanemab vs. placebo with the upper 95% CI bound failing to rule out differences as large as 2.3%. In addition, the conduct of Trial AACI further adds uncertainty to the observed mortality effect sizes and there is some evidence to suggest that additional
L59: vital status information from those that discontinued from the study may result in mortality effect sizes greater than those observed thereby suggesting increased levels of harm with donanemab relative to placebo. It's important that such uncertainty be incorporated into the benefit-risk assessment of donanemab along with the observed increased estimates of mortality risk of donanemab relative to placebo.
L60: Based on information submitted in SDN 168 and 169, the sponsor was able to retrieve 18 out the 22 participants that discontinued from the trial early due to lost to follow-up within less than 2 months from the informal request. This informed that the sponsor has a process to retrieve vital status information and an information request was sent on April 24, 2024 to request the vital status for all the participants that discontinued Trial AACI early. This information once available would help reduce uncertainties around the mortality findings.

L96: ## 1 Introduction
L97: From the clinical safety review, there was an observed imbalance in mortality from Phase 3 Trial AACI in BLA 761248. Given the complexities in the design and conduct of Trial AACI, DBVII was consulted to provide further analytical assessment of mortality in the donanemab development program.
L98: This memorandum is focused solely on the assessment of mortality in BLA 761248. It describes the approach taken to analyze mortality, provides findings from the analysis and provides some concluding comments on the observed findings.
L99: ## 2 Approach to Analysis
L100: The following details document the approach used in the assessment of mortality. Note that additional descriptive analyses are also provided in Section 3 to assess the extent of follow-up in Trial AACI and provide descriptive analyses to assess any potential differences in participants that completed the trial vs those that discontinued early1.
L101: The comparison of interest is donanemab to placebo, based on the treatment actually received in the clinical trial. Analyses will be based on the safety analysis population, defined as all subjects randomized to investigational product (IP) that received at least one dose of IP.
L102: 1 Descriptive analyses are based on data submitted in the original resubmission (SN104).
L103: The mortality assessment will be based on:
L104: - The placebo-controlled period of Trial AACI using data generated during the following two time points2:76 weeks which is the end of the double-blind period of Trial AACI
- 76 weeks + 57 days which includes 57 days of follow-up after the double-blind extension period3.
- An integrated analysis of Trial AACI and Phase 2 Trial AACG using the 76 week double-blind treatment period.
L105: To address the intercurrent event of treatment discontinuation, analyses will be based on an on-study approach. Such an approach considers participants at risk of death while exposed to randomized IP or not exposed to randomized IP but still being followed for vital status.
L106: The within arm summary measure will be based on the cumulative incidence of death at 76 weeks4. Cumulative incidence at week 76 will be estimated using Kaplan-Meier methods. Following the on-study approach to analysis the participant is censored at the time of study completion or last contact date5. Study-size adjusted estimates will be provided for integrated analyses.
L107: Comparisons of donanemab to placebo will be based on the absolute risk difference (RD) at 76 weeks as well as using a hazard ratio based on a Cox PH model without covariate adjustment. Comparisons will include an estimate of the treatment effect along with a 95% confidence interval. Integrated analyses will be based on incidence rates at 76 weeks and comparisons will be based on incidence rate differences (IRD). Pooled IRD will be calculated using Mantel-Haenszel methods.

To further inquire about this, prior information requests from the review team requested additional information about vital status follow-up from the sponsor. In response6, the sponsor stated, "vital status information is not available for the vast majority of participants who discontinued or were lost to follow-up in Study AACI."

L169: ## 5. Summary and Conclusions
L170: The assessment of mortality, primarily based on the placebo-controlled portion of Trial AACI, resulted in an estimated risk difference of 1.0% for death at week 76 with 95% CI of (-0.3%, 2.3%). This means that if 1000 patients from the population were treated with donanemab rather than placebo for 76 weeks, 10 additional patients would be expected to die. Estimates for mortality at 76 weeks + 57 days resulted in slightly increased estimate of the RD (1.1%). An integrated analysis that incorporates 76 week data from the Phase 2 trial, Trial AACG, resulted in similar findings: IRD = 0.59 with 95% CI (-0.22, 1.41).
L171: The mortality assessment utilized an on-study approach to address the intercurrent event of treatment discontinuation. Such an approach considers participants at risk of death while exposed to randomized IP or not exposed to randomized IP but still being followed for vital status through trial end (i.e. 76 weeks or 76 weeks + 57 days). However, the conduct of Trial AACI, resulted in high trial discontinuation rates of 26% for donanemab and 20% for placebo and the sponsor did not make efforts to obtain vital status for subjects discontinuing from the trial.
L172: Given the high percentage of subjects missing vital status information, the estimated effects from the mortality analysis would require the assumption that missing (i.e. censoring) is non-informative meaning that the reason a participant discontinues from the trial is unrelated to death. Based on the descriptive analyses shown in Section 3, such an assumption is likely implausible, especially with the high rate of study discontinuation and the reason for discontinuation being documented as due to an adverse event; both of which occur more frequently on donanemab than placebo.
L173: Overall, the mortality analyses identified a potential safety signal of an increased risk of death - the point estimate from the analysis of Trial AACI suggests an increase of 1.0% in the risk of death on donanemab vs. placebo with the upper 95% CI bound failing to rule out differences as large as 2.3%. Note the trial was not powered to assess all-cause mortality and importantly, not powered to assess any cause-specific death. In addition, the conduct of Trial AACI further adds uncertainty to the observed mortality effect sizes and there is some evidence to suggest that additional vital status information from those that discontinued from the study may result in mortality effect sizes greater than those observed thereby suggesting increased levels of harm with donanemab relative to placebo. It's important that such uncertainty be incorporated into the benefit-risk assessment of donanemab along with the observed increased estimates of mortality risk of donanemab relative to placebo.
L174: The following are some considerations on additional work beyond what is provided in this memo to further support the benefit-risk assessment of donanemab.
L175: - Additional analyses are unlikely to further elucidate the impact of missing vital status information on the observed mortality findings. A concerted effort on behalf of the sponsor to recover vital status information, assuming such an effort is ethically/legally viable and can retrieve a high percentage of vital status information, would result in a more reliable estimate of the effect of donanemab on mortality.
- As the benefit-risk assessment of donanemab involves consideration of the therapeutic context, including the benefit-risk profile of available therapies, it is of interest to understand the safety findings, including mortality results, for other approved drugs (e.g., lecanemab) to understand if the observed mortality effect is unique to donanemab.

L212: ## 1 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L213: Kisunla (donanemab-azbt) is an antibody directed at amyloid beta present only in brain amyloid plaques and intended for the treatment of Alzheimer's disease (AD). On June 12, 2023, Sponsor resubmitted the application to contain safety and efficacy data from the Phase 3 study AACI to address a complete response letter and apply for traditional approval.
L214: Study AACI was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study of participants with early symptomatic AD. Participants who met entry criteria were randomized 1:1 to donanemab or a matching placebo. The primary objective was to test the hypothesis that donanemab slowed the cognitive or functional decline of AD as measured by iADRS (integrated Alzheimer's Disease Rating Scale) compared to placebo in the population of participants with intermediate tau pathology at baseline and the overall population. The Division had not agreed to iADRS as the primary endpoint and had advised instead CDR-SB (Clinical Dementia Rating Scale – Sum of Boxes) as one of the alternatives. A natural cubic spline analysis with 2 degrees of freedom was pre-specified to assess the difference between treatment groups.
L215: Study AACI showed that the donanemab arm compared to the placebo arm had statistically significantly less decline at Week 76 as measured by iADRS: intermediate tau population (3.3; 95% CI 1.9, 4.6; p &lt; 0.001); overall population (2.9; 95% CI 1.5, 4.3; p &lt; 0.001). Compared to the range of the scale (0 to 141), the treatment difference of approximately 3 points was small but statistically significant. The donanemab arm compared to the placebo arm had statistically significantly less decline at Week 76 as measured by the secondary endpoint CDR-SB: intermediate tau population (-0.7; 95% CI -1.0, -0.4; p &lt; 0.001); overall population (-0.7; 95% CI -1.0, -0.5; p &lt; 0.001). The treatment effect was also small (CDR-SB ranged 0 to 18) but statistically significant.
L216: Sensitivity analyses of iADRS using mixed model with repeated measures, a model with less assumptions and more commonly used as the primary analysis of longitudinal AD data, produced a statistically significant treatment effect. A non-parametric rank analysis of covariance without imposition of parametric model assumption also produced a statistically significant treatment effect.
L217: Analysis of iADRS and CDR-SB with the unfavorable outcome of death imputed as the worst score produced a similar and statistically significant treatment effect compared to its primary analysis. However, the analysis with death imputed as worst score for ADAS-Cog13 (Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale – Cognitive subscale) change from baseline at Week 76 and time to substantial decline in CDR-G (CDR global score) did not achieve statistical significance when compared to p-value cutoffs that were multiplicity controlled.
L218: Study discontinuation was high with the donanemab arm having a higher rate compared to the placebo arm (27% vs. 20%). The tipping point analysis to assess sensitivity of the CDR-SB primary analysis to missing data assumptions showed that the results in both the intermediate tau and overall population were not statistically significant near the delta pair (0, 0.5), where 0.5 is the minimum within-subject scale increment change.
L219: Despite the potential for the primary analysis of CDR-SB to be sensitive to missing data assumptions, study AACI demonstrated evidence of effectiveness. The Phase 2 Study AACG provided confirmatory evidence of effectiveness with the primary endpoint of iADRS (3.2; 95% CI 0.1, 6.3; p-value = 0.04). Overall, we recommend approval of donanemab.
L220: For future studies of AD, we recommend that the Division provide specific protocol recommendations for sponsors to proactively follow up study participants to the end of the trial to minimize missing data. High missing data rates can result in efficacy and safety estimates that are unreliable for decision making. Additionally, pre-specifying a clinically relevant cutoff for a tipping point analysis would allow for a structured assessment of plausible missing data assumptions.
L221: ## 2 INTRODUCTION
L222: Kisunla (donanemab-azbt) is an antibody directed at the pyroglutamate modification of the third amino acid of amyloid beta epitope that is present only in brain amyloid plaques. Donanemab binds to deposited amyloid plaque and aids its removal through microglial-mediated phagocytosis. Accumulation of amyloid plaque has been proposed to be the primary driver of the Alzheimer's disease (AD) process. Other anti-amyloid beta-directed antibodies included aducanumab and lecanemab that received accelerated approval for the treatment of AD (specifically for patients with mild cognitive impairment or mild dementia stage of disease) based on the observed reduction of amyloid beta, a surrogate endpoint. Lecanemab received full approval on July 6, 2023, based on significantly less decline as measured by Clinical Dementia Rating Scale – Sum of Boxes (CDR-SB).
L223: Sponsor applied for accelerated approval for donanemab on May 18, 2022, based on evidence of amyloid plaque reduction from the Phase 2 study AACG. The Division issued a complete response letter (CRL), dated January 18, 2023, because the safety database was insufficient to adequately characterize the long-term safety of donanemab in the treatment of AD. On June 12, 2023, Sponsor resubmitted the application to contain safety and efficacy data from the Phase 3 study AACI to address the CRL and apply for traditional approval.
L224: While study AACI was being conducted, the Division disagreed with Sponsor's proposal to change the primary endpoint from CDR-SB to the integrated Alzheimer's Disease Rating Scale (iADRS), because there was uncertainty that iADRS could assess the effects of treatment on both cognition and function, independent of each other1. The Division advised the use of CDR-SB as the primary efficacy endpoint or the 13-item Alzheimer's Disease Assessment Scale – Cognitive (ADAS-Cog13) and the Alzheimer's Disease Cooperative Study – Instrumental Activities of Daily Living Scale (ADCS-iADL) as co-primary efficacy measures. However, Sponsor proceeded with iADRS as the primary endpoint based on the assertions that iADRS showed more consistent effects across trials, more reliable within-subject change, a smaller study sample size, shorter trial duration, and lower risk of a failed study.
L225: 1 March 2, 2021, Type C Meeting Minutes. Available in DAARTS.
L226: Additionally, there were multiple exchanges between Sponsor and the Division on the choice of primary statistical analysis model for study AACI. MMRM had typically been used in AD studies, including study AACG, as the primary analysis model. However, for study AACI, Sponsor proposed natural cubic splines with 2 degrees of freedom (NCS2) based on analyses that showed NCS2 attaining higher statistical power compared to MMRM. After Sponsor responded to the Division's concerns about bias and Type I error inflation2, the Division agreed to the NCS2 model as the primary analysis model.

L246: ## Overall Design
L247: Study AACI is a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, Phase 3 study of participants with early symptomatic AD. Participants who met entry criteria were randomized 1:1 to the following treatment groups:
L248: - Donanemab: 700 mg administered by intravenous (IV) infusion once every four weeks (Q4W) for the first 3 doses, then 1400 mg IV Q4W

These dose reduction rules were defined as: amyloid plaque level was &lt;11 centiloid (CL) at any single amyloid PET scan or 11 \le CL &lt; 25 from two consecutive amyloid PET scans.

L294: ## 3.2.2 Statistical Methodologies
L295: ## 3.2.2.1 Primary Analysis of iADRS
L296: The primary objective was to test the hypothesis that donanemab slowed the cognitive or functional decline of AD as measured by the iADRS score compared with placebo in the population of participants with intermediate tau pathology at baseline and the overall population.
L297: A natural cubic spline analysis (see Appendix 6.1 Natural Cubic Spline) with 2 degrees of freedom (NCS2) was pre-specified to assess the difference between treatment groups in iADRS change from baseline at Week 76. For the NCS2 model, 3 knots over the observation time were placed: 2 at the boundaries (minimum and maximum observation time) and 1 internal knot at the median observation time. The baseline estimates were restricted to be the same for treatment and placebo groups. The model was estimated using restricted maximum likelihood method. The iADRS score at baseline and at each of the scheduled post-baseline visits were included in model as a dependent variable. Study visit was assumed continuous with values equal to weeks between baseline and post-baseline exam dates. NCS basis functions were constructed using visits in weeks. The model included the fixed effects: NCS basis expansion terms (two terms), NCS basis expansion term-by-treatment interaction (two terms), baseline age, concomitant acetylcholinesterase inhibitor (AchEI) and/or memantine use at baseline (yes/no), and pooled investigator6. Baseline tau category was included as a covariate in the model for the overall population. An unstructured variance-covariance structure matrix was assumed for the within-subject variance-covariance errors. If the unstructured variance-covariance structure matrix resulted in a lack of convergence, the following structures were used in sequence: heterogeneous Toeplitz covariance structure, heterogeneous autoregressive order 1 covariance structure, heterogeneous compound symmetry covariance structure, and compound symmetry covariance structure. The Kenward-Roger approximation was used to estimate the denominator degrees of freedom. The treatment group contrast in mean change from baseline at Week 76 with 95% CI was estimated using NCS2. NCS2 assumed missing data were missing at random. Measurements after death were treated as missing.
L298: Percent slowing compared to the placebo group was calculated as: the difference of LS estimates of mean change from baseline between treatment groups at Week 76 divided by the LS estimate of mean change from baseline value of the placebo group.
L299: ## Reviewer Comment:
L300: - NCS2 is a generalized least squares model of longitudinal data that treated time as a continuous variable in the mean response. NCS2 modeled the temporal mean trend with polynomials, specifically with cubic polynomials, that were spliced together at knot locations. In contrast, mixed model with repeated measures (MMRM) treated time as a categorical variable and did not assume a trend over time. The MMRM had typically been the primary analysis model of longitudinal data in AD studies. However, Sponsor proposed NCS2 as the primary analysis model on the basis that NCS2 attained higher statistical power compared to MMRM (see Section 2 INTRODUCTION for the regulatory
L301: 6 Investigator sites with &lt;2 subjects were pooled together based on enrollment country.
L302: background). MMRM analysis of iADRS became a sensitivity analysis (Section 3.2.2.3 Sensitivity Analyses).
L303: - Ignoring deaths, a negative outcome, could bias the results. Although the Division had advised Sponsor to account for death in the primary efficacy analysis7 prior to the BLA submission, Sponsor instead assumed death was not informative and measurements after death were treated as missing. Sponsor instead pre-specified a sensitivity analysis imputing death as the worst outcome in the NCS2 analysis of iADRS (Section 3.2.2.3 Sensitivity Analyses).

L320: ## 3.2.2.3 Sensitivity Analyses
L321: ## MMRM
L322: The MMRM analysis of iADRS was similar to the MMRM analysis specified for CDR-SB. See Section 3.2.2.2 Analysis of Select Clinical Endpoints.
L323: ## NCS with 3 degrees of freedom (NCS3)
L324: NCS with three degrees of freedom model (NCS3) was fitted to iADRS. This model assumed two internal knots at equidistant percentiles of the scheduled study visit times. The model had the same set of covariates as described for NCS2, with the exception that three (instead of two) basis functions were included in the model. Similar to NCS2, data were assumed to be missing at random. Measurements after death were treated as missing.
L325: With an additional internal knot, NCS3 can provide a more flexible fit to the data compared to NCS2. NCS3 had a lower Akaike information criterion, which indicated a better fit, but a higher Bayesian information criterion, which indicated a worse fit, when fitted to the Phase 2 study AACG data10. In a simulation study conducted by Sponsor11, NCS3 showed less bias compared to NCS2 under simulation scenarios using data generated with an MMRM model. FDA requested NCS3 analysis to allow for an assessment of model fit under a different spline assumption compared to NCS2.

L327: The occurrence of Amyloid Related Imaging Abnormality – Edema (ARIA-E) and infusion related reaction (IRR) might potentially lead to functional unblinding of the study treatment. For the sensitivity analysis, iADRS measurements were censored after the first occurrence of ARIA-E or IRR. NCS2 was fitted to this censored dataset. Measurements after death were treated as missing.

L336: ## Tipping Point Analyses
L337: During the BLA review, we requested Sponsor to conduct a tipping point analysis to explore the plausibility of missing data assumptions under which the conclusions change, i.e., under which there is no longer a statistically significant effect shown for the endpoints of iADRS and CDR-SB change from baseline at Week 76 in the intermediate tau and overall population. Data were imputed using the fitted model as was done for the rank ANCOVA (see Appendix Section 6.3 Rank ANCOVA). Decreases on the iADRS scale represented worsening, so a range of delta values from 0 to -5 was explored for donanemab participants in increments of 1. For participants on placebo, delta ranged from 0 to -2 in increments of 1. Increases on CDR-SB represented worsening, so a range of delta values from 0 to 3 in increments of 0.5 was used for donanemab participants and 0 to 1.5 in increments of 0.5 for placebo participants. Delta values were added to each subject's imputed change from baseline. For a subject with imputed data, the delta value was a worse shift compared to observed subjects. Then the rank ANCOVA was conducted on the modified data and Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test statistics were combined using Rubin's rule. A p-value was provided for each pair of delta values. The sensitivity of the primary analysis result would depend on the plausibility of the missing data assumptions.

L355: ## 3.2.2.7 Sample Size Calculations
L356: The power and sample size determination of the trial were based on the intermediate tau pathology population. L390: ## 3.2.4 Results and Conclusions
L391: ## 3.2.4.1 Primary Analysis of iADRS
L392: At Week 76, the donanemab arm compared to the placebo arm had statistically significantly less decline as measured by iADRS. The NCS2 difference in adjusted mean change from baseline of iADRS in the intermediate tau population (3.3; 95% CI 1.9, 4.6; p &lt; 0.001) and the overall population (2.9; 95% CI 1.5, 4.3; p &lt; 0.001) are shown in Table 4 and Figure 6.
L393: Of the randomized subjects in the overall population, 1599/1736 (92%) subjects had both a baseline and post-baseline iADRS score. By Week 76, 1236/1736 (71%) had both a baseline and Week 76 iADRS score. The donanemab arm had higher percentages of missing data at baseline and at Week 76. Measurements after death were treated as missing. See Section 3.2.4.3 Sensitivity Analyses for an evaluation of the impact of missing data and the outcome of death on the primary analysis results.
L394: Additional NCS2 model fit information is provided in Appendix Section 6.5 NCS2 Model Fit Estimates for covariate estimates and covariance matrix estimate and Appendix Section 6.4 NCS2 Model Diagnostic for a discussion of model diagnostics.
L395: **Reviewer Comment:** The iADRS score ranged from 0 to 141 with lower scores reflecting worse performance. Although the treatment difference of about 3 points was significant, it is small in magnitude. We defer to the Clinical Team's efficacy review on the interpretation of the clinical meaningfulness of the treatment difference.

L447: ## CDR-SB

L478: ## 5 SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
L479: ## 5.1 Statistical Issues
L480: A major statistical issue is the sensitivity of the primary analysis of CDR-SB change from baseline at Week 76 to missing data assumptions. Study discontinuation was high with the donanemab arm having a higher rate compared to the placebo arm (27% vs. 20%). During the BLA review, the Sponsor conducted a sensitivity analysis of CDR-SB to varying missing data assumptions using tipping point analysis (Sections 3.2.2.3 Sensitivity Analyses, Section 3.2.4.3.4 Tipping Point Analyses). The primary analysis of CDR-SB change from baseline at Week 76 became non-significant near the delta pair (0, 0.5) where the treatment difference between imputed subjects might still favor donanemab. Also, the tipping point occurred with the minimum within-subject scale increment of 0.5.
L481: Another issue was the handling of death, which resulted in missing data, in the primary analysis of clinical endpoints. Although Sponsor was advised to account for death in the primary efficacy analysis, Sponsor instead assumed death was not informative and treated measurements after death as being missing; thereby, ignoring the severity of death in a patient's disease trajectory. Sponsor instead pre-specified a sensitivity analysis of iADRS change from baseline at Week 76 that imputed measurements after death as the worst outcome. There was not a similar sensitivity analysis pre-specified for CDR-SB, ADAS-Cog13, ADCS-iADL, and time to substantial decline in CDR-G. Ignoring deaths, a negative outcome, could bias the primary analysis result for these endpoints. For ADAS-Cog13 change from baseline at Week 76, the estimates with death imputed as the worst score in the intermediate tau population (-1.0; 95% CI -2.2, 0.1; p = 0.07) and the overall population (-1.0; 95% CI -2.0, 0.0; p = 0.04) were not statistically significant compared to the alpha allocation of 0.04 and 0.01, respectively. Based on the graphical testing hierarchy, results for time to substantial decline in CDR-G by Week 76 would not be statistically significant if ADAS-Cog13 change from baseline at Week 76 were not significant.
L482: ## 5.2 Collective Evidence
L483: The collective evidence included the Phase 2 study AACG and Phase 3 study AACI, which was the focus of this review. The number of randomized subjects in AACI (1736 subjects) was larger compared to AACG (257 subjects). Another difference between the two studies was the study population: AACG comprised subjects with intermediate tau pathology; AACI comprised subjects with intermediate and high tau pathology. In AACG, donanemab subjects had statistically significantly less decline at Week 76 than placebo subjects as measured by iADRS (MMRM analysis 3.2; 95% CI 0.1, 6.3; p-value = 0.04), thus meeting the applicant's primary objective for the study. The secondary endpoints of CDR-SB, ADAS-cog, MMSE, and ADCS-iADL were not significant based on the pre-specified alpha allocation. For a complete review of study AACG, refer to Massie 202314.
L484: Both AACG and AACI studies showed statistically significantly less decline at Week 76 among donanemab subjects as measured by iADRS with similar point estimates around 3.0, with the
L485: 14 Massie, T. Statistical Review and Evaluation of Kisunla (Donanemab) - Biomarker Accelerated Approval. January 17, 2023. Available in DARRTS.
L486: estimate from AACI having more precision because of a larger sample size. The significance of CDR-SB and ADCS-iADL change from baseline at Week 76 in AACI were supportive of a treatment effect.
L487: ## 5.3 Conclusions and Recommendations
L488: Study AACI showed, based on the primary analysis, that the donanemab arm compared to the placebo arm had statistically significantly less decline at Week 76 as measured by iADRS. Compared to the range of the scale (0 to 141), the treatment difference of approximately 3 points was small relatively but significant. The treatment effect estimates for secondary endpoints of CDR-SB and ADCS-iADL change from baseline at Week 76 were small but also significant, providing support for a treatment effect with donanemab.
L489: Sensitivity analyses of iADRS using mixed model with repeated measures, a model with less assumptions and more commonly used as the primary analysis of longitudinal AD data, produced a significant treatment effect. A non-parametric rank analysis of covariance without imposition of parametric model assumption also produced a significant treatment effect. Sensitivity analysis of iADRS with death imputed as the worst score produced a similar treatment effect that was also significant. Study discontinuation was high with the donanemab arm having a higher rate compared to the placebo arm (27% vs. 20%). Tipping point analysis to assess the sensitivity of the primary analysis for CDR-SB to missing data assumptions showed that the results for both the intermediate tau and overall population became non-significant near the delta pair (0, 0.5), where 0.5 is the minimum within-subject scale increment change.
L490: Despite the potential for the primary analysis of CDR-SB to be sensitive to missing data assumptions, study AACI demonstrated evidence of effectiveness. Study AACG provided confirmatory evidence of effectiveness. We recommend approval of donanemab.
L491: For future studies of AD, we recommend that the Division provide specific protocol recommendations for sponsors to proactively follow up study subjects to the end of the trial to minimize missing data. High missing data rates can result in efficacy and safety estimates that are unreliable for decision making. Additionally, pre-specifying a clinically relevant cutoff for a tipping point analysis would allow for a structured assessment of plausible missing data assumptions.

L495: ## 6.1 Natural Cubic Spline
L496: Sponsor referenced Donohue et al. (2022)15 for their use of the natural cubic spline analysis. Natural cubic splines model the temporal mean trend, assuming time to be continuous. A cubic spline is a function defined by cubic polynomials that are spliced together at knot locations, and the resulting function is restricted to be continuous and have continuous first and second derivatives. A natural cubic spline has a further constraint of having a second derivative of zero at the boundaries. A spline function f(t) of time t can be expressed as a linear combination of basis functions, b_k(t), and weights \beta_k: f(t) = \beta_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{m} \beta_k b_k(t). Once the basis functions are determined, the weights can be estimated by maximum likelihood estimation.
L497: The response for subject i = 1, \dots, N and study visit j = 1, \dots, K is modeled by natural cubic spline as:
L498: Y_{ij} = \beta_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{m} \beta_k b_k(t) + 1_i(x) \sum_{k=1}^{m} \gamma_k b_k(t) + \varepsilon_{ij}
L499: where x is the treatment variable, and the indicator 1_i(x) is 1 if subject i is in the treatment group and 0 otherwise. The residual vector \varepsilon_{ij} of each subject follows a multivariate normal distribution with mean zero and an assumed covariance structure. The resulting curve for the placebo group is defined by the natural cubic spline f(t) = \beta_0 + \sum_{k=1}^{m} \beta_k b_k(t); while the natural cubic spline
L500: g(t) = \sum_{k=1}^{m} \gamma_k b_k(t) represents the treatment group difference over time and is constrained to be zero at time zero. Given the known basis functions, estimation of the unknown parameters (\betas and \gammas) is accomplished with generalized least squares. Baseline covariates can be added to the model.
L501: Interior knots are typically spaced according to quantiles of t. For a natural cubic spline with 2 degrees of freedom (e.g., 2 knots at the boundaries, 1 knot at the median observation time), there are m = 2 basis functions. For a natural cubic spline with 3 degrees of freedom (e.g., 2 boundary knots, 2 internal knots over the time course, usually at 33rd and 67th percentiles), there are m = 3 basis functions.
L502: Note that the estimation of the covariance matrix used categorical time.

L504: ## 6.2 Rank ANCOVA
L505: Sponsor conducted a nonparametric rank analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) (Quade 196716) of multiply imputed iADRS and CDR-SB at Week 76 for the intermediate tau and overall population. The imputation model was a linear regression model adjusting for following baseline variables: iADRS score, pooled investigator, treatment, concomitant AChEI and/or memantine use, and age. Baseline tau category was included in the model for the overall population. To allow for imputation using the above model, the analysis dataset was restricted to all randomly assigned participants with a non-missing value for the variables in the model. One hundred datasets were created that included the observed Week 76 measurements as well as imputed values for the missing data. For participants who died during the trial, the worst possible score was used as their post-mortem measurement instead of the imputed score.
L506: A rank ANCOVA analysis was conducted to assess the treatment effect for each of the imputed datasets as follows:
L507: - Rank the dependent variable (change in iADRS or CDR-SB at Week 76) within each unique combination of the categorical variables of pooled investigator and concomitant AChEI and/or memantine use at baseline. For the analysis of the overall population, also include the baseline tau category as a categorical variable. That is, the ranking occurred without the treatment arm, age, or baseline value as factors.
- Conduct linear regression analysis using the ranks of the change from baseline iADRS or CDR-SB as the dependent variable and the continuous covariates (age and baseline iADRS or CDRSB, respectively) as the independent variables, within each unique combination of the categorical variables of pooled investigator and concomitant AChEI and/or memantine use at baseline. Obtain the residuals from the regression.
- For each of the imputed datasets, apply Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) mean score analysis to the obtained residual data to test for the differences between treatment groups across the levels of categorical covariates. To assess if the distribution of the residuals, differs by treatment group, the Hodges-Lehmann estimate, along with the 95% CI, was also calculated to estimate the median differences between treatment groups.
- Combine the statistics calculated for each imputed dataset from Step 3, according to Rubin's rule. There were 100 datasets. The CMH statistics were normalized with a Wilson-Hilferty transformation before combining to avoid the possible skewness from the chi-square distribution (Goria 199217; Wilson and Hilferty 193118).

L599: ## 1 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L600: The applicant submitted Study AACG to evaluate donanemab for treatment of Alzheimer's disease. In this double blind phase 2 study, the applicant's primary endpoint was an Alzheimer's disease composite, Integrated Alzheimer's Disease Rating scale (iADRS).
L601: The study initially had three arms: placebo, LY3002813 monotherapy and LY3002813 in combination with LY3202626. The combination arm was stopped after 15 patients were randomized to it due to evolving evidence on the safety and efficacy of BACE inhibitors, like LY3202626, as a class.
L602: As prespecified by the applicant in the original protocol, the trial was to be considered a success if LY3002813 alone and/or in combination with LY3202626 administered for up to 76 weeks decreased the cognitive and/or functional decline (based on iADRS) in patients with early symptomatic Alzheimer's Disease. Note that the Division had disagreed that the iADRS would be acceptable as a primary endpoint. At Week 76, donanemab-treated participants had statistically significantly less decline in cognition/function than placebo-treated participants as assessed by the iADRS, thus meeting the applicant's primary objective of the study. The treatment difference on LS mean change \pm SE was 3.20 \pm 1.56 (p=0.042).
L603: The applicant specified several key secondary endpoints including change from baseline in the following efficacy measures: ADAS-cog, CDRSB, MMSE and ADCS-iADL, followed by several other secondary endpoints including brain amyloid plaque deposition and change in brain tau deposition. A graphical multiplicity adjustment approach was specified to control for multiplicity of testing the primary iADRS and the ADAS-cog, CDRSB, MMSE and ADCS-iADL, but this plan did not include a pathway for multiplicity adjusted testing of change in brain amyloid deposition. All endpoints not included in this graphical approach should be considered exploratory.
L604: None of the secondary endpoints, ADAS-cog, CDRSB, MMSE and ADCS-iADL, met the alpha allocated to them based on the pre-specified graphical approach, though ADAS-Cog13 suggested a treatment effect [LS mean change difference (SE) = -1.86 (0.898), nominal p=0.040].
L605: Among the secondary endpoints, the applicant focused in the application on the effect of donanemab compared to placebo on brain amyloid pathophysiology at Week 76 of treatment in subjects with Early Alzheimer's Disease as measured by amyloid positron emission tomography (PET). The mixed model for repeated measures analyses of change from baseline in brain amyloid levels as measured by PET SUVr normalized to whole cerebellum mask at Week 76 estimated a reduction of 85 centiloids for LY3002813-M compared to placebo (nominal p-value of &lt;0.001).
L606: The impact of amyloid reduction on the clinical outcome is uncertain. The correlation at the patient level is 0.098 between change in iADRS and change in Amyloid PET SUV at Week 76 within the LY3002813 monotherapy arm. This correlation is small. Furthermore, there is no apparent treatment effect on the clinical endpoint in APOE4 non-carriers despite the numerically greater amyloid reductions compared to APOE4 carriers (see Figure 3).

L608: ## 2.1 Overview
L609: Donanemab is an IgG1 antibody directed at the N3pG A\beta epitope that is present only in brain amyloid plaques (Bridel et al. 2017). Donanemab binds to deposited amyloid plaque and aids its removal through microglial-mediated phagocytosis (DeMattos et al. 2012).
L610: The IND for this drug development is IND 109157. There is one completed phase 2 study, study AACG. The primary endpoint was the IADRS at 18 months. The effect(s) on Amyloid imaging was a secondary endpoint.

L627: ## Overview of Study Design
L628: Study AACG was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, Phase 2 study of donanemab (LY3002813) in participants with early symptomatic AD. The 133-week study
L629: included
L630: - a screening period of up to 9 weeks
- a treatment period of up to 72 weeks with final evaluations that occurred 4 weeks later at Week 76, and
- a 48-week immunogenicity and safety follow-up period.
L631: Participants who met entry criteria were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to 1 of the following treatments:
L632: - Donanemab (LY3002813): IV donanemab (700 mg Q4W for the first 3 doses, then 1400 mg Q4W) for up to 72 weeks, or
- Placebo: IV placebo Q4W for up to 72 weeks.
L633: Note: Under previous versions of the protocol, as a third treatment group, donanemab was administered in combination with 12 mg of LY3202626, an inhibitor of BACE1. However, as of amendment (d), the combination therapy and the corresponding oral placebo for blinding of the oral LY3202626 were discontinued from the study. L634: ## Determination of Sample Size
L635: Approximately 250 subjects were to be enrolled and randomized in a 1:1 ratio to the 2 treatment arms (placebo and LY3002813). It was expected that approximately 200 subjects would complete the double-blind treatment period of the study (approximately 100 per treatment arm). This sample size was expected to provide approximately 84% power to demonstrate that the active treatment arm had a \ge 0.6 posterior probability of slowing down iADRS progression over placebo by at least 3 points. The assumptions for the power calculation are that mean progression levels in the placebo and LY3002813 arms are approximately 12 and 6 points (50% slowing) over 18 months, respectively, with common standard deviation of 17. If the active treatment arm is placebo-like with no efficacy, the probability of passing the efficacy criterion specified above (i.e., false positive) is approximately 6%. The simulation for the power calculation and sample size determination was carried out in FACTS Version 6.0.
L636: Note that the sample size was originally determined under the originally planned 3 arm design (with the ultimately discontinued combination arm) as follows.
L637: Approximately 375 subjects were to be enrolled and randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to the three treatment arms (placebo, LY3002813-M, and LY3002813-C). It was expected that approximately 300 subjects would complete the double-blind treatment period of the study (approximately 100 per treatment arm). This sample size was expected to provide approximately 86% power to demonstrate that at least one active treatment arm has a \ge 0.6 posterior probability of slowing down iADRS progression over placebo by at least 3 points. The assumption for this power calculation was that mean progression levels in placebo, LY3002813-M and LY3002813-C arms would be approximately 12, 8 (33% slowing), and 6 points (50% slowing) over 18 months, respectively, with common standard deviation of 17. If both active treatment arms were placebo-like with no efficacy, the probability of passing the efficacy criterion specified above (i.e., false positive) is approximately 11% for the originally planned three arm design.

This study was originally planned in the context of a development plan including two follow-up phase 3 studies.*

L640: ## Primary Outcome and Methodology
L641: The primary analysis is considered a treatment policy strategy as outlined by ICH E9 guidelines. The primary objective of this study is to test the hypothesis that IV infusion of donanemab slows the cognitive and/or functional decline of AD as measured by the composite measure iADRS compared with placebo in patients with early symptomatic AD. This was to be assessed using an MMRM analysis comparing LY-M vs. PL. The change from baseline score on the iADRS at each scheduled postbaseline visit during the treatment period was to be the dependent variable. The model for the fixed effects was to include the following terms: baseline score, pooled investigator, treatment, visit, treatment-by-visit interaction, baseline-by-visit interaction, concomitant AChEI and/or memantine use at baseline (yes/no), and age at baseline. Visit was to be considered a categorical variable. The null hypothesis is that the contrast between the donanemab group versus placebo at the last visit equals 0. An unstructured covariance matrix was to be used to model the within subject variance-covariance errors. If the unstructured covariance structure matrix resulted in a lack of convergence, the following tests were to be used in sequence: heterogeneous Toeplitz covariance structure, heterogeneous autoregressive covariance structure, heterogeneous compound symmetry covariance structure, compound symmetry covariance structure. The Kenward-Roger approximation was to be used to estimate the denominator degrees of freedom. The primary time point for treatment comparison was to be at Week 76. The treatment group contrast in least-squares mean progression and its associated p-
L642: value and 95% CI were to be calculated for the treatment comparison of donanemab versus placebo using the MMRM model specified above. In addition, Bayesian posterior probability of the active treatment arm being superior to placebo by at least a margin of interest (25% slowing of placebo progression) was also to be calculated using a non-informative prior.

L646: ## Analysis of Amyloid PET
L647: At baseline, standardized uptake value ratio (SUVr) and centiloid value were to be calculated as a ratio of the composite summary region that is an average of 6 different cortical regions (anterior cingulate, posterior cingulate, medial orbital frontal, lateral temporal, lateral parietal, precuneus) with whole cerebellum as a reference region (SUVRCAA). The SUVRCAA was also
L648: used at visits 8 and 15 in order to determine if the amyloid level had been reduced enough in LY-treated patients to reduce the dose of LY.
L649: To evaluate the amyloid PET longitudinal change from baseline (SUVRCLAA), an MMRM analysis was to be used to compare change from baseline in SUVr at 76 weeks in the Evaluative Efficacy dataset. The model was to include the fixed, categorical effects of treatment, visit, and treatment-by-visit interaction, as well as continuous effects of baseline SUVr and age at baseline. Visit was to be considered a categorical variable with values equal to the visit numbers at which tau imaging was assessed. The null hypothesis is that the difference in LSM between the donanemab and placebo equals zero. Annualized change in the composite summary SUVRCLAA for each patient was to be calculated using the change at the last post-baseline visit.
L650: The annualized change was to be compared between the treatment groups with an ANCOVA. The ANCOVA model was to include the fixed effect of treatment as well as continuous effects of baseline amyloid PET and age at baseline. The null hypothesis is that the difference in LSM between donanemab and placebo equals zero.
L651: Spearman's rank correlation coefficient was to be calculated to assess the relationship of change in amyloid PET at Week 76 to change from baseline to Week 76 in iADRS, CDR-SB, ADCSiADL and MMSE by treatment. Partial correlation analyses were to be conducted using only patients who have the amyloid PET and scale result at Week 76 and adjusted for APOE4 carrier (Y/N), age and sex.
L652: Additionally, a linear regression analysis of change in iADRS versus change in amyloid PET endpoint in centiloids (SUVRCLAA) was to be graphed. The graph was to include the regression line, p-value for the explanatory variable (amyloid PET), and color to differentiate patients who are 'amyloid negative' (amyloid PET endpoint &lt; 24.1 centiloids). Change and annualized change analyses described above were to be repeated on the Full Analysis Set (Pooled LY). Treatment comparisons were to be made between LY (pooled LY-M and LY-C) and placebo.

L742: ## 4.1 Gender, Race, Age, and Geographic Region
L743: ## 4.1.1 Gender, Race, and Age
L744: Fifty two percent of the full analysis set population were Female. Mean Amyloid difference compared to placebo at Week 76 in Females was -83 [S.E.=5] and in Males was -89 [S.E.=5].
L745: Less than 5% were less than 65 years. Overall, there appeared to be a bigger amyloid reduction compared to placebo at Week 76 in Age \ge 75 (-95 [S.E.=5] than in Age&lt;75 (-73 [S.E.=6]).
L746: Sample sizes were too small to do meaningful subgroup analyses by Race because ninety five percent were white race.
L747: Seventy three percent were APOE carriers. Overall, there was a bigger amyloid reduction compared to placebo at Week 76 in non-carriers (-103 [S.E.=7] than in carriers (-80 [S.E.=5]).
L748: On the iADRS, primary clinical endpoint, there was a bigger placebo progression at Week 76 in ApoE4 carriers than in non-carriers (-11.3[SE=1.3] vs. -6.0[SE=2.1]). The Donanemab-M ApoE4 non-carriers were numerically worse than placebo non-carriers (-3.7 [SE=2.9]) while Donanemab-M carriers were better than placebo carriers (+5.7 [SE=1.8]).
L749: There were similar trends on the CDRSB at Week 76 with an estimated difference of Donanemab-M minus placebo of -0.64 [SE=.28] in carriers vs. +.34 [SE=.46], i.e., numerically worse than placebo, in non-carriers.

L756: ## 5 SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
L757: ## 5.1 Statistical Issues
L758: There was an interaction noted between ApoE4 status and treatment group for the Week 76 treatment group difference on iADRS with non-carriers being numerically worse than placebo. Although sample size is smaller for non-carriers, this pattern of slightly more reduction of Amyloid on SUV R than carriers but being numerically worse than placebo at Week 76 in non-carriers does not align with treatment effect on Amyloid being reasonably likely to predict treatment effect on the clinical endpoint at Week 76 over all patients, e.g., regardless of ApoE4 status.
L759: The study initially had three arms; placebo, LY3002813 monotherapy and LY3002813 in combination with LY3202626. The combination arm was stopped after 15 patients were randomized to it.
L760: The applicant specified several key secondary endpoints including change from baseline in the following efficacy measures: ADAS-cog, CDRSB, MMSE and ADCS-iADL, followed by several other secondary endpoints including brain amyloid plaque deposition and change in brain tau deposition. A graphical approach was specified to control for multiplicity of the primary iADRS and the ADAS-cog, CDRSB, MMSE and ADCS-iADL, but this plan did not include a pathway for multiplicity adjusted testing of change in brain amyloid deposition. All secondary endpoints not included in the graphical approach for multiplicity adjustment should be considered exploratory. Note also that the Division had not agreed that the iADRS would be acceptable as a primary endpoint.
L761: ## 5.2 Collective Evidence
L762: Collective evidence is not considered in this review since there was only one double-blind, controlled trial.
L763: ## 5.3 Conclusions and Recommendations
L764: The applicant submitted Study AACG to evaluate donanemab for treatment of Alzheimer's disease. In this double blind phase 2 study, the applicant's primary endpoint was an Alzheimer's disease composite, Integrated Alzheimer's Disease Rating scale (iADRS).
L765: The study initially had three arms: placebo, LY3002813 monotherapy and LY3002813 in combination with LY3202626. The combination arm was stopped after 15 patients were randomized to it, due to evolving evidence on the safety and efficacy of BACE inhibitors, like LY3202626, as a class.
L766: As prespecified by the applicant in the original protocol, the trial was to be considered a success if LY3002813 alone and/or in combination with LY3202626 administered for up to 76 weeks decreased the cognitive and/or functional decline (based on iADRS) in patients with early symptomatic Alzheimer's Disease. Note that the Division had disagreed that the iADRS would be acceptable as a primary endpoint.
L767: At Week 76, donanemab-treated participants had statistically significantly less decline in cognition/function than placebo-treated participants as assessed by the iADRS, thus meeting the applicant's primary objective of the study. The treatment difference on LS mean change \pm SE was 3.20 \pm 1.56 (p=0.42).
L768: The applicant specified several key secondary endpoints including change from baseline in the following efficacy measures: ADAS-cog, CDRSB, MMSE and ADCS-iADL, followed by several other secondary endpoints including brain amyloid plaque deposition and change in brain tau deposition. A graphical multiplicity adjustment approach was specified to control for multiplicity of testing the primary iADRS and the ADAS-cog, CDRSB, MMSE and ADCS-iADL, but this plan did not include a pathway for multiplicity adjusted testing of change in brain amyloid deposition. All endpoints not included in this graphical approach should be considered exploratory.
L769: None of the secondary endpoints, ADAS-cog, CDRSB, MMSE and ADCS-iADL, met the alpha allocated to them based on the pre-specified graphical approach, though ADAS-Cog13 suggested a treatment effect [LS mean change difference (SE) = -1.86 (0.898), nominal p=0.40].
L770: Among the secondary endpoints, the applicant focused in the application on the effect of donanemab compared to placebo on brain amyloid pathophysiology at Week 76 of treatment in subjects with Early Alzheimer's Disease as measured by amyloid positron emission tomography (PET). The mixed model for repeated measures analyses of change from baseline in brain amyloid levels as measured by PET SUVr normalized to whole cerebellum mask at Week 76 estimated a reduction of 85 centiloids for LY3002813-M compared to placebo (nominal p-value of &lt;0.001).
L771: The impact of amyloid reduction on the clinical outcome is uncertain. The correlation at the patient level is 0.098 between change in iADRS and change in Amyloid PET SUVr at Week 76 within the LY3002813 monotherapy arm. This correlation is small. Furthermore, there is no apparent treatment effect on the clinical endpoint in APOE4 non-carriers despite the numerically greater amyloid reductions compared to APOE4 carriers (see Figure 3).
