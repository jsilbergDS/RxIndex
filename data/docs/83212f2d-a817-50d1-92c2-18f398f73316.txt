L15: 1 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L16: Aeterna Zentaris submitted an addendum for the NDA for macimorelin for the proposed indication of the diagnosis of adult growth hormone deficiency (AGHD) (b) (4).
L17: The goal of this review is to examine the performance characteristics and accuracy of macimorelin in the diagnosis of ADHD. This test is intended to be an alternative to the traditional insulin tolerance test (ITT), which is currently used in clinical practice though, the ITT test is associated with significant risk for the patient. Because of this issue, the ITT test is not commonly performed in the United States.
L18: The submission is comprised of two Phase 3 trials (AEZS-130-047 and AEZS-130-052). Study AEZS-130-047 was previously submitted in November 2013. After the review cycle was completed, the Agency issued a Complete Response (CR) letter citing insufficient evidence to support the approval. Specifically, failure to reach the pre-specified primary goal for the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC), and questionable data quality were cited as deficiencies. Overall, the Agency was concerned with reproducibility of results of this study and recommended that the sponsor conduct a confirmatory trial. This study was reviewed by Dr. Lan Huang.
L19: The focus of this review is the newly submitted study AEZS-130-052.
L20: Performance characteristics of the macimorelin test: Based on the submitted data, the macimorelin test (MAC) does have skill in identification of subjects with AGHD. Although one of the co-primary endpoints was not met (achieving positive agreement with a lower confidence limit of 70% or more) the co-primary analyses did show a reasonable level of agreement between MAC and ITT in the study. The study provided the following results:
L21: Positive agreement of MAC and ITT was 74.32% (95% CI: 62.84, 83.78);
L22: Negative agreement of MAC and ITT was 93.94% (95% CI: 85.2, 98.32).
L23: When the study population included only subjects with intermediate likelihood of AGHD, i.e., those who would be more likely to receive an AGHD diagnostic test in clinical practice, the agreement between the tests weakened:
L24: Positive agreement of MAC and ITT became 61.11% (95% CI: 43.46, 76.86);
L25: Negative agreement of MAC and ITT became 92.68% (95% CI: 80.08, 98.46).
L26: My Recommendations:
L27: Based on results from the pre-specified statistical analyses, the target performance characteristics for the macimorelin test identified in the primary hypothesis were not met. However, given the issue of adverse effects of the comparator ITT, and the fact that the test using macimorelin might have a better safety profile, and the fact that the study demonstrated evidence that this test does
L28: provide a reasonable level of agreement with the ITT and has skill identifying subjects with AGHD, I recommend approval.
L29: That being said, the label should only reflect the results obtained using the pre-specified cut point for diagnosis. (b) (4)
L30: Definition of agreement metrics should also be added to the label.
L31: Statistical issues and findings:
L32: 1. Submitted study measures agreement, not accuracy. A very important consideration in the interpretation of the outcome of this study is the following: given that there is no reference standard available for diagnosis of AGHD, the only way to evaluate a new test is to compare it with another test that is currently utilized in clinical practice for the same purpose. The comparator test used in this study, the ITT, likely does not have perfect accuracy. That is why this study should be deemed as an agreement study. The important point is that we are evaluating agreement between tests, and that agreement does not always mean accuracy.2. Intended use population. Subjects with severe AGHD are not expected to be tested with the MAC or ITT test because their disease status would likely be determined by IGF-1 and prior medical history, thus leaving subjects with intermediate and low likelihood of AGHD as more representative of the intended use population for the MAC test. Therefore, the agreement estimates from the analysis of all groups together likely provides an inflated estimate of the performance of MAC in detection of AGHD, and results in the intermediate-likelihood subgroups B and C are important.3. One of the co-primary endpoints was not met. The benchmark targets for both co-primary endpoints were not met, as the confidence interval for the percent positive agreement did not rule out the pre-specified threshold of 70%. However, the estimated levels of negative and positive agreement between the MAC test and the ITT were reasonably high (94% and 74%, respectively). Given the known adverse effects associated with administration of the ITT and the absence of other tests for GHD in the US, I do not have a problem accepting as sufficient the levels of agreement that were achieved with the pre-specified cut point. The safety profile of the MAC test might outweigh the limited efficacy (i.e., diagnostic performance), given that some agreement with the ITT test was demonstrated. But I should be clear that agreement for the intended use population, subjects with moderate or low likelihood of AGHD, is moderate. The study has several flaws summarized here with implications on conclusions and labeling recommendations.
L33: 4. Secondary endpoints provided biased (overly optimistic) estimates of sensitivity and specificity. These estimates only included subjects on the extremes (subjects with severe AGHD and healthy subjects). Therefore, they do not directly address important questions about diagnostic performance in the intended use population2,3. (b) (4)
L34: [Redacted]. These types of evaluations are also not recommended for device clearance by CDRH1.
L35: 5. Healthy subjects came only from one center in one country. Such selection of controls could limit the generalizability of the study results. Furthermore, subjects in the control group were homogeneous with respect to other baseline characteristics: all of them were white, only two subjects had BMI above 30 kg/m2 and there were no subjects with BMI&lt;35kg/m2. Also, the small sample size and limited diversity of subjects made some of the subgroup analysis impossible to perform and the results of other subgroup analyses unreliable.6. Subgroup analyses of subjects with high BMI. On multiple occasions (during type A and type C meetings conducted after study 47 received a complete response and prior to submission of study 52), FDA requested that the sponsor provide sufficient data (and potentially select different cut points based on BMI) to evaluate the MAC test when conducted in subjects with high BMI. This study did not include many subjects with high BMI and specifically, none of the subjects in the control group had high BMI above 35 kg/m2. This issue could be mitigated by excluding subjects with high BMI from the intended use population in the labeling.7. Post-hoc analyses. Because one of the co-primary endpoints was not met, the sponsor conducted a post-hoc analysis considering alternative cut points to the one that was pre-specified. For the original pre-specified analysis, the cut point for the MAC test was chosen based on results of a previous Phase 3 trial (study 47). In this post-hoc analysis, the sponsor used a data-driven approach (b) (4)
L36: [Redacted] In my view, post-hoc analysis is very useful as a learning tool that would be helpful to prepare for a new confirmatory study. The results of the post-hoc analyses could be an artifact of chance and could potentially overestimate the true performance of the test. To mitigate this issue, the outcomes of these post-hoc analyses should be validated on a separate independent dataset.
L37: 2 INTRODUCTION
L38: 2.1 Overview
L39: A brief description of the drug indication and history of the submission is presented below.
L40: 2.1.1 Indication
L41: Macimorelin is an orally available peptidomimetic tripeptide with growth hormone secretagogue activity similar to ghrelin. The proposed indication for this drug is diagnosis of adult growth hormone deficiency (AGHD)
L42: (b) (4)
L43: This drug is not designed for treatment, it is only intended to be used for the purpose of diagnosis.
L44: 2.1.2 History of Drug Development
L45: Macimorelin was first developed under the sponsorship of Ardana Bioscience and then Aeterna Zentaris. The first study evaluating the diagnostic efficacy of macimorelin was started by Ardana as Study ARD-0705-001 and was renumbered AEZS-130-047 (study 47) after the transfer of the sponsorship to Aeterna Zentaris (AEZS).
L46: The initial submission of NDA 205598 was on November 4, 2013. The efficacy part of this submission was focused on study 47. A Special Protocol Assessment (SPA) agreement for this study was reached on December 17, 2010. On November 5, 2014, FDA issued a Complete Response (CR) letter, citing statistical issues in study 47. Those issues were that the lower bound of the confidence interval for the area under the curve (AUC) of the receiver operator characteristic (ROC) curve was below the pre-specified threshold and that data quality was questionable because there was a lack of complete and verifiable source data for determining whether patients were accurately diagnosed with the target disease.
L47: On March 6, 2015, the CR letter was followed up with a Type A meeting. During this meeting, the sponsor proposed a new study AEZS-130-052 (study 52) titled “Confirmatory validation of oral macimorelin as a GHST for the diagnosis of AGHD in comparison with the gold-standard insulin tolerance test (ITT).” At the Type A meeting, FDA requested that the sponsor recruit and conduct separate analyses in the higher BMI groups.
L48: In January 2017, it was found that Study 52 did not meet one of two co-primary endpoints of the confirmatory efficacy analyses applying the predefined GH cut-off points for the macimorelin test and ITT. At a Type C meeting on March 29, 2017, FDA stated that analyses using different cut off points for the macimorelin test would be post hoc and would be interpreted in that light, requested additional subgroup analyses within the Group B and C patients who would be most likely to benefit from a new GH stimulation test and within obese and younger subjects, and stated that the adequacy of the performance of the new test would be a review issue.

L50: This resubmission includes three additional studies (PK/PD trial (AEZS-130-054), QT trial (AEZS-130-055), and confirmatory trial (AEZS-130-052)). The focus of this review is the confirmatory trial, study 52. I did not review study 47, which was evaluated as part of the review of the original submission of NDA 205598. Additional details on the design and results of study 47 can be found in the Statistical Review by Dr. Lan Huang dated July 9, 2014.

L57: 3 STATISTICAL EVALUATION
L58: 3.1 Data and Analysis Quality
L59: The submission quality was found to be reasonable.
L60: 3.2 Evaluation of Efficacy
L61: Macimorelin is intended to be used as a diagnostic drug, and not intended for therapy. Therefore, the efficacy in this context is the ability of this test to separate subjects with disease, i.e. AGHD, from subjects who do not have disease. Because there is no reference standard for establishing
L62: the presence or absence of AGHD available, macimorelin is compared with the insulin tolerance test (ITT). The ITT test is currently used in clinical practice as one of the diagnostic tests for AGHD. Also, to show that a diagnostic test works, repeatability of the test needs to be confirmed.
L63: 3.2.1 Study Design and Endpoints
L64: Study 52 was an open-label, randomized, single dose, 2-way crossover trial to examine performance characteristics of the macimorelin growth hormone stimulation test ('MAC') when compared with the insulin tolerance test in detection subjects with AGHD.
L65: The study was conducted in 30 sites in 9 countries; 25 sites in Europe (Austria, Germany, Spain, France, Italy, Poland, Serbia, and UK) and 5 sites in the USA.
L66: Study subjects were assigned to groups of descending likelihood of having AGHD. The groups were defined as follows:
L67: Group A: High likelihood of GHD:
L68: • Structural hypothalamic or pituitary lesions and low IGF-1, and/or• Three or more pituitary hormone deficiencies (PHD) and low IGF-1, or• Childhood onset GHD with structural lesions and low IGF-1
L69: Group B: Intermediate likelihood of GHD:
L70: • Eligible subjects not qualifying for either high or low likelihood (Group A/C)
L71: Group C: Low likelihood of GHD:
L72: • One risk factor for GHD only, such as history of distant traumatic brain injury (TBI) or one PHD only with otherwise normal pituitary function, or• Isolated idiopathic childhood onset GHD without additional pituitary deficits
L73: Group D: Healthy control:
L74: • Healthy subjects matching Group A subjects by sex, age, body mass index (BMI), and estrogen status (females only)

L77: Out of 238 subjects screened, 166 subjects were enrolled into the study. A consort diagram showing subjects' disposition is presented in Figure 1.
L78: After the core phase that involved MAC and ITT testing was completed, a small group of subjects (24.3%) was selected to repeat the MAC test in order to determine reproducibility of findings based on the MAC test.
L79: For both the ITT and MAC tests, serum concentrations of growth hormone were measured at pre-defined time points (15, 30, 45, 60, 90) after GHST administration of macimorelin or insulin. According to the protocol, for the ITT, serum concentrations of growth hormone were also measured at 120 minutes after administration of insulin. Serum concentrations of GH for a given subject were provided by the central laboratory. Of note, the measurements performed at 120 minutes after test administration were mentioned in both the core study report (p. 25) and the clinical overview (p. 13), but the sponsor did not provide any analyses involving this time point and the measurements taken at 120 minutes after test administration were not present in the dataset provided to FDA.
L80: Low levels of growth hormone indicate a growth hormone deficiency. Since the goal of both the MAC and ITT tests is to stimulate GH, peak levels of this marker indicate whether the patient has or does not have growth hormone deficiency.
L81: The following cut points were pre-specified for the MAC and ITT tests based on the peak GH levels observed across the four measurement time points:
L82: • MAC: GH: 2.8 ng/mL• ITT: GH: 5.1 ng/mL,
L83: where GH values below 2.8 for MAC and 5.1 for ITT identified subjects as having AGHD. The cut point selection for the MAC test was based on experience in a prior study (study 47). The cut point for ITT was selected based on the guideline for clinical practice.
L84: The study cohort included all randomized subjects in whom both GHSTs were evaluable. The sequential order of the test was randomly determined for groups A, B, and C. Since subjects in group D were matched to subjects in group A, the order of testing in group D was determined by the order of testing selected for subjects in group A. The washout time between two tests was between 7 days and 1 month.
L85: The analyses were performed using the modified Intent-to-Treat population, i.e. including only subjects who had both tests.
L86: Of note, all subjects in the healthy control group (group D) were enrolled in one center in Poland. No controls were included from other sites. Repeatability of the MAC test was examined on selected subjects only in Serbia.
L87: Intended use population:
L88: In this submission, the sponsor does not clearly identify the intended use population. Following the text of this submission, the sponsor is intending the MAC test to replace ITT and therefore assuming that the intended use population for MAC test will be the same as intended use population for ITT test. The population of this trial includes subjects with severe disease identified through IGF-1 levels and prior history of pituitary lesions. In clinical practice, subjects with these characteristics will not be administered the ITT test because these parameters are clearly indicating growth hormone deficiency, and therefore, this group of subjects is not the population that will likely be getting the MAC test. Similarly, healthy subjects do not need testing. Therefore, the intended use population is more accurately represented by the subjects with intermediate (group B) and low likelihood (group C) of AGHD.
L89: Primary endpoints:
L90: Co-primary efficacy variables were ‘Percent Negative Agreement’ and ‘Percent Positive Agreement’ when using predefined cut-off points of both GHSTs. The benchmark for negative agreement was set to 75% and the benchmark for positive agreement was set to 70%. The goal for each of the co-primary endpoints was to show with high statistical confidence (based on the
L91: lower bound of the Clopper-Pearson confidence interval) that the agreement was equal or higher than each of the prespecified benchmarks.
L92: Secondary endpoints:
L93: Secondary endpoints were ‘Percent Overall Agreement’ and performance characteristics, i.e. sensitivity and specificity of both GHSTs (MAC and ITT) when using predefined cut-off points of both GHSTs. No specific benchmark levels for those performance characteristics were pre-determined.
L94: Sample size calculations:
L95: The sponsor calculated sample size on the assumption that the MAC and ITT test results would be independent, which is not correct because this was a crossover trial and each subject experienced both tests during the trial. The planned sample size listed in the protocol was 110 subjects to have 55 ITT-positive and 55 ITT-negative subjects. The plan was to have at least 25% of subjects with high likelihood of having AGHD. The expected level of agreement (the sponsor did not provide a definition of what is agreement in this context) was to be at least 82% with the lower bound of the 1-sided exact confidence interval to be at least 69.3%.
L96: 3.2.2 Statistical Methodologies
L97: Sponsor’s approach:
L98: There were two co-primary efficacy endpoints in this study: percent negative agreement and percent positive agreement, using the ITT as the comparator. For these analyses, the sponsor utilized the approach described in the “Statistical Guidance on Reporting Results from Studies Evaluation Diagnostic Devices”1. There is no reference standard (for example, pathology result) available for the diagnosis of AGHD, i.e. there is no specific test that can either confirm or deny presence of AGHD with perfect accuracy. In this scenario, performance characteristics of the new test (MAC) are evaluated based on comparisons to ITT, which is often used to diagnose AGHD in clinical practice but cannot be considered a true reference standard as it cannot identify AGHD 100% correctly. In this setting, the performance of the new test can be based on the level of agreement between the new test and the comparator test.
L99: As per the CDRH guidance for evaluation of agreement between two tests in circumstances of comparison to a non-standard, the performance characteristics of the MAC test were defined in the following manner:

L106: For the secondary endpoints, the sponsor compared subjects with high probability of AGHD (group A) with healthy subjects from group D. The sponsor-defined sensitivity (probability of positive test given presence of disease) and specificity (probability of negative test given absence of disease) were calculated based on the assumption that subjects in group A were “true” disease subjects and subjects from group D were “true” non-diseased subjects. Also, the sponsor conducted ROC analyses using only subjects from group A and D. For all these secondary analyses, subjects from groups C and B were excluded.
L107: Both primary and secondary endpoints were examined in the subjects who underwent both the MAC and ITT tests.
L108: Repeatability of the MAC test was examined on a subset of subjects. All subjects in the repeatability study were from one center in Serbia. For this evaluation, the subjects underwent a second MAC test. The difference between GH levels from both MAC tests was calculated.
L109: FDA approach:
L110: To thoroughly examine utility and external validity of a diagnostic test, it is important to address the following questions:
L111: 1. Who should be tested, i.e. what is the intended use population,
2. How accurate is the test (performance characteristics),
3. When should the test be conducted (timing of measurements), and
4. How reproducible are the test outcomes?
L112: Intended use population
L113: Regarding the intended use population, subjects with the highest probability of AGHD will most likely be identified by IGF-1 and prior medical history (as defined in selection criteria). Because
L114: these subjects could be identified without administration of an AGHD test, most likely they will not be receiving the test in clinical practice and therefore will not be a part of the intended use population.
L115: Subjects from group D will most likely not be tested because they are healthy and therefore will not be a part of the intended use population. That would leave subjects with intermediate likelihood of AGHD, groups B and C, in the intended use population. Because of this, I conducted a separate subgroup analysis involving only groups B and C.
L116: Performance characteristics of MAC
L117: To address the accuracy of the MAC test, evaluation of concordance between MAC and the currently standard diagnostic test (ITT) for AGHD is appropriate. I agree with the definition of primary outcomes, i.e. use of percent positive and negative agreement to compare the new test to a non-reference standard.
L118: In contrast to the definition of primary endpoints and definition of intended use population, the analysis and definitions of secondary endpoints proposed by the sponsor are problematic. For all secondary analyses, the sponsor excluded subjects from groups B and C. Thus, subjects who will likely be candidates for the ITT test (patients who would not be identified as having AGHD by IGF-1 or other criteria listed as criteria to identify subjects with high probability of AGHD) were excluded from the sponsor's evaluation. Furthermore, the sponsor-defined "sensitivity" and "specificity" are not valid estimates of those characteristics for the test in the intended use population. An analysis that does not include the intended use population but rather includes only subjects on the extremes introduces spectrum bias1, 2, 3 and leads to biased estimates of sensitivity and specificity (tending to overestimate the performance characteristics of the test).
L119: The outcomes of both AGHD tests are binary (disease or no disease) but the underlying biomarker (GH level) is quantitative; therefore, the binary test result is driven by selection of a cut point for the GH level. Because selection of a specific cut point for a biomarker is a subjective choice, it is important to learn how biomarkers (in this case GH obtained from ITT and GH resulting from the MAC test) relate to each other. Examination of agreement without use of cut points can provide a better understanding of GH patterns obtained with both tests. To explore the relationship between GH values obtained using ITT and GH values obtained using MAC test, I utilized Kendall's tau-b4, 5. Kendall's tau-b (tau-b) is an agreement measure that examines correlation between observations. The advantage of using tau-b is its scale-neutrality, it is a rank-based measure and because it is rank-based, subjects do not have to be on the same scale. The goal of this analysis is to measure the strength of association between biomarkers obtained using both tests. Tau-b was calculated for the entire study cohort and for each of the study groups separately.
L120: I also used scatter plots and box plots to illustrate the relationship between the biomarkers.
L121: Identifying optimal time window for testing
L122: For every diagnostic test, it is important to identify optimal conditions so that the test outcomes provide the most accurate information. In the case of ITT and MAC tests, the optimal point will
L123: likely be obtained at the time when the patient has the maximum GH level. Since GH is measured multiple times after the administration of the test, it is important to find the time window when this biomarker is likely at its maximum. For this analysis, I examined the distribution of the GH values by time of the test. I compared distributions of GH obtained in each time window to the distribution of GH at maximum and identified the time point when the mean of the maximum GH distribution and the mean of GH in the time window were close. To replicate my findings, I utilized the data obtained during the MAC repeatability test. Finally, I compared agreement metrics obtained using maximum values of GH with those obtained from the time window that was closest to the maximum.

L144: Missing Data
L145: Discontinuation of a subject from study procedures prior to randomization was not considered as a withdrawal but as a “screen failure.” Subjects who withdrew or dropped out before completing the crossover study with both GHSTs being evaluable were replaced. The following describes the extent of study completion:
L146: Number of ITT tested subjects: 157
L147: - 27 (17%) out of 157 subjects did not achieve hypoglycemia that is needed for an ITT to be evaluable and required the repetition of the test
- Among the 17 ITTs that were actually repeated, 4 tests (24%) did not provide an evaluable result even on the “second try”
- 143 subjects had an evaluable ITT of which 3 withdrew prior to MAC
- Thus, the number of completed and evaluable ITT tests was 140, and the missing fraction was 17/157 or 10.8%
L148: Number of MAC tested subjects: 154
L149: - 1 subject had apparent non-compliance or a dosing error, and the MAC test was repeated
- Other MAC tests did not require repetitions
- Thus, the number of completed and evaluable MAC tests was 153, and the missing fraction was 1/154 or 0.65%
L150: Overall, 140 out of the 157 enrolled subjects had both tests completed and evaluable (missing data in 17/157 or 10.8%). Because it was a crossover study, each subject underwent both tests. Therefore, the pre-specified primary analyses restricted to the subset of patients who completed both tests (the modified intention to treat population) still allows for a randomized comparison, and missing data for test comparisons was not as crucial as it would be in a parallel- group study. That being said, one reason for examining missing data in this setting is to examine the feasibility of obtaining test results. In this study, the ITT had most of the issues with feasibility
L151: (17 out of 157 patients tested required repetition of the test, and 4 of those did not provide an evaluable result even on the second try). The MAC test showed almost no issues related to the feasibility of results (153 out of 154 subjects completed the MAC test without issues).

L217: Post-hoc and secondary endpoint analyses
L218: Because one of the co-primary endpoints (the level of positive agreement) was not met, the applicant conducted a post-hoc analysis. For the original pre-specified analysis, the cut point for the MAC test of 2.8 ng/mL was chosen based on results of a previous Phase 3 trial and based on FDA recommendations to try to limit the likelihood of a false positive test. In this post-hoc analysis, the sponsor looked at all possible primary analysis outcomes for all possible cut points for the MAC test and selected a cut point yielding more favorable results, i.e., results in which the pre-specified benchmark for levels of agreement between the MAC test and ITT were satisfied.
L219: (b) (4)
A scatter plot showing the GH values based on the MAC and ITT tests, as well the pre-specified and post-hoc cut points is presented in Figure 13. In my view, post-hoc analysis is very useful as a learning tool that would be helpful to generate hypotheses to be tested in a new, independent confirmatory study. The results of post-hoc analyses, especially analyses that are clearly data-driven as were carried out here, could be an artifact of chance and are likely to overestimate the true level of agreement. L267: 5 SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
L268: 5.1 Statistical Issues
L269: 1. Submitted study measures agreement, not accuracy. A very important consideration in the interpretation of the outcome of this study is the following: given that there is no reference standard available for diagnosis of AGHD, the only way to evaluate a new test is to compare it with another test that is currently utilized in clinical practice for the same purpose. The comparator test used in this study, the ITT, likely does not have perfect accuracy. That is why this study should be deemed as an agreement study. The important point is that we are evaluating agreement between tests, and that agreement does not always mean accuracy.
2. Intended use population. Subjects with severe AGHD are not expected to be tested with the MAC or ITT test because their disease status would likely be determined by IGF-1 and prior medical history, thus leaving subjects with intermediate and low likelihood of AGHD as more representative of the intended use population for the MAC test. Therefore, the agreement estimates from the analysis of all groups together likely
L270: provides an inflated estimate of the performance of MAC in detection of AGHD, and results in the intermediate-likelihood subgroups B and C are important.
L271: 3. One of the co-primary endpoints was not met. The benchmark targets for both co-primary endpoints were not met, as the confidence interval for the percent positive agreement did not rule out the pre-specified threshold of 70%. However, the estimated levels of negative and positive agreement between the MAC test and the ITT were reasonably high (94% and 74%, respectively). Given the known adverse effects associated with administration of the ITT and the absence of other tests for GHD in the US, I do not have a problem accepting as sufficient the levels of agreement that were achieved with the pre-specified cut point. The safety profile of the MAC test might outweigh the limited efficacy (i.e., diagnostic performance), given that some agreement with the ITT test was demonstrated. But I should be clear that agreement for the intended use population, subjects with moderate or low likelihood of AGHD, is moderate.4. Secondary endpoints provided biased estimates of sensitivity and specificity and do not directly address important questions about diagnostic performance in the intended use population. (b) (4) These types of evaluations are also not recommended for device clearance by CDRH.5. Subjects from the control group came only from one center in one country. Such selection of controls could limit the generalizability of the study results. Furthermore, subjects in the control group were homogeneous with respect to other baseline characteristics: all of them were white with BMI&lt;35kg/m2. Also, the small sample size and limited diversity of subjects made some of the subgroup analysis impossible to perform and the results of other subgroup analyses unreliable.6. Subgroup analyses of subjects with high BMI. On multiple occasions (during type A and type C meetings conducted after study 47 received a complete response and prior to submission of study 52), FDA requested that the sponsor provide sufficient data (and potentially select different cut points based on BMI) to evaluate the MAC test when conducted in subjects with high BMI. This study did not include many subjects with high BMI and specifically, none of the subjects in the control group had high BMI. This issue could be mitigated by excluding subjects with high BMI from the intended use population in the labeling.7. Post-hoc analyses. Because one of the co-primary endpoints was not met, the sponsor conducted a post-hoc analysis considering alternative cut points to the one that was pre-specified. For the original pre-specified analysis, the cut point for the MAC test was chosen based on results of a previous Phase 3 trial (study 47). In this post-hoc analysis, the sponsor used a data-driven approach (b) (4)
L272: In my view, post-hoc analysis is very useful as a learning tool that would be helpful to prepare for a new confirmatory study. The results of the post-hoc analyses could be an artifact of chance and could potentially overestimate the true performance of the test. To mitigate this issue, the outcomes of these post-hoc analyses should be validated on a separate independent dataset.
L273: Also, Dr. Lili Duan, Scientific Reviewer from CDRH (CDRH/OIR/DCTD), was a consultant for this review. Here is her comment on the validity of post-hoc analyses:
L274: “We do not allow post-hoc analysis to redefine test cut-points, except when there is prospective data to analyze and confirm the validity of the new cut-points.” (b) (4)
L275: ”
L276: 5.2 Collective Evidence
L277: My recommendation for approval is based on the fact, emphasized by the clinical team, that the existing alternative can be life-threatening and is therefore used sparingly in the United States. The statistical evidence for efficacy is marginal albeit, in my view, sufficient given this circumstance. The applicant’s test did demonstrate agreement with the existing test although it did not satisfy the stringent prespecified primary endpoint.
L278: The results of previously submitted study 47 did not provide sufficient evidence to support the claims proposed for macimorelin. Study 47 failed in the pre-specified primary ROC analysis. The ROC outcomes showed possible confounding of the test with race and study site. The sensitivity and specificity results were not consistent in different subject groups (previously vs. newly enrolled). Different post-hoc cut points were selected (not pre-specified). Those cut points were selected based on one dataset with 100 subjects. No validation of these outcomes was provided.
L279: Based on the submitted data (study 52), the macimorelin test does have skill in identification of subjects with AGHD. Although one of the co-primary endpoints was not met (achieving positive agreement with a lower confidence limit of 70% or more), the co-primary analyses did show a reasonable level of agreement between MAC and ITT in the study. The study provided the following results using the prespecified (not post-hoc) cut point:positive agreement of MAC and ITT was 74.32% (95% CI: 62.84, 83.78);negative agreement of MAC and ITT was 93.94% (95% CI: 85.2, 98.32).
L280: When the study population was reduced to include only subjects who would be more likely to receive an AGHD test, e.g., the intended use population, the agreement between the tests was weakened:
L281: positive agreement of MAC and ITT became 61.11% (95% CI: 43.46, 76.86);
L282: negative agreement of MAC and ITT became 92.68% (95% CI: 80.08, 98.46).
L283: 5.3 Conclusions and Recommendations
L284: Currently, adult growth hormone deficiency is tested in clinical practice using the ITT test. Otherwise, clinicians rely on other factors, such as IGF-1 and prior medical history. The ITT introduces potential risks (because the patient must undergo an insulin-induced hypoglycemia), and is contraindicated in some patients. Because of these issues, the ITT is not commonly performed in the United States8. Although one of the co-primary endpoints was not met, given the unmet medical need for a safer diagnostic test, I would recommend approval if the safety profile of this drug meets FDA's requirements.
L285: If another Phase 3 trial were conducted to evaluate the performance of MAC (or another AGHD diagnostic test), I recommend the following:
L286: 1. The study population should be representative of subjects in the intended use population2. Control subjects should have diverse baseline characteristics and should not come from just one study site3. Subjects from higher BMI groups should be a part of the study population4. Similarly, because the sponsor would be seeking an approval in the United States, a sufficient number of US subjects should be included and the study should comply with current standards of clinical practice in the US5. The results of post-hoc analyses (e.g., suggesting alternative cut points) should be confirmed using data from the new study.

L319: 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L320: 1.1 Conclusions and Recommendations
L321: The statistical results in terms of accuracy do not provide enough evidence to support the claims proposed in this NDA. The sponsor failed in the pre-specified primary ROC AUC analysis. The ROC AUC results may be biased because of possible confounding of the test with race and study site. The sensitivity and specificity results are not consistent in different subject groups (previously vs. newly enrolled). Different cut-points can be selected according to the rules (not pre-specified) and the cut-points are selected based on the single data with 100 subjects, which may not be reproducible in another study. We recommend CR with additional confirmatory study with pre-specified cut-point and intended patient population.

L338: 1.3 Statistical Issues and Concerns
L339: Statistical significance was not achieved in the pre-planned primary analysis of the study. For the pre-planned primary analysis, the lower bound of the 99% confidence interval for the ROC AUC is &lt;0.85, the pre-specified threshold.
L340: There is a significant imbalance in Race between the AGHD group and the matched control group. Two of 52 AGHD patients (4%) and 18 out of 48 matched controls (38%) are Black or African American. Race may be a confounded factor for evaluating the diagnostic efficacy.
L341: There is a significant imbalance in study site between the AGHD group and the matched control group. For example, no AGHD patients were enrolled from Sites 11 and 17, and most of the matched controls were enrolled from Sites 09, 10, 11 and 17. Site may be a confounded factor for evaluating the diagnostic efficacy.
L342: The determination of the optimal cut-point is a challenge. Different approaches may lead to different cut-points. In addition, the selected cut-point was based on the same data that was used to determine its sensitivity and specificity. Therefore, the estimates of sensitivity and specificity may tend to overstate the truth. It is not clear if the sensitivity and specificity obtained in Study AEZS-130-047 can be reproduced in another study.
L343: For both sensitivity and specificity using cut-point 8.5 (pre-specified by the sponsor in the protocol) and 2.7 (optimal cut-point determined from the data by the sponsor), the performance for the newly enrolled patients is worse than the previously enrolled subjects. The results are not consistent (previously vs. newly enrolled).
L344: It is not clear if the matched control group includes subjects belonging to the intended patient population, who would seek for diagnosis of possible AGHD and would benefit from the test. The performance (ROC AUC, sensitivity and specificity) of Macimorelin for the intended patient population is not known.

L346: 2.1 Overview
L347: 2.1.1. Class and Indication
L348: Macimorelin (AEZS-130) is an orally-available peptidomimetic ghrelin receptor agonist with growth hormone secretagogue (GHS) activity. Ghrelin potently stimulates growth hormone (GH) release. The GH-releasing effects of ghrelin are thought to be mediated by specific receptors mainly present at the pituitary and hypothalamic level. In vitro, utilizing membrane preparations containing the GHS-receptor derived from human hypothalamus and pituitary gland, as well as in various functional cell-based assays covering GHS receptors of humans, mice and rats, it was demonstrated that macimorelin shows binding potency to the human GHS receptor comparable to that of its natural ligand, ghrelin. In vivo, macimorelin is readily absorbed from the gastrointestinal tract and induces potent GH release.

L414: 3.2.2 Statistical Methodologies
L415: The statistical analysis plan (SAP) was approved on 07-Jun-2011 and the database locked on 12-Aug-2011. The statistical methods described in the SAP are summarized below and the reviewer's comments will be in italics in this Section.
L416: Primary Efficacy Evaluation:
L417: The primary efficacy analysis is the ROC analysis performed on the peak GH levels when the subjects were treated with AEZS-130. The case or control status of the subject will be used as
L418: “truth” for AGHD. The ROC curve is created by plotting the true positive fraction (TPF or sensitivity) vs. the false positive fraction (FPF or 1-specificity) determined by moving the cut-point value along the peak GH level range.
L419: The diagnostic accuracy of AEZS-130 will be established if the AUC is greater than 0.85. This will be tested with \alpha=0.01 using the one sided hypotheses:
L420: H_0: AUC \leq 0.85; H_A: AUC &gt; 0.85
L421: The confidence interval of the AUC will be estimated using a bootstrap approach. The bootstrap process will involve drawing 1000 repeated independent samples (with replacement) from the original dataset and then estimating the AUC from the ROC analysis for each sample. The lower bound of the one-sided 99% confidence interval will be estimated directly from the 10,000 bootstrap samples using the 1%-tile of the samples (e.g., the 10th smallest sample of the 1000 samples). If the lower bound is greater than 0.85 then the null hypothesis will be rejected and AESZ-130 will be accepted as having diagnostic accuracy.
L422: Secondary Efficacy Analyses include:
L423: • As a secondary endpoint, at least 8 of the 10 newly enrolled AGHD patients should be correctly classified for a protocol pre-specified threshold of peak GH concentration level, which is 8.5 (ng/ml)• The sensitivity and its 95% confidence interval will be reported for peak GH\leqcut- point for all cut-points along the peak GH range for AEZS-130• The specificity and its 95% confidence interval will be reported for peak GH\leqcut-point for all cut-points along the peak GH range for AEZS-130• The CART analysis will estimate:○ Cut-point which minimizes the misclassification of case and control subjects○ Optimal decision tree for AEZS-130 which incorporates age, sex and BMI• Sensitivity and specificity will be summarized for age, gender, BMI and estrogen status subgroups containing n&gt;10• Logistic regression analysis with backward elimination of non-significant terms from a full model which includes subgroup covariates and covariate by AEZS-130 interactions.• ROC analysis will be performed for the subjects that received GHRH

L516: 5. SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
L517: 5.1 Statistical Issues
L518: 1) Statistical significance was not achieved in the pre-planned primary analysis of the study. For the pre-planned primary analysis, the lower bound of the 99% confidence interval for the ROC AUC is &lt;0.85, the pre-specified threshold.
2) There is a significant imbalance in Race between the AGHD group and the matched control group. Two of 52 AGHD patients (4%) and 18 out of 48 matched controls (38%) are Black or African American. Race may be a confounded factor for evaluating the diagnostic efficacy.
3) There is a significant imbalance in study site between the AGHD group and the matched control group. For example, no AGHD patients were enrolled from Sites 11 and 17, and most of the matched controls were enrolled from Sites 09, 10, 11 and 17. Site may be a confounded factor for evaluating the diagnostic efficacy.
4) The determination of the optimal cut-point is a challenge. Different approaches may lead to different cut-points. In addition, the selected cut-point was based on the same data that was used to determine its sensitivity and specificity. Therefore, the estimates of sensitivity and specificity may tend to overstate the truth. It is not clear if the sensitivity and specificity obtained in Study AEZS-130-047 can be reproduced in another study.
L519: 5) For both sensitivity and specificity using cut-point 8.5 (pre-specified by the sponsor in the protocol) and 2.7 (optimal cut-point determined from the data by the sponsor), the performance for the newly enrolled patients is worse than the previously enrolled subjects. The results are not consistent (previously vs. newly enrolled).6) It is not clear if the matched control group includes subjects belonging to the intended patient population, who would seek for diagnosis of possible AGHD and would benefit from the test. The performance (ROC AUC, sensitivity and specificity) of Macimorelin for the intended patient population is not known.
L520: 5.2 Collective Evidence
L521: This NDA submission includes five studies. Three studies enrolled only healthy volunteers and one study is ongoing. Therefore, only one study (Study AEZS-130-047) is relevant and all the evidence (summarized below) is from Study AEZS-130-047.
L522: The Study provided a 99% Lower Bound for the one-side CI for the AUC of .83 (less than the pre-specified threshold of 0.85) for mITT population, thus the pre-specified primary analyses failed. For PPS population defined by the sponsor after obtaining the data, the 99% Lower bound for the one-sided CI for the AUC is 0.851 (&gt;0.85).
L523: With the cut-points from 2 ng/mL to 7 ng/mL, the range of sensitivity and specificity values are from 80% to 90%, and percent of misclassification (disagreement between the test and the truth) ranges from 14% to 20% for the mITT population. A cut-point for the response variable (peak concentration following AEZS-130) of 2.7 ng / mL produced a Sensitivity of 0.81 (95 CI: 0.68, 0.90) and a Specificity of 0.92 (95% CI: 0.80, 0.98) for mITT population.
L524: 52 subjects (42 AGHD patients + 10 matched controls) were enrolled previously under the original protocol and received both Macimorelin (AEZS-130) and L-ARG+GHRH (the comparator). 48 additional subjects (10 AGHD patients and 38 matched controls) were enrolled under the SPA agreed by the sponsor and the Agency and received Macimorelin only because the comparator is not available during the study period.
L525: The performance (see details in Table 9) for the new enrolled patients is worse than the previously enrolled subjects (80%&lt;95% for sensitivity and 53%&lt;90% for specificity using the pre-specified cut-point 8.5 ng/mL). In addition, the specificity (30%) becomes worse if we only include the 10 newly enrolled matched controls for the 10 newly enrolled patients in the specificity analysis. The similar pattern is observed for another cut-point 2.7ng/mL determined using the data. The results are not consistent for subjects enrolled from different study portions.
L526: The addition of a covariate ( BMI) that separates the population into two groups – subjects with BMI &lt; 30\text{kg} / \text{M}^2 versus subjects with BMI \geq 30\text{kg} / \text{M}^2, followed by the ROC analysis performed independently on each of these groups, yielded the following results:
L527: For mITT population, BMI-adjusted ROC AUC = 0.918. Lower bound of 99% CI is 0.845 by bootstrap method. Using the combined criteria of BMI with cut-point 30kg / M2 and the peak concentration cut-point 6.8 ng/mL in subjects with BMI&lt;30 and 2.7 ng/mL for subjects with BMI&gt;=30, the sensitivity is 0.86 and specificity is 0.92 for the mITT population.
L528: Subgroup analyses were conducted for the mITT population.
L529: For the BMI &lt; 30kg / M2 group: AUC = .892, lower bound of 99% CI is 0.975. Sensitivity is 77% and specificity is 85% with cut-point 6.8 ng/mL.
L530: For the BMI &gt;= 30kg / M2 group: AUC = .939, lower bound of 99% CI is 0.851. Sensitivity is 83% and specificity is 96% with cut-point 2.7 ng/mL.
L531: For the 52 subject with both Macimorelin (AEZS-130) and L-ARG+GHRH (the comparator). The AUC value (95% CI) is 0.986 (0.961, 1) and 0.929 (0.848, 1) for Macimorelin and L-ARG, respectively. With the optimal cut-point 4.3 ng/mL for Macimorelin, the sensitivity is 0.91 (95% CI: 0.77, 0.97), and the specificity is 1.00 (95% CI: 0.69, 1.00). With the optimal cut-point 7.4 ng/mL for L-ARG, the sensitivity is 0.86 (95% CI: 0.71, 0.95), and the specificity is 0.90 (95% CI: 0.55, 1.00).
L532: The intended patient population is not clear and the performance of Macimorelin for the intended patient population is unknown from the pivotal study.
L533: 5.3 Conclusions and Recommendations
L534: The statistical results in terms of accuracy do not provide enough evidence to support the claims proposed in this NDA. The sponsor failed in the pre-specified primary ROC AUC analysis. The ROC AUC results may be biased because of possible confounding of the test with race and study site. The sensitivity and specificity results are not consistent in different subject groups (previously vs. newly enrolled). Different cut-points can be selected according to the rules (not pre-specified) and the cut-points are selected based on the single data with 100 subjects, which may not be reproducible in another study. We recommend CR with additional confirmatory study with pre-specified cut-point and intended patient population.
