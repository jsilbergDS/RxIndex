L10: ## 1. Benefit-Risk Assessment
L11: NDA 210656 was submitted under the 505(b)(1) pathway for the new molecular entity glasdegib, a small molecule that interacts with Smoothened and inhibits the hedgehog signaling pathway. The indication proposed by the applicant was
L12: Evidence of clinical benefit came from the Phase 2 portion of Study B1371003 (NCT01546038), which was a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial of glasdegib 100 mg daily on days 1 to 28 with low-dose cytarabine (LDAC) 20 mg subcutaneously twice daily on days 1 to 10 of a 28-day cycle or LDAC alone in 28-day cycles. Eligible patients were &gt; 55 years old with newly-diagnosed AML or high-risk MDS who fulfilled one of the following criteria: at least 75 years old, had severe cardiac disease (LVEF &lt; 45%), baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status (ECOG PS) of 2, or had a baseline serum creatinine &gt; 1.3 mg/dL. The trial overall was positive, but there were few patients with MDS accrued, so efficacy could be established only in the subgroup with AML.
L13: The Efficacy Assessment Population included 115 patients with newly-diagnosed AML (77 on glasdegib + LDAC and 38 on LDAC alone). The median age was 76 years (range, 58-92 years); 61% were \ge 75 years old, 71% were male and 98% were Caucasian. The study showed a survival benefit (OS) for the combination arm (HR 0.46; 95% CI 0.30, 0.71; p=0.0002) with median OS 8.3 months vs 4.3 months in the control arm. The treatment effect on OS was supported by a higher CR rate in the combination arm (18.2% vs 2.6%). Of note, these results are not clearly better than expected for standard intensive chemotherapy in younger patients without comorbidities, so the indication will need to be clear about the benefit being applicable only to those with such comorbidities. Moreover, the accrued population did not include patients with severe renal or hepatic impairment, and no studies have been performed to determine safe dosing with severe renal or hepatic impairment. Since these are clear comorbidities that would preclude use of intensive chemotherapy, labeling should be clear about this knowledge gap, and additional studies will be required postmarketing to address the knowledge gap.
L14: Safety data were available for 469 individuals exposed to glasdegib in the randomized trial, in other trials for patients with hematological malignancies or solid tumors, or in healthy volunteers. The safety profile of glasdegib was similar to that established for other hedgehog inhibitors. Cumulative toxicities identified included electrolyte, hepatic, muscle and renal parameters, and these risks would need to be addressed with monitoring recommendations and dose modifications in labeling. Based on nonclinical data, a thorough QT study, and clinical safety data, QT prolongation was identified as a serious potential risk that warrants a warning in labeling. Additionally, based on its mechanism of action and findings from nonclinical embryo-fetal developmental toxicity studies, glasdegib would be expected to cause embryo-fetal death or severe birth defects when administered to a pregnant woman. This also warrants a warning in labeling. One weakness in the submission was the lack of patient-reported outcomes addressing the impact of the toxicities.

L20: Dimension	Evidence and Uncertainties	Conclusions and Reasons
Analysis of Condition	With supportive care alone, patients with AML survive only weeks.
AML is a fatal disease.
Current Treatment Options	Intensive induction chemotherapy is standard-of-care first-line treatment of AML.
The risks of intensive induction chemotherapy are substantial in patients who have age- or disease-related organ impairment.
Mylotarg and venetoclax are approved for such patients, and azacitidine or decitabine are also used commonly. The response rates and survivals are limited with these treatments.
There is a need for an effective agent to treat AML in patients with comorbidities that preclude use of intensive chemotherapy.
Benefit	Study B1371003 was a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial of glasdegib plus LDAC vs LDAC alone.
The study included a subgroup of patients who were at least 75 years old, or who were > 55 years old with newly-diagnosed AML, had severe cardiac disease, ECOG PS 2, or had a baseline serum creatinine >1.3 mg/dL.
The study showed a survival benefit for the combination arm (HR 0.46; 95% CI 0.30, 0.71; p=0.0002) with median OS 8.3 months vs 4.3 months.
The treatment effect on OS was supported by a higher CR rate in the combination arm (18.2% vs 2.6%).
The accrued population did not include patients with PS of 3 or worse, or those with severe renal or hepatic impairment.
Based on the survival benefit, there is substantial evidence that glasdegib in combination with LDAC is active as first-line treatment of AML in patients with some comorbidities that would preclude use of intensive chemotherapy. Safe dosing has been established for patients with more severe renal or hepatic impairment.
Risks and Risk Management	The most common adverse reactions are anemia, fatigue, hemorrhage, febrile neutropenia, musculoskeletal pain, nausea, edema, thrombocytopenia, dyspnea, decreased appetite, dysgeusia, mucositis, constipation, and rash.
Additional clinically-significant adverse reactions included dental disorders, alopecia, and QT prolongation.
Cumulative toxicities noted were muscle spasms, decreased appetite, low magnesium, elevated creatinine, elevated transaminases and elevated CPK.
The safety profile is similar to that of other hedgehog pathway inhibitors. Warnings are warranted for embryofetal toxicity and QT prolongation. The safety profile is acceptable for the intended population in the short-term with clinical and laboratory monitoring to minimize risks.

L28: ## 2.2 Therapeutic Context
L29: Approximately 20,000 new cases of AML are estimated to occur annually.1 The median age at diagnosis is 68 years, but the disease occurs across the entire age range from neonates to the elderly. The current standard of care for treatment of patients with newly-diagnosed AML includes intensive induction chemotherapy (with midostaurin for those with a FLT3 mutation) when treating with intent to cure.
L30: Published reports in the literature, however, suggest that almost half of adult patients with newly-diagnosed AML are not treated with intensive induction chemotherapy,2 presumably due to a perception that the toxicity of such therapy outweighs potential benefit. L78: ## 4.1 Nonclinical Pharmacology
L79: In an in vitro competitive binding assay, glasdegib competed with a radiolabeled chemical ligand for SMO, a transmembrane protein in the Hh signaling pathway, at an IC50 of 7.7 nM. With regard to downstream effects, glasdegib inhibited Hh signaling via the Gli1 transcription factor with an IC50 of 5.2 nM in a cell-based reporter gene assay, and reduced Shh-stimulated Gli1 mRNA levels at 40 nM in normal human fibroblasts. Glasdegib is considered a hedgehog pathway inhibitor.
L80: Glasdegib was studied in vivo in two AML xenotransplant murine models. In the model using the AML-183 cells, AML cell counts were not reduced in mice given glasdegib alone, minimally reduced in mice given LDAC, and reduced by ~75% in mice given the combination of glasdegib and LDAC. In the model using the AML009 cells, AML cell counts were not reduced in mice given glasdegib alone, and reduced by ~75% in mice given LDAC or the combination.
L81: Among the additional in vitro binding or cell-based functional assays of the activity of glasdegib for other receptors, enzymes, transporters and ion channels, glasdegib was antagonistic of the histamine H1 receptor (Kb 40 nM) and the adenosine A1 receptor (Kb550 nM). Additionally, glasdegib demonstrated a concentration-dependent inhibition of the hERG current in vitro with an IC50 2.8 \muM (1049 ng/mL).
L82: In safety pharmacology studies in rats, glasdegib had no substantial effect on respiratory parameters, and neurological parameters appeared to be affected only at toxic doses. In the cardiovascular safety study in dogs, glasdegib induced QT prolongation in a dose-dependent manner.
L83: ## 4.2 Nonclinical Toxicology
L84: Repeat-dose toxicology studies were conducted with durations of 1 month, 13 weeks and 26 weeks in rats with doses up to 100 mg/kg/day, and with durations of 1 month, 13 weeks and 39 weeks in dogs with doses up to 30 mg/kg/day. The major target organs of toxicity in both species were the kidney, liver, skin and bone; the testis and peripheral nerves were also affected in rats, and the adrenals, gastrointestinal tract, lymph nodes and lungs in dogs. In rats, the effects of glasdegib on bone and teeth were characteristic of SMO inhibitors, including histological changes in epiphyses, decreased medullary trabeculae of femur and sternum bone sections with marrow hypercellularity, and missing or broken teeth. Additional findings included alopecia and muscle tremors/twitching.
L85: Glasdegib was not mutagenic in vitro in the Ames test, and it was not clastogenic in vitro in a human chromosome aberration assay or in vivo in a rat bone marrow micronucleus test. Carcinogenicity studies were not conducted.

L93: ## 5.1 Pharmacokinetics
L94: The pharmacokinetics evaluation showed:
L95: - Glasdegib is associated with dose-proportional glasdegib peak concentrations (C_{max}) and area-under-the-curve over the dosing interval (AUC_{tau}) over the range of 5 mg to 600 mg once daily (0.05 to 6 times the recommended dose).
- Steady-state plasma levels are reached by 8 days of daily dosing. The median accumulation ratio of glasdegib ranged from 1.2 to 2.5 following once-daily dosing.
- At 100 mg once daily, the geometric mean (geometric coefficient of variation, % CV) of glasdegib C_{max} was 1252 ng/mL (44%) and AUC_{tau} was 17210 ng*hr/mL (54%) in patients with cancer.
- The mean absolute bioavailability of glasdegib is 77%. Following 100 mg once daily dosing, glasdegib median time to peak concentrations (T_{max}) at steady-state ranged from 1.3 hours to 4.1 hours.
- A high-fat, high-calorie meal reduced area under the curve over time to infinity (AUC_{inf}) by 16% and C_{max} by 31%.
- Glasdegib is 91% bound to human plasma proteins in vitro. The geometric mean (% CV) apparent volume of distribution (V_{z}/F) was 188 L (20%) in patients with hematologic malignancies.
- Glasdegib has a mean (\pm SD) half-life of 17.4 h (3.7 h) and geometric mean (% CV) apparent clearance of 6.45 L/h (25%) following 100 mg once daily dosing in patients with hematologic malignancies.

L110: ## 5.2 Pharmacodynamics
L111: A QTc prolongation effect of glasdegib (single doses of 150 mg and 300 mg) was detected in the thorough QT Study B1371023, and the effect was dose-/concentration-dependent. The largest upper bounds of the 2-sided 90% CI for the mean difference between glasdegib and placebo for QTc were above 10 ms. A warning regarding QT prolongation is recommended, and labeling should indicate that coadministration of QTc-prolonging drugs should be avoided.
L112: ## 5.3 Dose Considerations
L113: The recommended dose of glasdegib is 100 mg orally once daily with or without food and with dose reductions for QT prolongation or severe toxicities. There was no significant exposure-response relationship for OS in patients with AML or MDS who received a dose of 100 mg once daily (Cycle 1 cumulative area-under-the-curve in cycle 1 range of 56 to 1223 \mu\text{g}\cdot\text{h}/\text{mL}). Exposure-safety relationships were significant for dysgeusia, muscle spasms, renal toxicity, and QTc interval prolonged; toxicity is expected to be lower with the 100 mg dose than with higher doses. The Clinical Pharmacology reviewer concluded that the proposed glasdegib dosing regimen is appropriate for the intended population.

L131: ## 7.2 Issues Related to Study B1371003
L132: ## *Design*
L133: The Phase 2 portion of Study B1371003 (NCT01546038) was a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial. Eligible patients were \ge 55 years old with newly-diagnosed AML or high-risk MDS and who fulfilled one of the following criteria: at least 75 years old, had severe cardiac disease (LVEF &lt; 45%), had a baseline Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status of 2, or had a baseline serum creatinine &gt;1.3 mg/dL. Patients were randomized 2:1 to receive glasdegib 100 mg daily days 1 to 28 with low-dose cytarabine (LDAC) 20 mg subcutaneously twice daily on days 1 to 10 of a 28-day cycle or LDAC alone in 28-day cycles until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. There was no planned crossover between arms.
L134: Randomization was stratified by cytogenetic risk (good/intermediate or poor).7 The primary efficacy endpoint was OS, defined as the time from randomization to death from any cause. A sample size of 132 subjects was calculated based on 92 OS events at the final analysis to demonstrate a hazard ratio of 0.625 (median OS 5 months for the control arm and 8 months for the combination arm) with 80% power at a 1-sided alpha of 0.1. The efficacy population was all randomized patients. The stratified log-rank test was used for the analysis of OS.
L135: The following issues were considered regarding the study design:
L136: - The Applicant based the recommended dose of glasdegib 100 mg with LDAC on tolerability of that dose in the combination dose-escalation cohorts, confirmation of modulation of hedgehog pathway signaling at that dose, adequate pharmacokinetics, and a safety margin to account for interactions with CYP3A4 inhibitors used commonly in the intended population.
L137: 7 Patients with inv(3), t(6;9), 11q23, -5, -5q, -7 or abnl (17p) complex karyotype were consider poor risk. Patients with none of the features were classified as having good/intermediate risk disease.

L141: The Clinical Reviewer observed no dose-response relationship in the combination dose-escalation studies, and the Clinical Pharmacology Reviewer reported no exposure-response relationship (Section 5.3 above). It was concluded that the glasdegib dose was justified adequately.

L174: ## 7.4 Efficacy Conclusions
L175: The Statistical Reviewer concluded that the data from Study B1371003 provided adequate evidence to support the claim that glasdegib + LDAC is efficacious and superior to LDAC alone with respect to OS in the overall population and in the subgroup of patients with AML. There was not sufficient data to establish effectiveness in the MDS subgroup, so that subgroup should be excluded from the intended population. The characteristics of the accrued study participants are consistent with the parameters of age and comorbidity that FDA has accepted as defining a population for whom intensive chemotherapy may be precluded, and that should be clear in the indication statement, since there is no evidence that glasdegib + LDAC is superior to 7+3 given with curative intent for those who can tolerate intensive chemotherapy. Study B1371003 enrolled patients only 58 years of age or older, but there are no data suggesting that efficacy as seen in this study would not be applicable to younger patients with comorbidities that preclude use of intensive chemotherapy. In summary, the data would support the indication:
L176: “in combination with low-dose cytarabine for the treatment of newly-diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia (AML) in adult patients who are \ge 75 years old or who have comorbidities that preclude use of intensive induction chemotherapy.”
L177: It should be noted that Study B1371003 accrued no patients with severe renal disease or with moderate-severe hepatic disease, so a survival benefit in such patients cannot be confirmed. This warrants a Limitation of Use.

L214: The issues considered closely included:
L215: - The Clinical Reviewer identified 6 fatal adverse reactions due to glasdegib (sudden death, cardiac arrest, MI, pneumonia, and unknown cause - one each). Overall, however, the early mortality in the combination arm did not appear to be worse than that in the control arm.
- The most common (\ge 5\%) serious adverse reactions in the combination arm were febrile neutropenia (29%) pneumonia (23%), hemorrhage (12%), anemia (7%) and sepsis (7%).
- The most common (\ge 2\%) reasons for dose reductions due to adverse reactions were muscle spasms (5%), fatigue (4%), febrile neutropenia (4%), anemia (2%), thrombocytopenia (2%), and ECG QT prolonged (2%).
- The most common (\ge 2\%) reasons for permanent discontinuation were pneumonia (6%), febrile neutropenia (4%), sepsis (4%), sudden death (2%), myocardial infarction (2%), nausea (2%), and renal insufficiency (2%).
L216: The cardiac-related events, including fatal events, raised the greatest concern, especially considering the known effect of glasdegib on the QTc interval (see Sections 4.1 and 5.2). Of the 98 evaluable patients treated with glasdegib 100 mg in combination with LDAC, 5% were found to have a QTc interval greater than 500 ms and 4% of patients had an increase from baseline QTc greater than 60 ms. The observed major cardiac-related safety events warrant a warning in labeling. Of note, the clinical trial excluded patients with baseline QTc of greater than 470 ms or with a history of long QT syndrome or uncontrolled cardiovascular disease, and this should be clarified in labeling as well.
L217: Although there is no experience with use of glasdegib in the treatment of pregnant patients with AML, based on its mechanism of action and findings from animal embryo-fetal developmental toxicity studies (see Section 4.2), glasdegib would be expected to cause embryo-fetal death or severe birth defects when administered to a pregnant woman. This also warrants a warning in labeling. The requirement of a pregnancy registry was considered, but it was concluded that the risk of pregnancy in the intended population was too low to require such a burden. To confirm that would be the case, however, enhanced pharmacovigilance will be requested for actual or suspected pregnancy and/or embryo-fetal exposures postmarketing.
