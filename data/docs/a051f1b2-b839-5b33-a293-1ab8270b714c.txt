Subject: Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy
L5: Title IX, Subtitle A, Section 901 of the Food and Drug Administration Act of 2007 (FDAAA) amends the Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act to authorize FDA to require the submission of a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) if FDA determines that such a strategy is necessary to ensure that the benefits of the drug outweigh the risks (section 505-1(a)). Section 505-1(a) provides the following factors:
L6: (A) The estimated size of the population likely to use the drug involved;

(B) The seriousness of the disease or condition that is to be treated with the drug;

(C) The expected benefit of the drug with respect to such disease or condition;

(D) The expected or actual duration of treatment with the drug;

(E) The seriousness of any known or potential adverse events that may be related to the drug and the background incidence of such events in the population likely to use the drug;

(F) Whether the drug is a new molecular entity.
L7: After consultations between the Office of New Drugs and the Office of Surveillance and Epidemiology, we have determined that a REMS that includes elements to assure safe use is necessary for PROMACTA to ensure that the benefits of the drug outweigh the following risks: 1) risk of hepatotoxicity; 2) risk for marrow fibrosis; 3) risk for hemorrhage following discontinuation of eltrombopag due to worsened thrombocytopenia than was present at baseline; 4) risk for thrombotic/thromboembolic complications due to excessive platelet counts; 5) risk for hematologic malignancy. In reaching this determination, we considered the following:
L8: A. The prevalence rate of chronic (adult) immune thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) was estimated at 24 cases per 100,000 persons or 52,700 adult cases in the United States based on the 2005 census estimates. This prevalence (published in International Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis by Feudjo-Tepie et al in 2008) was based upon analyses of the Integrated
L9: Healthcare Information System (IHCIS) database, one of the largest US health care managed databases. McMillan (Ann Intern Med: 1997) estimates that 30% of chronic ITP cases are "refractory" and this subset is the group of patients who are most likely to receive eltrombopag. Hence, approximately 16,000 patients in the US (based on 2005 census) are general candidates for PROMACTA therapy, as indicated.
L10: B. PROMACTA is indicated to treat a very serious stage of chronic ITP that is generally regarded as so serious that patients are at imminent risk of death due to hemorrhage.

C. PROMACTA has been shown to be effective in 60 to 70% of treated chronic ITP patients. This treatment effect is a remarkable response since these patients had failed most (if not all) prior therapies. The specific treatment benefit is an increase in blood platelet counts to a level that lessens the risk for serious hemorrhage.

D. PROMACTA therapy is anticipated as life-long (many years) therapy for many patients since the indicated patient population is one that generally has no other long term therapeutic options and chronic ITP rarely spontaneously resolves.

E. The serious adverse events signaled in the clinical development of PROMACTA are risks for hepatotoxicity and risk for hemorrhage following discontinuation of PROMACTA due to worsened thrombocytopenia than was present at baseline. Hepatotoxicity is not an event with an established background incidence in patients with chronic ITP. Hence, hepatotoxicity in the PROMACTA development program provides strong evidence of a drug-related effect.
L11: A review describing the clinical manifestations of chronic ITP notes that almost all manifestations of chronic ITP relate to thrombocytopenia, hemorrhage and bone marrow alterations. (New England Journal of Medicine 2002:346; 995-1007). The worsened thrombocytopenia has been hypothesized as due to suppression of intrinsic thrombopoietin (TPO) levels by PROMACTA therapy.
L12: PROMACTA also has potentially serious risks due to the risks associated with other members of the class of thrombopoietin products. These risks include the following: a risk for hematologic malignancy due to stimulation of the thrombopoietin receptor (a risk supported by *in vitro* studies); a risk for thrombotic/thromboembolic risks associated with excessive platelet counts (as may occur either due to medication errors or as patient-specific responses to the drug) as well as a risk for bone marrow fibrosis (as documented by animal studies of thrombopoietin receptor products).
L13: The background incidence of hematologic malignancy, bone marrow fibrosis or thrombosis in chronic ITP is not known, since most patients either respond to initial therapy with prednisone or require more aggressive immunosuppressive medications or splenectomy and these therapies may alter the risks. In a long term (median of 92 months), follow-up study of 402 patients who underwent splenectomy (the patient population potentially at greatest risk for malignancy or marrow fibrosis), only 2% of patients experienced thromboses, malignancy occurred in 3%, and marrow fibrosis was not reported (Haematologica 2005: 90 (1); Vianelli et al). Together these data suggest that the risk for marrow fibrosis, malignancy and thrombosis are relatively low within the subset of patients who undergo splenectomy for chronic ITP.
L14: ## F. PROMACTA is a new molecular entity.
L15: In accordance with section 505-1 of the FDCA, as one element of a REMS, FDA may require the development of a Medication Guide as provided for under 21 CFR Part 208. Under 21 CFR 208, the sponsor is responsible for ensuring that the Medication Guide is available for distribution to patients who are dispensed PROMACTA. Pursuant to 21 CFR Part 208, FDA has determined that PROMACTA poses a serious and significant public health concern requiring the distribution of a Medication Guide. The Medication Guide is necessary for patients' safe and effective use of PROMACTA. FDA has determined that PROMACTA is a product that has serious risks of which patients should be made aware because information concerning the risks could affect patients' decisions to use, or continue to use, PROMACTA.
L16: The elements of the REMS will be a Medication Guide, communication plan, elements to assure safe use (including that healthcare prescribers are specially certified, that practitioners and hospitals/institutions that dispense the drug are specially certified, and safe-use conditions), an implementation system, a timetable for submission of assessments, and assessments of the REMS.

L38: ## 1. Introduction:
L39: This New Drug Application (NDA) was submitted for eltrombopag tablets, a new molecular entity within the class of products generally referred to as "thrombopoietin (TPO) receptor agonists." TPO receptor agonists are products that stimulate the TPO receptor on bone marrow megakaryocytes and through a series of biological consequences (cell multiplication, maturation) result in blood platelet production. To date, FDA has approved one member of the TPO receptor agonist class of products—romiplostim, a biological product approved earlier in 2008.
L40: This review will focus upon the regulatory basis for the approval recommendation, particularly considering the relatively "short term" nature of the phase 3 clinical studies and the recommendation for approval under Subpart H.
L41: ## 2. Background:
L42: Clinical investigation of TPO receptor products began in the early 1990's following recombinant-technology-based synthesis of TPO and TPO-mimetic proteins. The initial clinical studies of TPO-mimetic products showed that some patients developed neutralizing antibodies to TPO, including antibodies that neutralized native, intrinsic TPO such that certain healthy volunteers developed thrombocytopenia. Consequently, subsequent product development focused upon the use of TPO receptor agonists that were molecularly designed to minimize the potential for immunogenicity.
L43: In 2008, FDA approved romiplostim, a recombinant TPO receptor agonist that was molecularly engineered to specifically minimize the potential for immunogenicity. The approval of romiplostim was based upon two clinical studies conducted over a six month period of time. Eltrombopag is recommended for approval under the accelerated approval mechanism. This recommendation is based predominantly upon the use of clinical data from two controlled studies in which eltrombopag was administered over six weeks or less; in this situation the relatively "short term" platelet response is viewed as a surrogate endpoint reasonably likely to predict long term benefit. Additionally, the approval is recommended based upon the sponsor's requirement to verify the long term benefit in a controlled clinical study of six months or longer duration.
L44: Eltrombopag is a chemically synthesized molecule that is proposed to bind to a transmembrane portion of the TPO receptor and initiate downstream processes within bone marrow megakaryocytes that ultimately result in platelet production. Eltrombopag is administered as a tablet and the dose is titrated to achieve and maintain the desired platelet count response.
L45: During the clinical development of eltrombopag, the company performed an exploratory clinical study that demonstrated eltrombopag prompted platelet count increases in healthy subjects. These drug-stimulated platelets had *in vitro* platelet function characteristics generally typical of platelets. Hence, this study supported the generally accepted use of
L46: platelet counts as an "accepted" measure of clinical benefit for clinical studies of TPO receptor agonists among patients with chronic ITP.
L47: The clinical development of eltrombopag for chronic ITP proceeded along a pathway focused upon two indications, one related to a "short term" indication (for example, use of the product around the time of a surgical procedure) and another related to a "long term" indication. Specifically, two phase 3 clinical studies of six weeks duration were submitted with this current application in order to support a "short term" indication. The company has recently completed a phase 3 study (referred to as the RAISE study) in which patients received study drugs over a six month period of time. The results of the RAISE study are proposed to address the Subpart H considerations, specifically the need for a randomized, controlled study that verifies the ability of eltrombopag to maintain platelet counts over a prolonged (at least six months) period of time.
L48: As described below, the "short term" clinical studies demonstrated that eltrombopag stimulated platelet counts among a population of patients with relatively refractory chronic ITP. However, discontinuation of eltrombopag at the end of the study resulted in an unacceptable amount of serious hemorrhage. Hence, one of the major findings in this NDA review was that the "short term" indication proposal was not a reasonable clinical goal among patients with chronic ITP who had few, if any alternative drug therapies.
L49: The review team worked with the company to develop an indication for eltrombopag to be used as a chronic ITP therapy among relatively refractory patients. This indication is based upon the two phase 3 clinical studies of six weeks duration plus the supportive information from the single arm extension study. To reiterate, the recommendation for Subpart H approval is based upon:
L50: - use of the "short term" platelet count response as a surrogate marker for longer platelet count responses (platelet counts are recognized as acceptable measures of clinical benefit for patients with chronic ITP)
- chronic ITP is a serious medical condition
- eltrombopag approval would provide a meaningful therapeutic benefit to patients over existing treatments because of its minimal risk for immunogenicity (based upon the small molecule characteristics). The labeling for romiplostim, the only currently marketed TPO receptor agonist, includes information regarding the risks for immunogenicity. These risks are not applicable to eltrombopag.
L51: During this review cycle, a major amendment was assessed (related to clinical data from the extension study) that resulted in a longer review time line. Nevertheless, the pdufa goal was exceeded, in large part due to the challenges associated with development of the Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) document.
L52: ## 3. Chemistry, Manufacturing and Controls:
L53: I concur with the conclusions reached by the chemistry reviewer regarding the acceptability of the manufacturing of the drug product and drug substance. Manufacturing site inspections were acceptable. Stability testing supports an expiry of 36 months. There are no outstanding issues.
L54: ## 4. Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology:
L55: I concur with the conclusions reached by the pharmacology/toxicology reviewer that there are no outstanding pharm/tox issues that preclude approval. Importantly, most of the nonclinical studies were conducted among animal species that did not respond to eltrombopag with an increase in platelet count. Hence, the animal data were interpreted with the knowledge of this important limitation and the inability of these data to fully model the anticipated human response.
L56: ## 5. Clinical Pharmacology/Biopharmaceutics:
L57: I concur with the conclusions reached by the clinical pharmacology/biopharmaceutics reviewer that there are no outstanding clinical pharmacology issues that preclude approval. The clinical pharmacology team's findings were similar to those of the company in that eltrombopag appears to importantly undergo metabolism in the liver and the drug is vulnerable to drug interactions. Additionally, eltrombopag may function as a chelator with certain compounds in the diet (such as calcium—chelation within the intestine may impair absorption of the complex), such that dietary intake can importantly change eltrombopag absorption. These limitations are described in considerable length within the eltrombopag labeling.
L58: Additionally, limited clinical data suggested that a 25 mg dose of eltrombopag was appropriate initial therapy for patients of Asian ethnicity as well as patients with moderate or severe liver impairment. The recommended initial eltrombopag dose is 50 mg for other patients.
L59: ## 6. Clinical Microbiology:
L60: Eltrombopag is a tablet for oral administration. Hence, microbiology concerns are negligible and a microbiology review was not performed.
L61: ## 7. Clinical/Statistical-Efficacy:
L62: As previously noted, this NDA was originally submitted with a request for a "short term" usage of eltrombopag (as may be necessitated by the need for elective surgery among patients who are thrombocytopenic). In support of this "short term" indication, two phase 3 clinical studies were submitted. These studies are referred to as Studies 773A and 773B, as described below.
L63: ## a) Design of phase 3 studies:
L64: Study 773 A and Study 773B were randomized, double-blind, placebo controlled studies conducted among adult patients with chronic ITP who had baseline platelet counts &lt; 30,000/mL despite completion of at least one prior ITP therapy. Eligible patients included those who had, as well as those who had not, undergone splenectomy. Patients were to receive the study drugs daily for no more than six weeks with termination of all dosing if a platelet count exceeded 200,000/mL. Patients were observed for six weeks following discontinuation of the study drug.
L65: The primary endpoint in each study was the same: a "response" defined as a platelet count \ge 50,000/mL by day 43 or drug discontinuation due to a platelet count &gt; 200,000/mL. Multiple secondary endpoints explored various other measures of platelet count responses, as well as changes in the World Health Organization (WHO) bleeding scores.
L66: In Study 773 A, patients were randomized among four groups: placebo or eltrombopag at one of three dose regimens (30 mg, 50 mg or 75 mg) with the study drugs administered daily.
L67: In Study 773B, patients were randomized between placebo and eltrombopag administered as 50 mg daily. The study drug could be increased to 75 mg daily, if patients failed to experience a predefined increase in platelet counts.
L68: ## ***b) Results of the phase 3 studies:***
L69: Both phase 3 studies generally enrolled patients who had undergone multiple prior ITP therapies and who appeared to need an option for chronic ITP therapy. Notably, the patients were not selected based upon any specific need for "short term" therapy with a new ITP treatment. Overall, the median age was 50 years in the two studies and 60% were female. The median baseline platelet count was 18,000/mL among all groups in the two studies.

L80: During the phase 3 studies, only seven patients underwent "hemostatic challenge" (such as a surgical procedure), consisting of three placebo group patients and four eltrombopag group patients. Additional ITP medications were required in all placebo group patients but none of the eltrombopag group patients. Only one patient needed a blood transfusion (a placebo group patient).
L81: One of the secondary endpoints that received considerable attention during the review was the data obtained from baseline and follow-up WHO bleeding scale scores. This scale is a scale that assigns numeric grades (0 through 4), based upon subjective assessments of the extent of bleeding at any time point. The scale has not been verified for clinical meaningfulness, especially with respect to incremental changes (for example, a change from a grade 2 to a grade 1). The limitations of the scale were exemplified by the study findings, in that all changes were within the lowest grades (0 through 2) and largely by a transition from "petechaie" (grade 1) to the absence of petechaie (grade 0). Given the unclear clinical meaningfulness of the scale and the relatively trivial changes detected during the study, the review team regarded the WHO bleeding scale as solely exploratory data.
L82: In addition to these phase 3 clinical studies, an open label, single arm study of 109 patients who had participated in an earlier eltrombopag clinical study showed that eltrombopag could achieve and maintain platelet counts above 50,000/mcL in a sizable proportion of patients. The most common eltrombopag dosage was 50 mg daily in this study, a finding that supports the recommended dosage in the eltrombopag label.
L83: I concur with the major findings from the statistical and clinical review team.
L84: ## 8. Safety:
L85: Overall, 313 patients with chronic ITP were exposed to eltrombopag, including 81 patients exposed for at least six months and 39 patients exposed for at least one year.
L86: The major safety findings related to a risk for hepatotoxicity and hemorrhage following eltrombopag discontinuation. Additional concerns related to a risk for cataract formation/worsening as well as the general anticipated risks for all members of the TPO receptor/agonist product class.
L87: ## a) Hepatotoxicity:
L88: The risk for hepatotoxicity prompted the development of a boxed warning for eltrombopag. In the two phase 3 clinical studies, one patient experienced grade 4 (CTC scale) elevations in serum liver tests during eltrombopag therapy. The patient had extensive underlying disease (chronic obstructive lung disease and heart disease) but liver test progressively increased during eltrombopag therapy, including marked increases immediately prior to hospitalization and his subsequent death. The autopsy findings did not show explicit findings of drug damage to the liver but the overall clinical pattern of the events suggested that eltrombopag hepatotoxicity could have importantly contributed to the patient's worsened condition and death. No grade 4 liver test elevations occurred in the placebo groups.
L89: Overall, serum liver test abnormalities (predominantly grade 2 or less in severity) were reported in 10% and 8% of the eltrombopag and placebo groups, respectively.
L90: The clinical studies showed that, with close monitoring of patients who had eltrombopag discontinued because of liver test abnormalities, eltrombopag could be safely introduced for a second attempt at therapy. The liver test findings from the clinical studies prompted the label citation to a need for regular liver test monitoring during eltrombopag therapy (every two weeks during the dose adjustment phase and then monthly).
L91: ## b) Serious hemorrhage following eltrombopag discontinuation:
L92: In the phase 3 studies, patients were followed for six weeks after eltrombopag discontinuation. This post-therapy observation period revealed the problem with use of eltrombopag solely as a "short term" therapy among patients with chronic ITP. Specifically, seven eltrombopag group patients experienced a serious hemorrhagic event and five of these patients experienced the event while "off therapy." Only one placebo group patient experienced a serious hemorrhage event (an event that occurred while "on therapy"). Hence, the studies importantly signaled that "short term" therapy was inappropriate among patients who had few, if any, alternative drug therapies for chronic ITP.
L93: ## c) Cataracts:
L94: In animal studies, eltrombopag was associated with the development of cataracts during repetitive drug administration. In the controlled clinical studies, cataracts developed or worsened in 5% of patients who received eltrombopag 50 mg daily and 3% of placebo group patients. The eltrombopag label describes the risk for cataract and recommends a baseline examination for cataract along with regular monitoring of cataract signs and symptoms.
L95: ## d) Other risks common to TPO receptor products:
L96: Eltrombopag shares risks common to other TPO receptor products, consisting of a risk for:
L97: - thrombosis due to excessive platelet count increases (platelet counts must be monitored regularly during therapy);
- a risk for hematologic malignancy, especially among patients with myelodysplasia syndrome (based upon *in vitro* observations that the TPO receptor may function in a form of tumor promotion/facilitation; additionally limited clinical data signals a malignancy risk);
- bone marrow reticulin formation and risk for marrow fibrosis; in the extension study, seven patients had reticulin fiber deposition reported in bone marrow biopsies, including two patients who also had collagen fiber deposition.
L98: The extent of safety concerns have necessitated development of:
L99: - labeling to include a boxed warning for hepatotoxicity
- a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) that provides for physician and patient education, collection of major safety outcome data in a registry-type format and restriction of prescription/dispensation to prescribers and facilities enrolled in a special program called "*Promacta Cares*."

L156: ## Brief Overview of Clinical Program
L157: - Eltrombopag is an orally bioavailable small molecule thrombopoietin receptor (TPO-R) agonist that interacts with the transmembrane domain of TPO-R and initiates a signaling cascade similar, but not identical, to that of endogenous thrombopoietin.
- Eltrombopag is indicated for the short-term treatment of previously treated patients with chronic idiopathic thrombocytopenic purpura (ITP) to increase platelet counts and reduce or prevent bleeding.
- The safety and efficacy of eltrombopag was evaluated in two randomized, double-blind pivotal studies: TRA 100773A and TRA 100773B.
- Study TRA 100773A and enrolled 118 patients. In this study 30 patients were enrolled in the placebo group as well as the 30 mg and 50 mg eltrombopag treated groups. There were 28 patients enrolled in the 75 mg eltrombopag group.
- Study TRA 100773B enrolled 38 patients into the placebo group and 76 patients into the 50 mg eltrombopag treated patient group.
- At the time of the NDA submission 269 patients had been exposed to eltrombopag during the clinical development program. At the time of the 120 day safety update an additional 61 patients had been exposed to eltrombopag. At the time of the 120 day safety update is estimated that 150 patients had been exposed to eltrombopag for \ge six months.
L158: ## Efficacy
L159: The study was a double-blind, randomized, multi-institutional, placebo controlled, parallel group, repeat-dose study conducted in 2 parts (part A and part B) and at essentially the same study sites. Part A was a phase 2 study while part B was a Phase 3 study. Screening was to take place up to 28 days before dosing. The “on-dose” phase of the study (both part A and B) was to involve once daily dosing of eltrombopag for 6 weeks. The primary efficacy endpoint was increased in platelet count to \ge 50,000/\text{mcl} on day 43 after starting study treatment. On completion of the dosing period, subjects were to undergo follow-up assessments for 6 weeks following the final dose. The six week follow up period was proposed by the sponsor to assess the durability of the platelet response.
L160: The study was to be conducted in adult subjects with refractory chronic ITP, defined as patients who have failed at least one previous treat for ITP, including corticosteroids, immunoglobulins, immunomodulators and/or splenectomy. Patients receiving maintenance corticosteroid therapy could participate providing their corticosteroid dose had been stable for at least 1 month prior to screening. A sufficient number of subjects were to be enrolled so that a total of 422 subjects complete this 2 part study, i.e. 272 subjects in part A and 150 subjects in part B. The sponsor was able to enroll 30 patients each into the placebo, 30 and 50 mg dose cohorts and 28 patients in the 75mg dose cohort for part A of the study (total 118 patients). In part B of the study the sponsor was able to enroll 38 patients into the placebo arm and 76 patients into the 50mg dose arm (total 114 patients).
L161: In study TRA 100773A analysis of the primary endpoint showed that eltrombopag gave a dose dependent increase in the proportion of patients with chronic ITP who achieved a platelet count of \ge 50,000/\text{mcl} on day 43 starting from a baseline platelet count &lt; 30,000/\text{mcl}: 11% (3/27) of
L162: patients on placebo, 28% (8/29) of patients on 30 mg of eltrombopag, 70% (9/27) of patients on 50 mg of eltrombopag and 81% (21/26) of patients on 75 mg of eltrombopag. Patients treated with eltrombopag 50 mg or 75 mg had statistically significant greater odds of responding at day 43 compared to patients treated with placebo (p&lt;0.001). Median platelet counts for patients treated with eltrombopag 50 mg or 75 mg were higher than for patients treated with placebo at each on therapy visit. Median platelet counts in all eltrombopag treatment groups returned to baseline within two weeks of discontinuation of therapy. The sponsor reported that there was a statistically significant decrease in the percentage of eltrombopag 50 mg and 75 mg treated patients (16/27 and 12/26 patients, respectively) with any bleeding observed during the on therapy phase of the trial compared to placebo (22/27 patients). The percentage of patients with any bleeding returned to baseline levels during the off therapy phase (26/27 in the 50 mg treatment group and 26/26 in the 75 mg treatment group compared to 25/27 in the placebo treatment group). WHO grade 2-4 bleeding occurred in 5/26 (19%) of placebo treated patients and 9/39 (23%) of eltrombopag treated patients who were nonresponders compared to 3/48 (6%) of eltrombopag treated patients who were considered responders. Statistically significant interactions were observed between study medication and the use or non-use of concomitant ITP medication at the time of randomization. Logistic regression analysis showed an interaction at the 10% level of significance between the response to treatment and the use of ITP medication at randomization (p=0.093). In the subgroup of patients who did not report the use of concomitant ITP medication, a dose-dependent increase in the proportion of responders was observed. A similar dose response was observed in the subgroup of patients who did report concomitant use of the ITP medications; however, the proportion of responders in the 75 mg treatment group (6/26) did not increase to the same degree as in the 50 mg treatment group (8/27). A statistically significant interaction was also observed between the study medication and baseline platelet count at randomization. Logistic regression analysis showed an interaction at the 10% level of significance between the response to treatment and baseline platelet count at randomization (p=0.042). In both subgroups (\le 15,000/\text{mcl} and &gt; 15,000/\text{mcl}) the proportion of responders increased in a dose-dependent manner. However the proportion of responders was higher for the 50 mg and 75 mg treatment groups with baseline platelet counts &gt; 15,000/\text{mcl} (13/16 and 12/12 patients respectively) compared to the subgroup with platelet counts \le 15,000/\text{mcl} (6/11 of patients and 9/14 patients respectively). The interaction between treatment and splenectomy status was not statistically significant. There was no statistically significant difference in the response to treatment when assessed for subgroups age, sex and race. However, data interpretation is limited due to the small number of patients in the race subgroup.
L163: In study TRA 100773B analysis of the primary endpoint showed that eltrombopag increases platelet counts in patients with relapsed or refractory chronic ITP. In this study 16.2% (6/37) of patients on the placebo treatment arm compared to 58.9% (43/73) of patients on the eltrombopag treatment arm achieved a platelet count \ge 50,000/\text{mcl} on day 43 from a baseline platelet count &lt; 30,000/\text{mcl}. Treatment with eltrombopag should be discontinued if the platelet count is above 200,000/mcl. The sponsor proposes that 25 mg orally once daily, ________ for patients of the East Asian ancestry. The support for the dosing regimen is established in the pharmacology program and pivotal trials of eltrombopag. However, whether patients of East Asian ancestry should be started on a lower dose of eltrombopag is based on population pharmacokinetic studies done in Japan. Similar studies performed in other Asian countries did not show a clear increase in exposure after dosing of eltrombopag in volunteers in these Asian countries. L195: ## Drug-Drug Interactions
L196: Drug-Drug interaction studies were performed during the development program for eltrombopag. These studies showed that eltrombopag should be administered to patients in a fasted state or with foods low in calcium to avoid significant reductions in plasma eltrombopag exposure. Eltrombopag chelates with polyvalent cations such as aluminum, calcium, iron, magnesium and zinc. Administration of a single dose of 75 mg of eltrombopag they polyvalent cation containing antacid decreased plasma eltrombopag AUC and Cmax by 70%. Therefore, in order to avoid clinically significant reductions in plasma eltrombopag exposure, eltrombopag should not be given concurrently with other products containing polyvalent cations such as mineral supplements, antacids or dairy products. The doses should be separated by at least — hours.
L197: Administration of eltrombopag 75 mg once daily for five days with a single dose of rosuvastatin 10 mg increased plasma rosuvastatin Cmax by 2.03 fold and AUC by 55%. Therefore when coadministered with eltrombopag a reduced dose of rosuvastatin should be considered and careful monitoring for rosuvastatin adverse effects should be undertaken. Eltrombopag is an
L198: inhibitor of the anion transporting polypeptide OATP1B1. Therefore concomitant administration of eltrombopag and other OATP1B1 substrates should be used with caution.

L201: Native TPO is a 60-70 k Dalton heavily glycosylated polypeptide of 332 amino acids produced by the liver, bone marrow and sinusoidal epithelial kidney cells.
L202: The diagnosis of ITP remains one of exclusion. The routine diagnostic tools are complete blood count, peripheral blood film, patient history, physical examination and prompt response to high-dose steroids. In the diagnosis of ITP, the most important clinical finding is thrombocytopenia. The peripheral blood film is used to help confirm the thrombocytopenia and help rule out other potential causes of the thrombocytopenia. The patient's history may include the complaint of easy bruising or bleeding. Physical examination can show evidence of bleeding or bruising and may rule out hypersplenism as the cause of the thrombocytopenia. Some patients may undergo a bone marrow biopsy to evaluate for platelet production problems as the cause of the thrombocytopenia. A typical bone marrow finding in ITP is increased megakaryocyte count. In a European study the overall incidence of ITP among adults ranges from 1.6-3.0 per 100,000 person years of observation.2 Prevalence estimates ranges from 2.1 to approximately 36.4 per 100,000 persons.3 There is no reason to expect that ITP in the United States is different compared to any other part of the world. Therefore it is possible to extrapolate the findings from the study above to the United States.
L203: ITP is an autoimmune disorder characterized by autoantibody introduced platelet destruction and reduced platelet production leading to a low peripheral blood platelet count. ITP may be acute (&lt; six-month duration) or chronic (&gt; six-month duration). Acute ITP is predominantly a childhood illness and is generally self-limited. In adults chronic ITP is predominant with an onset that is often insidious and spontaneous recovery is uncommon. The clinical hallmark of the disease is an increased tendency to bleed. Peripheral blood platelets in this disease are considered to be functional however as platelet count decreases to levels \le 10,000/\text{mcl} the risk of spontaneous bleeding increases. Patients with platelet count between 30,000/\text{mcl} and &gt; 10,000/\text{mcl} are generally considered treatment candidates due to a slightly increased risk of spontaneous bleeding or increased risk of bleeding due to potential trauma. Patients with platelet counts between 30,000-50,000/\text{mcl} are generally not treated unless there is evidence of bleeding, increased risk of bleeding due to hemostatic challenge such as surgery or other daily activity of living. Patients with platelet counts \ge 50,000/\text{mcl} generally do not require treatment unless the patient is to undergo a hemostatic challenge which may require a higher platelet count in order for this hemostatic challenge to be undertaken. Normal platelet counts are generally between 150,000-450,000/\text{mcl}.4 The frequency of death from hemorrhage in patients with platelet counts below 30,000/\text{mcl} is estimated to be between 1.6 and 3.9% per patient year although the frequency is influenced by age as well as the degree and duration of thrombocytopenia.5

L221: ## 2.2 Animal Pharmacology/Toxicology
L222: The sponsor states that eltrombopag causes morbidity and mortality in repeat dose oral toxicology studies in mice at 150 mg/kg/day following nine weeks of dosing and in rats at 60 mg/kg/day following two weeks of dosing. Systemic exposure at the above doses in the mouse and rat was 664 \mu\text{g} \cdot \text{h}/\text{ml} and 1044 \mu\text{g} \cdot \text{h}/\text{ml}, respectively. Mortality was observed in rabbits at 200 mg/kg/day after nine days at an AUC of 124 \mu\text{g} \cdot \text{h}/\text{ml}. The mortality observed in rabbits at 5-10 fold lower exposure than the rats is considered by the sponsor to be related to the increased sensitivity of rabbits to gastrointestinal toxicity compared to other preclinical species. Body weight loss or decreased body weight gain (\ge 40 \text{ mg}/\text{kg}/\text{day}) was observed in rats and mice at doses of 300 mg/kg/day. Signs of gastrointestinal disturbance were observed in dogs given doses \ge 100 \text{ mg}/\text{kg}/\text{day}.
L223: At non-tolerated doses \ge 60 mg/kg/day in rats and dogs and mice at 300 mg/kg/day, these animals had eye degeneration and/or necrosis accompanied by markedly increased serum liver enzymes. In dogs only increased serum liver enzymes was observed. A microscopic change in the rat liver (midzonal hepatocellular vacuolation) was evident after 14 days at doses \ge 20 mg/kilograms/day (AUC 341 \mug.h/ml). In the 52-week oral toxicity study in dogs there were no adverse treatment related findings at no adverse event level doses up to 30 mg/kg/day. Treatment related cataracts were observed in mice given 75 or 150 mg/kg/day and rats given 40 mg/kg/day. In these rodents the ocular changes were observed after seven weeks of treatment. Given the sensitivity of the mouse to treatment related ocular effects at exposure levels approximately twofold of the clinical exposure, the sponsor recommends that ophthalmologic examinations be performed as part of the routine safety monitoring.
L224: Data from genotoxicity assessments suggest that eltrombopag does not present a genotoxic hazard to humans. An *in vitro* phototoxicity study was conducted and eltrombopag was observed to be toxic in the presence of ultraviolet illumination. These results suggest a potential risk for phototoxicity in humans.
L225: Eltrombopag did not affect female fertility at doses up to 20 mg/kg/day in rats and was not teratogenic in rats or rabbits. However, at a maternally toxic dose of 60 mg/kilograms/day in rats, eltrombopag treatment was associated with embryo lethality in female fertility studies. Eltrombopag did not appear to affect male rat fertility at doses up to 40 mg/kilograms/day.
L226: A single dose safety and pharmacology study was conducted in female chimpanzees with eltrombopag given orally at doses up 0.3, 1, 3 and 10 mg/kilogram. The sponsor reports that all doses were well tolerated and that there were no remarkable hematologic or clinical chemistry findings up to 15 days after dosing. A repeat dose safety and pharmacology study was conducted in female chimpanzees in which doses up to 10 mg/kilograms/day were administered for five days orally. All doses appear to be well tolerated. The platelet counts increased up to twofold above baseline.

L284: Eltrombopag is absorbed with a peak concentration occurring 2 to 6 hours after oral administration. Polyvalent cation-containing antacids reduced plasma eltrombopag AUC_{0-\infty} by 70% and a meal with dairy- and calcium-containing products decreased plasma eltrombopag AUC_{0-\infty} by 59%. The absolute oral bioavailability of eltrombopag after administration of PROMACTA in humans has not been established. Based on urinary excretion and biotransformation products eliminated in feces, the oral absorption of drug-related material following administration of a single 75 mg solution dose was estimated to be at least 52%. Eltrombopag is highly bound to human plasma proteins (&gt;99%). Eltrombopag is not a substrate for p-glycoprotein or OATP1B1. Absorbed eltrombopag is extensively metabolized, predominately through pathways including cleavage, oxidation, and conjugation with glucuronic acid, glutathione, or cysteine. In a human radiolabel study, eltrombopag accounted for approximately 64% of plasma radiocarbon AUC_{0-\infty}. Minor metabolites, each accounting for &lt;10% of the plasma radioactivity, due to glucuronidation and oxidation were also detected. The predominant route of eltrombopag excretion was via feces (59%), and 31% of the dose was found in the urine. Unchanged eltrombopag in feces accounted for approximately 20% of the dose; unchanged eltrombopag was not detectable in urine. The plasma elimination half-life of eltrombopag is approximately 21 to 32 hours. Plasma eltrombopag exposure was approximately 70% higher in East Asian (i.e., Japanese, Chinese, Taiwanese, and Korean) subjects as compared to non-East Asian subjects who were predominantly Caucasian. Based on a human study with radiolabeled eltrombopag, glucuronidation plays a minor role in the metabolism of eltrombopag. Human liver microsome studies identified UGT1A1 and UGT1A3 as the enzymes responsible for eltrombopag glucuronidation. Eltrombopag inhibited several UGT enzymes in vitro. Clinically significant drug interactions involving glucuronidation are not anticipated due to the limited contribution of individual UGT enzymes to the glucuronidation of eltrombopag and potential co-medications.

Therefore it was recommended that the dosing of the study drug be discontinued when the platelet count was \ge 150,000/\text{mcl}.
- In order to fully demonstrate a robust efficacy for the drug the sponsor should have as a primary endpoint the proportion of subjects with platelet counts of \ge 50,000/\text{mcl}. The sponsor should perform a secondary analysis for those subjects who achieve a platelet count \ge 50,000/\text{mcl} and at least two times baseline for all subjects enrolled in the study.
