L12: I. SUMMARY
L13: The purpose of this addendum is to update the clinical review of NDA 208,794 dated January 12, 2017. The updated issues are listed as follows:
L14: • Upon extensive review of the literature, a role of tryptophan hydroxylase in niacin synthesis was not identified. Therefore, we conclude that inhibition of this enzyme by Xermelo does not cause niacin deficiency in patients with carcinoid tumors.• Detailed information in the risk/benefit framework is also provided.
L15: II. ADDENDUM
L16: 1) Niacin Deficiency
L17: Background:
L18: We commented in the previous NDA review that “The potential risk of Xermelo in causing niacin deficiency-induced diarrhea was not studied.”
L19: Tryptophan is an essential amino acid and a precursor of both serotonin (5-HT) and niacin biosynthesis. The majority of dietary tryptophan is used for synthesis of secretory 5-HT in patients with carcinoid tumors, causing niacin deficiency in some patients. Although Xermelo inhibits the rate limiting step of 5-HT synthesis catalyzed by tryptophan hydroxylase., we did not identify pellagra symptoms (result of niacin deficiency) including diarrhea, dermatitis, and dementia during the trials.
L20: Addendum:
L21: We conducted an extensive literature search and did not identify a role of tryptophan hydroxylase in niacin synthesis. Therefore, we conclude that inhibition of tryptophan hydroxylase by Xermelo does not cause niacin deficiency.
L22: 2) Xermelo Risk/Benefit Framework
L23: Analysis of Condition and analysis of Current Treatment Options
L24: Disease Background and Scientific Rationale
L25: 1) Carcinoid Syndrome and Multi-mediator Diarrhea
L26: Carcinoid syndrome (CS) occurs when carcinoid tumor cells secrete large amounts of serotonin and other vasoactive products into the systemic circulation. The secretory products include serotonin, kallikrein/bradykinin, prostaglandin, tachykinins, and histamine. In general, symptoms associated with CS include cutaneous flushing, diarrhea, wheezing, abdominal pain, and valvular heart disease. More than 90% of patients with carcinoid syndrome have hepatic metastases, usually from carcinoids of small bowel origin. When tumors are confined to the intestine, the bioactive substances released are metabolized to inactive forms by liver, a “first pass” effect similar to that on oral drugs. However, when tumors invade into hepatic non-portal region, the bioactive secretory products of tumor cells will be released directly into venous circulation and by-pass the “first-pass” effect. Thus, carcinoid syndrome is strongly associated with metastasis to liver.
L27: It is estimated that 80% of symptomatic carcinoid patients present with diarrhea. There are 2 types of CS diarrhea: (1) bioactive products released from carcinoid tumors stimulate secretory diarrhea; and (2) niacin deficiency induced malabsorption diarrhea.
L28: Carcinoid tumors have at least 5 types of bioactive secretory products that may associate with CS diarrhea:
L29: • Serotonin: stimulates intestinal secretion, motility, and decrease of absorption, causing secretory diarrhea.• Kallikrein/Bradykinin: dilates blood vessels, causing flushing, palpitations, low blood pressure, and diarrhea.• Prostaglandin E, and F: cause diarrhea.• Tachykinins: cause diarrhea and inflammation.• Histamine: causes flushing and diarrhea.
L30: CS patients may also have niacin-deficiency diarrhea. Tryptophan is an essential amino acid for niacin synthesis. Normally, 1% of dietary tryptophan is converted to serotonin. In CS patients, 70% of dietary tryptophan is converted to serotonin, which limits the availability of tryptophan for niacin synthesis. Clinically, niacin deficiency presents as pellagra (characterized by dermatitis, and mental disturbance) and diarrhea.
L31: Because CS diarrhea is caused by multiple mediators, and the proportion of 5-HT diarrhea is unknown, “averaged daily BMs” as an endpoint may dilute the signal of Xermelo effectiveness. The pre-specified supplemental endpoint “durable response analysis” should also be used to define the effectiveness of Xermelo.
L32: 2) Mechanisms of CS diarrhea
L33: Mechanisms of CS diarrhea involve activation of electrolyte secretion by colonic epithelial cells. The diarrhea mediators increase the intracellular concentrations of secondary messengers: cAMP, Ca2+, cGMP, protein kinase C (PKC), and calmodulin-dependent kinase (CAMK). The secondary messengers, in turn, stimulate secretion of KCl and NaCl by activation of transport proteins in luminal (CFTR Cl- channels, K+ channels) and basolateral (Na+ - 2Cl--K+ co-transporter, K+ channels, Na+-K+-ATPase) membranes. Therefore, CS diarrhea is a refractory diarrhea as compared to other types of diarrhea, such as malabsorption diarrhea, osmotic diarrhea, exudative diarrhea, and motility disorder diarrhea.
L34: 3) Analysis of Current Treatment Options
L35: Patients with carcinoid syndrome are treated with somatostatin analog octreotide, 150 to 250 mcg subcutaneously three times daily or administered as a long-acting intramuscular depot formulation. The treatment relieves CS diarrhea in 90% of patients for 1 year (Papadakis, 2015)1. After that, many patients stop responding to octreotide. In selected patients with refractory carcinoid syndromes, resection of hepatic metastases may provide improvement. Hepatic artery occlusion, liver-directed debulking procedures, and chemotherapy may also provide symptomatic improvement in patients with hepatic metastases.
L36: 4) Octreotide and Resistance
L37: Octreotide, a 14-aminopeptide somatostatin analog, inhibits the release of gastrointestinal hormones and peptide from carcinoid tumor cells and inhibits carcinoid tumor cell growth. However, 60% of CS patients develop resistance to Octreotide therapy after 3 months. The potential mechanisms of drug resistance include: (1) increase of the number of drug resistant tumor cells under drug selective pressure, and (2) decrease of cell surface somatostatin receptors. Somatostatin receptor is a G-

L43: Clinical Benefit and Risk
L44: 1) Strengths and limitations of the clinical trials, and the clinical relevance of the endpoints
L45: Strengths:
L46: The Applicant has provided substantial evidence of effectiveness to support the approval. The durable responder-analyses show that more than 20% of CS patients who had SSA-refractory diarrhea at baseline had greater than 30% reduction of the BMs/day for over 50% of the 12-week treatment period. Although this was not the primary analysis and was not statistically valid (b) (4) from the clinical reviewer standpoint, these results in treating drug-resistant diarrhea are clinically meaningful.
L47: Limitations:
L48: • The primary analysis results show that the means of differences comparing to the control were -0.81 and -0.83 BMs/day for the 250 mg and 500 mg arms, respectively. Numerically, the reduction of 0.8 BM per day, from baseline are small and do not appear to be clinically significant to the reviewer but are supported by supplemental analyses. Considering CS diarrhea has multiple mediators, the reviewer relies on the non-pre-specified supplemental analysis, durable responder analysis, to further support efficacy and clinical meaningfulness.
• Serotonin is not the only mediator causing CS diarrhea. Study LX301 shows that approximate 60% of the treated patients did not respond to Xermelo treatment.
L49: • Carcinoid tumor is not a common malignancy with only &lt;10% of patients with carcinoid tumor developing carcinoid syndrome. Consequently, this NDA submission is based on clinical trials of limited sample sizes.
L50: 2) Adequacy of Safety Database
L51: The primary safety database consisted of two phase 3 trials (LX301 and LX303 with 211 CS patients).
L52: The integrated safety database includes 14 trials (508 subjects) as follows:
L53: • 9 completed phase 1 studies (N = 259 healthy subjects)• 2 completed phase 2 studies (N = 38 patients with CS)• 2 ongoing phase 3 studies (N = 211 patients with CS)
L54: Because &lt;10% of patients with carcinoid tumors have carcinoid syndrome, the safety database is small.
L55: 3) Safety data from Individual Phase 3 Study
L56: In the original clinical review of NDA 208,794 dated January 12, 2017, we reviewed the pooled safety database. Because the phase 3 clinical trials play an important role in the regulatory approval, the relevant safety data of both Studies LX301 and LX303 are included in the Addendum to the clinical review.

L96: 1 Executive Summary
L97: 1.1. Product Introduction
L98: Xermelo is an oral tryptophan hydroxylase inhibitor. The Applicant Lexicon Pharmaceuticals submitted this original NDA for the indication of (b) (4) treatment of carcinoid syndrome (CS) in adult patients with metastatic neuroendocrine tumors who are refractory to somatostatin analog therapy.
L99: This review primarily assesses the efficacy and safety of two (pivotal study LX301 and confirmative study LX303) randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (PC, 12 weeks) and placebo-free (PF, 36 weeks), multi-center clinical studies (n=135 and n=76, respectively) in patients with metastatic neuroendocrine tumor (NET) and \geq 4 bowel movements (BM)/day on stable somatostatin analog therapy (SAT) (Pivotal Study LX301), and &lt; 4 BM/day plus daily stool consistency \geq 5 on the Bristol Stool form Scale (Confirmative Study LX303). Diarrhea was defined as persistently loose stools for at least 3 months prior to the Run-in Period, despite use of somatostatin analogs (long acting release or depot somatostatin analog therapies).
L100: The two phase 3 studies had a two-stage design: a 12-week placebo control stage and a 36-week open-label extension stage.
L101: In the placebo controlled phase of LX301, 45 patients received Xermelo 250 mg tid, 500 mg tid, and placebo, respectively. In the placebo-free phase, 115 patients received 500 mg Xermelo tid for 36 weeks. All patients were on stable somatostatin analog therapy (SAT) at the same time. Similarly, Study LX303 enrolled 25 patients in the group of Xermelo 250 mg tid, and 500 mg tid, respectively; and 26 patients in the placebo group in the controlled phase; and 67 patients in the placebo-free phase receiving 500 mg Xermelo tid.
L102: The primary endpoint was “the change from baseline in the number of BMs/day averaged over the 12-week DBT Period”. The results show that the arithmetic means of differences comparing to the control were -0.81 and -0.83 BMs/day for the 250 mg and 500 mg arms, respectively. At baseline, the averaged numbers of BMs were 6.1, 5.1, and 5.2 BMs/day for Xermelo 250 mg, 500 mg, and placebo (SSA only), respectively. A statistical method Hodges-Lehmann estimator was used to evaluate the treatment difference between Xermelo and placebo groups. The results show -0.813 and -0.689 for Xermelo 250 mg and 500 mg, respectively. These values suggest significantly greater reductions in daily bowel movement frequency averaged over 12 weeks in both doses of Xermelo treatment as compared to placebo (p &lt; 0.001). Based on exit interviews in patients who completed Study LX301, the proportions who were “very satisfied” with the blinded study drug for treating CS were 0/9 (0%) from the placebo group, 5/9 (56%) from the 250 mg tid group, and 7/15 (47%) from the 500 mg tid group.
L103: Because 5-HT is not the only bioactive secretory product released by carcinoid tumors, a pre-specified supplemental efficacy endpoint “durable clinical response” was used to further characterize the primary endpoint. The durable response was defined as \geq 30\% reduction in BMs/day for \geq 50\% of the 12 weeks of treatment period. The results show that both Xermelo dose groups had larger proportions of responders as compared with the control group. In Study LX301, the durable responders were 44.4% (p=0.011), 42.2% (p=0.020), and 20.0% for Xermelo 250 mg, Xermelo 500 mg, and the control, respectively. Study LX303 had similar results.
L104: The secondary efficacy analysis of the pivotal study (LX301) was based on the changes from baseline of urinary 5-HIAA (5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid) level compared with the control at Week 12. The results show that Xermelo treatment reduced the levels of urinary 5-HIAA at Week 12 from baseline by 30.1 mg/24 hour and 33.8 mg/24 hour as compared to the placebo for Xermelo 250 mg and 500 mg groups, respectively (p &lt; 0.001 for each comparison). Study LX303 had similar results.
L105: In the 36-week PF phase of the pivotal Study LX301, both the patients who crossed over from placebo and the patients who continued on Xermelo received 500 mg tid per day in the presence of SSA therapy. The results show that Xermelo treatment persistently reduced approximately 2 BMs/day from baseline throughout the open-label extension period. For the patients who were crossed over from the placebo group, the baseline BMs was defined as the frequency of BMs at Week 12. The PF phase of Study 303 had similar results.

L126: Disease Background and Scientific Rationale
L127: 1) Carcinoid syndrome and multi-mediator diarrhea
L128: Carcinoid tumors are the most common neuroendocrine tumors of the gastrointestinal tract with an estimated incidence approximate 50,000 cases per year (Yao, 2008). According to the embryonic origin, carcinoid tumor can be divided into 3 types:
L129: (1) Foregut carcinoid:• 30% of patients are symptomatic,• Located at intrathoracic, gastric, and 2/3 duodenal• Some releasing histamine, instead of 5-HT(2) Midgut carcinoid:• 70% of patients are symptomatic• Located at small intestine, appendix, proximal colon(3) Hindgut carcinoid:• Symptomatic only when patients have liver metabolism• Located at distal colon and rectum
L130: Carcinoid syndrome (CS) occurs when carcinoid tumor cells secrete large amounts of serotonin and other vasoactive products into the systemic circulation. The secretory products include serotonin, kallikrein/bradykinin, prostaglandin, tachykinins, and histamine. In general, symptoms associated with CS include cutaneous flushing, diarrhea, wheezing, abdominal pain, and valvular heart disease. More than 90% of patients with carcinoid syndrome have hepatic metastases, usually from carcinoids of small bowel origin. When tumors are confined to the intestine, the bioactive substances released are metabolized to inactive forms by liver, a “first-pass” effect similar to that on oral drugs. However, when tumors invade into hepatic non-portal region, the bioactive secretory products of tumor cells will be released directly into venous circulation and by-pass the “first-pass” effect. Thus, carcinoid syndrome is strongly associated with metastasis to liver.
L131: It is estimated that 80% of symptomatic carcinoid patients present with diarrhea (Wilson, 2009). There are 2 types of CS diarrhea: (1) Bioactive products released from carcinoid tumors stimulate secretory diarrhea; and (2) Niacin deficiency induced malabsorption diarrhea. Carcinoid tumors have at least 5 types of bioactive secretory products that may associate with CS diarrhea:
L132: • Serotonin: stimulates intestinal secretion, motility, and decrease of absorption,
L133: • causing secretory diarrhea.• Kallikrein/Bradykinin: dilates blood vessels, causing flushing, palpitations, low blood pressure, and diarrhea.• Prostaglandin E2 and F2: cause diarrhea.• Tachykinins: cause diarrhea and inflammation.• Histamine: causes flushing and diarrhea.
L134: CS patients may also have niacin-deficiency diarrhea. Tryptophan is an essential amino acid for niacin synthesis. Normally, 1% of dietary tryptophan is converted to serotonin. In CS patients, 70% of dietary tryptophan is converted to serotonin, which limits the availability of tryptophan for niacin synthesis. Clinically, niacin deficiency presents as pellagra (characterized by dermatitis, and mental disturbance) and diarrhea.
L135: Medical Officer Comments:
L136: Because CS diarrhea is caused by multiple mediators, and the proportion of 5-HT diarrhea is unknown, “averaged daily BMs” as an endpoint may dilute the signal of Xermelo effectiveness. The pre-specified supplemental endpoint “durable response analysis” should also be used to define the effectiveness of Xermelo.
L137: 2) Mechanisms of CS diarrhea
L138: Mechanisms of CS diarrhea involve activation of electrolyte secretion by colonic epithelial cells. As shown in Figure 1, the diarrhea mediators increase the intracellular concentrations of secondary messengers: cAMP, Ca2+, cGMP, protein kinase C (PKC), and calmodulin-dependent kinase (CAMK). The secondary messengers, in turn, stimulate secretion of KCl and NaCl by activation of transport proteins in luminal (CFTR Cl- channels, K+ channels) and basolateral (Na+-2Cl--K+ co-transporter, K+ channels, Na+-K+-ATPase) membranes.
L139: Some important stimulatory mediators (most of them are also secreted by CF tumor cells) are shown in the left side of the figure, such as vasoactive intestinal polypeptide (VIP), acetylcholine (Ach), histamine, secretin, leukotrienes, serotonin, adenosine, and nitric oxide (NO). Other bioactive secretory products, kallikrein/bradykinin, prostaglandin E and F, and tachykinins are also involved. These secretory mediators are secreted in paracrine or autocrine fashion by tumor cells or are released from enteric nerves or immune cells (mast cells and lymphocytes) triggered by tumor bioactive products, or are transported by capillaries.

L143: 3) Octreotide and resistance
L144: Octreotide, a 14-aminopeptide somatostatin analog, inhibits the release of gastrointestinal hormones and peptide from carcinoid tumor cells and inhibits carcinoid tumor cell growth. However, 60% of CS patients develop resistance to Octreotide therapy after 3 months. In brief, the potential mechanisms of drug resistance include: (1) increase of the number of drug-resistant tumor cells under drug selective pressure, and (2) decrease of cell surface somatostatin receptors. Somatostatin receptor is a G-protein-coupled receptor. Prolonged stimulation by an agonist triggers desensitization through mechanisms involving internalization and recycling, or receptor degradation (Figure 2). As a result, carcinoid tumor cells become resistance to octreotide therapy. The current medical treatment of octreotide-resistant carcinoid syndrome is surgical resection of hepatic carcinoid metastatic tumor mass.

L148: 2.2. Analysis of Current Treatment Options
L149: Patients with carcinoid syndrome are treated with somatostatin analog octreotide, 150 to 250 mcg subcutaneously three times daily or administered as a long-acting intramuscular depot formulation. The treatment relieves CS diarrhea in 90% of patients for 1 year (Cornett, 2015). After that, many patients stop responding to octreotide. In selected patients with refractory carcinoid syndromes, resection of hepatic metastases may provide improvement. Hepatic artery occlusion, liver-directed debulking procedures, and chemotherapy may also provide symptomatic improvement in patients with hepatic metastases.

L198: 4.5.1. Mechanism of Action
L199: Xermelo is a serotonin synthetic inhibitor. It inhibits tryptophan hydroxylase, the rate-limiting enzyme of serotonin synthesis. Inhibition of serotonin production will reduce the amount of serotonin secreted from carcinoid tumor cells, thus reduced the diarrhea mediator serotonin acting on the surface of colonic epithelial cells. As a result, the amount of KCl and NaCl secretion of colonic epithelial cells will be reduced.
L200: Xermelo inhibits neither the growth of tumor cells nor the secretion of CS bioactive mediators. Since CS involves multiple secretory mediators, the claim by the Applicant to use Xermelo (b)(4) lacks scientific evidence.
L201: One of the adverse reactions of inhibition of tryptophan hydroxylase by Xermelo is to induce niacin deficiency; and niacin deficiency may lead to malabsorption diarrhea. Tryptophan is an essential amino acid that must be obtained from dietary intake. In CS patients, inhibition of tryptophan synthesis may limit the amount of niacin synthesis causing niacin deficiency and malabsorption diarrhea. The potential risk of Xermelo in causing niacin deficiency-induced diarrhea was not studied.

Outside the cell, Serotonin in blood is taken up by platelets or converted to 5-hydroxyindoleacetic acid in urine by Monoamine oxidase and Aldehyde dehydrogenase. A red line from the top right indicates inhibition of the conversion of Tryptophan to 5-hydroxytryptophan.
L204: Pathways of tryptophan and serotonin metabolism in the carcinoid tumor cell. Patients with the carcinoid syndrome often have increased levels of 5-HIAA excretion in the urine and serotonin in the blood; urinary serotonin excretion is either normal or slightly increased.
L205: 4.5.2. Pharmacodynamics
L206: A pharmacodynamic study in human was not conducted. Nonclinical pharmacodynamic studies show that Xermelo inhibited purified human tryptophan hydroxylase type 1 and type 2 activities in vitro, and caused a dose-dependent delay in GI transit and gastric emptying in rats. At higher doses (up to 400 mg/kg/day), Xermelo did not alter brain 5-HT levels in any of the rodent studies.
L207: 4.5.3. Pharmacokinetics
L208: Summary of PK in Healthy Subjects and Carcinoid Patients
L209: Following oral administration, telotristat etiprate dissociates to telotristat ethyl which is rapidly converted to its active metabolite LP-778902. Plasma concentrations of telotristat ethyl were significantly lower than its active metabolite LP-778902 at all dose levels tested. Under fed conditions, telotristat ethyl median Tmax in healthy subjects and in subjects with CS was 1.5 hours and approximately 1.0 hour, respectively, and LP-778902 median Tmax was 2.3 hours (LX107) and approximately 2.0 hours, respectively.
L210: Non-compartmental PK analysis suggested that exposure was generally similar between patients with CS on stable SSA therapy and healthy subjects. Mean plasma AUC0-6hr and Cmax were approximately 22.0 ng•hr/mL, and 7.00 ng/mL, respectively, for telotristat ethyl and approximately 3000 ng•hr/mL and 900 ng/mL, respectively, for LP-778902 in patients with CS on long term SSA therapy following administration of multiple doses of telotristat etiprate 500 mg tid.
L211: In the [14C]-telotristat etiprate mass balance study in healthy subjects, the major route of elimination was the feces (92.78%) with &lt; 1% of the total radioactivity recovered in the urine.

L238: 6.1.1. Study Design
L239: Overview and Objective
L240: Study Title: “A Phase 3, Randomized, Placebo-controlled, Parallel-group, Multicenter, Double-blind Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of Telotristat Etiprate (LX1606, Xermelo) in Patients with Carcinoid Syndrome not Adequately Controlled by Somatostatin Analog (SSA) Therapy”
L241: The primary objective of this trial was to assess the effectiveness of Xermelo in reducing the number of daily bowel movements (BMs) from baseline averaged over the 12-week double-blind portion (DBT Period) of the study in patients not adequately controlled by current SSA therapy.
L242: Trial Design
L243: This was a phase 3, randomized, active controlled, multicenter, double-blind study in patients with CS not adequately controlled by SSA therapy with the goal to evaluate two oral dose levels of Xermelo, 250 mg and 500 mg, tid, versus placebo in the presence of stable SSA therapy.
L244: Patients with histopathologically confirmed metastatic NET and a documented history of CS, who were currently experiencing \geq 4 BMs/day and who were on a minimum stable-dose of SSA therapy were screened for this study. Patients were allowed to continue medications as background therapy provided they remained on stable doses throughout the DBT Period.
L245: Patients entered into a 3- to 4-week Screening/Run-in Period to establish baseline symptoms. Stable-dose SSA therapy was defined as LAR or Depot SSA therapy or a continuous subcutaneous infusion via a pump at the same dose level and frequency for at least 3 months before the Run-in Period. The SSA must have been approved for use in CS in the patient's country of residence or prescriber's country of practice. In addition, therapy with rescue short-acting SSA was permitted as clinically indicated. If taken, patients recorded the frequency and dose of subcutaneous injections of rescue short-acting octreotide in the daily diary. Only those patients who completed the Screening/Run-in Period and who were 75% compliant with diary entries were eligible for the DBT Period of the study.
L246: After the Screening/Run-in Period, eligible patients were randomly assigned (1:1:1 ratio) on Day 1 to receive 1 of 2 oral dose levels of Xermelo (250 or 500 mg) or placebo, each given tid for 12
L247: consecutive weeks in an out-patient environment. A blinded titration was performed during the first 7 days for patients assigned to 500 mg tid. During the titration, all patients were to take 2 tablets tid (1 × 250-mg telotristat etiprate tablet and 1 placebo tablet or 2 placebo tablets). After 7 days, patients were to receive their assigned treatment and dose level for the remaining 11 weeks of the DBT Period.
L248: Upon completion of the DBT Period, patients continued to an OLE Period in which all patients received active study drug at the 500 mg tid dose level. A blinded titration occurred during the first 7 days of the OLE Period (Week 13). During the titration, patients initially assigned to placebo were to take 2 tablets tid as 1 × 250-mg Xermelo tablet and 1 placebo tablet. Patients initially randomized to 250 or 500 mg tid took 2 tablets tid as 2 × 250-mg telotristat etiprate tablets. After 7 days, all patients received open-label Xermelo at the 500 mg tid dose level for the duration of the OLE Period.
L249: A Data Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) reviewed the safety data at specified time points throughout the study and ad hoc at the discretion of the DSMB or the Sponsor (e.g., in the event of unexpected SAEs) to review data and report its recommendations to the Sponsor.

L315: Protocol Violations/Deviations (Study LX301)
L316: Protocol deviations were captured during monitoring visits or identified during clinical review of the data. Before the database extraction was performed and unblinding occurred, the Applicant reviewed all protocol deviations and determined their classification (i.e., major or minor) and whether or not they were significant protocol deviations (i.e., those that would impact the patient's evaluability in the per protocol analysis).
L317: Significant protocol deviations during the DBT Period occurred in 17 patients (12.6%) in the Safety Population: 4 patients (8.9%) in the Xermelo 250 mg group, 6 patients (13.3%) in the Xermelo 500 mg group, and 7 patients (15.6%) in the placebo group. Sixteen of the significant deviations involved study drug; of these, all but 2 were related to study drug compliance &lt;75% during the DBT Period. The remaining study drug deviations occurred in patient 1205-001 (placebo group), who never returned study drug bottles or patient diaries, as such compliance was unable to be confirmed, and patient 2212-002 (placebo group), who used the wrong blinded titration wallet (i.e., the Week 1 wallet rather than the Week 13 wallet) from a replacement kit during the second titration. One deviation involved safety reporting; specifically, the blinded titration wallets were exchanged in error and the patient (1201-002, 250 mg group) received the wrong wallet (i.e., received the Week 13 wallet on Day 1 and received the Week 1 wallet at Week 13).
L318: Eleven patients had protocol deviations regarding consent that were not considered significant as they did not result in exclusion from the Per Protocol Population. These patients did not provide initial consent for the pharmacogenomic substudy; however, samples were collected on Day 1 and stored. This error occurred because the pharmacogenomic sampling tubes were automatically included in the Day 1 laboratory kits for Visit 1, and the sites unintentionally failed to remove them if the patient did not provide consent. No testing was done on any of the samples, and subsequently, patients were either consented for the pharmacogenomics substudy or the samples were destroyed. All appropriate authorities were notified as required.
L319: While these significant deviations could have impacted treatment efficacy and/or safety in the DBT Period, the Applicant did not believe these deviations had an impact on the overall study results.

L366: Efficacy Results – Primary Endpoint (Study LX301)
L367: The primary endpoint was “the change from Baseline in the number of BMs/day averaged over the 12-week DBT Period”. The results show that the net differences comparing to the control in arithmetic means were -0.81 and -0.83 BMs/day for the 250 mg and 500 mg arms, respectively. At baseline, the averaged numbers of BMs were 5.2, 6.1 and 5.1 BMs/day for placebo (SSA only), Xermelo 250 mg tid, and Xermelo 500 mg tid, respectively. The total changes during the DBT Period were -0.6, -1.4, and -1.5 BMs/day for SSA only, 250 mg Xermelo, and 500 mg Xermelo, respectively.
L368: The efficacy of Xermelo was demonstrated through significantly greater reductions in daily bowel movement frequency averaged over 12 weeks in both doses compared to placebo (p &lt; 0.001).
L369: Based on exit interviews from patients completing Study LX301, a reduction of at least two bowel movements per day was considered clinically meaningful, with 33% of the patients who received Xermelo 250 mg achieving this level of improvement compared to 4% of placebo.

L505: Trial Design (LX303)
L506: This was a confirmative phase 3, randomized, active controlled, multicenter, double-blind study in patients with CS to evaluate 2 oral dose Xermelo, 250 mg and 500 mg tid, versus placebo.
L507: Patients with histopathologically confirmed, well-differentiated NET and a documented history of CS were screened for this study. This companion study to the LX301 study was designed to enroll patients receiving SSA therapy who had &lt;4 BMs/day with \geq 1 signs/symptoms of CS or patients who were not receiving SSA therapy with \geq 1 signs/symptoms of CS.
L508: Patients entered Screening/Run-in Period of at least 3 weeks to establish baseline symptoms. During the Run-in Period, no changes to SSA therapy occurred. Patients receiving SSA therapy continued to receive stable-dose SSA therapy and those not currently receiving SSA therapy remained without SSA therapy. Stable-dose SSA therapy was defined as LAR or depot SSA therapy or a continuous subcutaneous infusion via a pump at the same dose level and frequency for at least 3 months before the Run-in Period. In addition, therapy with rescue short-acting SSA was permitted. Only patients who completed the Screening/Run-in Period and were \geq 75\% compliant with diary entries were considered eligible for the DBT Period of the study. Patient diaries were completed at the end of each study day. In the daily diary, patients recorded the number of BMs over a 24-hour period, consistency of each BM based on the Bristol Stool Form Scale, abdominal pain, nausea, frequency of rescue SSA use, number of flushing episodes, and presence of urgency to defecate.
L509: After the Screening/Run-in Period, eligible patients were randomly assigned (1:1:1 ratio) on Day 1 to receive 1 of 2 oral dose levels of Xermelo (250 or 500 mg) or placebo given tid for 12 consecutive weeks. A blinded titration occurred during the first 7 days for patients assigned to the 500 mg tid dose group. During the titration, all patients took 2 tablets tid (1 \times 250\text{-mg} Xermelo tablet and 1 placebo tablet or 2 placebo tablets). After 7 days, patients received their assigned treatment and dose level for the remaining 11 weeks of the DBT Period. No changes or initiation of SSA therapy were to be permitted during the DBT Period.
L510: Upon completion of the placebo-controlled DBT Period, patients continued in an OLE Period in which all patients received active study drug at the 500 mg tid dose level. A blinded titration was to occur during the first 7 days of the OLE Period (Week 13). After 7 days, all patients were to receive open-label Xermelo 500 mg tid for the duration of the OLE Period.
L511: Downward dose level adjustment of Xermelo was to be permitted if evidence of intolerability emerged during the OLE Period. Patients who experienced intolerability at the 250 mg tid dose level must have been discontinued from the study. L634: Medical Officer Comments:
L635: (1) There were large variations of u5-HIAA levels at baseline: (a) it may reflect the Heterogeneity of carcinoid origins: Foregut carcinoids (jejunum and ileum) lack the decarboxylase enzyme required for synthesizing the precursor of 5-HT, resulting in minimal to no elevation in urinary 5-HIAA levels. Hindgut carcinoids (rectal and cecum) rarely produces excess 5-HT or 5-HIAA. In contrast, most midgut carcinoids (small intestine, appendix, and proximal colon) secrete 5-HT; and (b) it may be due to the Size of carcinoid
L636: tumor: The more carcinoid tumor cells, the more 5-HT and u5-HIAA are produced.
L637: (2) Regarding disproportionate reduction of u5-HIAA as compared to the reduction of BMs by Xermelo treatment: It is most likely due to the confounding first-pass effect: (a) If the metastatic carcinoid tumor cells are located at portal tracts area (Zone 1) or surrounded by viable hepatocytes, like most orally administered drugs, the secretory product 5-HT is subject to metabolism (inactivation) by hepatic enzymes (monoamine oxidase) before 5-HT can enter systemic circulation (First-pass effect). This proportion of metabolites (5-HIAA) is produced before stimulating diarrhea, and that the reduction has nothing to do with the anti-diarrheal activity of Xermelo. (b) On the other hand, if the metastatic carcinoid cells are located at the areas of terminal hepatic veins (Zone 3), or tumor cells invaded hepatic veins and no surrounding viable hepatocytes, the secretory product 5-HT can directly enter systemic circulation, and stimulates CS diarrhea. Only the reduction of this part of u5-HIAA is proportionate to the BMs.(3) Regarding the prescribing information in label: It appears that the trend of changes by Xermelo treatment can be presented descriptively in the label.

L686: 7.1.1. Primary Endpoints
L687: The primary efficacy endpoint of the pivotal study (LX301) was to measure the “Reduction of daily bowel movement frequency from baseline averaged over the 12-week double-blind period”. Because CS diarrhea has multiple mediators, to use “averaged bowel movements” to characterize Xermelo activity is inappropriate:
L688: ● “Averaged bowel movements” relies on the hypothesis that 5-HT is the only mediator of CS diarrhea, and that change of averaged bowel movements is proportionate to Xermelo activity.● In reality, the more types of CS diarrhea mediators in the system, the less the Xermelo activity is measured.
L689: The Division asked the Applicant to conduct a durable responder analysis as the supplemental analysis of the primary endpoint on 10/9/2013. The responder criterion was defined as “≥30%
L690: reduction in daily number of BMs for \geq 50\% of time over the DBT Period of the study”. The reviewer conducted Post-Hoc analyses. The results show that the pre-specified responder analysis can be used to characterize the activity of Xermelo.
L691: The responder analysis of Study LX301 shows that Xermelo treatment increased the proportions of responders as compared with placebo control. The proportions of patients with durable response were 20%, 44% (p=0.024), and 42% (p=0.040), respectively for placebo, 250 mg, and 500 mg tid of Xermelo. The odds ratio for 250 mg tid was 3.49 (p=0.011, 95% CL 1.33, 9.16) and for 500 mg tid was 3.11 (p=0.020, 95% CL 1.20, 8.10), relative to placebo.
L692: These values suggest significantly greater reductions in daily bowel movement frequency averaged over 12 weeks in both doses of Xermelo treatment as compared to placebo (p &lt; 0.001). Based on exit interviews in patients who completed Study LX301, a reduction of at least two bowel movements per day was considered clinically meaningful. There were 33% of the patients who received Xermelo 250 mg achieved this level of improvement as compared to 4% of the placebo.
L693: Similarly, Study LX303 shows that the responders of Xermelo groups were also higher than the placebo control.

L728: 7.2. Additional Efficacy Considerations
L729: 7.2.1. Considerations on Benefit in the Postmarket Setting
L730: Xermelo treatment reduced the frequency of 5-HT CS diarrhea as demonstrated in Studies LX301 and LX303. The product did not reduce the episodes of cutaneous flushing and abdominal pain. Therefore, it should not be used to treat carcinoid syndrome other than diarrhea. Furthermore, Xermelo benefits the treatment of 5-HT-mediated refractory CS diarrhea, but does not have therapeutic effects against the non-5-HT-mediated diarrhea.

L733: 7.3. Integrated Assessment of Effectiveness
L734: The review primarily assesses the efficacy and safety of two randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled (12 weeks) and open-label (36 weeks), multi-center clinical studies (LX301 and LX303) (n= 211) in CS patients on stable SSA (somatostatin analog) therapy with a history of diarrhea for one month or more. Diarrhea was defined as either persistently loose stools despite regular use of SSA therapy or \geq 4 BMs daily without SSA use.
L735: In the controlled phases of Studies LX301 and LX303, 140 patients were randomized to receive Xermelo (70 patients for 250 mg and 50 mg, respectively), and 71 patients received placebo.
L736: The primary efficacy analysis was based on demonstration of clinical response in the controlled phases. Clinical response was defined as \geq 30\% reduction of daily bowel movements for at least 50% of the 12-week study periods.
L737: • The results show that a larger proportion of patients in the Xermelo 250 mg and 500 mg groups achieved clinical response as compared with the placebo group (Study LX301: 42.2% : 44.4% : 20.0% for 250 mg, 500 mg, and placebo groups, respectively; Study LX303: 40% : 40% : 0%, for 250 mg, 500 mg, and placebo groups, respectively).• The reduction of daily bowel movements as measured by the responder analysis is correlated with the reduction of urinary 5-HIAA levels.• Also, the time to the first occurrence of sustained \geq 30\% improvement from baseline in BM frequency during the DBT Period shows faster improvements of Xermelo treated groups as compared to placebo in Kaplan-Meier curves.• At the end of double-blind phase of Study LX301, some patients were interviewed. Among interview patients, the proportions who were “very satisfied” were 0/9 (0%) on placebo, 5/9 (56%) on 250 mg and 7/15 (47%) on 500 mg Xermelo groups.• Urinary 5-HIAA was reported to be a biomarker of prognosis for patients with midgut carcinoid tumor (van der Horst-Schrivers, 2007). Low urinary 5-HIAA levels are associated with favorable survival rates. 5-HT is the major secretory product along with tachykinin. Both Studies LX301 and LX303 show that Xermelo treatment significantly decreased the levels of urinary 5-HIAA.
L738: In conclusion, from the clinical reviewer standpoint, the evidence submitted supports the effectiveness of Xermelo in the treatment of CS diarrhea caused by 5-HT mediator secreted by carcinoid tumor.

L847: Constipation
L848: In the Placebo-controlled Safety analyses (Studies LX301 and LX303), 2 (3%), 4 (6%) and 5 (7%) patients in the placebo, Xermelo 250 mg, and Xermelo 500 mg treatment groups experienced constipation, respectively; there was no report of fecaloma; the one case of ileus was reported in the placebo group.
L849: In the Overall Safety analyses, 28 (12%) patients experienced constipation and 2 (1%) patients experienced fecaloma. Three of the patients experienced constipation that was reported as SAEs. Additionally, 1 (0.4%) patient experienced small intestinal obstruction, and 1 (0.4%) patient each experienced ileus, intestinal obstruction and subileus (the same patient as one of the patients experiencing fecaloma), which were reported as SAEs.
L850: The nonserious TEAEs of constipation were all of mild or moderate intensity and mostly resolved with no action taken.
L851: Medical Officer Comments:
L852: The narratives of patients with constipation were reviewed. All of the patients had metastatic NET of the GI tract. Other common attributive factors included elder age, concomitant morphine, Vicodin and Loperamide. Because most of the SAEs occurred in open-label studies, a definitive causal relationship cannot be established. However, the role of Xermelo in causing constipation and in exacerbation of the underlying conditions cannot be ruled out.
L853: In reviewing the narratives of constipation from the Open-label Extension Safety database, 9 patients were found to have constipation or intestinal obstruction, leading to perforation. All 9 patients had pre-existing carcinoid tumor (NET) of GI tract (location not specified). Six patients had constipation reported as SAE, while 3 patients (PIDs 1001-007, 0001-010, and 009-002) did not have constipation. Among the 3 patients who did not have constipation, 2 patients experienced tumor progression, leading to intestinal perforation and surgical resection. Among the 6 patients who had constipation, one patient later developed intestinal obstruction, peritonitis, sepsis and death (PID 0606-011) and another patient developed ileal perforation, septic shock and death (0601-004). The causes of deaths were reportedly due to carcinoid tumor progression, respectively.
L854: Among the rest, 4 patients who had constipation, one patient (PID 0603-503) had surgery due to terminal ileum adhesion that was caused by previous multiple surgeries. The remaining 3 patients (PIDs 0108-008, 0709-001, and 0108-003) received medical treatment and resolved in days.
L855: Based on this analysis, no evidence supports the hypothesis that Xermelo treatment causes intestinal obstruction and perforation. On the other hand, literature search shows that intestinal fibrosis and stenosis are associated with small intestinal carcinoids.
L856: Notes: Literature search shows that the majority of intestinal carcinoid tumors arise in the small intestine; the remainder develop in the rectum, and, uncommonly, in the colon. It is reported that 31% of small-intestinal carcinoids presents with intestinal obstruction, and 39% of rectal carcinoids presents with bleeding and 17% presents with constipation (Jensen, 2001). It is also reported that ileal carcinoids may be associated with extensive fibrosis and present clinically as intestinal stenosis (mimicking Crohn's disease). Mesenteric and retroperitoneal fibrosis may also be present (Moreira, 2010).
L857: Medical Officer Comments:
L858: Because the role of Xermelo treatment in causing constipation cannot be ruled out, and that constipation may exacerbate the pre-existing condition of intestinal obstruction in patients with metastatic carcinoid tumors, the reviewer suggests that “Constipation” remains in the Warnings and Precautions section of the label.

L881: 8.4.9. QT
L882: A randomized double-blind, placebo-controlled single dose (1500 mg Xermelo) QT study (LX105) was conducted with 42 healthy adult subjects. The upper bound of the 2-sided 90% CI was below 10 msec at all postdosing time points.

L917: Summary of Depression-related AEs
L918: The overall incidence of AEs related to depression in the Placebo-controlled Safety population remained numerically higher in the Xermelo 500 mg tid group (9 [13%] patients), compared with the placebo and the Xermelo 250 mg tid groups (5 [7%] patients each). The event rates were 0.46, 0.32, and 0.60 event / person-year for placebo, Xermelo 250 mg, and Xermelo 500 mg groups, respectively. All depression-related AESIs in this Update were reported by the Investigators as mild or moderate in intensity. None of the events were reported as an SAE, and generally the events did not require treatment or limit study drug treatment.
L919: Summary of Hepatic Enzyme Abnormality-related AEs
L920: The Applicant reported elevation of hepatic enzymes in association with Xermelo administration, predominantly in GGT. The increases did not lead to a clinically significant outcome, and drug-induced liver injury was not found.
L921: Summary of Constipation-related AEs
L922: For the DBT period, only 1 new TEAE of constipation was reported; the event was in the 250 mg tid group. As a result, in the Placebo-controlled Safety population, 2 (3%), 5 (7%) and 5 (7%) patients in the placebo, Xermelo 250 mg tid, and Xermelo 500 mg tid treatment groups experienced constipation, respectively.
L923: For the Overall Safety population, a total of 5 new events of constipation were reported. Cumulatively, 33 (14%) patients experienced constipation, and the incidence rates remained unchanged. None of the new events of constipation were reported as serious.
L924: In summary, no new safety signal observed from the 120-day safety update.

L929: 8.10. Integrated Assessment of Safety
L930: A total of 239 CS patients with refractory diarrhea to SSA were treated. The mean duration was 41 weeks and the highest dose was 500 mg tid. There was no significant safety signal reported.
L931: In the Placebo-controlled Safety analyses, the incidences of SAEs were 12 (16.9%) patients on placebo, 8 (11.4%) patients on Xermelo 250 mg tid, and 10 (14.3%) patients on Xermelo 500 mg tid; and SAEs leading to death were 3 (4.2%) patients on placebo, 1 (1.4%) patient on Xermelo 250 mg tid and 1 (1.4%) patient on Xermelo 500 mg tid. Most SAEs, including the few deaths, were assessed as unrelated to Xermelo. The overall incidences of TEAEs and TEAEs leading to study drug discontinuation were similar among the placebo and treatment groups.
L932: In the Overall Safety analyses, the safety profile was similar to the controlled. The most frequently reported TEAEs across studies and analysis pools by SOC were Gastrointestinal Disorders, and by PT were nausea and abdominal pain. The characteristic TEAE of Xermelo was nausea. Nausea tended to occur early after treatment initiation, was mostly mild or moderate, did not limit study treatment, and was likely related to a delay in gastric emptying, a local GI effect seen in animal studies.
L933: The overall incidence of AESI (adverse event of special interest) is related to GI symptoms was similar between placebo and active treatment groups; among these events, the incidence of constipation showed a potential dose-response relationship, and that a few events were reported as serious. There was a numerical imbalance in the incidence of AESIs related to depression for the Xermelo 500 mg tid treatment group compared with that for the placebo
L934: and Xermelo 250 mg tid treatment groups for the Placebo-controlled Safety population. These events were reported by the Investigators as mild or moderate in intensity, and generally did not limit treatment. There were no AESIs related to depression reported as an SAE.
L935: There were no clinically significant findings in laboratory values, vital sign measurements, or ECG parameters, except for increased hepatic enzymes. Some patients who had initially normal values experienced elevations of ALT and AST. No patient met Hy's Law criteria of ALT/AST &gt; 3x ULN in association with bilirubin elevation to 2x elevation in the absence of an alternative explanation. There were overall mild to moderate increases in ALT, AST, ALP and GGT levels during the treatment as compared to controls. The elevations of GGT were the most frequent abnormalities identified. However, there were no cases of induction of significant liver injury that was probably related to the treatment.
L936: There was a numerical increase in weight gain at Week 12 of the double-blind Study LX301 in patients treated with Xermelo. Whereas there was no weight gain in the placebo group, suggesting that the clinical improvement was associated with the reduction of diarrhea by Xermelo treatment. Study LX303 had a similar improvement. There were a higher proportion of patients who had weight gain in Xermelo groups as compared to the placebo.
L937: In addition, in a randomized controlled phase 1 QT study (LX105), there was no significant QTc prolongation effect of a single dose of 1500 mg of Xermelo.
L938: In summary, the clinical safety assessment did not identify any significant safety issues of Xermelo based on the submitted double-blind safety and overall safety database.
