L10: ## 1. BACKGROUND
L11: Gepirone HCl (previously Org 33062) is a 5HT1A agonist. The IND development plan was to market this drug as an antidepressant. The IND 23,952 for the immediate release (IR) formulation was submitted on 04/2/84; and the IND 33,626 for the extended release (ER) formulation was submitted 08/28/89. Since there was poor tolerability (i.e., dizziness, nausea, insomnia) of the IR formulation, the sponsor decided to further develop their clinical programs with Gepirone ER. In 1992, the original sponsor, Bristol-Myers Squibb made their business decision to discontinue all gepirone clinical development programs and transferred the INDs to Fabre-Kramer Pharmaceuticals, Inc. There was additional ownership license transfer and agreement to Organon, Inc. in 1998; and then transferred back to Fabre-Kramer since June 2005.
L12: The original NDA for the ER formulation was initially submitted on 09/30/99 by Organon, Inc. The application was not filed by the Agency as the submission did not include positive results from 2 adequate and well-controlled studies using gepirone ER.
L13: The NDA was resubmitted on 05/18/01. The first NA letter was issued on 03/15/02, citing inadequate evidence of efficacy and inadequate long-term safety data. We considered one IR study [03A7A-003] and one ER study positive [134001]. We asked for one additional positive ER study. In the first NA letter in 2002, we noted our concerns about multiple negative trials in the overall development program for both the IR and ER formulations.
L14: The second NDA resubmission was on 12/23/03 with data from a randomized withdrawal relapse prevention study [28709]. However, the division did not consider this randomized withdrawal trial a positive study, and issued a second NA letter on 06/23/04, citing a need for a robustly positive short-term trial and a positive randomized withdrawal trial. We again noted our concern about continued accumulation of negative data and asked the sponsor to address such a preponderance of negativity.
L15: Since the time of issuance of the second NA letter in 2004, we held a couple of meetings with the sponsor, Fabre-Kramer to discuss their planned resubmission of the NDA and we gave feedback on several topics. Based on the meeting minutes of 10/12/05 and 05/31/2006 (teleconference), the discussion items included:
L16: - the format and contents of their planned NDA resubmission in response to the CMC, OCP and Clinical issues raised in the 06/23/2004 second NA letter
- Our general agreement with their methodology proposed for the integrated efficacy summary focusing on two pivotal studies that they considered positive and their proposal for meta-analysis of pooled data
- Most of the analyses of routine safety data would focus on ER data but the safety data in regards to deaths, serious adverse events and adverse dropouts should include all gepirone exposure data (both IR and ER studies) in the integrated safety summary.
- Regarding their intended claim on potential improvement of sexual function, we pointed out that gepirone should consistently show an advantage over other antidepressants on sexual dysfunction in circumstances when all the drugs are used optimally with regard to dosing, and gepirone ER is consistently shown to be non-inferior to placebo.
- Dissolution profile data for the planned resubmission
L17: On May 1, 2007, the sponsor Fabre-Kramer submitted in their response to the 06/23/2004 NA letter to the above referenced NDA. They included the results of two additional short-term gepirone ER studies (FK-GBE-007 &amp; 008) in MDD. This submission also includes a meta-analysis of pooled data from 12 short-term placebo-controlled studies. Despite our clear statement in the second NA letter that we did not consider their prior relapse prevention study 28709 a positive study and had asked for another positive relapse prevention study with adequate stabilization period, in this submission, the sponsor just provided their post-hoc reanalysis results of study 28709 and intends to seek a claim for Gepirone ER in maintenance treatment of MDD. The sponsor also proposes to include a labeling statement that patients treated with Gepirone had statistically significantly better sexual functioning than placebo or SSRI-treated patients.
L18: The proposed target dose for treatment of MDD is 60-80 mg per day. The proposed recommended starting dose is 20 mg once daily, with an increase to 40 mg once daily at Day 4 if tolerated; to 60 mg after 1 week; and then to 80 mg after additional week. The drug will be available as 20, 40, 60 and 80 mg strength extended-release tablets.

L40: ## 5.1.2 Summary of Study Pertinent to Efficacy Claim of gepirone ER
L41: ## 5.1.2.1 Positive studies
L42: ## Study FK-GBE-007
L43: This was a 8-week, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, parallel-group, multicenter, outpatient study in patients with a DSM-IV diagnosis of major depressive disorder comparing flexible doses of gepirone ER (20-80 mg/day) vs. placebo. Subjects who were assigned to gepirone ER was titrated to 20 mg on Days 1-3, 40 mg on Days 4-7. Based on subject's tolerability, gepirone ER was then administered at 40 to 60 mg on Days 8-14; and 40 to 80 mg on Days 15-56. Mean daily dose of gepirone ER was 58.2 mg.
L44: The study was conducted in 9 U.S. study sites. A total of 248 subjects were randomized and received at least one dose of study medication (124 in the gepirone ER group and 124 in the placebo group); 238 subjects were analyzed for efficacy in the intent-to treat (ITT) population (116 in the gepirone ER group and 122 in the placebo group). 199 subjects (97 in gepirone vs. 102 in placebo) completed the study and 49 discontinued. The overall discontinuation rate was slightly higher for the gepirone ER group; 3.2% vs. 2.4%, for lack of efficacy; 4% vs. 2.4% for AE; respectively.
L45: The mean (SD) age of all subjects was 38.1 (range 18 to 64) years. The majority of subjects were female (70%) and Caucasian (64.7%). Demographic characteristics were similar for both treatment groups in the ITT population.
L46: The primary efficacy measure was the change from baseline to the end of study of the HAMD-17 total score. The treatment efficacy was analyzed by the primary efficacy analysis using ANOVA with LOCF data (see table 2 below). ANCOVA analysis was also used. The difference between the ANOVA and ANCOVA was use of HAMD-17 baseline as covariate. Similar result was obtained (p=0.018). Dr. Kong confirmed the study results. Sensitivity analysis was conducted using OC.

L69: ## 5.1.3 Comments on Other Important Clinical Issues
L70: ## 5.1.3.1 Subgroup analysis of study FK-GBE-007
L71: Dr. Kong performed exploratory subgroup analyses for the pivotal study FK-GBE-007 based on age, gender (M/F) and race (Caucasian versus non-Caucasian). The treatment effect is significantly greater in female (-2.8) for gepirone ER vs. placebo (p=0.04). L72: ## 5.1.3.2 Dose-response relationship
L73: As can be seen in table 1, the sponsor has not conducted any adequate studies with gepirone ER to explore dose-response relationship. Almost all of these studies were flexible dose studies. In earlier studies, there were some fixed flexible dosage groups; two of the studies (CN105-057 and 064) used inadequate ER dosing (below 40 mg/day) which failed to demonstrate efficacy and two other studies were terminated early (CN105-078 and 083).
L74: ## 5.1.3.3 Preponderance of negative and failed studies
L75: This issue has been repeatedly mentioned in prior review memoranda and the NA action letters for this drug. The 7 studies which the division had determined as negative were noted to be reasonably adequate in study design, gepirone ER dose and study conduct. As stated above, the sponsor now
L76: came back with 2 positive studies with gepirone ER; however, such preponderance of negative and failed studies with this drug remains to be the focus of our attention in determining overall efficacy of the drug. Interestingly, Dr. Kong also pointed out that placebo is numerically better than gepirone ER in 4 studies.
L77: In this context, although data from the IR studies will not be absolutely essential in determining the effectiveness of the ER formulation, I believe it is worthy to mention that there was only 1 positive IR study (study 037A-003) out of a total of 10 well-controlled studies conducted using the IR formulation. Although study 037A-003 was determined positive, this study enrolled MDD patients (N=60) with atypical features and conducted at a single U.S. center. Three of these IR studies were considered failed studies as the active control at an adequate dose failed to beat placebo.

L86: ## 5.1.3.5 Reanalysis of randomized withdrawal study 28709
L87: The result from this study was previously reviewed in 2003 and the Division did not consider this a positive study, and asked for another positive study with adequate stabilization period.
L88: Briefly, this study consisted of an 8-12 week open-label phase in which subjects who was classified to be in remission with gepirone ER (titrated ER dose between 40-80 mg) treatment (remission was defined as HAMD-17 \le8 at week 8 or 12) was followed in a 44 week double-blind, placebo-controlled continuation phase of observation for relapse (relapse was defined as HAMD-17 \ge16 or discontinuation due to LOE determined by Investigator). This study was conducted in 29 non-U.S sites (Germany, France, Finland, Estonia and Turkey). Primary efficacy was proportion of patients relapsed. The original ITT population was a total of 250 subjects; 126 in gepirone ER and 124 in placebo.
L89: We found the sponsor's positive original CMH analysis problematic [i.e., gepirone ER 29/126 (23%) vs. placebo 43/124 (35%); p=0.024] because prior to unblinding, the sponsor identified 5 patients on gepirone who had relapsed based on discontinuation due to LOE as determined by investigator; but, the sponsor decided not to include these 5 patients as relapsers in their analysis. The study results changed to negative (p= 0.101) if these 5 subjects were included. In addition, the original analysis failed to include all ITT patients (i.e., 32 patients from centers that had patients with only 1 treatment arm or had no relapses were not included). Our analysis with by grouping of small centers or grouping by country, the results were found to be negative with or without the 5 relapsed patients stated above.
L90: In this 2007 submission, the sponsor provided their reanalysis results and still seeks for a claim of maintenance treatment. They modified the original ITT population (N=250) by excluding a total of 40 subjects (22 gepirone ER and 18 placebo) who were considered protocol violators based on the following conditions.
L91: 1) Improper randomization (subjects who did not achieve a score of HAMD-17 \le8 until after Week 12 but entered into the continuation period): 13 gepirone ER-treated and 14 placebo-treated subjects

2) Non-compliance (serious overall non-compliance with the intake of the trial medication defined as &lt;75% of the scheduled trial medication during the continuation phase): 5 gepirone ER-treated and 2 placebo-treated patients.
L92: 3) Prohibited medication (Use of disallowed concomitant medication between the last assessment of the open-label phase and the first efficacy assessment of the continuation phase): 4 gepirone ER-treated and 2 placebo-treated patients. The prohibited medications that these patients used were meprobamate, zopiclone or benzodiazepines such as clonazepam, oxazepam and lorazepam.
L93: The per-protocol (PP) population consisted of 210 subjects were included in their reanalysis. The treatment groups were compared by means of the log-rank test for PP analysis dataset.
L94: Based on the PP analysis, the sponsor claimed that gepirone ER relapse rate was significantly lower than placebo [i.e., 25/104 (24%) vs. 41/106 (38.7%); p=0.023 without adjustment for pooled center or country]. The sponsor also claimed that gepirone ER achieved statistical significance relative to placebo in terms of time to relapse (p=0.032).
L95: I feel this sponsor's reanalysis was based on the above mentioned post-hoc definitions in their attempt to come up with a favourable result for gepirone ER. For example, we generally find occasional use of sleep medications in MDD trials despite these agents would be listed as part of the prohibited medications; and in most of the cases, we might not have strong objections including such patients in the efficacy analysis for longer-term studies.
L96: Section 7.1.2 of the protocol stated that "All protocol violations will be determined by medical, clinical and biometrics personnel prior to breaking the blind and will be done at least during blind review." Both Dr. Kong and Dr. Hearst considered this study reanalysis unacceptable. As stated by Dr. Kong in his review that the sponsor's exclusion of these 40 patients was not justified and "PP" analysis results did not, therefore, provide valid evidence supporting the efficacy of gepirone ER over placebo in reducing relapse rate. I agree with him.
L97: ## 5.1.4 Conclusions Regarding Efficacy Data
L98: The efficacy analyses did not show any convincing evidence regarding efficacy of gepirone ER in treatment of MDD. This could still be arguable given that the sponsor submitted efficacy results from two positive studies for gepirone ER. However, these two positives were out of a total of 12 short-term well-controlled studies. Based on our discussion with our statistical colleagues, my understanding is that it is likely that the chance of having at least 2 false positive studies out of 12 studies would be higher (i.e., false positive rate of 3.5% as compared to the rate of 0.00625% in 2 out of 2 studies). We considered results from multiple studies as negative for gepirone ER. In out of 5 active and placebo-controlled studies, the finding of active controls in 3 different studies statistically superior to gepirone ER and the active control is significantly better than placebo in 2 active controlled studies adds to our general concern that gepirone ER lacks robust efficacy in acute treatment of depression. I believe we usually find positive randomized withdrawal studies with the approved SSRI/SNRI antidepressants. In case of gepirone ER, we did not find the relapse prevention study with gepirone ER a positive study. The meta-analysis result of short-term studies was not found to be in support of efficacy of gepirone ER.

L153: ## 1.0 BACKGROUND
L154: Gepirone ER is an extended release formulation of gepirone, an azapirone that is structurally and pharmacologically similar to buspirone, a drug marketed as Buspar for GAD. Like buspirone, gepirone acts primarily at 5HT1A receptors, as a full and partial agonist. Gepirone is not approved for any indications, and this NDA provides data in support of a claim for MDD, in a dose range of 20 to 80 mg/day. No other drugs with this particular pharmacological profile are approved for MDD.
L155: This NDA was most recently submitted 5-18-01, and a nonapprovable letter was issued 3-15-02. I refer the reader to my 5-5-02 memo for a detailed summary of the very complicated development and regulatory history for this product. Prior to the 5-18-01 submission of this NDA, we had reached agreement with the sponsor that one additional positive short-term trial with the ER formulation would be sufficient, providing there was independent evidence for the IR formulation. We agreed with the sponsor that ER study 134001 could be considered a positive study. At the time of issuing the 3-15-02 letter, however, we did not consider any of the 3 candidates for IR studies to be positive. We subsequently modified our view on IR study 03A7A-003, and did consider that a positive study (see minutes for 7-3-02 meeting with sponsor), but not the other two IR studies. At the 7-3-02 meeting, the sponsor indicated that the results from another short-term ER study (134004) would soon be available, as well as results from a longer-term randomized withdrawal study (28709). We indicated that, if study 134004 were robustly positive, they would have sufficient efficacy data for filing the application.
L156: However, in a 4-17-03 package outlining their proposed response to our 3-15-02 nonapprovable letter, we learned that study 134004 failed to distinguish either active drug (gepirone and fluoxetine) from placebo. They claimed, however, that their randomized withdrawal study (28709) was positive, and argued that this combination of two positive short-term studies and one positive long-term study should be sufficient to file the application. In a 7-14-03 telcon, we agreed only that we would likely be willing to file such an application, without reaching any agreement that this combination of studies would be sufficient. Part of the difficulty was a preponderance of negative studies for gepirone in the application, making the overall program very weak indeed.
L157: The other reason for issuing a nonapprovable letter for the 5-18-01 application was a failure to provide sufficient longer-term safety data at relevant doses. Based on an overview of the accumulated efficacy data, we considered the possibly effective dose range to be 40-80 mg/day. The sponsor clearly did not have sufficient longer-term safety data in this range.
L158: The sponsor has sought to respond to the deficiencies in the 5-18-01 application by submitting (1) efficacy data from their randomized withdrawal study (28709), and (2) longer-term safety data at relevant doses. L170: ## 5.1.1 Overview of Studies Pertinent to Efficacy
L171: As noted under background, the two positive short-term trials for gepirone coming out of the 5-18-01 application, one for IR (03A7A-003) and one for ER (134001), were derived from a total of 18 short-term placebo controlled trials. Thus, the finding of only one of four short-term ER studies having a positive outcome was noted to be a concern. As noted, the most recent ER study (134004) has in fact failed, both for fluoxetine and gepirone. However, it is of concern that the results for gepirone are actually numerically worse than placebo, while the results for fluoxetine are numerically superior to placebo. Given this very marginal picture for the gepirone short-term efficacy data, a robustly positive result for the randomized withdrawal study became even more pressing.
L172: The efficacy review for this resubmission focused exclusively on the results for the randomized withdrawal study (28709).
L173: ## 5.1.2 Study 28709
L174: This was a multicenter study [29 European centers (Germany, France, Poland, Finland, Turkey)]. This was a typical randomized withdrawal study in depression, with an 8-12 week open label phase, followed by a double-blind phase in which patients responding to open treatment with gepirone (HAMD-17 total score \le 8, at week 8 or week 12) were randomized to either continue on gepirone (at the same dose that was associated with a “response”) or switch to placebo, with a 40-44 week period of observation for relapse (defined as a HAMD-17 \ge 16, or discontinuation due to lack of efficacy as determined by the investigator). The total period of participation for any individual patient was to have been 1 year. This trial recruited adult patients (\ge 18) who met DSM-IV criteria for major depressive disorder (MDD). During the open label phase, patients were titrated up to 40 mg of gepirone by day 4, and were then titrated within a range of 40-80 mg/day for the remainder of that phase. The double-blind phase involved the random assignment of responding patients (1:1) to either gepirone (at their last dose during the open label phase) or placebo. Patients were then observed for relapse over the next 40-44 weeks.
L175: The primary outcome was the proportion of patients who had relapsed at the end of the observation period, and the primary analysis was CMH, with adjustment for centers. Time to relapse was a secondary endpoint. Survival curves were estimated using Kaplan-Meier methodology and the log-rank test was used to compare survival distributions. Both analyses were based on a modified intent-to-treat sample, i.e., all patients randomized who received at least 1 dose of assigned treatment and who had at least 1 postbaseline efficacy evaluation.
L176: There was an excess of females compared to males (about 68% female), the mean age was about 45, and patients were almost entirely Caucasian white (about 99%).
L177: Out of a total of n=303 patients who completed the open label phase, n=250 were randomized and had at least one dose of assigned medication and at least one efficacy assessment during postbaseline, i.e., the ITT population (n=126 to gepirone and n=124 to placebo).

L203: ## 5.1.4 Conclusions Regarding Efficacy Data
L204: The sponsor has, in my view, not provided sufficient evidence to support the claims of short-term and longer-term antidepressant efficacy for gepirone ER. Based on earlier discussions with the sponsor, we had agreed that a single positive short-term trial with the ER formulation, in the face of independent evidence for the antidepressant efficacy of the IR formulation, would be sufficient to support a claim for the antidepressant efficacy of the ER formulation. The sponsor has not, in my view, provided such evidence. It is true that they have provided evidence from 2 short-term studies, however, with different formulations (1 for IR and 1 for ER). We have never accepted this level of evidence to support a short-term claim, and it remains my view that such data would not be sufficient to support a claim for gepirone ER, since it does not meet my requirement for a “bridge” between the 2 formulations. Nevertheless, we did agree to file the application, given the sponsor’s claim that they had a positive longer-term study, but with no guarantee that we would
L205: consider this additional study as sufficient to support these claims. Given the negative outcome on study 28709, I continue to feel that the evidence in support of an antidepressant claim for this drug is not adequate to justify an approvable action. Furthermore, as the negative data continue to accumulate, it becomes difficult to advise the company about what further work they might do to address such a preponderance of negativity. At this point, I would want at least a “robustly positive” short-term trial with gepirone ER and a positive randomized withdrawal study. The short-term trial should look at different fixed doses of gepirone ER, and the randomized withdrawal study would need to involve a period of “response” for a minimum of 6 months before randomization.

L273: ## 1.0 BACKGROUND

L363: ## 5.1.3 Comment on Other Important Clinical Issues Regarding Gepirone ER for MDD
L364: ## Evidence Bearing on the Question of Dose/Response for Efficacy
L365: In my view, there are no data in this application directly pertinent to the question of dose response for efficacy. Given the uniformly negative results in studies with a maximum dose of \le 40, it's probably a reasonable conclusion that doses lower than 40 mg are not likely to be effective. However, it would be much preferable to have the finding of lack of effect below 40 mg/day in the same study in which doses above 40 mg/day were shown to be effective, i.e., a dose response study. If this drug were to be approved, the dosing instructions in labeling would need to focus on the titration approach utilized in whatever positive studies supported the approval.
L366: ## Clinical Predictors of Response
L367: Exploratory analyses were done to detect subgroup interactions on the basis of gender and age for the 3 short-term trials, and the 1 longer-term trial proposed by the sponsor as positive. While there were scattered differences in various subgroups across these 4 studies, there were no consistent patterns to suggest a preferential response in one subgroup over another, in my view. However, there was also likely not adequate power to detect such differences.
L368: ## Size of Treatment Effect
L369: The effect size as measured by difference between drug and placebo in change from baseline in the HAMD-17 observed in the 1 short-term study that I consider positive was similar to that seen in other positive antidepressant trials.
L370: ## Duration of Treatment
L371: In my view, there are no data in this application pertinent to the question of longer-term efficacy.
L372: ## 5.1.4 Conclusions Regarding Efficacy Data
L373: The sponsor has, in my view, not provided sufficient evidence to support the claim of short-term antidepressant efficacy for gepirone ER. Based on earlier discussions with the sponsor, we had agreed that a single positive short-term trial with the ER formulation, in the face of independent evidence for the antidepressant efficacy of the IR formulation, would be sufficient to support a claim for the antidepressant efficacy of the ER formulation. The sponsor has not, in my view, provided such evidence.
L374: I am in agreement with Drs. Kelly/Mahjoob/Hearst regarding the short-term gepirone ER study (134001). I also consider this to be a positive study in support of gepirone ER in MDD. However, I do not feel that any of the 3 gepirone IR studies proposed by the sponsor as support for the MDD claim can, in fact, be considered positive studies in support of this claim.
L375: -Study 03A7A-003: As I've indicated, it's not clear to me what population was actually studied in this admittedly positive study. In my view, there is serious question about whether or not the population studied could have met the usual criteria for MDD, based on the HAMD-17 baseline scores, despite the assertion
L376: that most did meet MDD criteria. Thus, I consider this an uninterpretable trial with regard to the efficacy of gepirone in MDD.
L377: -Study 03A7C-001-B: I agree with Drs. Kelly/Mahjoob that the very striking treatment by center interaction, i.e., the interaction revealing that the overall positive outcome from this trial derives entirely from the smallest center with a placebo response rate that is so low that it falls far outside the norm for this kind of trial, is a fatal flaw in the results that cannot be overlooked. Thus, I consider this another uninterpretable trial.
L378: -Study 03A7A-002: Despite the flawed design of this unusual study, it may have provided some indication of antidepressant activity, had the appropriate outcomes been designated as primary in the protocol. However, as I've indicated, the outcomes that were designated in the protocol as important did not yield positive results, and for the more appropriate outcomes defining various states of relapse, only some of these yielded positive results, and all were post hoc. Thus, I consider this another uninterpretable study.
L379: It is unfortunate that these deficiencies regarding the IR studies were not recognized earlier in the process, e.g., at the initial refusal to file, however, we were dealing with summary data at the time, rather than the more complete reports that were necessary to fully recognize these problems. In addition, the primary focus was on the inadequacies in study 53, the 1 ER study considered by the sponsor to be positive at that time. The sponsor, of course, may wish to challenge this conclusion that none of these 3 gepirone IR studies can count in support of the efficacy of gepirone ER in MDD. Of these 3 studies, only 03A7A-003 might conceivably withstand such a challenge, however, and that would still yield only 1 positive study for each of the IR and ER formulations. In my view, that still not would not be sufficient to support a claim for gepirone ER, since it does not meet my requirement for a "bridge" between the 2 formulations.
L380: ## Other Issues:
L381: Drs. Kelly/Mahjoob raise a concern about the many negative trials in the overall development program for both the IR and ER formulations. I fully agree, as noted in my introductory comments to this efficacy discussion. I consider the following 3 gepirone ER studies to be negative (CN105-078; CN105-083; 134002) and also 1 gepirone IR study (03A7C-001A-2496). All 4 studies were, in my view, sufficiently adequate by design, including gepirone dose, duration, population studied, assessment methods, and study conduct otherwise, to be considered relevant to the efficacy of gepirone, and yet all 4 failed to show a benefit for gepirone. While I agree that these studies are not readily interpretable, I still think they need to be considered as negative trials for gepirone, and therefore, should be considered in the overall benefit assessment for this drug. Thus, in summary, of 5 studies that appear, on face, to be relevant for consideration of the efficacy of gepirone, 1 of 4 gepirone ER studies yielded a positive result, and a fifth study, for gepirone IR, failed to yield a positive result. Generally we have found that, overall, roughly half of antidepressant trials that appear to be adequate by design fail to beat placebo, even for drugs that we know are effective. Thus, this result for gepirone, i.e., 1 of 5 relevant studies being positive, while of concern, is not a result that would lead one to conclude that gepirone is ineffective as an antidepressant.
L382: Nevertheless, in my view, 1 additional positive study for the ER formulation would be both necessary and persuasive of the antidepressant efficacy for this formulation.
L383: Drs. Kelly/Mahjoob also raise a concern about what they consider to be high dropout rates for the short-term trials. Our experience with antidepressant trials has been to expect fairly high rates of dropout, and I don't consider the rates for the gepirone trials generally excessive, with a few exceptions in some of the earlier trials. Some of the earlier high dropout rates may have been a result of intolerability for the gepirone IR, given the way it was being used. In fact, the dropouts are generally lower for the 1 positive study for the ER formulation (134001), in particular for adverse events. This formulation was developed to specifically address the tolerability findings with the IR formulation, and this is the formulation that will be marketed if this drug is to be approved.
L384: ## 5.2 Safety Data
L385: ## 5.2.1 Clinical Data Sources for Safety Review
L386: The development program for gepirone extended over a long period of time, and ownership transferred twice. In addition, there was a shift in focus from an initial program with the IR formulation to a later program focusing on the ER formulation. The earlier data were generated by the BMS program, and the more recent data by Organon.
L387: This development program, including all patients exposed to both the IR and ER formulations, up through the 9-18-01 safety update, contained 3,631 subjects exposed to 1 or more doses of gepirone. Of these exposures, 2,708 came from phase 2-3 studies. As noted, the long-term exposure was limited, with 227 exposed to a modal dose of \ge 20 mg/day for at least 6 months, and 80 subjects exposed to a modal dose of \ge 20 mg/day for 1 year. The mean age of patients was roughly 40, with less than 2-3% &gt;65. Patients were predominantly Caucasian, and about 2/3 female.
L388: ## 5.2.2 Adverse Event Profile for Gepirone ER
L389: ## 5.2.2.1 Overview
L390: Based on the data available, given the noted deficiencies, the adverse event profile included the following adverse events with an incidence of \ge 5\% or more for gepirone ER and at least twice the placebo rate include: dizziness, nausea, insomnia, paresthesia, and vomiting. These same events also led to discontinuation in some patients. Little can be said about dose relatedness for adverse events, since the studies were all flexible dosing. There were no consistent effects on vital signs, at least in the sitting position. There was a suggestion of an orthostatic effect of gepirone (see below under 5.2.2.2). Generally there was no suggestion of gepirone related changes in chemistry, hematology, or urinalysis, with 2
L391: exceptions (see below under 5.2.2.2). Generally there was no suggestion of a gepirone related effect on ECG parameters.
L392: ## 5.2.2.2 Adverse Events of Particular Note for Gepirone ER
L393: ## Dizziness
L394: Dizziness was reported in 34% of gepirone ER exposed patients (vs 13% pbo) in ph2-3 depression trials and led to discontinuation in 2.3% (vs 0.4% pbo). Orthostatic changes also occurred more frequently in gepirone ER exposed patients (1% GER vs 0.4% pbo) in ph2-3 depression trials, and may have accounted for some of the reports of dizziness. We are proposing to emphasize this rather common AE in Precautions, given the implications for driving, etc.
L395: ## Lack of Sexual Dysfunction
L396: The sponsor has proposed a (b) (4) the apparent finding of no difference between GER and placebo in sexual dysfunction. However, it is not clear how sensitive the approach was for detecting sexual dysfunction, and in the absence of a sensitive method, I am not inclined to permit them to promote GER as a drug that is free of such effects. We will request more information regarding the method, and suggest a separate sensitive study (or studies) of sexual dysfunction to support such a claim.
L397: ## Lack of Assessment for Discontinuation Emergent Symptoms
L398: The sponsor suggests that there is no evidence for discontinuation emergent symptoms associated with stopping gepirone, however, it seems clear that there was no attempt, even in the randomized withdrawal study (002), to sensitively assess for such symptoms. Thus, it is unknown whether or not such symptoms occur.
L399: ## Neutropenia in Absence of Leukopenia
L400: Dr. Racoosin has discussed the unusual finding of a slight excess of low ANC's in gepirone ER (GER) pts vs gepirone IR (GIR) patients, in the absence of an effect on total WBC's. This finding is of unknown clinical significance, and may be spurious.
L401: ## Hyperbilirubinemia
L402: Dr. Racoosin has discussed the finding of an excess GIR/GER patients with slight increases in total bilirubin vs pbo. These generally were not persistent changes, and were not associated with transaminase increases or any apparent clinical findings. These changes are of unknown significance.
L403: ## Allergic Reaction
L404: Dr. Racoosin has discussed the possibility of allergic reactions to GIR/GER. This is based on a severe case of hepatosplenomegaly associated with fever, rash, transaminitis, and eosinophilia, along with AE rates for the BMS ph 2-3 trials suggesting a slight excess of events that may indicate allergic reaction, i.e., arthralgia and myalgia. We will ask the sponsor to explore for this possibility.
L405: ## Weight Reduction
L406: Although the sponsor proposes labeling language suggesting a slight weight loss for gepirone compared to a slight weight gain for placebo, apparently the data are not consistent, and we have asked in the action letter for additional analyses to try to clarify what data would best characterize the effects of gepirone on weight, for purposes of labeling.
L407: ## Suicidal Ideation and Attempts
L408: There was a slight excess of risk for these events in GIR/GER patients vs pbo, however, this may be explained by greater exposure time for GIR/GER patients. We will ask the sponsor to adjust for time at risk with total person time.
L409: ## **5.2.2.3 Conclusions Regarding Safety of Gepirone ER**
L410: While there are no safety findings that would preclude the approvability of gepirone ER for MDD, there are several critical deficiencies in the safety data that need to be addressed before a final action can be taken on this NDA:
L411: - The minimum ICH criteria for long-term exposure data have not been met for gepirone.
- The safety data have not been presented in a manner that permits a separate characterization of the safety profile for the IR and ER formulations. This is clearly of interest, since it is the ER formulation that is to be marketed, and in fact, the ER formulation was presumably developed because of tolerability problems with the IR formulation.
- The data have not been presented in a manner that permits a pooling of the BMS and Organon data for safety analyses.
- The data have not been presented in a manner that permits a separation of safety findings in the depression population as distinct from other populations studied.
- The event “dizziness” has not been adequately explored, e.g., the time and severity course for this event, its relationship with orthostatic events, etc.
- The QTc data have not been adequately evaluated regarding the proportion of patients having changes from baseline of fixed magnitude, e.g., \ge 30 or \ge 60 msec.
- For 18% of adverse dropouts, the specific event(s) leading to dropout were not identified; there needs to be an effort to identify, where possible, these events.
L412: - The literature review is inadequate, including only references to 286 published papers, with the actual paper being provided in only 21 instances, and with no commentary on the relevance to safety of any findings reported in these papers. We will ask the sponsor to more fully address this requirement.
- The sponsor will be notified/reminded of these deficiencies in the action letter.

SUBJECT: Refusal to File NDA 21-164 for gepirone (for the treatment of depression)

TO: NDA 21-164
[Note: This memo should be filed with the 9-30-99 original submission of this NDA.]
L444: Refer to an 11-15-99 review of the 9-30-99 submission (for filing purposes) by Susan Molchan, M.D., for additional details on this application, and to HFD-120's refusal to file letter, issued 11-30-99.
L445: The sponsor has submitted an NDA for an extended release formulation of gepirone, including data from depression studies with both the immediate release (IR) and the extended release (ER) formulations. Since the IR and ER forms are unlikely to be bioequivalent, it seems clear that we would not be able to rely on efficacy data only from IR studies. This was noted at our 3-20-98 preNDA meeting with the sponsor. In general, it has been division policy to accept clinical evidence of efficacy from 1 additional study involving an ER formulation when a company has already demonstrated efficacy for an IR formulation. That policy would apply here as well.
L446: In this submission, the sponsor identifies studies 003 and 001B as short-term studies of the IR formulation that support the antidepressant efficacy of the IR, as well as study 002, a relapse prevention trial involving the IR formulation as an additional source of support for the IR. On face, I would agree that these trials are of adequate design and, if positive as suggested by the sponsor, would support the IR.
L447: The difficulty is with the ER data. There is only 1 study (53) that, on face, could provide support for the antidepressant efficacy of the ER formulation, since the other 2 credible ER studies, 78 and 83, were positive only on evaluable subset analyses, according to the sponsor. Thus, study 53 would need to be positive in order for this NDA to have any chance of approval. Unfortunately we were misled on the details of this study at our 3-20-98 preNDA meeting. It was our clear impression from that meeting that this study was designed as 2 independent trials at 2 separate sites, and given
L448: that primary plan, we agreed with the sponsor that there would be no basis for combining data from the 2 sites (see 3-20-98 minutes from that meeting). However, given that we did not have details from the protocol for that meeting, we cautioned that this issue would be a matter for review. As it turns out, the protocol for this study is quite clear that this was planned as a 2-center study with the intent of pooling the data, as in any multicenter trial. Since the sponsor seems to agree that the overall analysis of the data for this trial does not yield a positive outcome, there is no basis for proceeding to the analysis of the individual sites. Thus, on face, this study could not support the approvability of this NDA as a bridge from the IR data.
L449: As noted, we informed the sponsor of this conclusion in our refusal to file letter dated 11-30-99.
