L185: ## Bacterial Vaginosis (BV) and Pregnancy3
L186: - BV is a poly-microbial clinical syndrome resulting from replacement of the normal hydrogen peroxide producing Lactobacillus sp. in the vagina with high concentrations of anaerobic bacteria (e.g., Prevotella sp. and Mobiluncus sp.), G. vaginalis, Ureaplasma, Mycoplasma, and numerous fastidious or uncultivated anaerobes.

L213: ## Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling
L214: On June 30, 2015, the “*Content and Format of Labeling for Human Prescription Drug and Biological Products; Requirements for Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling*,”26 also known as the Pregnancy and Lactation Labeling Rule (PLLR) went into effect. The PLLR requirements include a change to the structure and content of labeling for human prescription drug and biologic products with regard to pregnancy and lactation and create a new subsection for information with regard to females and males of reproductive potential. Specifically, the pregnancy categories (A, B, C, D and X) are removed from all prescription drug and biological product labeling and a new format is required for all products that are subject to the 2006 Physicians Labeling Rule27 format to include information about the risks and benefits of using these products during pregnancy and lactation.

L290: **Study SYM-1219-201:** *A Phase 2, Multi-center, Prospective, Randomized, Double-blind, Placebo-controlled Study to Evaluate the Effectiveness and Safety of SYM-1219 for the Treatment of Women with Bacterial Vaginosis*
L291: This Phase 2 randomized controlled trial (RCT) was conducted in 215 women with BV at 24 clinical investigator (CI) sites in the United States (US). The primary study objective was to evaluate the safety and efficacy of SYM-1219 relative to placebo in treating BV.
L292: - Subjects were randomized in equal ratio to: (1) SYM-1219, 1 gram, single oral dose; (2) SYM-1219, 2 grams, single oral dose; or (3) placebo. Randomization was stratified by frequency of BV over the last 12 months, \le 3 versus \ge 4 (including current episode).
- The study medication was self-administered at randomization (Day 1) without regard to meals. Subjects completed an efficacy questionnaire, by phone interview daily on Days 1-7 and in person at the Test of Cure (TOC) visit or at the End of Study (EOS) visit (both conducted between Days 21-30). Subjects completed a safety questionnaire once about adverse events (AEs) by phone interview between Days 8-10.
- Primary Endpoint and Analysis: Clinical response (CR) at TOC visit (Days 21-30), with CR defined as (all of the following): (1) normal vaginal discharge, (2) negative 10% KOH Whiff test, and (3) clue cells fewer than 20% of epithelial cells (saline wet mount)

L528: ## 3.2.8.2 Statistical Analyses
L529: ## 3.2.8.2.1 Primary Analysis
L530: The sponsor used a mixed effects model with the change from baseline measurement in QTc as the dependent variable; treatment, time, period, sequence, and the time-by-treatment interaction as fixed factors; baseline QTc values as a covariate; and subject within sequence as random effect. An unstructured covariance structure for the repeated measures at postdose timepoints for subjects within treatment period was chosen. L631: Preclinical Cardiac Safety	
The in vitro and nonclinical data to date suggest no impact of secnidazole on the QTc interval.
A single oral dose cardiovascular safety pharmacology study in beagle dogs was conducted to evaluate the potential cardiovascular responses to administration of secnidazole. Female Beagle dogs received doses of at 20, 60, and 200 mg/kg of secnidazole. No animals died or were deemed moribund during the study. There were no test article-related clinical observations.
Administration of secnidazole orally at 200 mg/kg resulted in significantly lower systolic blood pressure and mean blood pressure. No significant changes in diastolic blood pressure were seen in response to secnidazole. Heart rate was significantly increased and a significantly shorter P-R interval was observed for the high dose group. The increased heart rate was interpreted to be a baroreflex response to the drop in systolic and mean blood pressure, whereas the shorter PR interval was related to the increased heart rate. These were considered secondary responses and not test article-related. No other changes in ECG parameters were considered test article-related. Based on effects of secnidazole on blood pressure the NOEL (No-Observable-Effect Level) was determined to be 60 mg/kg.
The in vitro effects of secnidazole on the hERG (human ether-à-go-go-related gene) channel current (a surrogate for IKr, the rapidly activating delayed rectifier cardiac potassium current) at near-physiological temperature were studied. Two concentrations of SYM-1219 were tested, 10 \muM and 300 \muM. SYM-1219 inhibited hERG current by (Mean \pm SEM; n = 4) 2.9 \pm 0.9% at 10 \muM and 3.0 \pm 1.2% at 300 \muM versus 1.6 \pm 0.7% (n = 3) in control, but these changes were not statistically significant (P < 0.05) when compared to vehicle control values. The IC50 for the inhibitory effect of SYM-1219 on hERG potassium current was not calculated but was estimated to be greater than 300 \muM.

L635: **ECG Findings.** In SYM-1219-101 (Parts A1 and A2) and SYM-1219-102 triplicate ECGs were recorded pre-dose and at 14 post-dose time points. The most consistent ECG finding across the 2 studies was a small increase in heart rate in all treatment conditions at most post-dose time points. In SYM-1219-101 peak change in heart rate was observed at 8 hr post-dose with both the 1 g (\Delta heart rate=9\pm 5.3 bpm, p&lt;0.001) and 2 g (\Delta heart rate=11\pm 8.5 bpm, p&lt;0.001) treatments. In SYM-1219-102 the peak change among all 3 groups was observed with the SYM-1219 (manufactured by Catalent) in the fed condition at 0.25 hr Post-dose (\Delta heart rate=15\pm 7.5 bpm, p&lt;0.001); the other 2 treatments showed smaller increases at this time. In both studies the heart rate changes were consistent with changes in posture and activity along with circadian variation in heart rate. The changes in heart rate likely accounted for the small changes in PR, QT, QTc, QTcB and QTcF intervals in both studies; and none of these mean interval changes was clinically important.
L636: In SYM-1219-101 the changes in QTcF were small and negative at most post-dose time points with both the 1 g and 2 g treatments. The largest observed absolute change with either dose was \DeltaQTcF=-8\pm 6.8 msec (p&lt;0.001) with the 2 g treatment at 8 Hr Post-dose. In SYM-1219-102 the mean change from Pre-dose also was negative (\DeltaQTcF&lt;0) at most post-dose time points for all 3 treatments except at 18 Hr Post-dose when mean change was positive (\DeltaQTcF&gt;0) for all 3 treatments. Since the mean change at 12 Hr Post-dose was \DeltaQTcF&lt;0 and at 24 Hr Post-dose was \DeltaQTcF\le0 with all 3 treatments, the increase at 18 Hr Post-dose appeared to be a sporadic aberration of no clinical importance.
