L267: ## **BACKGROUND: **
L268: Biomarin Pharmaceutical is developing Phenoptin (6R-tetrahydrobiopterin, sapropterin hydrochloride) as a treatment for phenylketonuria (PKU). PKU is an autosomal recessive genetic metabolic disease caused by mutations in the phenylalanine hydroxylase gene (PAH) that leads to elevated levels of phenylalanine (Phe) in the blood, brain, and other tissues. Elevated Phe levels are neurotoxic and result in mental retardation, neurocognitive, and other neuropsychiatric disorders if left untreated. Currently, the only treatment is a highly restrictive, low-Phe diet, beginning at birth. ————————————————————————————————————
L269: Biomarin has received orphan drug designation for Phenoptin treatment of PKU from the FDA Office of Orphan Products Development.
L270: ## Biomarin submitted specific questions for Discussion:
L271: ## Clinical Questions:
L272: 1. Is long-term reduction in blood Phe levels compared to placebo acceptable as the primary endpoint for assessment of efficacy in the Phase 3 study?
L273: ## **DMEDP** response:
L274: Yes, blood phenylalanine levels are an acceptable primary endpoint in the clinical studies submitted to the NDA. However, the division will request that the sponsor submit a plan for post-approval follow up of treated patients to assess long term potential benefits of treatment (e.g., IQ and neuropsychological status).
L275: 2. Is the statistical analysis plan declared in Protocol PKU-003 acceptable?
L276: ## DMEDP response:
L277: *The data should be analyzed by both LOCF and longitudinal methods, however, the primary analysis conducted should be LOCF.*
L278: *The sponsor was asked why "100" and "0" were both to be excluded from the confidence interval for treatment difference and was informed that it was only necessary to exclude "0" in order to demonstrate a statistical difference. The confidence interval will also be examined from a clinical perspective.*
L279: 3. Is Protocol PKU-003 adequate as the pivotal protocol for NDA approval?
L280: ## DMEDP response:
L281: *The division feels that studies 003 and 005 may not be adequate to permit labeling with regard to dosing. Therefore, we suggest you redesign study 003 as a placebo controlled trial with a forced titration scheme. Responders could still be defined using the data from study 001 in patients treated with 10mg/kg/day. Patients in the drug group would receive 5mg/kg/day for 2 weeks followed by 10mg/kg/day for 2 weeks and finally 20mg/kg/day for the last 2 weeks. Responders could be randomized to drug or placebo asymmetrically so that more patients would be in the active treatment group. Patients should be stratified depending on whether or not they are on a low Phe diet.*
L282: *The sponsor was asked to include age appropriate prespecified escape criteria in the trial based on blood phenylalanine levels.*
L283: 4. Does the Division have any comments regarding the design of the extension study PKU-004 or the dosing study PKU-005?
L284: ## DMEDP response:
L285: *Instead of treating everyone with 10mg/kg/day as originally proposed, it is recommended that the information from the forced dose titration phase, study 003, be used to determine the dose for each patient enrolled in the open label extension trial 004. Alternatively, this open label study can be used by individual physicians to titrate patients to the most effective dose. Since the original trial 003 will be only 6 weeks instead of 12 weeks, it is recommended that this trial is extended to 18 weeks so that there will still be 6 month data available. Age appropriate prespecified escape criteria based on blood phenylalanine level should be included in the trial 004 as well.*
L286: *Study 005 is no longer needed if study 003 is redesigned to include a forced titration.*
L287: 5. Is the Daiichi Suntory PK data sufficient for registration?
L288: ## DMEDP response:
L289: *No, the data are not sufficient. We would request a Pk study in pediatric patients and a population PK approach can be considered if there are some limitations for blood samplings or other study conditions for pediatric patients.*
L290: *In addition, since fruit juices can alter the bioavailability of drug, we recommend a bioavailability study be conducted to assure that fruit juices are comparable to water. L499: ## 2.0 DISCUSSION
L500: - BioMarin proposes to submit separate ISS documents for the Daiichi Asubio clinical study data and the BioMarin Pharmaceutical study data. Is this acceptable to the Agency?
L501: ## **Response:**
L502: No. Phenoptin™ (sapropterin dihydrochloride; tetrahydrobiopterin; BH4) and Biopten® appear to be the same molecular species, and we require a single integrated summary of safety (ISS) be submitted for review for your NDA according to 21 CFR 314.50 (d) (5) (vi) (a). L682: ## **Disease Background**
L683: PKU is a rare, inborn error of metabolism (autosomal recessive) that results from the deficient activity of the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase. Patients with PKU have disordered metabolism of phenylalanine (Phe) that results in elevated blood Phe levels on exposure to Phe in the diet. The clinical manifestations of PKU are due to elevated blood Phe levels which are toxic. If left untreated, elevated blood Phe levels cause progressive neurocognitive impairment. Cardiac manifestations/pathology are not typically found in PKU. Currently available treatment for PKU is dietary restriction of Phe and supplementation with low-Phe medical foods, which is difficult to maintain throughout life. Compliance with therapy, particular in older children, adolescents, and adults, is extremely difficult. Thus, new treatments for PKU are needed.

L702: Sapropterin was found to reduce blood Phe levels in BH4-responsive PKU patients, and to have a generally favorable risk-benefit profile. Thus, the Division anticipates that sapropterin will likely receive approval for the treatment of PKU on or around November 28, 2007. However, no routine ECG or cardiac safety monitoring was performed during the clinical development program.
L703: In the hypertension studies performed to date, patients were evaluated by ECG monitoring at doses up to 11 mg/kg, less than the proposed dosing for PKU of up to 20 mg/kg. A thorough QT study has not been performed as part of the clinical development program.

The study duration does not meet the usual 104-Week study duration. Percentage survival was at least 48% for males and 58% for females in each group surviving for 78 weeks. The incidence of benign adrenal pheochromocytoma was dose-related and significantly increased in HD males relative to the control group (by Fisher's Exact Probability test applied as a one-tailed test). L1075: ## Appendix A to Action Package Checklist
L1076: An NDA or NDA supplemental application is likely to be a 505(b)(2) application if:
L1077: - It relies on published literature to meet any of the approval requirements, and the applicant does not have a written right of reference to the underlying data. If published literature is cited in the NDA but is not necessary for approval, the inclusion of such literature will not, in itself, make the application a 505(b)(2) application.
- Or it relies for approval on the Agency's previous findings of safety and efficacy for a listed drug product and the applicant does not own or have right to reference the data supporting that approval.
- Or it relies on what is "generally known" or "scientifically accepted" about a class of products to support the safety or effectiveness of the particular drug for which the applicant is seeking approval. (Note, however, that this does not mean *any* reference to general information or knowledge (e.g., about disease etiology, support for particular endpoints, methods of analysis) causes the application to be a 505(b)(2) application.)
L1078: Types of products for which 505(b)(2) applications are likely to be submitted include: fixed-dose combination drug products (e.g., heart drug and diuretic (hydrochlorothiazide) combinations); OTC monograph deviations (see 21 CFR 330.11); new dosage forms; new indications; and, new salts.
L1079: An efficacy supplement can be either a (b)(1) or a (b)(2) regardless of whether the original NDA was a (b)(1) or a (b)(2).
L1080: An efficacy supplement is a 505(b)(1) supplement if the supplement contains all of the information needed to support the approval of the change proposed in the supplement. For example, if the supplemental application is for a new indication, the supplement is a 505(b)(1) if:
L1081: - The applicant has conducted its own studies to support the new indication (or otherwise owns or has right of reference to the data/studies).
- And no additional information beyond what is included in the supplement or was embodied in the finding of safety and effectiveness for the original application or previously approved supplements is needed to support the change. For example, this would likely be the case with respect to safety considerations if the dose(s) was/were the same as (or lower than) the original application.
- And all other "criteria" are met (e.g., the applicant owns or has right of reference to the data relied upon for approval of the supplement, the application does not rely for approval on published literature based on data to which the applicant does not have a right of reference).
L1082: An efficacy supplement is a 505(b)(2) supplement if:
L1083: - Approval of the change proposed in the supplemental application would require data beyond that needed to support our previous finding of safety and efficacy in the approval of the original application (or earlier supplement), and the applicant has not conducted all of its own studies for approval of the change, or obtained a right to reference studies it does not own. For example, if the change were for a new indication AND a higher dose, we would likely require clinical efficacy data and preclinical safety data to approve the higher dose. If the applicant provided the effectiveness data, but had to rely on a different listed drug, or a new aspect of a previously cited listed drug, to support the safety of the new dose, the supplement would be a 505(b)(2).
- Or the applicant relies for approval of the supplement on published literature that is based on data that the applicant does not own or have a right to reference. If published literature is cited in the supplement but is not necessary for approval, the inclusion of such literature will not, in itself, make the supplement a 505(b)(2) supplement.
- Or the applicant is relying upon any data they do not own or to which they do not have right of reference.
L1084: If you have questions about whether an application is a 505(b)(1) or 505(b)(2) application, consult with your ODE's Office of Regulatory Policy representative.
