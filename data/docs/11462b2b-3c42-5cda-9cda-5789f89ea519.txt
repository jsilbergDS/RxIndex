L51: ## 1. Executive Summary
L52: ## 1.1. Product Introduction
L53: In hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (hATTR amyloidosis), misfolding of the transthyretin (TTR) protein leads to its aggregation and the formation of amyloid fibrils, which interfere with the normal function of affected organ systems, including the peripheral nervous system, which was evaluated for this product.
L54: Vutrisiran is a chemically modified double-stranded small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) that targets variant and wild-type transthyretin (TTR) messenger RNA (mRNA) and is covalently linked to a ligand containing three N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc) residues to enable delivery of the siRNA to hepatocytes. It is related to patisiran, approved for the same indication in 2018, but vutrisiran has been chemically modified for greater metabolic stability, prolonged liver residence time, and less frequent dosing (every 3 months) compared to the dosing every 3 weeks for patisiran.
L55: Note that reviewer commentary throughout the text is presented in *italics*.
L56: ## 1.2. Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
L57: Study HELIOS-A is a single adequate and well-controlled study that can support approval of vutrisiran for the treatment of adults with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis with polyneuropathy (hATTR-PN), also known as transthyretin familial amyloid polyneuropathy (TTR-FAP).
L58: The HELIOS-A study was a well-designed, multinational, 18-month study that has provided reliable, clinically meaningful, and statistically strong evidence of an effect of vutrisiran on polyneuropathy in subjects with hATTR. The placebo group from the approved patisiran NDA 210922 phase 3 APOLLO study, which enrolled a similar subject population and incorporated similar endpoints to HELIOS-A, was used as an external control group for the efficacy analyses of vutrisiran. The mean scores on the HELIOS-A study's primary efficacy endpoint in vutrisiran-treated subjects were notably improved from baseline, which is inconsistent with the known course of the condition. Although the trial was not designed to demonstrate that subjects statistically improved on treatment, it is clear that treatment with vutrisiran resulted in at least the stability of polyneuropathy in many subjects. The results of the primary analyses from this trial were also supported by highly statistically significant positive results on the trial's pre-specified secondary efficacy endpoints at Month 9.

L61: ## 1.3. Benefit-Risk Assessment
L62: ## Benefit-Risk Integrated Assessment
L63: Vutrisiran (Amvuttra™, called ALN-TTRSC02 in clinical trials) is a chemically modified double-stranded small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) that targets transthyretin (TTR) messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) in order to suppress the production of TTR protein. Clinical studies evaluated subjects with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis with polyneuropathy (hATTR-PN). Based on the positive results of a single adequate and well-controlled study (Study HELIOS-A) and confirmatory evidence from pharmacodynamic/mechanistic data of transthyretin reduction with vutrisiran and other drugs in the class (i.e., patisiran, inotersen), it is the conclusion of this reviewer that the effectiveness of vutrisiran has been established for the treatment of adults with hATTR-PN.
L64: Hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (hATTR amyloidosis) is an autosomal dominant disorder (&gt;120 TTR gene mutations known) that is characterized by the slowly progressive buildup of amyloid protein in the peripheral and central nervous systems, heart, kidneys, eyes, bone, and gastrointestinal tract. Death usually occurs within 5 to 12 years after onset, most often due to cardiac dysfunction, infection, or cachexia. The exact incidence of hATTR amyloidosis is unknown and varies geographically, but is estimated to be 1/100,000 in U.S. Caucasians. Approximately 100 to 2500 individuals are estimated to have hATTR-PN in the United States. These subjects typically develop sensorimotor polyneuropathy with numbness, pain, and weakness, as well as focal nerve lesions (e.g., carpal tunnel syndrome) and autonomic dysfunction (e.g., orthostatic hypotension).
L65: Patisiran and inotersen are the two drugs approved for the treatment of the neuropathy of hATTR amyloidosis in the U.S. Vutrisiran is related to patisiran, approved for the same indication in 2018, but vutrisiran has been chemically modified for less frequent subcutaneous dosing (every 3 months) compared to the intravenous dosing every 3 weeks for patisiran. Inotersen is a transthyretin-directed antisense oligonucleotide indicated for treatment of the polyneuropathy of hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis in adults. Inotersen is administered by weekly subcutaneous injection. Inotersen is available only through a restricted distribution program called the TEGSEDI REMS Program because of the risks of thrombocytopenia and glomerulonephritis. Liver transplant may be an option for some patients. Tafamidis (approved for treatment of the cardiomyopathy of wild type or hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis in adults, but not for the treatment of hATTR-PN) and diflunisal are reported to be used off-label.
L66: An 18-month external placebo-controlled multinational study of vutrisiran in adults with hATTR-PN (122 vutrisiran, 77 external placebo control) evaluated polyneuropathy through clinical neurological examinations and tests of nerve conduction, sensation, and postural blood pressure. The placebo-treated subjects worsened on these measures during the course of the trial, as would be expected from disease progression. In contrast, the average scores in the vutrisiran-treated subjects improved modestly from baseline. Whereas only approximately 18% of placebo-
L67: treated subjects improved by month 9, approximately 50% of vutrisiran-treated subjects demonstrated numerical improvements of polyneuropathy scores during the trial. There was a similar pattern of positive results on a subject-reported subjective assessment of the clinical impact of polyneuropathy on their quality of life. Vutrisiran-treated subjects had a mean serum TTR reduction of 81% from baseline at treatment Month 9, compared to a 1.5% serum TTR reduction in the external placebo control group.
L68: The most commonly observed (\ge 5\%) adverse events associated with the use of vutrisiran in the 18-month external placebo-controlled study were arthralgia, vitamin A decreased, and dyspnea. The proportion of subjects experiencing serious adverse reactions was 24% in the vutrisiran group and 40% in the external placebo group. The adverse events that caused the most subjects to stop taking vutrisiran across all clinical studies were cardiac failure or cardiac death (3 subjects, 2.5%).
L69: The conclusion of this review is that substantial evidence of clinical effectiveness and an acceptable safety profile have been established to support an approval of vutrisiran for the treatment of polyneuropathy in adults with hATTR.

L77: ## 2. Therapeutic Context
L78: ## 2.1. Analysis of Condition
L79: Hereditary transthyretin-mediated amyloidosis (hATTR amyloidosis) is an autosomal dominant disorder (&gt;120 TTR gene mutations known) that is characterized by the slowly progressive buildup of amyloid protein in the peripheral and central nervous systems, heart, kidneys, eyes, bone, and gastrointestinal tract. TTR is a tetrameric protein primarily produced in hepatocytes. The disease is caused by genetic mutations in the TTR gene that lead the tetrameric TTR protein to break into monomeric units that misfold and aggregate as amyloid fibril deposits.
L80: There are three general forms of the disease, although patients can have overlapping symptoms from all three forms. The neuropathic form (hereditary TTR amyloidosis polyneuropathy [hATTR-PN], also known as transthyretin familial amyloid polyneuropathy [TTRFAP]), is defined by the presence of sensorimotor peripheral neuropathy (with symptoms of numbness, pain, and weakness), focal nerve lesions (e.g., carpal tunnel syndrome), autonomic dysfunction (e.g., orthostatic hypotension, gastrointestinal dysfunction), vitreous opacity of the eye, and glaucoma. The leptomeningeal form is defined by the presence of stroke, intracranial hemorrhage, hydrocephalus, ataxia, spastic paralysis, seizures, dementia, psychosis, and vision impairment. The cardiac form is defined by the presence of arrhythmia, cardiomegaly, heart failure, and death.
L81: The exact incidence of hATTR is unknown and varies geographically, but is estimated to be 1/100,000 in U.S. Caucasians. Between approximately 100 to 2500 individuals are estimated to have hATTR-PN in the United States (Schmidt et al., 2018).
L82: Symptom onset occurs between 20 and 70 years of age. Death usually occurs within 5 to 12 years after diagnosis, most often due to cardiac dysfunction, infection, or cachexia. L135: ## Overview and Objective
L136: The HELIOS-A study is a Phase 3 global, randomized, open-label, externally controlled study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of vutrisiran (ALN-TTRSC02) in subjects with polyneuropathy caused by hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis (hATTR Amyloidosis).
L137: The objectives of the study are described by the Applicant as follows:
L138: - The primary objective of the study is to determine the efficacy of vutrisiran in subjects with hATTR amyloidosis by evaluating the effect on neurologic impairment relative to an external control group.
- The secondary objectives of the study are to determine the efficacy of vutrisiran on quality of life, gait speed, neurologic impairment, nutritional status, and disability; and to demonstrate the noninferiority of vutrisiran compared to patisiran with respect to serum transthyretin (TTR) levels.
- The exploratory objectives of the study are to determine the effect of vutrisiran on the following clinical parameters:Disability and nutritional status
- Manifestations of cardiac amyloid involvement
- Other assessment of neurologic impairment
- Other assessments of quality of life
- Disease stage
- Performance of daily activities
L139: Additional exploratory objectives are
L140: - To characterize the pharmacodynamic (PD) effect of vutrisiran and patisiran on serum TTR and vitamin A levels
L141: - To characterize plasma pharmacokinetic (PK) of vutrisiran and patisiran
- To assess presence of antidrug antibodies (ADAs) to vutrisiran and patisiran.
L142: The safety objective is to determine the safety and tolerability of vutrisiran in subjects with hATTR amyloidosis.
L143: ## Trial Design
L144: This is an ongoing, global, Phase 3, randomized, open-label, externally controlled study designed to evaluate efficacy, safety, PK, and PD of vutrisiran in adult subjects with hATTR amyloidosis with polyneuropathy. The study is being conducted in 2 parts: an 18-month treatment period, with the primary efficacy analysis at Month 9 and efficacy analyses at Month 18, followed by an 18-month treatment extension period, in which subjects initially treated with patisiran are switched to vutrisiran. The placebo group of the APOLLO study (the pivotal Phase 3 study from the approved patisiran NDA 210922) is used as an external control for the primary and most secondary and exploratory efficacy analyses of vutrisiran.
L145: - **Choice of control group:**The placebo group of the APOLLO (ALN-TTR02-004) study is used as an external control for the primary, most secondary, and most exploratory efficacy analyses. This external placebo group consists of subjects with hereditary transthyretin amyloidosis with polyneuropathy (hATTR-PN) who are comparable to the hATTR-PN subjects enrolled in the HELIOS-A study. There were no clinically significant differences in the enrollment criteria for the APOLLO and HELIOS-A studies. The HELIOS-A study also includes an active control group receiving the approved treatment patisiran.

L164: ## **Reviewer Comment:**
L165: *The primary endpoint, mNIS+7, is composed of a clinical exam-based neuropathy impairment score (NIS) combined with electrophysiologic measures of small and large nerve fiber function (+7) such as nerve conduction studies (NCS), quantitative sensory testing (QST), and measurement of autonomic function (postural blood pressure). Many of the individual components of the score, such as nerve conduction studies, are clearly biomarkers that do not, of themselves, represent direct clinical benefit. Other components of the score, such as motor and sensory function by neurological exam, also are not direct measures of clinical benefit, as differences detected by the physician might not be perceptible to the subject or result in improved function in daily activities. The mNIS+7 is an acceptable endpoint, but the results should be considered in the context of the results of the secondary endpoints, particularly the Norfolk QOL-DN as that is an assessment of patient function. Note that the mNIS+7 and Norfolk QOL-DN were also the efficacy endpoints for the APOLLO clinical study of patisiran, which was approved for the treatment of hATTR amyloidosis in 2018.*

L173: ## Norfolk QoL-DN
L174: The Norfolk QoL-DN questionnaire is a standardized 35-item patient-reported outcomes measure that is sensitive to the different features of diabetic neuropathy - small fiber, large fiber, and autonomic nerve function. There are 35 questions divided into 5 domains. The range of possible total scores is -4 to 136. Higher scores indicate a worse quality of life. A negative score is possible because in Question 31, "Very Good" is scored as -1, and "Excellent" is scored as -2. In Question 32, "Somewhat better" is scored as -1 and "Much better" is scored as -2.

L304: ## 7.3. Integrated Assessment of Effectiveness
L305: The external placebo-controlled HELIOS-A Study is the one adequate and well-controlled efficacy study, with confirmatory evidence from pharmacodynamic/mechanistic data of transthyretin reduction, that can support approval of vutrisiran for the treatment of hATTR polyneuropathy. It is a single well-designed multicenter study that has provided reliable and statistically strong (p&lt;0.0001) evidence of an important clinical benefit, an effect on polyneuropathy as measured by the Modified Neurological Impairment Score +7 (mNIS+7). Vutrisiran-treated subjects demonstrated a numerical improvement on mNIS+7 scores from baseline, which is inconsistent with the natural course of the disease. The study has also shown a clinically meaningful and statistically strong (p&lt;0.0001) improvement in quality of life, as measured by the Norfolk Quality of Life-Diabetic Neuropathy (Norfolk QOL-DN) score as a key secondary endpoint. Further, vutrisiran showed statistically significant improvement on the secondary endpoint of gait speed (10-MWT). Additionally, the inclusion of an active comparator arm showed that efficacy on clinical outcomes with vutrisiran appeared to be comparable to the available therapy, patisiran.
L306: The pathophysiology of hATTR amyloidosis and the mechanism of action of vutrisiran are well understood. Mutant TTR protein causes amyloid deposition, which damages the involved organ systems. Prior to the approvals of patisiran and inotersen for hATTR polyneuropathy and

Pharmacodynamics data for vutrisiran show a reduction in TTR levels that mirrors the efficacy assessments in a manner consistent with the drug's mechanism of action. These data provide confirmatory evidence to support the substantial evidence of effectiveness of vutrisiran.
L309: ## 8. Review of Safety
L310: ## 8.1. Safety Review Approach
L311: Safety was assessed by evaluating the results from the Phase 3, randomized, open-label, externally controlled HELIOS-A (ALN-TTRSC02-002) study in adult subjects with hATTR amyloidosis with polyneuropathy, including both the treatment and extension periods. The study also included a smaller active control arm with patisiran to allow for relative comparisons for safety. The available narratives for deaths, serious adverse events, laboratory studies, and vital signs were reviewed. Reviewer analyses were conducted on the submitted datasets for the HELIOS-A trial, including adverse events, serious adverse events, deaths, and laboratory value assessments. The medical scientific literature was searched where appropriate for additional information. The Phase 1 study (Study 001) had a small population, short treatment duration, and small drug doses, yielding correspondingly limited safety information and therefore forms only a small part of the safety review. Additional blinded safety reports from the ongoing Phase 3 HELIOS-B study in hATTR cardiomyopathy subjects have also been submitted for review.
L312: Note that the safety analysis incorporates the additional safety data from the 120-Day Safety Update to this NDA submission.
L313: *It is also important to note that there are marked limitations to the ability to make safety comparisons between the vutrisiran treatment arm and the external placebo control group. The two groups are not concurrent, not randomized, and the APOLLO study that provides the placebo arm was conducted before the COVID pandemic. Therefore, there are limitations that preclude direct comparison of the two groups; however, the placebo group serves as a useful comparator to assess relative risks of adverse events for vutrisiran. Similarly, there are limitations in the ability to make direct comparisons to the patisiran arm. Although the patisiran arm is a concurrent arm, it enrolled fewer patients. However, as with the placebo arm, it provides important context for a relative comparison to the safety of patisiran.*

L349: ## 8.4. Safety Results
L350: ## 8.4.1. Deaths
L351: There were three deaths (2.5%) in the vutrisiran group in the HELIOS-A study: two deaths in the main study and one death in the extension period. The deaths were related to 1) Covid-19 with cardiac amyloidosis, heart failure, and pneumonia; 2) cardiac amyloidosis, cardiac failure, and iliac artery occlusion; and 3) sudden cardiac death (in a subject with a history of cardiac amyloidosis and congestive heart failure), respectively.
L352: In the patisiran active comparator group there were three deaths (7%) related to cardiac arrhythmia, Covid-19 infection, and ischemic heart disease, respectively.
L353: There were 6 deaths (8%) in the external placebo control group from the APOLLO study.
L354: Clinical Review
Rainer W. Paine, MD, PhD
NDA 215515
Amvuttra (vutrisiran)
L355: Reviewer Comment: Subjects in all of these studies were quite ill, with complex, chronic multisystem diseases and complicated courses. It is not possible to attribute the deaths to the investigational drug; conversely, it is not possible to be confident that the drug was not in some way contributory.
L356: The death rates are similar to rates that have been reported in the literature for the hATTR amyloidosis. In conclusion, it appears unlikely that these deaths were caused by the investigational drug.
L357: Although mortality was numerically lower in the vutrisiran group (2.5%) than in the external placebo group (8%) or concurrent comparator patisiran (7%), the HELIOS-A study was not designed to statistically evaluate an effect on survival and no conclusion regarding a potential long-term survival effect can be made.

L374: In the externally controlled HELIOS-A Study, 3 (2.5%) subjects in the vutrisiran group and 11 subjects (14.3%) in the external placebo group had an AE leading to treatment discontinuation. The third subject (b) (6) discontinued treatment with vutrisiran because of an adverse event of cardiac failure on study day 467 that resolved on study day 478. This subject had a medical history of coronary arterial stent insertion, coronary artery disease, cardiac failure congestive, acute myocardial infarction, first degree atrioventricular heart block, cardiac amyloidosis, cardiomyopathy, left anterior fascicular block, and non-sustained ventricular tachycardia. Because of these prior cardiac conditions it is not possible to conclude a causal association with the vutrisiran treatment.
L375: In addition to the AEs leading to treatment discontinuation described above, 1 subject (b)(6) discontinued vutrisiran treatment during the extension period due to a fatal event of sudden cardiac death on study day 602. L512: ## 8.5.3. Ocular Effects of Vitamin A reduction
L513: An assessment of ocular adverse events was conducted because TTR reduction in subjects treated with vutrisiran is associated with concomitant reduction in circulating serum levels of Retinol binding protein and vitamin A. L514: Adverse events with preferred terms in the system order class for eye disorders occurred in 27% of vutrisiran group subjects and 26% of the external placebo group. Night blindness was reported in 1 (0.8%) subject in the vutrisiran group and in 1 (1.3%) subject in the external placebo group. The vutrisiran group subject with night blindness (b)(6) had a medical history of optic ischemic neuropathy that was ongoing at the start of the study. The ophthalmologic examination conducted after the AE of night blindness did not report findings suggestive of vitamin A deficiency.
L515: Overall, there were no ocular vitamin A related abnormalities identified in the HELIOS-A study or open-label extension study.

L559: ## 8.10. Integrated Assessment of Safety
L560: The overall conclusion of this safety review is that the safety profile of vutrisiran is acceptable given the evidence of efficacy in the treatment of hATTR-PN and the poor prognosis for hATTR amyloidosis patients. The most commonly observed (\ge 5\% and at least 2% more frequently in the vutrisiran group than in the external placebo group) adverse events associated with the use of vutrisiran in the 18-month external placebo-controlled study were arthralgia, vitamin A decreased, and dyspnea. Vutrisiran reduces vitamin A levels in the body. All subjects received the recommended daily amount of vitamin A as a supplement and no ocular AEs related to low vitamin A levels were observed.
L561: Consistent with the natural history of hATTR amyloidosis, the adverse events that caused the most subjects to stop taking vutrisiran across all clinical studies were cardiac failure or cardiac death (3 subjects, 2.5%). Review of cardiac adverse events generally found no clinically significant difference in cardiac AEs and SAEs between the external placebo and vutrisiran cardiac subpopulations or total study populations.
L562: Mortality was numerically lower in the vutrisiran group (2.5%) than in the external placebo group (8%) or concurrent comparator patisiran group (7%). However, the Phase 3 HELIOS-A clinical study was not designed to statistically evaluate an effect on survival and no conclusion regarding a potential long-term survival effect can be made.
L563: This reviewer concludes that the safety profile of vutrisiran is acceptable, but that the label should include under Warnings and Precautions a description of the need for vitamin A supplementation to avoid possible vitamin A deficiency.
