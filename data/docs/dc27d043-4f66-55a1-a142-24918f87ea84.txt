L36: ## 1.0 BACKGROUND
L37: Novimmune S.A. states that NI-0501 is a fully human high affinity anti-interferon gamma (IFN\gamma) monoclonal antibody (mAb) that binds to and neutralizes human IFN\gamma. NI-0501 binds to soluble and receptor (IFN\gammaR1)-bound forms of IFN\gamma. After binding to its receptor, IFN\gamma acts to produce a variety of physiological and cellular responses.
L38: NI-0501 is being developed for the treatment of hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) in pediatric patients, primary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (pHLH) with refractory disease or with recurrent or progressive disease during conventional HLH therapy or who are intolerant to conventional to HLH therapy.
L39: On March 11, 2016, the Agency granted Breakthrough Designation for NI-0501 for the treatment of patients with primary HLH with refractory disease or with recurrent or progressive disease during conventional HLH therapy.
L40: On October 19, 2017, the Sponsor requested a type B meeting to gain Agency agreement that the results of the NI-0501-04 and NI-0501-05 studies are adequate to support a Biologics License Application (BLA) submission.
L41: FDA sent Preliminary Comments to Advyzom on December 22, 2017.
L42: ## 2.0 DISCUSSION
L43: ## Question 1: Safety and Efficacy
L44: a) Does the Agency agree that that the efficacy of emapalumab as demonstrated by the primary and secondary endpoints in NI-0501-04 and NI-0501-05, coupled with its safety profile as currently known, support a BLA filing of emapalumab for the treatment of patients with primary HLH?

b) Does the Agency have any specific comments on the safety and efficacy results from the NI-0501-04 and NI-0501-05 studies?

c) Does the Agency have any comments on the immunogenicity results to be included in the BLA from the NI-0501-04 and NI-0501-05 studies in addition to the data from NI-0501-03 and CU patients?
L45: ## FDA Response to Question 1a:
L46: Based on our current understanding of the topline efficacy and safety data, it appears that you could support an application for patients who have primary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) with refractory disease or with recurrent or progressive disease during conventional HLH therapy. We will conduct our own independent analysis of the datasets submitted in the application to confirm the efficacy claims.
L47: Based on your pre-specified primary analyses and current study population, we believe that the study results would only support a 2nd line treatment (primary hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) with refractory disease or with recurrent or progressive disease during conventional HLH therapy) indication. The final determination for the indication will be made during the review process.
L48: We note that single-arm trials do not adequately characterize time-to-event endpoints such as overall survival.
L49: In the BLA submission, you will need to address all the concerns conveyed in the September 11, 2017, communication. A decision on filing will be based on the completeness of the data provided in the application during the filing review.
L50: At the meeting, discuss the proposed timelines for BLA submission, including the timing of the 90 or 120 safety cutoff date.
L51: In general, we recommend that the data cutoff date is within 6 months of the submission of the application.

L100: ## **Additional Comments**
L101: In reference to the November 29, 2017, submission providing a proposed plan to determine the potential effects of emapalumab on metabolic cytochrome P450, we have the following comments:
L102: - We realize that TP-DI cocktail studies conducted in pediatric or adult patients with HLH may not be feasible due to the severity of the disease and the high number of required co-medications and procedures in this patient population. We also agree that the extent of the DDI might not be similar if conducted in non-HLH populations given that inflammatory cytokine levels are expected to be much higher in HLH population vs. non-HLH populations.
- We recommend that you collect PK data of commonly used concurrent medications, that are the substrates of CYP enzymes, and monitor the effectiveness of these drugs before, during and after treatment of emapalumab in all your proposed trials in order to have sufficient information that is useful to guide the dose adjustments for the substrates of CYP enzymes in your proposed label, if necessary. The adequacy of your assessments of potential effects of emapalumab on metabolic CYP enzymes will be a review issue after your BLA submitted.

L111: Because this drug product for this indication has an orphan drug designation, you are exempt from these requirements. L398: ## **Brief Description of the Drug**
L399: NI-0501 is a fully human anti-interferon gamma monoclonal antibody that binds to and neutralizes human IFN{gamma}. IFN{gamma} is produced predominately by natural killer and natural killer T cells as part of the innate immune response and by CD4+ Th1 and CD8+ Cytotoxic T Lymphocyte effector T cells once antigen-specific immunity develops. The rationale for the use of NI-0501 in Hemophagocytic Lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is based on animal models that demonstrates neutralization of the high levels of IFN{gamma} and correction of the biochemical and hematological abnormalities associated with HLH.
L400: 2 For a definition of available therapy refer to Guidance for Industry: “Expedited Programs for Serious Conditions—Drugs and Biologics” http://www.fda.gov/downloads/Drugs/GuidanceComplianceRegulatoryInformation/Guidances/UCM358301.pdf
L401: ## Brief Description of the disease and intended population
L402: Hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis (HLH) is a rare and severe disorder characterized by overwhelming hyperactivation of the immune system. A defect in the triggering of apoptosis results in decreased NK cell function and T-cell cytotoxicity leading to an unbridled activation of the immune system. The ensuing hypercytokine storm results in damage to tissues which can lead to systemic infections, multi-organ failure and death. The clinical scenario of HLH consists of hepatomegaly, fever, splenomegaly, neurological symptoms and rash. Common laboratory abnormalities include cytopenias, hyperferritinemia, hypertriglyceridemia, hypofibrinogemia and lymphohistiocyte infiltrate of the bone marrow and meninges, elevated levels of soluble IL-2 receptor (CD25), elevated IL-6 levels, and decreased NK cell function.
L403: HLH is primarily a pediatric disorder (infancy and early childhood) but it can be diagnosed in all ages including adults. HLH occurs as either a genetic disorder (primary HLH) or as an acquired disorder associated with underlying triggers (autoimmune disease, malignancies, infections). Most commonly an inheritable gene mutation that affects proteins such as perforin or munc 13-4 can be identified in primary HLH. The diagnosis of HLH is based on a molecular diagnosis of HLH or a demonstration of clinical (fever, splenomegaly, cytopenias, hepatitis) and laboratory parameters (increased ferritin, increased serum IL-2 levels, absent or decreased NK function, or demonstration of hemophagocytosis).
L404: If untreated, most patients will die within 2 months due to systemic infections, uncontrolled systemic inflammation, and multi-organ failure. The goal of therapy is to suppress the immune reaction and allow the patient to proceed to hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT). HLH-94 was the first prospective international treatment study for HLH and the cumulative experience from the HLH-94 protocol led to the development to the HLH-2004 protocol. The HLH-2004 protocol consists of 8 weeks of induction chemotherapy in combination with corticosteroids and immunosuppressive drugs (etoposide 150mg/m2, dexamethasone 10mg/m2, cyclosporine 3mg by mouth twice daily and intrathecal methotrexate). Induction therapy is followed by continuation therapy (weeks 9 to week 40 or longer) with the same drugs given at longer intervals. Continuation treatment is continued until hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is performed if donor available. If patients have disease reactivation or recurrent disease during continuation, re-intensification therapy with the initial intensity of protocol regimen is given. HSCT is the only potential curative therapy for patients with primary HLH (identified genetic deficiency and in very severe cases with no disease-associated mutations).
L405: The five year survival of patients with primary HLH treated with the HLH-94 protocol is 54% with 29% of patients dying during initial or continuation therapy. Approximately 40% of the initial responses to treatment are complete responses. The five year survival for patients with primary HLH treated with HLH-94 protocol followed by a HSCT was 66% (Trottestam 2011). ATG in combination with corticosteroids, and cyclosporine is sometimes used as initial treatment and has reported initial complete response of 73% (Mahlaoui 2007) however early relapses are common and 25% of patients died before HSCT. Additional immunosuppressive therapy is frequently employed in patients with suboptimal responses including alemtuzumab with only partial responses of 64% and no complete responses (Marsh 2013). There are no approved therapies for the treatment of refractory HLH.
