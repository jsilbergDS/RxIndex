L25: ## 1 INDICATIONS AND USAGE
L26: Picato® gel is indicated for the topical treatment of actinic keratosis.

L28: For topical use only; Picato® gel is not for oral, ophthalmic, or intravaginal use.

Patients should wash their hands immediately after applying Picato® gel and take care not to transfer the applied drug to other areas, including the eye. Patients should avoid washing and touching the treated area for a period of 6 hours after application of Picato® gel. Following this time, patients may wash the area with a mild soap.

L36: ## 5 WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS
L37: ## 5.1 Eye Exposure
L38: Eye disorders, including severe eye pain, eyelid edema, eyelid ptosis, periorbital edema can occur after exposure [see Adverse Reactions (6)]. Patients should wash hands well after applying Picato® gel, and avoid transfer of the drug to the periocular area during and after application [see Patient Counseling Information (17)]. If accidental exposure occurs, the area should be flushed with water and the patient should seek medical care as soon as possible [see Adverse Reactions (6)].
L39: ## 5.2 Local Skin Reactions
L40: Severe skin reactions in the treated area, including erythema, crusting, swelling, vesiculation/postulation, and erosion/ulceration, can occur after topical application of Picato® gel [see Adverse Reactions (6)]. Administration of Picato® gel is not recommended until the skin is healed from any previous drug or surgical treatment.

L60: ## 8 USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS
L61: ## 8.1 Pregnancy
L62: Pregnancy Category C
L63: There are no adequate and well-controlled studies of Picato® gel in pregnant women. Picato® gel should be used during pregnancy only if the potential benefit justifies the potential risk to the fetus.
L64: Systemic embryofetal development studies were conducted with ingenol mebutate in rats and rabbits. Intravenous doses of 1.5, 3, and 5 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day} (9, 18, and 30 \mu\text{g}/\text{m}^2/\text{day}) ingenol mebutate were administered during the period of organogenesis (gestational days 6 – 16) to pregnant female rats. No treatment related effects on embryofetal toxicity or teratogenicity were noted at doses up to 5 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day} (30 \mu\text{g}/\text{m}^2/\text{day}). Intravenous doses of 1, 2, and 4 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day} (12, 24, and 48 \mu\text{g}/\text{m}^2/\text{day}) ingenol mebutate were administered during the period of organogenesis (gestational days 6 – 18) to pregnant female rabbits. An increase in embryo-fetal mortality was noted at 4 \mu\text{g}/\text{kg}/\text{day} (48 \mu\text{g}/\text{m}^2/\text{day}). An increased incidence of fetal visceral and skeletal variations was noted in all three ingenol mebutate dose groups. The clinical relevance of these findings is unclear since systemic exposure of ingenol mebutate was not detected in subjects with actinic keratosis treated with Picato® gel, 0.05% applied to a 100 \text{cm}^2 treatment area [see *Clinical Pharmacology* (12.3)].
L65: ## 8.4 Pediatric Use
L66: Actinic keratosis is not a condition generally seen within the pediatric population.
L67: The safety and effectiveness of Picato® gel for actinic keratosis in patients less than 18 years of age have not been established.
L68: ## 8.5 Geriatric Use
L69: Of the 1165 subjects treated with Picato® gel in the clinical trials, 56% were 65 years and older and, 21% were 75 years and older. No overall differences in safety or effectiveness were observed between these subjects and younger subjects.

L87: ## 12.3 Pharmacokinetics
L88: ## Absorption
L89: The systemic exposure to Picato® gel, 0.05% was assessed in two studies in a total of 16 subjects with AK, following application of approximately 1 g of Picato® gel, 0.05% to an area of 100 cm2 of the dorsal forearm once daily for two consecutive days. In these studies, the blood levels of ingenol mebutate and two of its metabolites (acyl isomers of ingenol mebutate) were measured. Blood levels of ingenol mebutate and the two metabolites were below the lower limit of quantification (0.1 ng/mL) in all the blood samples of the subjects evaluated.
L90: ## Drug Interactions
L91: In vitro studies demonstrated that [3H]-ingenol mebutate undergoes extensive metabolism in human hepatocytes.
L92: In vitro studies to assess the potential of ingenol mebutate to inhibit or induce human cytochrome P450 (CYP) enzymes demonstrated that ingenol mebutate does not inhibit CYP 1A2, 2A6, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, 2E1, and 3A4 or induce CYP 1A2, 2C9, and 3A4. The estimated expected systemic exposure (&lt; 0.1 ng/mL) following topical application of Picato® gel, 0.05% to AK subjects in the pharmacokinetic studies described above is negligible compared to the concentrations of ingenol mebutate evaluated in the in vitro studies.
L93: ## 13 NONCLINICAL TOXICOLOGY
L94: ## 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility
L95: Long-term animal studies have not been performed to evaluate the carcinogenic potential of Picato® gel or ingenol mebutate. The effects of ingenol mebutate on fertility have not been evaluated.
L96: Ingenol mebutate was negative in the Ames test, in vitro mouse lymphoma assay, and in vivo rat micronucleus test, but positive in the Syrian hamster embryo (SHE) cell transformation assay.
L97: ## 14 CLINICAL STUDIES
L98: ## 14.1 Actinic Keratosis of the Face and Scalp
L99: In two double-blind, vehicle-controlled, clinical trials, 547 adult subjects with AK on the face or scalp were randomized to treatment with either Picato® gel, 0.015% or vehicle gel for 3 consecutive days, followed by an 8 week follow-up period. The studies enrolled subjects with 4 to 8 clinically typical, visible, discrete AK lesions within a 25 cm2 contiguous treatment area. Hypertrophic and hyperkeratotic lesions were excluded from treatment. On each scheduled dosing day, the study gel was applied to the entire treatment area. A total of 536 subjects (98%) completed these studies. Study subjects ranged from 34 to 89 years of age (mean 64 years) and 94% had Fitzpatrick skin type I, II, or III. Approximately 85% of subjects were male, and all Picato®-treated subjects were Caucasian.
L100: Efficacy was assessed at Day 57. Complete clearance rate was defined as the proportion of subjects with no (zero) clinically visible AK lesions in the treatment area. Partial clearance rate was defined as the proportion of subjects with 75% or greater reduction in the number of AK lesions at baseline in the selected treatment area. L137: ## How should I use Picato® gel?
L138: - Use Picato® gel exactly as your healthcare provider tells you. **Picato® gel is for skin use only.**
L139: - Your healthcare provider will tell you where to apply Picato® gel, and how often, and how long to apply it. Do not apply Picato® gel to other areas.

Call your healthcare provider if you get skin redness, flaking or scaling, crusting, or swelling that is more severe, or if you get blisters, pus, ulcers or breakdown of your skin.

L169: ## Instructions for Use
## PICATO® [Pih-KAY-toe]
## (ingenol mebutate)
## gel
## 0.015%
L170: Be sure that you read, understand, and follow these Instructions for Use before you use Picato® gel for the first time. Also read the Patient Information leaflet that comes with Picato® gel.
L171: ## **Important:**
L172: - **Always use Picato® gel exactly as your healthcare provider tells you.** Check with your healthcare provider or pharmacist if you are not sure.
- Only use **Picato® gel, 0.015%** to treat actinic keratosis on your **face and scalp**.
- Apply Picato® gel, 0.015% to the skin area to be treated **1 time each day for 3 days in a row**. Use a new tube for each day of treatment.
- Avoid touching the treatment area or doing activities that cause a lot of sweating for 6 hours after applying Picato® gel. After 6 hours you may wash the treatment area with a mild soap and water.
L173: ## **Do not:**
L174: - apply right after taking a shower or less than **2 hours** before bedtime.
- cover the treated area with bandages or other dressings after you have applied Picato® gel.
- **get Picato® gel in your eyes.** **Do not touch your eyes while you are applying Picato® gel.** Wash your hands well with soap and water after applying it. After applying Picato® gel, be careful to keep Picato® gel on the treated area from coming into contact with your eyes. Irritation may happen if you get Picato® gel in your eyes. **If you accidentally get Picato® gel in your eyes**, flush them with large amounts of water and get medical care as soon as possible. Also see the section of Patient Information leaflet section called “What are the possible side effects of Picato® gel?”
L175: ## **Applying Picato® gel.**
L176: **Step 1.** Open a new tube each time you use Picato® gel.
L177: **Step 2.** Remove cap from tube just before use. (See Figure A.)
L178: Figure A: Diagram showing hands opening a tube labeled 'Open one tube'.
L179: Figure A
L180: **Step 3.** Squeeze the gel from the tube onto a fingertip. (See Figure B.) Only use enough gel needed to cover the affected area, as directed by your healthcare provider. One tube contains enough gel to cover a skin area of about 2 inches by 2 inches.
L181: Figure B: Illustration showing a hand squeezing contents of a tube onto a fingertip.
L182: Figure B
L183: **Step 4.** Spread the gel evenly over only the skin area to be treated. Allow the treated area to dry for 15 minutes. (See Figure C.)
L184: Figure C: Illustration showing a hand applying product to a treatment area on the face.
L185: Figure C
L186: **Step 5.** Wash your hands right away after applying Picato® gel. (See Figure D.)
L187: Figure D: Illustration showing hands being washed.
L188: Figure D
L189: **Step 6.** Safely throw away (dispose of) the tube after use.
L190: Repeat the above steps for each day of treatment.
L191: ## How should I store Picato® gel?
L192: - Store Picato® gel in a refrigerator at 36°F to 46°F (2°C to 8°C). Do not freeze.
- Picato® gel has an expiration date (exp) marked on the end of the tube. Do not use the gel after this date.
- Safely throw away used Picato® gel tubes in household trash.
