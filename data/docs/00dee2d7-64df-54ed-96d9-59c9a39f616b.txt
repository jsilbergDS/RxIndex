L33: ## 3. Benefit-Risk Assessment
L34: Benefit-risk considerations for sutimlimab to reduce the need for transfusions in adult patients with CAD were based on the results of a well-controlled (baseline control) and adequate trial, BIVV009-03 Part A, also referred to as CARDINAL. CARDINAL was an international, single-arm, open-label trial in 24 adult patients with CAD and at least one blood transfusion in the 6 months prior to enrollment, hemoglobin (Hgb) \le 10 \text{ g/dL}, and symptomatic disease. The primary endpoint was the percent of patients who met criteria of the responder definition. Patients met the responder definition if they demonstrated an increase from baseline in hemoglobin (Hgb) level \ge 2 \text{ g/dL} or normalization of Hgb level to \ge 12 \text{ g/dL} at the treatment assessment time point (mean value from weeks 23, 25, and 26) and did not receive a blood transfusion from week 5 through week 26 or any treatment for CAD beyond what was permitted in the protocol. Results from the prospectively planned primary composite endpoint and analyses of the individual components demonstrated a response for patients with CAD. In total, 13 out of the 24 patients [54.2% (95% confidence interval (CI): 32.8%, 74.4%)] in full analyses set met the responder criteria the composite primary endpoint and the lower bound of the 95% CI of response was greater than the prespecified value of 30%. The results for each of the components of the composite endpoint were also persuasive: 15 (62.5%) patients met the Hgb endpoint, 17 (70.8%) were transfusion free from Week 5 to Week 26 and 22 (91.7%) patients did not receive protocol prohibited CAD medications. To further support efficacy, biomarkers of hemolysis including lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and bilirubin were measured. Among 14 patients with baseline and follow-up values, the mean bilirubin was 55 \mu\text{mol/L} (2.7-fold the upper limit of

The incidence is estimated at approximately 1 to 1.8 per million with a prevalence of approximately 13 to 16 per million, based on retrospective reviews from Scandinavian countries.
This rare disease primarily affects middle-aged to elderly adults. Cold Agglutinin Disease CAD is a form of Autoimmune Hemolytic Anemia (AIHA) that results from a clonal B-cell proliferative disorder of the bone marrow. Symptoms related to anemia such as fatigue, dyspnea, and weakness can substantially impair quality of life. In addition, patients often have symptoms such as acrocyanosis and may be at an increased risk for thromboembolic events.
CAD is rare, serious disease with chronic morbidities with many patients experiencing flares of hemolysis and anemia that can be severe, and can require RBC transfusion and be associated with significant impairment in quality of life.

Current Treatment Options	Treatment of CAD often consists of supportive therapies, including avoidance of cold, blood transfusions, and warming of blood during transfusions.
Other therapies used off-label include rituximab (off-label) as monotherapy or in combination with cytotoxic agents; however, these may not be effective or durable and can be associated with significant toxicities.
Corticosteroids generally have no role in the treatment of CAD.

L49: ## 4.1.1. Product Information
L50: Sutimlimab-jome (proprietary name, ENJAYMO) is a humanized immunoglobulin G subclass 4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody that inhibits the classical complement pathway (CP) and specifically binds to complement protein component 1 s subcomponent (C1s), a serine protease which cleaves C4.
L51: ## 4.1.2. Therapeutic Context
L52: Cold Agglutinin Disease is a type of autoimmune hemolytic anemia caused by IgM induced complement activation. CAD is diagnosed by the presence of IgM antibodies that agglutinate red blood cells at temperatures below normal body temperature, chronic hemolytic anemia and its resultant symptoms. Chronic hemolysis is due the binding of the cold agglutinin IgM antibodies to the I antigen, a glycoprotein membrane antigen universally present on RBCs. Binding of IgM antibodies to the I antigen results in the recruitment of activated complement fragments on the RBC, which leads to uptake and lysis of the red blood cell by the reticuloendothelial system with intravascular hemolysis and anemia. The anemia can result in symptoms such as fatigue dizziness, dyspnea and acrocyanosis. Chest pain and Raynaud syndromes are also reported patients are predisposed to thrombotic events. LDH typically increases because of the hemolysis. As complement coated RBCs are usually cleared through extravascular hemolysis and phagocytosis by liver macrophages, elevated total bilirubin is also typical with the disease.
L53: CAD is a rare disease that mostly affects middle-aged and elderly patients with median age of onset of 67 years. There is a female predominance, although this may be due to the longer lifespan than males. Patient with CAD can display varying levels of chronic hemolysis with

L59: There are currently no FDA-approved therapies for the treatment of CAD. Treatments are considered if the patient develops symptomatic anemia or disabling cold-induced circulatory symptoms. Supportive nonpharmacological therapies include keeping warm avoidance of cold infusions and blood transfusions and folic and erythropoietin may be used as supportive therapies. Corticosteroids are often not effective in CAD. Patients may be treated with rituximab monotherapy (off-label) or in combination with cytotoxic agents, however these therapies may not always be effective or durable and can be associated with significant toxicities.

L69: ## Mechanistic Support from Study BIVV009-01 Parts C and E
L70: This section clarifies the original clinical review for this application, i.e., that evidence of mechanistic support (confirmatory evidence) was provided in the original Application submission from study BIVV009-01 Part C and E. BIVV009-01 Parts A-C and E, was a first-in-human, phase I study composed of a single ascending dose (SAD) part in healthy volunteers, multiple ascending dose (MAD) part in healthy volunteers, and multiple dose part in patients with complement mediated disorders (CMDs). Part C consisted of patient with CMDs including patients with CAD, bullous pemphigoid (BP), warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (WAIHA) and antibody-mediated reaction (AMR) after renal transplant. All patients received a single IV test dose of sutimlimab at 10mg/kg IV followed by 4 weekly doses of 60mg/kg. In Part E, 4 patients with CAD who responded to sutimlimab in Part C restarted sutimlimab at fixed doses of 5.5 g for every 2 weeks. Doses were later increased and based on weight categories (6.5 g for patients &lt;75 kg, 7.5 g for patients ≥75 kg) under a protocol amendment.
L71: In total, 6 patients with CAD and a hemoglobin &lt;11g/dL were enrolled and completed Part C. The median baseline hemoglobin was 7.7g/dL (range: 6.8-8.3g/dL) and all patients were transfusion dependent. Following completion of a sutimlimab washout, the hemoglobin values declined to pretreatment levels in 5 patients with a median hemoglobin level of 7.3g/dL (range 7.1-8.8g/dL). L502: ## 1. Executive Summary
L503: ## 1.1. Product Introduction
L504: Sutimlimab-jome (proprietary name, ENJAYMO), a new molecular entity (NME), is a humanized immunoglobulin G subclass 4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody that inhibits the classical complement pathway (CP) and specifically binds to complement protein component 1, s subcomponent (C1s), a serine protease which cleaves C4. The classical CP is activated in cold agglutinin disease (CAD) and leads to chronic hemolysis.
L505: The Applicant proposes the following indication: the treatment of hemolysis in adult patients with CAD.
L506: The proposed dosing regimen is 6.5g [REDACTED](b) (4) or 7.5g (patients \ge 75\text{kg}) depending on the patient's body weight at baseline, which is to be administered by intravenous (IV) infusion over 1-2 hours once per week for the first two doses followed by every other week dosing.
L507: ## 1.2. Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
L508: The Applicant has demonstrated substantial evidence of effectiveness. This conclusion is based upon the results of a global, multi-center, single-arm, open-label trial (BIVV009-03 Part A; CARDINAL study) in 24 adult patients with symptomatic CAD who had at least one transfusion in the previous 6 months and a hemoglobin (Hgb) level \le 10\text{g/dL}.
L509: The primary endpoint was the percent of patients who met criteria of the responder definition. Patients met the responder definition if they demonstrated an increase from baseline in Hgb level \ge 2\text{ g/dL} or normalization of Hgb level \ge 12\text{ g/dL} at the treatment assessment time point (mean value from weeks 23, 25, and 26) and did not receive a blood transfusion from week 5 through week 26, in addition to not receiving additional treatment for CAD beyond what was permitted in the protocol. The proportion of patients who met the primary composite endpoint was 54.2% (95% CI: 32.8%, 74.4%). CAD is a rare and serious life-long disease with significant morbidity for which there is no available therapy and has an unmet medical need.
L510: Thus, the multicenter trial results indicate that in patients with CAD, the use of sutimlimab-jome confers substantial benefit in terms of transfusion independence and improvement in hemoglobin with a manageable toxicity profile. The benefits of sutimlimab-jome outweigh its risk. Approval on the basis of single study is entirely appropriate in a rare disease with unmet medical need the benefits which include transfusion independence with statistically significant results and concordance of secondary endpoints.

L513: ## 1.3. **1.3 Benefit-Risk Assessment**
L514: ## Benefit-Risk Integrated Assessment
L515: Sutimlimab is a first-in-class, humanized IgG4 monoclonal antibody that targets the classical complement pathway (CP) by inhibition of CP-specific serine protease, complement component 1 (C1), s subcomponent (C1s).
L516: Cold Agglutinin Disease (CAD) is a rare type of autoimmune hemolytic anemia (AIHA). CAD is characterized by a low-grade clonal B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder and chronic hemolytic anemia. Cold agglutinins activate the classical CP leading to deposition of C3b on red blood cells which are phagocytosed in the liver resulting in extravascular hemolysis, the predominate mechanism of hemolysis. While hemolysis is chronic, disease severity can fluctuate throughout a patient's lifetime. CAD is a serious disease and patients may have increased mortality. Anemia can be mild to severe, it has been estimated that approximately half of patients require transfusions. Symptoms of anemia can greatly affect quality of life. Most patients have cold induced symptoms ranging from acrocyanosis to debilitating Raynaud's phenomenon. In addition, patients with CAD may be at increased risk for thrombosis. There are no approved therapies for CAD. Patients often require supportive therapies such as transfusions and avoidance of cold temperatures. Immunosuppressants that target the B cell clone such as rituximab as monotherapy or in combination with other cytotoxic agents are standard therapies for symptomatic patients. Limitations of these therapies may include; not all patients respond to treatment, response may not be durable, there is a slow onset to response, and depending on the regimen, significant toxicities may occur.
L517: The determination of efficacy is based primarily upon the results from a single study (BIVV009-03 Part A, CARDINAL study). The CARDINAL study was a single-arm, open-label trial that included 24 adult patients with primary CAD who had at least one blood transfusion in the previous 6 months, Hgb \le10g/dL and were symptomatic. The primary composite endpoint was the proportion of patients that met the responder definition. A patient was considered a responder if he or she did not receive a blood transfusion from Week 5 through Week 26 and did not receive treatment for CAD beyond what was permitted per protocol. Additionally, the patient's hemoglobin (Hgb) level must have met either of the following criteria; Hgb level \ge12 g/dL or Hgb level increased by \ge2 g/dL from baseline at the treatment assessment endpoint. In total, 13 out of the 24 (54.2%, 95% CI: 32.8, 74.4) patients in the full analysis set met the composite primary endpoint criteria and the lower bound of the response rate was &gt;30%. As for each component of the composite endpoint; 15 (62.5%) patients met the Hgb endpoint, 17 (70.8%) patients were transfusion free from week 5 to 26, and 22 (91.7%) patients did not receive protocol prohibited CAD medications. To further support efficacy, markers of hemolysis including lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and bilirubin were measured. Among 14 patients with baseline and follow-up bilirubin values, the mean was 55 \mumol/L (2.7-fold ULN) at baseline and 15 \mumol/L (0.8-fold ULN) at the treatment assessment timepoint. Among 17 patients with baseline and follow-up LDH values, the mean LDH was 424 U/L (1.7-fold ULN) at baseline and 301 U/L (1.2-
L518: fold ULN) at the treatment assessment timepoint. The decrease in mean bilirubin and LDH values compared to baseline are further evidence of decreased hemolysis.
L519: The determination of substantial evidence of efficacy was based on the clinically meaningful and statistically persuasive results of the proportion of patients that achieved the responder definition in a patient population with a serious, life-long disease, with significant disease burden, unmet medical need and no approved therapies.
L520: The primary safety database consisted of 34 patients (30 patients with CAD, 3 with cold agglutinin syndrome (CAS) and 1 CAD/warm autoimmune hemolytic anemia (WAIHA)). The 24 patients with CAD from the pivotal study (BIVV009-03 Part A), were included in the primary safety population. An additional 96 normal healthy volunteers (NHVs) and 32 patients with other complement-mediated disorders (CMDs) were included to further support the safety database. In general, the safety profile for sutimlimab was manageable in a population compromised of older patients with multiple comorbidities and receiving concomitant medications. No patient discontinued therapy due to an adverse reaction (AR). Common ARs (&gt;10%) reported in the pivotal study included; respiratory tract infections, viral infections, bleeding, hematoma, diarrhea, dyspepsia, cough, arthralgia, arthritis, and peripheral edema. In total, 6 out of 24 (25%) patients experienced a serious adverse reaction. The most common type of serious adverse reaction were infections in 3 out of 24 (12.5%) of patients. Six out of 24 (25%) patients had dose interruptions, with infection and infusion reactions as the most common ARs leading to dose interruption. Infusion-related reactions occurred in 2 out of 34 (5.9%) patients, no infusion-related reactions led to drug discontinuation.
L521: Sutimlimab may increase the risk of serious infections, including encapsulated organisms. L534: ## 2. Therapeutic Context
L535: ## 2.1. Analysis of Condition
L536: Autoimmune hemolytic anemias (AIHAs) is a group of disorders characterized by autoantibodies targeting red blood cell surface antigens resulting in hemolysis and anemia[1]. AIHA is further subdivided into cold or warm mediated disease based on the optimum temperature used to detect autoantibodies and CAD accounts for roughly 15-25% of AIHAs[2]. CAD is a primary chronic clonal B-cell lymphoproliferative disorder of the bone marrow that is characterized by the presence of cold induced clinical symptoms and AIHA [3]. It should be distinguished from secondary CAS, as CAS occurs in the presence of overt malignancies (lymphoid malignancies), autoimmune disease, and infections[2]. CAD is rare, in a Norwegian study it was estimated the prevalence was 16.2 cases per million[4]. It mostly affects middle aged and elderly patients (median age of onset is 67 years old), rare cases have been reported in patients as young as 30 years old[3]. There is a female predominance, although this may also be secondary to women living longer than men[3].
L537: CAD is caused by cold agglutinins (CA) which are autoantibodies (mostly of immunoglobulin M class) that binds predominantly to the I carbohydrate antigen on the surface of erythrocytes resulting in agglutination maximally at a low temperatures (optimally at 3-4°C)[1, 2]. The complex of antigen bound to CA on the erythrocyte cell surface triggers activation of the classical complement pathway by interaction with the C1 complex ultimately leading to C3b-coated red blood cells [2]. Hemolysis occurs predominantly through extravascular destruction in the liver of C3b coated red blood cells, although C5-mediated intravascular hemolysis can occur[2].
L538: Diagnostic criteria for CAD includes; chronic hemolysis, positive polyspecific DAT, monospecific DAT strongly positive for C3d, CA titer \ge 64 at 4°C and absence of an underlying clinical disease such as overt malignancy[2]. Hemolysis is typically assessed by markers such as lactate dehydrogenase, bilirubin, and haptoglobin[3]. In addition, a work up should include a bone marrow biopsy which typically shows a lymphoid infiltration consisting of nodular B-cell aggregates (without characteristics of lymphoplasmacytic lymphoma), consistent with lymphoproliferative disorder[3].
L539: The severity of CAD is mostly dependent on the thermal amplitude of cold agglutinins and the intensity of symptoms can fluctuate throughout a patient's lifetime[5]. There are conflicting reports regarding the risk of increased mortality, although it is likely there is at least slight increase in mortality for patients with CAD[6]. Anemia is more often mild to moderate, but it can be severe. Hemolysis tends to exacerbate in the setting of infections, cold temperatures, surgery, and trauma[7]. Patients often have symptoms related to anemia such as chronic
L540: fatigue, dyspnea, and weakness which can be associated with substantial impairment of quality of life[5]. The majority of patients need transfusions at some point in their disease[5]. The percentage of patients that are transfusion dependent is difficult to quantify, larger studies have estimated 40-50% of CAD patients are transfusion dependent[3]. In a retrospective longitudinal study in the United States, it was estimated that transfusions were given to at least 65% of patients [5]. Alloimmunization and iron overload may occur in individuals who require frequent blood transfusions[8]. Most patients have symptoms related to cold-induced circulatory symptoms, such as acrocyanosis to Raynaud's phenomena[2]. Jaundice and hemoglobinuria may also occur secondary to hemolysis. Patients are likely at increased risk for venous and arterial thrombosis, particularly if they have severe anemia[3].
L541: ## 2.2. Analysis of Current Treatment Options
L542: There are currently no FDA approved therapies for the treatment of CAD. Treatments are considered if the patient develops symptomatic anemia or disabling cold-induced circulatory symptoms. It has been estimated at 70-80% of patients have received therapy for CAD and many patients require trials of 1 or more therapies [3]. Supportive nonpharmacologic therapies include keeping warm, avoidance of cold infusions, and blood transfusions[2]. During a blood transfusion the extremity should be kept warm, and an in-line blood warming tubing is recommended, failure to adhere to these precautions may result in significant morbidity and could be life threatening[9]. Folic acid and erythropoietin may be used as supportive therapies.
L543: Corticosteroids, azathioprine, and cyclophosphamide are not effective in CAD [7]. Splenectomy is not effective, which is expected as extravascular hemolysis is mostly localized to the liver.
L544: Most successful treatments have been with targeting the pathogenic B-cell clones[3]. Rituximab monotherapy has been used with success as a single-agent and is often considered for first-line therapy. In prospective uncontrolled trials, rituximab has had an overall response rate of approximately 50%, with a median Hgb increase of 4g/dL[7]. Although, response time was often slow (median time to response was 1.5 months) and not durable (median duration approximately one year)[7]. Rituximab has also been studied in combination with bendamustine and can be considered as first-line therapy in fit patients or second-line therapy[7]. In a non-randomized study, 71% of the 45 patients had a response and only 10% of patients relapsed after 32 months. The median hemoglobin response in complete responders was 4.4g/dL, and 3.9g/dL in partial responders. Still significant toxicities were noted, 33% of patients experienced grade 3-4 neutropenia and 11% had infections[10].
L545: As for second-line therapies, rituximab has also been combined with fludarabine. In a prospective uncontrolled trial, a response rate of 76% was observed in 29 patients. Median increase in Hgb was 3.1g/dL and median response of duration was 66 months. Increased toxicities were observed, 41% of patient experienced grade 3-4 hematologic toxicities and 59%
L546: experienced grade 1-3 infection[11]. Bortezomib has been studied as well as monotherapy, with an overall response rate of 32% out of 19 patients[12]. While some case reports have reported benefit with eculizumab (C5 monoclonal antibody), a prospective study that was not randomized demonstrated a reduction in transfusions and intravascular hemolysis in 7 out of 13 patients, but an increase in hemoglobin level of only 0.9g/dL [13].

L616: ## Blood Transfusion (CARDINAL)
L617: The purpose of this study was to provide pivotal efficacy and safety data for the evaluation of sutimlimab for patients with primary CAD and a recent blood transfusion.
L618: Study initiation: March 5th, 2018
Study completion: July 11th, 2019 (Part A)
L619: ## Primary Objective
L620: - • To determine whether sutimlimab administration resulted in a \ge 2 g/dL increase in hemoglobin levels or increased hemoglobin to \ge 12 g/dL and obviated the need for blood transfusion during treatment in patients with CAD who had a recent blood transfusion.
L621: ## Secondary Efficacy Objectives:
L622: - • To assess the effect of sutimlimab on clinical events and laboratory parameters related to hemolysis and anemia in patient with CAD
- • To assess the effect of sutimlimab on quality of life (QOL) in patients with CAD
L623: ## Exploratory objectives:
L624: - • To assess the effect of sutimlimab on specific complications of CAD including; acrocyanosis, Raynaud's syndrome, hemoglobinuria, and thromboembolism
- • To evaluate the effect of sutimlimab on certain disease-related biomarkers in patients with CAD
- • To evaluate the pharmacokinetics of sutimlimab
L625: ## **Trial Design**
L626: Study BIVV009-03 (CARDINAL study) is an open-label, single-arm, 2-part study designed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of sutimlimab in patients with CAD and a recent history of blood transfusion in Part A. Part B is ongoing and will evaluate the long-term safety, tolerability and durability of response of sutimlimab in patients with CAD.
L627: In the study, which includes part A only, 24 patients with a diagnosis of CAD who had at least one blood transfusion in the previous 6 months prior to enrollment were enrolled. Patients were administered sutimlimab IV of either 6.5g (if &lt;75 kg) or 7.5g (if \ge 75kg) on days 0, 7, and biweekly thereafter, to day 175 (week 25). Patients who missed a dose (&gt;17 days) received an additional loading dose. End of treatment was defined as day 182 (week 26).

L629: The dose regimen used in this study was based on the population PK model from studies BIVV009-01 (Part A,B&amp;C) and BIVV009-02, in order to maintain complement pathway inhibition at the minimum concentration.
L630: Patients received blood transfusions as needed based on predefined criteria (Table 3).
L631: ## **Table 3. Transfusion Criteria**
L632: A patient was to receive a transfusion during Part A or Part B if his or her hemoglobin level met either of the following criteria:
L633: - • The hemoglobin level was &lt;9 g/dL, and the patient was symptomatic.
- • The hemoglobin level was &lt;7 g/dL, and the patient was asymptomatic.
L634: Source: Table 1 of Applicant's CSR
L635: Beyond the permitted concomitant medications, study drug, and transfusions, patients were not allowed to receive other therapies for the treatment of CAD while on study. Prohibited therapies included rituximab monotherapy or rituximab combination therapies (bendamustine, fludarabine, ibrutinib or cytotoxic agents). Erythropoietin and or steroids (equivalent to \le 10 mg/day of prednisone) were acceptable as long as patients had been on a stable dose for the previous 3 months.
L636: The study included patients \ge 18 years of age with a confirmed diagnosis of CAD based on the following criteria; chronic hemolysis, polyspecific direct antiglobulin test (DAT) positive, monospecific DAT strongly positive for C3d, cold agglutinin titer \ge 64 at 4^{\circ}\text{C}, and IgG DAT \le 1+. In addition, patients must have had at least one blood transfusion in the preceding 6 months and hemoglobin \le 10 g/dL. Lastly, patients were symptomatic defined as the presence of 1 or more signs or symptoms within 3 months of screening; symptomatic anemia, acrocyanosis, Raynaud's syndrome, hemoglobinuria, disabling circulatory symptoms, and major adverse vascular events. Patients had a bone marrow biopsy 6 months prior to screening and were included if there was no overt evidence of lymphoproliferative disease or other hematological malignancy. Patients were excluded if they had cold agglutinin syndrome secondary to infection, rheumatologic disease or active hematologic malignancy.
L637: The following immunizations were required within 5 years prior to enrollment; Neisseria meningitidis (including serogroup B meningococcus where available), Hemophilus influenzae, and Streptococcus pneumoniae.
L638: A total of 22 sites screened at least one patient and 16 sites enrolled at least one patient from 8 countries (Australia, Germany, France, Italy, Japan, Norway, United Kingdom and the United States).

L646: ## **Reviewer comment:**
L647: *A control group was not included in the study. This was determined to be acceptable prior to the BLA submission given the stringent composite efficacy endpoints and lack of available effective therapies for a patient population that is symptomatic and requiring blood transfusions. The CAD patient population while mostly enrolled in Europe, has similar characteristics to the US population. The inclusion criteria clearly defined a symptomatic patient population with CAD and in need of therapy for hemolytic anemia. Diagnostic criteria for CAD used in the study is consistent with what is widely accepted as diagnostic for CAD in the community.*
L648: ## **Study Endpoints**
L649: ## **Primary Endpoint**
L650: The primary efficacy endpoint was responder rate, which was a composite endpoint. A patient was considered a responder if he or she did not receive a blood transfusion from Week 5 through Week 26 (end of treatment) and did not receive treatment for CAD beyond what was permitted per protocol. Additionally, the patient's Hgb level must have met either of the following criteria:
L651: - • Hgb level \ge12 g/dL at the treatment assessment endpoint (defined as the mean value from Weeks 23, 25, and 26) or
- • Hgb level increased by \ge2 g/dL from baseline (defined as the last hemoglobin value
L652: before administration of the first dose of study drug) at the treatment assessment endpoint.
L653: ## Secondary Endpoints
L654: - • Mean change from baseline in bilirubin (excluding patients with Gilbert's syndrome) at the treatment assessment endpoint (defined as the mean value of Weeks 23, 25, and 26)
- • Mean change from baseline in QOL, as assessed by the change in FACIT-F scale scores at the treatment assessment endpoint
- • Mean change from baseline in lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) at the treatment assessment endpoint
- • Number of transfusions and number of units after the first 5 weeks of study drug administration
- • Mean change from baseline in hemoglobin at the treatment assessment endpoint
L655: ## Exploratory Endpoints
L656: - • Time to first transfusion after the first 5 weeks
- • Multiple endpoints considering QOL measures
- • Incidence of solicited symptomatic anemia at EOT (end of treatment)
- • Proportion of patients with hemoglobin level &gt;12 g/dL at EOT
- • Median time to normalization and proportion of patient with normalization of bilirubin, LDH, and haptoglobin
- • Incidence of hemolytic breakthrough
L657: *Clinical reviewer comment: The reviewer agrees that the primary composite endpoint of responder rate represents clinical benefit. All three components (hemoglobin increase, transfusion independence, and not receiving other therapies for CAD) are equally important to assessing the response to sutimlimab. Inclusion of primary and secondary endpoints in the USPI was based on robustness of results and on the number of evaluable patients. Interpretation of PROs is limited given the open-label design and lack of a comparator arm, and therefore was not included in the USPI.*
L658: ## **Statistical Analysis Plan**
L659: ## Sample Size and Power Considerations
L660: It was planned that approximately 20 patients with CAD who had a recent history of transfusion would be enrolled (24 were actually enrolled). If the true responder rate was estimated to be 66% and a minimum of 30% was required for success, then with 20 patients there was 90% probability that the lower limit of the 95% CI was at least 30%.
L661: ## Analysis Populations
L662: - - Full analysis set (FAS) included all patients who received at least 1 dose (including partial dose) of study drug. Efficacy analysis was performed on the FAS.
- - Per-protocol (PP) set included a subset of FAS patients who did not have any important protocol deviations impacting efficacy assessments.
- - Safety analysis set included patients who received at least 1 dose (including partial dose) of study drug.
- - PK analysis set included all patients who received at least one dose of study drug and had at least 1 evaluable sample for baseline and PK concentrations.
- - PD (pharmacodynamic) analysis set included all patients who received at least one dose of study drug and had at least 1 evaluable sample for baseline and PD concentrations.
L663: ## Analyses of the Primary Efficacy Endpoint
L664: Efficacy analyses were performed on the FAS. Each patient in the FAS population was classified as meeting the criteria of the primary endpoint (responder) or not meeting the criteria of the primary endpoint (non-responder). The 95% CI for the proportion of responders was calculated using Clopper-Pearson exact method. Any patient withdrawing from the study prior to the Week 23 visit was considered a non-responder.
L665: The primary analysis of the primary endpoint was based on the Composite Estimand, for which any missing response was considered a non-responder. Sensitivity analyses were carried out based on the Completer Estimand and the Per-protocol Estimand, respectively. Subgroup analyses for the primary endpoint were performed by age (&lt;65 and \ge65 years), gender, baseline weight (&lt;75 and \ge75 kg), number of transfusions within 12 months prior to study entry (\le2, 3-4, &gt;4), baseline hemoglobin level (&lt;8.5 and \ge8.5 g/dL), and previous rituximab therapy and/or cytotoxic therapy (yes/no). For these analyses, if there were &lt;5 patients in a category, the cutoff may have been adjusted.
L666: ## Analyses of Secondary Efficacy Endpoints
L667: All secondary efficacy endpoints (change from baseline in hemoglobin, bilirubin, LDH, and FACIT-Fatigue) were analyzed using the Mixed Model for Repeated Measures (MMRM) at the treatment assessment endpoint. Analyses were performed based on the Hypothetical Estimand and the De-facto Estimand, respectively. Additional sensitivity analyses were carried out using multiple imputations.

L696: ## Protocol Violations/Deviations
L697: Major protocol deviations occurred in 10 (41.7%) of patients. L796: An additional benefit of this drug is the relatively quick improvement of hemoglobin within the first few doses of sutimlimab. Compared to standard therapies such as rituximab, which may take several weeks to show a response.
L797: ## 7.3. Integrated Assessment of Effectiveness
L798: A single pivotal study, BIVV009-03 part A (CARDINAL study), was submitted to determine efficacy of sutimlimab for patients with CAD and a recent blood transfusion. The efficacy of sutimlimab was demonstrated by the proportion of patients that met the responder definition. Patients were classified as a responder if they met the following criteria; no blood transfusion from week 5 to 26 and no treatment for CAD beyond what was permitted per protocol; and achieved a hemoglobin level \ge 12 g/dL or an increase from baseline in hemoglobin \ge 2 g/dL at
L799: the treatment endpoint (mean value from week 23, 25, and 26). In total, 13 out of the 24 (54.2%) patients in the full analysis set met the composite primary endpoint criteria and the lower bound of the response rate was &gt;30%. It was pre-determined (and agreed upon by the Agency) to be successful the lower bound of the 95% CI had to exceed 30%. As for each component of the composite endpoint; 62.5% of patients met the hemoglobin endpoint, 70.8% were transfusion free from week 5 to 26, and 91.7% of patients did not receive protocol prohibited CAD medications. Each component of the composite endpoint is important to defining clinical benefit and will be presented in the USPI. The proportion of patients who met the responder definition is clinically meaningful to a patient population who are symptomatic from their disease, had a recent transfusion, and there are there are no approved therapies.
L800: Patients with CAD have chronic hemolytic anemia, that can be exacerbated in the setting of febrile infections or cold temperatures. Patients are often symptomatic from anemia and may require transfusions. Symptoms related to anemia can be severe and debilitating. Furthermore, standard therapies often consist of immunosuppressive agents such as rituximab alone or in combination with other agents. These therapies may be ineffective, not durable, or associated with significant toxicities. Therefore, therapy that increases hemoglobin and decreases transfusion burden, while not receiving immunosuppressant therapies is considered direct clinical benefit.
L801: Efficacy of sutimlimab is further supported by secondary endpoints of the study, such as decreased bilirubin and LDH. Among 14 patients with baseline and follow-up bilirubin values, the mean was 55 \mumol/L (2.7-fold ULN) at baseline and 15 \mumol/L (0.8-fold ULN) at the follow-up time point. Among 17 patients with baseline and follow-up LDH values, the mean LDH was 424 U/L (1.7-fold ULN) at baseline and 301 U/L (1.2-fold ULN) at the follow-up time point. This data demonstrates that sutimlimab decreases hemolysis. It was expected that the decrease in bilirubin would be more dramatic than the decrease in LDH, as the mechanism of hemolysis in CAD is predominately extravascular and not all LDH is RBC derived.

L852: ## 8.3.2. Categorization of Adverse Events
L853: Adverse events were classified using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) system organ classes (SOC) and preferred terms (PT). MedDRA version 21.0 was used for the integrated analysis. An AE was determined to be TEAE if it had an onset date after receiving the first administration of study drug. TEAEs were collected from the first study drug administration to discontinuation or the safety follow up. Adverse events were classified using Common Toxicity Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE) version 4.03 in all studies except the healthy volunteer study for BIVV009-05, in which severity was classified as mild, moderate, and severe. Events were classified as serious adverse events if the AE resulted in death, life-threatening,
L854: inpatient hospitalization, prolongation of existing hospitalization, persistent or significant disability, or birth defect. An event could also be deemed a SAE if the event jeopardized the subject or required intervention to prevent the above listed outcomes. Adverse events of special interest included; thromboembolic events, infections, development of SLE and hypersensitivity reactions.

L1034: *It is possible that sutimlimab contributes to the increase risk of infections given the mechanism of action of inhibiting the complement pathway which is part of the innate immune system. Direct killing of gram negative bacteria is done thorough the membrane attack complex (C5b-9). Gram positive bacteria and fungi tend to be resistant to the membrane attack complex. Sutimlimab does not inhibit the alternative complement pathway, therefore this function may remain intact. It has been estimated that approximately 65% of complement deficient patients experience severe infections mostly caused by encapsulated bacteria, including Neisseria meningitidis and Streptococcus pneumoniae[15]. Even more relevant to sutimlimab, patients with inherited complement deficiencies in the classical complement pathway (including C1s) are at increased risk for encapsulated bacteria such as Neisseria meningitidis, Streptococcus pneumoniae, and Haemophilus influenzae[16]. While there are important differences between inherited complement deficiency of C1s and acquired by pharmacologic suppression, this is a relevant comparison we have to assess this risk. Serious infections including increased susceptibility to encapsulated bacteria will be included in the Warnings and Precautions of the USPI. Patient should be closely monitored for infection and if suspected warrant early and immediate intervention. L1053: *Clinical reviewer comment: The risks of autoimmunity associated with inhibition of the proximal component of the classical CP are not known, but theoretically inhibition of the classical CP could increase the risk for development or worsening of autoimmune diseases such as SLE or immune complex diseases. This risk is based on observations that patients with certain types of autoimmune diseases, in particular SLE have low individual CP components. In addition, patients with hereditary deficiency of complement components of the early phase of the classical CP such as C1q, C1r, C1s, C4 and C2 are associated with increased susceptibility to SLE and SLE-related diseases[17]. The increased risk of SLE is likely most strongly associated with C1q deficiency. Deficiencies in C1r and/or C1s have been linked to glomerulonephritis, SLE, SLE-like disease and other autoimmune diseases[18]. The components of the classical complement pathway may be protective against SLE due to its roles in clearance of immune complexes and apoptotic cell debris, in addition to the role of complement-dependent B cell tolerance [15, 19]. For these reasons the Applicant monitored autoantibody panels and the development or worsening of autoimmune disease in patients exposed to sutimlimab, an inhibitor of the classical complement pathway.*

L1056: *populations, there was no clear trend of autoantibody panels turning positive and overt symptoms of autoimmune disease, although the median time a patient was exposed to sutimlimab was relatively short, and therefore long-term risk has not been assessed. As there remains a theoretical risk of autoimmunity, and development or worsening of autoimmune disease can be serious and is associated with significant morbidity, autoimmune disease will be listed in the Warnings and Precautions in the USPI. L1093: ## 8.10. **Integrated Assessment of Safety**
L1094: The safety analysis for sutimlimab-jome was focused on the primary safety population, which consisted of 34 patients with CAD from the following studies; BIVV009-01 Part C and Part E (N=10), and BIVV009-03 Part A and B (N=24). Studies BIVV009-01 Part E and BIVV009-03 Part B were ongoing at the time of this report. The primary safety population consisted mostly of patients with CAD (88%), a few patients with CAS (8.8%), and one patient with mixed CAD/WAIHA (2.9%). Given the sample size of the primary safety population was small, supportive safety populations were also included. This included 32 patients with other CMDs (AMR, WAIHA, BP, ITP) and 96 NHVs.
L1095: In the primary safety population, the median duration of dosing was 38.4 weeks, with a range of 3.1-90.3. In study BIVV009-01 Part C, patients were administered a single IV test dose of 10mg/kg dose of sutimlimab followed by four weekly doses of 60mg/kg. In study BIVV009-01 Part E, patients were administered a single test dose of 5.5g at week 0, 1 and then every two weeks. Dosing was modified to patients &lt;75kg received fixed doses of 6.5g, and patients \ge 75g received fixed doses of 7.5g. In study BIVV009-03 Part A and B, patients &lt;75kg received fixed doses of 6.5g, and patients \ge 75g received fixed doses of 7.5g, on day 0, 7 and 14, followed by every two weeks.
L1096: The most common TEAEs (closely related terms were grouped or FMQs) reported in &gt;20% the primary safety population included respiratory tract infections, UTI, and fatigue. In the pivotal study BIVV009-03 Part A, the most common TEAE by FMQ reported in &gt;10% of the population were respiratory tract infections, viral infections, bleeding, hematoma, diarrhea, dyspepsia, cough, arthralgia, arthritis, and peripheral edema.
L1097: Serious TEAEs occurred in 35% of the 34 patients of the primary safety population and 25% of 24 patients within the pivotal study. The most frequent SAEs in the primary safety population included sepsis (8.8%), bacterial infection (5.9%), bleeding (5.9%), cholecystitis or cholelithiasis (5.9%), malignancy (5.9%) and anemia (5.9%). The events of malignancy and anemia were related to underlying pathologies and are likely not adverse reactions.
L1098: Infections are of particular concern for patients with CAD as it may exacerbate hemolysis. As the classical complement pathway is part of the innate immune system, it is possible that sutimlimab may increase the risk of non-serious and serious infections. Infections occurred in 58.8% of the 34 patients in the primary safety population and 58.3% of the 24 patients in the pivotal study. Infections with encapsulated organisms occurred in 8.8% of the 34 patients in the primary safety population. Following the 90-day safety update, one patient who received
L1099: required vaccinations developed pneumococcal sepsis. This was the only case of a patient developing an infection of a vaccinated encapsulated bacteria. Overall, bacterial and viral infections occurred at similar frequency, fungal infections were less common. No patient died or discontinued therapy due to infections.
L1100: Infusions reactions occurred in 2 out of 34 (5.9%) patients in the primary safety population and in one out of the 32 (3.1%) patients with CMDs. There were no events of anaphylaxis. Sutimlimab dosing was interrupted but not discontinued.
L1101: There is a theoretical risk that inhibition of the classical complement pathway may increase the risk of development of autoimmune disease, in particular SLE. During the study there was no clear trend of autoantibody positivity and clinical symptoms of SLE. There was one patient with who experienced arthralgias during the study and developed a positive ANA after exposure to sutimlimab. This case is confounded by the patient's pretreatment history of polymyalgia rheumatica and arthralgias prior to exposure to sutimlimab, and ultimately the ANA reverted back to negative at a later date. The risk of autoimmune development is difficult to assess given the small patient population and lack of long-term data, therefore a PMR will be issued to continue to assess autoimmune disease development and/or worsening.
L1102: Two of the 34 (5.9%) patients in the primary safety discontinued treatment. One patient due to death from hepatocellular carcinoma that was diagnosed after only two doses of sutimlimab, and one patient discontinued due to polymyalgia rheumatica, which was diagnosed prior to exposure to sutimlimab. Both patients' discontinuations were likely unrelated to sutimlimab.
L1103: In summary, sutimlimab was well tolerated and had low rates of discontinuations and deaths, in an older population with multiple comorbidities and concomitant medications. The relatively high incidence of AEs and SAEs is therefore not unexpected. The most common SAE associated with sutimlimab were infections, which in most cases the infections were manageable with screening and aggressive supportive care. Overall the safety profile is acceptable for patients with CAD. Uncertainties remain given the small sample size, lack of a placebo arm, and limited data on long term outcomes, and it is possible that new safety signals will be revealed in ongoing studies and the postmarketing period. Therefore, a PMR was issued to complete and submit the ongoing studies and a PMR to assess the risk of autoimmune disease and serious infections including encapsulated bacteria.
