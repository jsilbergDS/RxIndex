L36: ## 1 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
L37: Ocrelizumab has been developed by Genentech for the treatment of Relapsing forms of Multiple Sclerosis (RMS) and Primary Progressive Multiple Sclerosis (PPMS). This application is seeking approval for the following indications:
L38: - Ocrelizumab is indicated for the treatment of patients with relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (b) (4)
- Ocrelizumab is indicated for the treatment of patients with primary progressive multiple sclerosis (PPMS) (b) (4)
L39: Two pivotal trials, WA21092 and WA21093, were conducted to support the claims for treatment of RMS and one pivotal trial, WA25046, was conducted to support the claims for treatment of PPMS.
L40: Trials WA21092 and WA21093 had an identical design: both were randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, parallel-group, active controlled Phase III studies with treatment duration of 96 weeks. The two trials were designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ocrelizumab 600 mg every 24 weeks in comparison to interferon beta-1a 44 µg SC in patients with RMS. The primary endpoint was the protocol-defined annualized relapse rate evaluated at 96 weeks. A key secondary endpoint was time to onset of disability progression confirmed at 12 weeks.
L41: Statistically significant treatment difference was shown in annualized relapse rate in both pivotal trials WA21092 and WA21093. Both trials also showed significant treatment difference in 12-week confirmed disability progression.
L42: Trial WA25046 was a randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, multicenter study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of 600 mg ocrelizumab, administered as two 300 mg IV infusions separated by 14 days at a scheduled interval of every 24 weeks, as compared with placebo in adults with PPMS. Patients were treated for a variable duration with the clinical cut-off date (CCOD) occurring after a minimum of 120 weeks and approximately 253 events had been accrued, resulting in at least five treatment doses per patient. The primary endpoint was the time to onset of disability progression that was confirmed at 12 weeks over the treatment period. Secondary endpoints included 24-week confirmed disability progression and change in timed 25-foot walk speed from baseline.
L43: Statistically significant treatment difference was shown in the primary endpoint of time to 12-week confirmed disability progression as well as 24-week confirmed disability progression in trial WA25046.

L62: ## 3.2.1 Evaluation of Efficacy for Studies WA21092 and WA21093
L63: ## 3.2.1.1 Study Design
L64: Studies WA21092 and WA21093 had an identical design. The primary objective of the two studies was to assess whether the efficacy of ocrelizumab 600 mg every 24 weeks was superior to interferon beta-1a 44 µg SC as measured by the protocol-defined annualized relapse rate by 96 weeks in patients with RMS.
L65: The two studies WA21092 and WA21093 were randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, parallel-group, active controlled Phase III study with treatment duration of 96 weeks.
L66: Eligible patients were randomized 1:1 to a regimen of either ocrelizumab 600 mg by IV infusion every 24 weeks (given as two infusions of 300 mg on Days 1 and 15 of the first 24-week treatment period, and as a single infusion of 600 mg subsequently) or interferon beta-1a 44 µg by SC injection 3 times per week for 96 weeks. Randomization was stratified by region (United States versus rest of the world [ROW]) and baseline EDSS (&lt;4.0 versus \ge 4.0).
L67: During the 96-week treatment patients were required to attend 10 scheduled assessment visits. Additionally, unscheduled visits were made for the assessment of potential relapses. A schematic of the study design is presented in Figure 1.

L80: ## 3.2.1.2.1 Definition of Efficacy Endpoints
L81: ## *Protocol Defined Relapse*
L82: The primary efficacy endpoint was the protocol-defined annualized relapse rate (ARR) at 96 weeks. A protocol-defined relapse (PDR) was described by all of the following criteria:
L83: - occurrence of new or worsening neurological symptoms attributable to MS
- symptoms persisting for &gt;24 hours
L84: - symptoms not attributable to confounding clinical factors (e.g., fever, infection, injury, adverse reactions to medications)
- symptoms immediately preceded by a stable or improving neurological state for at least 30 days
- symptoms accompanied by objective neurological worsening consistent with an increase of at least half a step on the EDSS scale, or 2 points on one of the appropriate FSS, or 1 point on two or more of the appropriate FSS; the change had to affect the selected FSS (i.e., pyramidal, ambulation, cerebellar, brainstem, sensory, or visual).
L85: ## Confirmed Disability Progression Definition
L86: Disability progression was defined as an increase from the baseline in the EDSS score of \ge 1.0 point if the baseline score was \le 5.5 or at least 0.5 point if the baseline score was &gt; 5.5.
L87: Disability progression was considered confirmed when the increase in the EDSS was confirmed at a regularly scheduled visit at least 12 weeks (for 12-week CDP) or 24 weeks (for 24-week CDP) after the initial documentation of neurological worsening.
L88: Initial progression could occur at any visit during the 96-week treatment period. Confirmation of disability progression had to occur at a regularly scheduled visit during the treatment phase or in the SFU phase or OLE phase after study-drug discontinuation that was at least 12 weeks (or 24 weeks) after the initial progression. If a patient had a missing EDSS at the scheduled visit occurring at least 84 days after an initial progression or the scheduled visit occurred several days before the 84-day window after an initial progression (e.g., the visit window is \pm 4 days), confirmation of the disability progression had to be on the basis of the assessment at the next scheduled visit.
L89: Disability progression could not be confirmed unless the EDSS values met the minimum change required for progression. The non-confirmatory EDSS assessments (if any) between the initial disability progression and the confirmation of disability progression had to be at least as high as the minimum change required for progression.
L90: Patients who, according to the above definition, did not have onset of disability progression by the Week-96 visit, by the time of the early discontinuation of treatment, or were lost to follow up were to be censored at the date of the last EDSS assessment during the 96-week treatment period.
L91: ## Confirmed Disability Improvement Definition
L92: Disability improvement was assessed only for the subgroup of patients with a baseline EDSS score of \ge 2.0. It was defined as a reduction from baseline in EDSS score of \ge 1.0 point if the baseline score was \ge 2 and \le 5.5 and \ge 0.5 point if the baseline EDSS score was &gt; 5.5.
L93: All patients without confirmed disability improvement were counted as not improved, independent of follow-up time.
L94: ## No Evidence of Disease Activity (NEDA) Definition
L95: NEDA was defined only for patients with a baseline EDSS score \ge 2.0. Patients who completed the 96-week treatment period were considered as having evidence of disease activity if at least one PDR, a CDP event or at least one MRI scan showing MRI activity (defined as Gd-enhancing T1 lesions, or new or enlarging T2 lesions) was reported during the 96-week treatment period; otherwise the patient was considered as having NEDA. Patients who discontinued treatment early with at least one event before early discontinuation were considered as having evidence of disease activity.
L96: ## 3.2.1.3 Statistical Methodologies
L97: ## 3.2.1.3.1 General Consideration
L98: All randomized patients were to be included in the intent-to-treat (ITT) population according to their randomized treatment. All efficacy analyses were to be performed using the ITT population.
L99: The baseline EDSS was defined as the average EDSS score from the screening and baseline visit. For the analysis of mean change of EDSS, disability progression, and disability improvement, the exact value of the average EDSS score was to be used. However, for the stratification factor and for the randomization algorithm, the average was rounded up to the next EDSS score in order to assign a valid score (e.g., round 3.75 to 4.0 and round 5.25 to 5.5). If one of the EDSS scores from the screening or baseline visit was missing, the other was to be used. If EDSS score was missing at both screening and baseline visit, the patient was not to be randomized.
L100: All primary and secondary efficacy endpoints were to be analyzed, adjusted or stratified by geographical region (United States vs. ROW) and baseline EDSS (&lt; 4.0 vs. \ge 4.0).
L101: The null hypothesis to be tested was that there was no statistically significant difference between ocrelizumab group (OCR group) and the interferon beta-1a group (IFN group). All statistical hypotheses for the primary and secondary endpoints and treatment comparisons were to be tested at the 5% significance level (\alpha = 0.05) against two-sided alternatives.

L106: ## 3.2.1.3.2 Analyses of the Primary Endpoints
L107: The ARR by 96 weeks was to be analyzed using a negative binomial model, adjusting for region (United States versus ROW) and baseline EDSS (&lt; 4.0 versus \ge 4.0). In order to account for different study treatment durations among patients, the exposure of each patient was to be taken into account by including log-transformed exposure time in the model as an “offset” variable for appropriate computation of relapse rate.
L108: Sensitivity analyses of the primary endpoint included:
L109: - ARR calculated including all protocol-defined relapses occurring during the treatment period or the SFU, up to 96 weeks after randomization. This analysis used all available data and estimated the treatment effect up to 96 weeks irrespective of whether patients were on study treatment or not.
- Per-protocol population
- Safety population
- Adjustment by additional covariates (number of relapses within 2 years prior to study entry, baseline Gd lesions [presence vs. absence], prior MS treatment, age [&lt;40, \ge 40 years])
- Poisson model with the same covariates as in the negative binomial regression
- Use of two different methods for handling missing data (patients prematurely withdrawn from treatment), to explore the potential influence of informative dropouts on the results of the primary efficacy analysis (only data from the treatment period was considered):Multiple imputation: for patients that discontinued early during the double-blind, double-dummy treatment period without any PDR in the 30 days prior to discontinuation, 50% of patients were randomly assigned an event of relapse on day of discontinuation, and 50% were censored on day of discontinuation;
- With imputation: patients that discontinued early during the double-blind, double-dummy treatment period without any PDR in the 30 days prior to discontinuation, were counted as having had a relapse on day of discontinuation.

L215: ## 3.2.2 Evaluation of Efficacy for Protocol WA25046
L216: ## 3.2.2.1 Study Design
L217: The primary objective of this study was to investigate the efficacy of ocrelizumab compared with placebo in patients with PPMS. The primary endpoint was the time to onset of CDP over the treatment period, defined as an increase in the EDSS score that was sustained for at least 12 weeks.
L218: Study WA25046 was a Phase III, randomized, double-blind, parallel-group, multicenter study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of 600 mg ocrelizumab, administered as two 300 mg IV infusions separated by 14 days (subsequently referred to as one treatment dose) at a scheduled interval of every 24 weeks, as compared with placebo in adults with PPMS.
L219: A schematic of the study design is presented in Figure 5.
L220: **Figure 5 Overview of the Study Design**
L221: Source: CSR
L222: The study consisted of a screening period, a double-blind treatment period, and a safety follow-up (SFU) followed by B-cell monitoring, during which patients did not receive any study treatment.
L223: Eligible patients were randomized 2:1 to a regimen of either 600 mg ocrelizumab or placebo, respectively. Patients were treated for a variable duration with the clinical cut-off date (CCOD) occurring after a minimum of 120 weeks and approximately 253 events had been accrued, resulting in at least five treatment doses per patient. Until that time, patients were to continue with blinded dosing every 24 weeks.
L224: Patients who prematurely discontinued study medication during the double-blind treatment period attended a withdrawal visit for the purpose of collecting end-of-treatment assessments.
L225: The study enrolled PPMS patients aged 18 to 55 years with an EDSS score of 3.0-6.5 and duration of disease less than 10 years if EDSS \le 5 or less than 15 years if EDSS &gt; 5 at screening.
L226: ## 3.2.2.2 Study Endpoint
L227: The primary efficacy endpoint was the time to onset of 12-week CDP during the double-blind treatment period.
L228: Disability progression was defined as an increase in EDSS from baseline of \ge 1.0 point, if the baseline EDSS value was \le 5.5 points, or \ge 0.5 points, if the baseline EDSS was &gt; 5.5 points. Assessments within 30 days after a protocol-defined relapse were not used for confirmation of disability progression. The initial disability progression had to occur when the patient was still on treatment. Non-confirmatory EDSS assessments (if any) between the initial and confirmation of disability progression had to fulfill the requirements for progression.
L229: Secondary Endpoints were rank ordered as follows:
L230: - Time to onset of CDP for at least 24 weeks (\ge 161 days, 24-week CDP)

L231: ## 3.2.2.3 Statistical Analysis Methods
L232: ## 3.2.2.3.1 General Considerations
L233: All efficacy analyses were to be performed using the intent-to-treat (ITT) population, which was defined as all randomized patients.
L234: For the primary endpoint and secondary efficacy endpoints related to confirmed disability progression, where the initial event of worsening occurred during the double-blind treatment period, confirmation of the event could utilize EDSS scores that were collected during SFU. All other endpoints were to use only data collected from patients during the double-blind treatment period in accordance with the SAP.
L235: All primary and secondary efficacy endpoints were to be analyzed, adjusted or stratified by geographical region (United States vs. ROW) and age (\le 45 years versus &gt; 45). For CDP, if both screening EDSS and randomization EDSS scores were collected, the average of both values was to be considered as baseline EDSS score for all analyses. If one of the EDSS scores was missing, the other was to be used. For all other assessments, the baseline value was to be used as the last non-missing value on or before the date of the first infusion of study drug.
L236: The null hypothesis was that there was no difference in the underlying endpoint between the ocrelizumab and placebo groups. All statistical hypotheses for the primary and secondary endpoints and treatment comparisons were to be tested at the 5% significance level against two-sided alternatives. Following the analysis of primary endpoint, secondary endpoints were to be analyzed in a hierarchical order in the given rank order.
L237: ## 3.2.2.3.2 *Analysis of the Primary Endpoint*
L238: The time to CDP for the ocrelizumab and the placebo groups was to be compared using a two-sided log-rank test stratifying by geographic region (US versus ROW) and age (\le 45 versus &gt; 45). The proportion of patients with confirmed disability progression was to be estimated using Kaplan-Meier methodology. The overall hazard ratio was to be estimated using a stratified Cox regression model with the same stratification factors used in the log-rank test.
L239: The protocol stated that a PPMS patient who experienced initial disease progression (IDP) had an increased risk of disability progression compared to other patients without an initial event who were still ongoing in the treatment period. Patients who had an IDP and then discontinued the treatment early with no confirmatory EDSS assessments were, therefore, not censored as this would introduce substantial bias. This IDP was used as an event and these events were subsequently referred to as imputed events.
L240: Patients who had initial disability progression with no confirmatory EDSS assessment and who were on treatment at time of CCOD were censored at the date of their last EDSS assessment. Patients who did not have initial disability progression at time of CCOD, time of early discontinuation, or loss to follow up were censored at the date of their last EDSS assessment that occurred during the treatment period.
L241: The CCOD occurred when all patients had completed at least 120 weeks on study and the number of CDP events for the primary analysis was approximately 253.

L338: ## *Time to 24-week Confirmed Disability Progression*
L339: The results from analysis of 24-week CDP were almost identical to the ones from analysis of 12-week CDP with the same issues.
L340: ## *Analyses of Timed 25-Foot Walk Score*
L341: There was a substantive revision of SAP (Version E) made after the study completion and data cut-off date (July 24, 2015). The Version E of the SAP was submitted to the Division on September 4, 2015 prior to data unblinding. Because the submission came in at such a late stage, the Division did not have sufficient time to review the revised SAP and did not provide comments to the sponsor.
L342: The changes made in the Version E of the SAP involved analyses of secondary endpoints of timed 25-foot walk (T25FW) and T2 lesion volume; both were revised previously in Version D.
L343: The analysis of T25FW was changed from MMRM model on the absolute change from baseline to Week 120 to ranked ANOVA on the last observation carry forward (LOCF) in percentage change from baseline to Week 120. The treatment effect was to be derived from the MMRM analysis on the log transformed ratio of post-baseline/baseline value.
L344: The sponsor stated that the rationale for the change was that the experience of trials WA21092 and WA21093 and blinded review of data from this trial indicated that the normal assumption would be violated in the distribution of the residuals.
L345: In the study report, the sponsor reported a p-value of 0.0404 from the ranked ANOVA analysis for T25FW and the treatment difference was represented by a ratio of adjusted genomic mean of 0.896 from the MMRM analysis on the log transformed ratio of T25FW at each visit and T25FW at baseline (\log[\text{T25FW}@visit / \text{T25FW}@baseline]).

L399: ## 5 SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS
L400: ## 5.1 Statistical Issues
L401: The two pivotal studies WA21092 and WA21093 conducted in relapsing forms of MS patients produced similar and consistent efficacy results including results on the annualized relapsing rate and confirmed disability progression. No major issues were identified in the efficacy results of the two studies.
L402: In study WA25046 conducted on the PPMS patients, a possible bias in the primary analysis was identified and a sensitivity analysis failed to address the issue.
L403: The primary analysis was based on the imputed events such that 21 of the confirmed disability progression events (12 in the placebo group and 9 in the ocrelizumab group) were imputed and not actually confirmed. These 21 events were imputed as CDP due to patient withdrawal after initial disability progression. Because the placebo group had a higher rate of withdrawal, the primary analysis with imputed events might have contributed bias in favor of ocrelizumab group in determining the treatment difference and its significance. Significance of the treatment difference was lost when censoring was applied at withdrawal to these patients.
L404: It should also be noted that in the PPMS study, the power to detect treatment difference was driven by events. It was estimated that about 253 events in total were needed to detect a treatment difference. A total of 256 events, including 21 imputed events, were actually occurred. Therefore, the loss of significance in the sensitivity analysis on events without imputation could be partly due to the removing of bias and partly due to the loss in the number of events.
L405: The study also showed difference in efficacy between males and females. It appeared that there was little or no treatment benefit among female patients, which constituted half of the patient population.
L406: ## 5.2 Collective Evidence
L407: Studies WA21092 and WA21093 met the study objectives by demonstrating statistically significant treatment effects in the primary endpoint of annualized relapse rate and the key secondary endpoint of disability progression. The results appeared to be consistent under sensitivity analyses and across demographic and baseline characteristics in both studies.
L408: It was in an agreement between the sponsor and the Division that the analysis of disability progression was to be based on the pooled data of the two studies due to the difficulty to achieving the required power to detect the treatment difference in individual studies. However, the two studies were able to show a statistically significant treatment effect individually.
L409: In Study WA25046, which was conducted in PPMS patients, a statistically significant treatment difference was achieved in both 12-week confirmed disability progression (the primary endpoint) and 24-week confirmed disability progression (a secondary endpoint) in the protocol-defined primary analysis. The protocol-defined primary analysis allowed initial onset of disability progression for patients who withdrew from the study to be imputed as confirmed without actual confirmation. In both 12-week CDP and 24-week CDP, the study failed to achieve statistically significant treatment effect in a sensitivity analysis on un-imputed data, which is commonly used in the primary analysis for disability endpoint.
L410: ## 5.3 Conclusions and Recommendations
L411: Studies WA21092 and WA21093 provided consistent evidence that the treatment of ocrelizumab was effective as compared to interferon beta-1a in reducing the relapse rate and delaying the disability progression in patients with relapsing forms of MS.
L412: Study WA25046 provided data that were indicative of efficacy in the treatment of ocrelizumab in delaying the disability progression in patients with PPMS. The evidence of the effectiveness was weakened by the failure of the study to withstand an important sensitivity analysis on un-imputed data, which is commonly used as the standard primary data for disability progression endpoint.
