L71: ## 6.1 Clinical Trials Experience
L72: Because clinical trials are conducted under widely varying conditions, adverse reaction rates may not reflect the rates observed in practice.
L73: Overall, 382 adult and pediatric subjects with *Zaire ebolavirus* infection received INMABEZ in one clinical trial (the PALM trial) and as part of an expanded access program conducted in the Democratic Republic of Congo during a *Zaire ebolavirus* outbreak in 2018-2019. In the PALM trial, the safety of INMABEZ was evaluated in a multi-center, open-label, randomized controlled trial, in which 154 subjects (115 adult subjects and 39 pediatric subjects) received INMABEZ [50 mg of atolivimab, 50 mg of mafivimab, and 50 mg of odesivimab per kg (3 mL/kg)] intravenously as a single infusion and 168 subjects received an investigational control [see *Clinical Studies (14)*]. All subjects received optimized standard of care treatment. During the
L74: same outbreak, INMAZEB [50 mg of atolivimab, 50 mg of mafivimab, and 50 mg of odesivimab per kg (3 mL/kg)] was given to 228 subjects (190 adult subjects and 38 pediatric subjects) in the expanded access program.

L92: ## 6.2 Immunogenicity
L93: As with all therapeutic proteins, there is potential for immunogenicity. The detection of antibody formation is highly dependent on the sensitivity and specificity of the assay. Additionally, the observed incidence of antibody (including neutralizing antibody) positivity in an assay may be influenced by several factors including assay methodology, sample handling, timing of sample collection, concomitant medications, and underlying disease. For these reasons, comparison of the incidence of antibodies in the study described below with the incidence of antibodies in other studies or to other atolitivimab, mafitivimab, and odesivimab products may be misleading.
L94: The development of anti-atolitivimab, anti-mafitivimab, and anti-odesivimab antibodies was evaluated in 24 healthy adults in a single dose, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, dose escalation study. Immunogenic responses against atolitivimab, mafitivimab, and odesivimab were not detected at baseline or through 168 days post-dose in any subjects.
L95: ## 7 DRUG INTERACTIONS
L96: ## 7.1 Vaccine Interactions
L97: No vaccine-therapeutic interaction studies have been performed in human subjects using INMAZEB. However, because of the potential for INMAZEB to inhibit replication of a live vaccine virus indicated for prevention of *Zaire ebolavirus* infection and possibly reduce the efficacy of the vaccine, avoid the concurrent administration of a live vaccine during treatment with INMAZEB. The interval between live vaccination following initiation of INMAZEB therapy should be in accordance with current vaccination guidelines. The efficacy of INMAZEB among subjects who reported receipt of a recombinant live vaccine prior to their enrollment in the PALM clinical trial was similar to subjects who did not receive a vaccine.
L98: ## 8 USE IN SPECIFIC POPULATIONS
L99: ## 8.1 Pregnancy
L100: ## Risk Summary
L101: *Zaire ebolavirus* infection is life-threatening for both the mother and fetus and treatment should not be withheld due to pregnancy (see *Clinical Considerations*). Available data from the PALM trial and an expanded access program in which pregnant women with *Zaire ebolavirus* infection were treated with INMABEB demonstrate the high rate of maternal and fetal/neonatal morbidity consistent with published literature regarding the risk associated with underlying maternal *Zaire ebolavirus* infection. These data are insufficient to evaluate for a drug-associated risk of major birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal/fetal outcome. Animal reproduction studies with INMABEB have not been conducted. Human monoclonal antibodies, such as INMABEB, are transported across the placenta; therefore, INMABEB has the potential to be transferred from the mother to the developing fetus.
L102: ## Clinical Considerations
L103: ## *Disease-associated maternal and/or embryo/fetal risk*
L104: Maternal, fetal and neonatal outcomes are poor among pregnant women infected with *Zaire ebolavirus*. The majority of such pregnancies result in maternal death with miscarriage, stillbirth, or neonatal death. Treatment should not be withheld due to pregnancy.
L105: ## 8.2 Lactation
L106: ## Risk Summary
L107: The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention recommend that patients with confirmed *Zaire ebolavirus* not breastfeed their infants to reduce the risk of postnatal transmission of *Zaire ebolavirus* infection.
L108: There are no data on the presence of atolivimab, maftivimab, and odesivimab-ebgn in human or animal milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. Maternal IgG is known to be present in human milk. The effects of local gastrointestinal exposure and limited systemic exposure in the breastfed infant to atolivimab, maftivimab, or odesivimab-ebgn are unknown.
L109: ## 8.4 Pediatric Use
L110: The safety and effectiveness of INMABEB for the treatment of infection caused by *Zaire ebolavirus* have been established in pediatric patients from birth to less than 18 years of age. Use of INMABEB for this indication is supported by evidence from a multi-center, open label, randomized controlled trial of INMABEB in adults and pediatric subjects that included 39 pediatric subjects birth to less than 18 years of age, including neonates born to a mother who is RT-PCR positive for *Zaire ebolavirus* infection. The 28-day mortality and safety in adult and pediatric subjects treated with INMABEB were similar (see *Adverse Reactions (6.1) and Clinical Studies (14)*). An additional 38 pediatric subjects from birth to less than 18 years of age received INMABEB in an expanded access program.
L111: ## 8.5 Geriatric Use
L112: Clinical studies of INMAZEB did not include sufficient numbers of subjects aged 65 and over to determine whether they respond differently from younger subjects. Of the 154 subjects with *Zaire ebolavirus* infection who received INMAZEB in the randomized controlled trial, 5 (3.2%) were 65 years or older. The limited clinical experience has not identified differences in responses between the elderly and younger subjects.

L130: ## 12.4 Microbiology
L131: ## Mechanism of Action
L132: INMAZEB is a combination of three recombinant human IgG1κ monoclonal antibodies each targeting the *Zaire ebolavirus* glycoprotein (GP). *Zaire ebolavirus* encodes a sole envelope protein, the glycoprotein, which mediates virus attachment and membrane fusion with the host cell membranes. In addition, GP is expressed on the surface of *Zaire ebolavirus* infected host cells making it a target for antibodies that can mediate killing of these cells by antibody dependent cellular cytotoxicity and/or other effector functions. The 3 antibodies that make up the combination bind to 3 non-overlapping epitopes on GP and all 3 antibodies can bind the GP simultaneously. The mean KD values for atoltivimab, maftivimab, and odesivimab were 7.84 nM, 3.34 nM, and 8.26 nM, respectively, as determined by surface plasmon resonance. Maftivimab is a neutralizing antibody that blocks entry of the virus into susceptible cells. Odesivimab is a non-neutralizing antibody that induces antibody-dependent effector function through FcyRIIIa
L133: signaling when bound to its target. Odesivimab also binds to the soluble form of *Zaire ebolavirus* glycoprotein (sGP). Atoltivimab combines both neutralization and FcyRIIA signaling activities.
L134: ## Antiviral Activity
L135: In a live virus infection assay on Vero cells, mafitivimab neutralized Mayinga, Kikwit, and Makona strains of *Zaire ebolavirus*, with a concentration between 0.2 and 1.2 nM (0.03 and 0.18 \mug/mL) providing 80% inhibition of viral infection in a plaque-reduction neutralization test (PRNT-80). Atoltivimab and odesivimab did not demonstrate any neutralizing activity in this assay. Effector function activity of INMAZEB individual antibodies was assessed with an EBOV Makona-GP expressing cell line and Jurkat/NFAT-Luc/FcyRIIA reporter effector cells. The EC50 values of atoltivimab and odesivimab were 2.9 nM and 1.6 nM, respectively, whereas mafitivimab did not exhibit any FcyRIIA signaling activity at the maximum concentration tested, 40 nM.
L136: Treatment of *Zaire ebolavirus* infected rhesus macaques with a single intravenous dose of INMAZEB (50 mg of atoltivimab, 50 mg of mafitivimab, and 50 mg of odesivimab per kg) generally protected infected animals from *Zaire ebolavirus* mediated death when drug was administered 5 days post-infection.
L137: ## Resistance
L138: No clinical data are available on the development of EBOV resistance to INMAZEB. The cell culture development of EBOV resistance to INMAZEB has not been assessed to date. Amino acid substitutions in GP have been identified in isolates collected through routine surveillance in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (E280G), studies conducted in non-human primates (G528R, N563T, E564A, E564K), and published literature (I274M, W275L, T544I, H549R). The clinical significance of these substitutions is unknown.
L139: ## Binding Activity
L140: Binding was assessed with an ECL-based assay using virus like particles (VLPs) pseudotyped with wild-type or variant EBOV GP. None of the GP substitutions evaluated impacted binding of INMAZEB by &gt;2.5-fold based on affinity assessments. GP substitution E280G impacted the binding of atoltivimab with a \ge 186-fold decrease in affinity. GP substitutions G528R, N563T, and E564K impacted the binding of mafitivimab with \ge 454-fold decreases in affinity. GP substitution I274M impacted the binding of odesivimab with a 4.5-fold decrease in affinity.
L141: ## Neutralization Activity
L142: Neutralization by atoltivimab, mafitivimab, and INMAZEB was assessed using VLPs pseudotyped with wild-type or variant EBOV GP. Odesivimab did not demonstrate any neutralizing activity in this assay. GP substitutions W275L and E280G impacted atoltivimab neutralization with \ge 170- and \ge 445-fold increases in EC50 value and had no impact on INMAZEB neutralization potency. GP substitutions G528R, N563T, E564A, and E564K impacted mafitivimab neutralization with EC50 value increases of \ge 81-, \ge 81-, 38-, and \ge 451 fold, respectively, and had no impact on INMAZEB neutralization potency. GP substitutions I274M, T544I, and H549R did not impact the neutralization potency of atoltivimab, mafitivimab or INMAZEB. GP substitutions W275L and E564A did not have any significant impact on binding but resulted in a loss of neutralization activity for atoltivimab or mafitivimab, respectively.
L143: ## Immune Response
L144: Interaction studies with recombinant live EBOV vaccines and INMAZEB have not been conducted [see *Drug Interactions (7.1)*].

L146: ## 13.1 Carcinogenesis, Mutagenesis, Impairment of Fertility
L147: Carcinogenicity, genotoxicity, and fertility studies have not been conducted with INMAZEB.
L148: ## 14 CLINICAL STUDIES
L149: The efficacy of INMAZEB was evaluated in PALM, a multi-center, open-label, randomized controlled trial sponsored by the National Institute of Allergy and Infectious Diseases (NIAID; NCT03719586). The trial was conducted in the Democratic Republic of Congo, where an outbreak began in August 2018, and enrolled 681 subjects of all ages, including pregnant women, with documented *Zaire ebolavirus* infection and symptoms of any duration who were receiving optimized standard of care (oSOC). Subjects were randomized to receive INMAZEB (50 mg of atolvivimab, 50 mg of mafivimab, and 50 mg of odesivimab per kg) intravenously as a single infusion, an investigational control 50 mg/kg intravenously every third day, for a total of 3 doses, or other investigational drugs. Eligible subjects had a positive reverse transcriptase-polymerase chain reaction (RT-PCR) for the nucleoprotein (NP) gene of *Zaire ebolavirus* and had not received other investigational treatments (with the exception of experimental vaccines) within the previous 30 days. Neonates \le7 days of age were eligible if the mother had documented infection. Neonates born to a mother who had cleared *Zaire ebolavirus* following a course of her assigned investigational medication were also eligible to be enrolled at investigator discretion regarding the likelihood that the neonate was infected. Randomization was stratified by reverse transcription-PCR cycle threshold calculated using NP targets (CtNP \le22.0 vs &gt;22.0; corresponding to high and low viral load, respectively) and Ebola Treatment Unit (ETU) site. All subjects received oSOC consisting of a minimum of intravenous fluids, daily clinical laboratory testing, correction of hypoglycemia and electrolyte imbalances, and broad-spectrum antibiotics and antimalarials, as indicated.
L150: The primary efficacy endpoint was 28-day mortality. The primary analysis population includes all subjects who were randomized and concurrently eligible to receive either INMAZEB or the investigational control during the same time period of the trial.
