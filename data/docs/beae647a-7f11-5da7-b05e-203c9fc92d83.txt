L10: ## 2. Background
L11: Humans and higher apes do not possess the metabolic pathway by which most other animals handle uric acid—i.e., creation of allantoin by oxidation of uric acid, using urate oxidase. Intravenous rasburicase, a recombinant urate oxidase produced by a genetically modified *Saccharomyces cerevisiae* strain (and initially cloned from a strain of *Aspergillus flavus*), was approved July 12, 2002 for the indication of “initial management of plasma uric acid levels in pediatric patients with leukemia, lymphoma, and solid tumor malignancies who are receiving anti-cancer therapy expected to result in tumor lysis and subsequent elevation of plasma uric acid.” It is now also approved for adults with a similar indication. Repeated use of rasburicase is limited by the immunogenic responses to it, which can result in serious infusion reactions, hypersensitivity and anaphylaxis; pegloticase was developed as a PEGylated product in the hopes of reducing the immunogenic response.
L12: The clinical trials submitted in support of this application were two replicate, randomized Phase 3 trials in gout patients who had failed or who were intolerant to allopurinol, one of which (Study C0405) was the subject of a Special Protocol Assessment (SPA) agreement (May 3, 2006). Patients were randomized 2:2:1 to pegloticase 8 mg IV every 2 weeks, pegloticase 8 mg IV every 4 weeks or placebo, respectively. As noted in the first-cycle clinical reviews, these two trials demonstrated convincing evidence of the efficacy of IV pegloticase over placebo for the primary endpoint of lowering serum uric acid to less than 6 mg/dL.
L13: The primary safety issues of concern pertained to the risk of serious infusion reactions, hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, and an imbalance of cardiovascular serious adverse events. During the first cycle, these issues were discussed in the context of pegloticase’s efficacy and the underlying patient population at the Arthritis Advisory Committee meeting of June 16, 2009. The panel for this meeting included members of the Cardiovascular and Renal Drug Products Advisory Committee and the Drug Safety Advisory Committee. The panel voted 14 to 1 in favor of approval. Although there was no consensus from the panel regarding whether the imbalance of cardiovascular SAE represented a true signal, there was general agreement that additional postmarketing safety information in this regard should be collected, possibly via an observational epidemiologic study. Panel members also considered it important to limit the use of the product to patients who were truly refractory to conventional therapy. There was also support for a sponsor proposal to incorporate instructions in labeling regarding cessation of treatment when uric acid rises above 6 mg/dL in order to minimize the risk of infusion reactions. These issues will be discussed in further detail below.

L113: The nonclinical toxicology program consisted of single-dose and repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and dogs administered either via subcutaneous or via intravenous route. Repeat-dose studies of up to 54 days duration in rats or up to 39 weeks in dogs were performed. The 39-week dog study is the primary chronic long-term toxicity study; this was not done in a second species as per previous agreements between the Applicant and the Agency. Both 12-week and 39-week dog studies demonstrated vacuolation of spleen; other organs affected in the 39-week study only included adrenal cortex, duodenum, heart, jejunum, and liver. No reversibility after a 12-week recovery period was observed. Dr. Mukherjee believes these vacuoles to be expected histological effects of pegylated protein degradation, and that these had not demonstrated toxicological effects. The hypothesis that these vacuoles contained PEG was not reassuring to Dr. Hayes, Dr. Mellon, and Dr. Topper, in light of the lack of demonstrated clearance of these vacuoles and the potential for continued accumulation of PEG with increasing exposure. Nonetheless, in response to the Agency's preliminary discipline comments associated with the first-cycle BLA, the Applicant submitted a proposal to conduct an 18-month study in dogs to evaluate the impact of cytoplasmic vacuoles in the adrenal gland and the aortic outflow tract of the heart. This study proposed dosing dogs for 12-months, at up to 5-fold the human dose every 14 days, followed by a recovery period of 6 months. This had been accepted by the review team involved at that time. Since the majority of the Pharmacology-Toxicology review team has determined this study to be necessary, and agreement has already been reached with the Applicant, this study will remain as a post-marketing requirement.

L138: The details of the Phase 3 trials submitted to support this BLA, and the regulatory interactions during the clinical development program, may be found in Dr. Neuner's and Dr. Siegel's first cycle reviews of this BLA. In summary, the Applicant submitted two replicate studies, C0405 and C0406, in adults 18 years of age or older with symptomatic gout and inadequate control with or intolerance to optimal therapy with allopurinol. Symptomatic gout was defined as either 3 or more gout attacks in the preceding 18 months, or a documented tophus, or gouty arthritis. Patients were also required to have a screening uric acid of 8 mg/dL or higher. The study excluded patients with uncontrolled cardiac disease or end-stage renal disease.
L139: A total of 100 subjects was planned for each of the Phase 3 trials. Randomization was carried out in a 2:2:1 ratio for the pegloticase 8 mg every 2 week, pegloticase 8 mg every 4 week and placebo study arms, respectively. Randomization was stratified for the presence/absence of tophi. The primary endpoint was the proportion of patients with a plasma uric acid below 6 mg/dL at least 80% of the time during months 3 and 6. The prespecified primary analysis was the intent-to-treat (ITT) population analyzed based on Fisher's exact test. A non-responder imputation was specified for missing data. No multiplicity adjustment was specified for the two treatment arms. As previously mentioned, the Agency entered into SPA agreement with the Applicant regarding these details as proposed for Study C0405.
L140: As shown in Table 1 below, treatment with pegloticase was associated with a higher proportion of patients maintaining a plasma uric acid level below 6 mg/dL at least 80% of the time during months 3 and 6. Approximately 40-50% of patients treated with pegloticase 8 mg every 2 weeks were able to achieve this magnitude of response. Interestingly, the 8 mg every 4 week dose was associated with a lower proportion of responders compared to 8 mg every 2 week regimen in Study C0405 but not Study C0406. In either case, a treatment effect in favor of pegloticase was clearly demonstrated, as no placebo patients were able to achieve this target, and differences between pegloticase and placebo were statistically significant for either dosing regimen.
L141: The 8 mg every 2 week dose regimen also demonstrated effectiveness for the major secondary endpoint of resolution of tophi. The reader is referred to first-cycle clinical and statistical reviews for additional details of the efficacy findings.

L179: Immunogenicity-related assays were reviewed in the first-cycle BLA submission by Dr. Pedras-Vasconcelos. The definition of high titer (&gt;1:7920 dilution) was derived empirically by the Applicant based on thresholds observed for correlations between antibody titers and clinically observed responses, which is appropriate. The anti-pegloticase IgE assay did not include a suitable positive control, as the Applicant was unable to develop one. L201: ## *Anaphylaxis and Infusion Reactions*
L202: In light of the fact that humans do not possess urate oxidase, and thus the enzyme sequences had to be derived from other mammals (specifically pig), an increased risk for immunogenicity and allergic reactions was anticipated for this product, and this expectation has largely held true. The first-cycle review team consulted the Division of Pulmonary and Allergy Products regarding cases of anaphylaxis, which were not explicitly sorted from other cases of infusion reactions.
L203: Based on review of individual line listings, the allergy consultant, Dr. Susan Limb, identified at least 14 cases meeting the NIAID/FAAN clinical criteria for anaphylaxis (See Table 7 below, from Sampson HA et al. J Allergy Clin Immunol 2006;117:391-7).
L204: ## Copyright Material
L205: Using these criteria, the estimated frequency of anaphylaxis is 5.1% (14 of 273 patients who received IV pegloticase in the clinical development program). For the Q2wk regimen, the frequency is 7.3% (9 out of 123); for the Q4wk regimen, the frequency is 3.9% (4 out of 126), which is a reverse of the trend observed for infusion reactions as a whole, but may just be random chance given the small numbers involved. The true rate of anaphylaxis and hypersensitivity may be underestimated due to the mandatory prophylactic administration of antihistamines, acetaminophen and IV corticosteroids prior to pegloticase infusion. Based on the cases Dr. Limb reviewed, the presence of IgE, IgG or IgM antibodies against the drug did not appear to have a predictive effect. Other screening assessments such as skin test or graded challenge were not performed. Dr. Limb concluded that an estimated rate of anaphylaxis of 5% is not unusually high compared to similar porcine-derived biologic products. Because it may not be readily apparent to prescribers that pegloticase is a foreign protein, I believe a boxed warning is warranted to draw attention to this life-threatening risk.

L214: ## *Gout Flares*
L215: Although the mechanisms underlying a flare are not completely clear, it is currently hypothesized that a gout attack may be triggered by a release of uncoated crystals (for example due to partial dissolution of microtophi caused by changing serum uric acid levels), or by precipitation of crystals in a supersaturated microenvironment. Stable uric acid crystals become coated with serum proteins that physically inhibit binding of crystals to cell receptors, thus do not precipitate the inflammatory response caused by naked crystals.1 Because of pegloticase's mechanism of action, which would result in direct breakdown of existing uric acid stores, it would be expected to dissolve stable clumps of uric acid crystals and microtophi and thereby increase acute flares. In addition, the clinical trials targeted patients with severe refractory gout, with a high baseline history of flare (an average of 10 gout flares in the previous 18 months).

L226: ## - **Discussion of notable safety issues (resolved or outstanding).**
L227: The notable safety issues are the immunogenicity of this product and the risk of anaphylaxis and infusion reactions associated with treatment. Because the premarketing safety database for this product is so limited (though justified by the limited numbers in the target population), it is possible that rare adverse events and adverse events that might occur with prolonged exposure, such as immune-complex-mediated adverse effects, may not have been captured. Therefore a postmarketing observational study should be required to enable assessment of a larger, longer duration experience than has been provided in the BLA.
L228: The imbalance of cardiovascular adverse events noted in the controlled period of the trials is difficult to interpret given the small number of events and the high underlying risk in the patient population. Because pegloticase is intended for the treatment of a small, refractory patient population, OSE/DEPI believes that an observational registry is unlikely to result in better data than postmarketing spontaneous adverse event reporting. I concur with their recommendation that standard postmarketing pharmacovigilance is warranted at this time.
L229: ## 9. Advisory Committee Meeting
L230: During the first cycle, the primary safety issues of concern were identified as the risk of serious infusion reactions, hypersensitivity/anaphylaxis, and the imbalance of cardiovascular serious adverse events in the controlled period. These issues were discussed at the Arthritis Advisory Committee meeting of June 16, 2009, in the context of the efficacy of pegloticase and the refractory population targeted. The panel for this meeting included members of the Cardiovascular and Renal Drug Products Advisory Committee and the Drug Safety Advisory Committee. The panel voted 14 to 1 in favor of approval. Although there was no consensus from the panel regarding whether the imbalance of cardiovascular SAE represented a true signal, there was general agreement that additional postmarketing safety information in this regard should be collected, possibly via an observational epidemiologic study. Panel members also considered it important to limit the use of the product to patients who were truly refractory to conventional therapy. There was also support for a sponsor proposal to incorporate instructions in labeling regarding cessation of treatment when uric acid rises above 6 mg/dL in order to minimize the risk of infusion reactions.
L231: ## 10. Pediatrics
L232: Gout is quite rare in children, occurring almost exclusively in the setting of hypoxanthine-guanine phosphoribosyltransferase (HGPRT) deficiency (also known as Lesch-Nyhan syndrome and Kelley-Seegmiller syndrome). Therefore it would not be possible to conduct studies in children due to the small number of patients available and the Applicant would qualify for a waiver to conduct studies in children under PREA. However, since the indication of gout refractory to conventional treatments was granted orphan designation, this application is exempt from PREA.

L256: ## • **Risk Benefit Assessment**
L257: Because of its direct activity in breaking down uric acid, pegloticase targets a unique niche in the therapeutic armamentarium for gout. This distinct mechanism provides for the possibility of control of disease that has historically been difficult to control with uric acid lowering treatments (i.e. uricosurics and xanthine oxidase inhibitors), including tophaceous gout. As shown in Table 10 below, using the data from the controlled trials, only 2 patients needed to be treated to achieve a treatment benefit, as described by the primary endpoint of plasma uric acid less than 6 mg/dL over 80% of the time in months 3 and 6 combined. More remarkably, only 5 patients with tophi need to be treated for one to experience complete resolution of at least one tophus. However this is negatively balanced by the high rate of infusion reactions (all severities), which also approached 1 in 5. For the most severe reactions (i.e. anaphylaxis), 20 patients were treated for every case of anaphylaxis observed. This relative balance in risks vs. benefits should give pause to prescribers contemplating off-label use for patients with uncomplicated forms of gout. However, for the target population of patients who are refractory to conventional therapy, the lack of alternatives makes the risk:benefit balance clearly favorable.

L260: ## - **Recommendation for Postmarketing Risk Evaluation and Management Strategies**
L261: The risks of pegloticase are not insignificant, and a risk evaluation and mitigation strategy (REMS) consisting of a Medication Guide and Communication Plan, is warranted, as originally agreed upon by the original review team, OSE, and the Applicant in the first cycle. It was determined at that time that Elements to Assure Safe Use (ETASU) were not considered necessary, as the risk of life threatening infusion reactions has always been a concern with biologic products, and this has historically been adequately addressed by labeling and medical supervision/routine emergent management as per standard of care in the infusion center setting. The second cycle review team for DRISK concurs that this is reasonable. Therefore, in order to entail the least burden possible consistent with the safety goals, it is appropriate that the REMS consist of the Medication Guide and Communication Plan, focusing on the primary risks of anaphylaxis and infusion reactions as well as the need for premedication of infusions, monitoring and stopping treatment for loss of therapeutic response.
