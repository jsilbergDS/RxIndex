L13: ## Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment
L14: Taltz (ixekizumab) is a humanized immunoglobulin G subclass 4 (IgG4) monoclonal antibody produced by recombinant DNA technology. Ixekizumab selectively binds with interleukin-17A (IL-17A) and inhibits its interaction with the IL-17 receptor. IL-17A is a naturally occurring cytokine that is involved in normal inflammatory and immune responses. Ixekizumab inhibits the release of proinflammatory cytokines and chemokines. Taltz (ixekizumab) is administered by subcutaneous injection, and supplied in a single-dose prefilled autoinjector or a single-dose prefilled syringe delivering 80 mg of ixekizumab.
L15: I concur with the recommendation of the Division of Dermatology and Dental Products to approve Taltz (ixekizumab) injection for the treatment of adult patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy. Substantial evidence of effectiveness was demonstrated in three randomized controlled clinical trials, and the safety profile of ixekizumab was consistent with that of other approved systemic biologic therapies for adult patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis. The safety concerns identified with use of Taltz (ixekizumab) in clinical trials can be adequately managed by product labeling, a Medication Guide, that will be required as part of approved labeling to communicate important safety information to patients, and pharmacovigilance in the postmarket setting. A Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) will not be required.
L16: Psoriasis is a common, chronically remitting and relapsing inflammatory disease. Skin and joint manifestations predominate. Approximately 20% of patients have moderate-to-severe disease affecting more than 5% of their body surface area. Patients with psoriasis face numerous social and psychological challenges. The incidence of clinical depression and suicidality is higher among patients with psoriasis than in the general population.
L17: There are several FDA-approved therapies for adult patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis who are candidates for systemic therapy, including the tumor necrosis factor inhibitors, adalimumab and etanercept, apremilast (a phosphodiesterase 4 inhibitor), ustekinumab (an antagonist of IL-12 and IL-23), and secukinumab (an antagonist of IL-17A). Product efficacy, as measured by the PASI 75, ranges from highly efficacious (adalimumab, ustekinumab and secukinumab), to moderately efficacious (etanercept), to somewhat efficacious (apremilast). Approved systemic therapies for moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis also have attendant serious side effects including risk of serious infections, and risk of malignancy/lymphoma. Thus, alternative efficacious therapies with potentially better safety profiles are needed.
L18: Taltz (ixekizumab) injection was highly efficacious for the treatment of adults with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis in clinical trials. Evidence of ixekizumab efficacy included 1) superior clinical response at Week 12 compared to placebo, 2) superior clinical response at Week 60 among responding ixekizumab-treated patients receiving continued ixekizumab treatment compared to those switched to placebo maintenance therapy after Week 12, and 3) achievement of a second clinical response among ixekizumab-treated patients who responded initially and later relapsed on placebo maintenance therapy. In addition, clinical response to ixekizumab was superior to that of etanercept at Week 12.

These are known common, and potentially serious, adverse reactions associated with approved systemic biologic therapies for adult patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis. These reactions will be highlighted in the Warnings and Precautions section of product labeling and medical interventions that could be employed should these reactions occur will be described.

Analysis of Condition	
Psoriasis is a chronically remitting and relapsing inflammatory disease affecting 2-3% of the U.S. population. Skin and joint manifestations predominate. Plaque psoriasis (Psoriasis vulgaris) is the most common form, accounting for 85-90% of cases. Skin lesions are circumscribed, scaly and erythematous and often symmetrically distributed over the body. Affected skin may be painful, cracked or itchy. The majority of cases are mild to moderate, but 20% have moderate to severe disease affecting more than 5% of the body surface area.
Patients with psoriasis face numerous social and psychological challenges. In a study conducted in the U.K.'s General Practice Research Database, the incidence of clinical depression and suicidality (including suicidal ideation, attempt or suicide completion) in patients with psoriasis was higher than in the general population. Among patients with psoriasis, the incidence of clinical depression and suicidality was higher for those on systemic therapy than for those who were not (i.e., for patients presumed to have severe vs. mild disease).1

Benefit	
The efficacy of Taltz (ixekizumab) was established in three randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trials involving a total of 3866 adult patients with plaque psoriasis. Eligible patients had a minimum body surface area involvement of 10%, a static Physician Global Assessment (sPGA) score of 3 or greater (on a scale of 0 to 5), a Psoriasis Area and Severity Index (PASI) score of 12 or greater, and were candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy. Overall, 44% had received prior phototherapy, 49% had prior conventional systemic therapy, and 26% had prior systemic biologic therapy.

Depression and Suicidality. A comprehensive review of the risk of depression and suicidality in ixekizumab clinical trials was undertaken concurrent with a review of clinical trials for another anti-psoriatic agent targeting the IL-17 receptor that reported an excess of suicide completions. This review included staff from the Division of Psychiatry Products and the Division of Epidemiology I.
The three ixekizumab clinical trials excluded patients with a history of suicide attempt, with significant uncontrolled neuropsychiatric disorders, or with frequent active suicidal ideation as assessed by the Quick Inventory of Depressive Symptomatology Self-Report (QIDS-SR). During the trials, the QIDS was performed at Weeks 12, 24, 36, 52 and 60. Since the QIDS queries about events during the previous seven days, it is possible that events occurring at other times could have been missed. Nevertheless, these evaluations showed that most patients enrolled did not have depression or thoughts of death or suicide either at baseline or during the course of these trials.
To further assess the risk of suicidality in ixekizumab-exposed patients, the Division requested that the applicant conduct a retrospective analysis using the Columbia Classification Algorithm of Suicide Assessment (C-CASA). A total of nine ixekizumab-treated patients reported suicidal ideation during treatment (and a tenth after treatment); one placebo-treated patient and one etanercept-treated patient reported suicidal ideation. Exposure-adjusted rates of suicide ideation were 0.14 per 100 patient-years for ixekizumab vs. 0.58 per 100 patient-years for placebo. There were no reports of completed suicides.
Patients reporting suicide attempts also reported other risk factors, such as prior attempts, depression, bipolar disorder, anxiety, alcohol or other substance abuse or other psychosocial triggers. There was no temporal association of suicidal ideation events to either the onset or duration of treatment. In addition, there was no evidence for treatment-emergent worsening of depression, anxiety or other psychiatric adverse events. Taken together, the available data did not support an association of ixekizumab treatment with suicidality

Specific Populations. The safety of ixekizumab in specific populations can be summarized as follows:
Pregnancy and Lactation. There are no available data on ixekizumab use in pregnant women to inform any drug associated risks. Human IgG is known to cross the placental barrier; therefore, ixekizumab may be transmitted from the mother to the developing fetus. An embryofetal development study in pregnant cynomolgus monkeys administered ixekizumab weekly by subcutaneous injection during organogenesis at doses up to 19 times the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) revealed no evidence of harm to the developing fetus. When drug exposure continued until parturition, neonatal deaths were observed at 1.9 times the MRHD. The clinical significance of these findings is unknown.
There are no data on the presence of ixekizumab in human milk, the effects on the breastfed infant, or the effects on milk production. Ixekizumab was detected in the milk of lactating cynomolgus monkeys.
Pediatrics. The safety and effectiveness of ixekizumab have not been evaluated in pediatric patients with moderate-to-severe plaque psoriasis aged less than 18 years of age.
Elderly. A total of 301 ixekizumab-treated patients were 65 years or older, and 36 were 75 years or older. Although no differences in safety or efficacy were observed between older and younger patients, the number of patients aged 65 and over is not sufficient to determine whether they respond differently from younger patients.

Risk Mitigation. A Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) will not be required for Taltz (ixekizumab). A Medication Guide will be required as part of approved labeling to communicate important safety information to patients.
Ixekizumab shares many of the same serious risks as other systemic biologic therapies approved for plaque psoriasis. Infliximab, etanercept and adalimumab each had a REMS to address the risks of serious infections and malignancy. The activities supporting the Communication Plans to practitioners in these REMS were completed and REMS assessments demonstrated that the REMS goals were being met. In 2011, these products were released from their REMS requirements but continue to have Medication Guides as part of approved labeling.
Ustekinumab was approved with a REMS in 2009 to address the risks of serious infections, malignancy and reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome. The Medication Guide was removed from the REMS in 2012. The Communication Plan was modified in 2013 to include rheumatologists when a supplemental indication of psoriatic arthritis was approved. Communication Plan activities were completed in 2014 and will be assessed in the next REMS assessment report.
Thus, it is likely that practitioners who will prescribe ixekizumab for plaque psoriasis will be aware of the attendant risks of systemic biologic therapies.

The required pediatric study represents an evolution in the Division's thinking. Historically, due to concerns about serious safety risks, including malignancies, reported with other currently available systemic therapies (e.g., methotrexate, cyclosporine, the tumor necrosis factor inhibitors), the Division has either waived pediatric studies (e.g., for adalimumab, infliximab) or imposed delayed study timelines to allow for the collection of additional safety data in adults and from pediatric patients exposed in utero or via breastfeeding (e.g., ustekinumab).
Since the highly efficacious IL-17 inhibitors, such as ixekizumab, may have a more favorable benefit/risk profile than other currently available systemic therapies, the Division has determined that timelines for initiating pediatric studies of ixekizumab may be accelerated. An accelerated timeline for pediatric studies has also been implemented for the recently approved IL-17 inhibitor, secukinumab.
Furthermore, the Division has determined that full extrapolation of adult efficacy data to pediatric patients is appropriate.
