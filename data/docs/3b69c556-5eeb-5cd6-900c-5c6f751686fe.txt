L37: DATING PERIOD
L38: Based on the stability data submitted to date, the expiry dating period for Zegalogue (dasiglucagon) injection shall be 36 months from the date of manufacture when stored at 2°C to 8°C.
L39: ADVISORY COMMITTEE
L40: Your application for Zegalogue was not referred to an FDA advisory committee because outside expertise was not necessary; there were no controversial issues that would benefit from advisory committee discussion.
L41: REQUIRED PEDIATRIC ASSESSMENTS
L42: Under the Pediatric Research Equity Act (PREA) (21 U.S.C. 355c), all applications for new active ingredients (which includes new salts and new fixed combinations), new indications, new dosage forms, new dosing regimens, or new routes of administration are required to contain an assessment of the safety and effectiveness of the product for the claimed indication in pediatric patients unless this requirement is waived, deferred, or inapplicable.
L43: We are waiving the pediatric study requirement for ages 0 to less than 1 year because necessary studies are impossible or highly impracticable due to the low incidence of type 1 diabetes mellitus in patients below 1 year of age.
L44: 2 We update guidances periodically. For the most recent version of a guidance, check the FDA Guidance Documents Database https://www.fda.gov/RegulatoryInformation/Guidances/default.htm.
L45: We are deferring submission of your pediatric study for ages 1 year to less than 6 years for this application because this product is ready for approval for use in adults and the pediatric study has not been completed.
L46: Your deferred pediatric study required by section 505B(a) of the FDCA is a required postmarketing study. The status of this postmarketing study must be reported annually according to 21 CFR 314.81 and section 505B(a)(4)(C) of the FDCA. This required study is listed below.
L47: 4034-1 Conduct an open-label pediatric study to evaluate the safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetics/pharmacodynamics of dasiglucagon in pediatric patients ages 1 year to &lt;6 years with type 1 diabetes mellitus.
