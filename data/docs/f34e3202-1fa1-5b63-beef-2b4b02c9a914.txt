L27: ## I. Executive Summary
L28: ## 1. Overview
L29: ## 1.1. Summary of Regulatory Action
L30: This new drug application (NDA), 217370 for Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003%, was submitted in accordance with Section 505(b)(1) of the Federal Food Drug and Cosmetic Act and 21 CFR 314.50 of the U.S. Code of Federal Regulations, by Alcon Laboratories, Inc. (Alcon), for the treatment of the signs and symptom of dry eye disease (DED) in adults when dosed twice daily in each eye.
L31: The Applicant submitted three studies supporting the efficacy of Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% (one Phase 2b and two Phase 3 pivotal studies). Each of these studies was prospective, randomized, and double-masked. Initial eligibility was assessed at the Screening visit, based on signs and symptoms of DED (anesthetized Schirmer test score, total corneal fluorescein staining score, global Symptoms Assessment iN Dry Eye [SANDE] score, and ocular discomfort score [ODS]). Subjects also had to demonstrate an increase in signs and symptoms of DED following exposure to a controlled adverse environment (CAE) at Screening. All qualified subjects at the Screening visit entered a 2-week run-in period with the vehicle. Following requalification of subjects based on signs and symptoms of DED, all eligible subjects were randomized to twice daily (BID) treatment in both eyes (OU) with acoltremon ophthalmic solution or vehicle for up to 3 months.
L32: Both Phase 3 studies, COMET-2 and COMET-3, demonstrated safety and efficacy for the treatment of dry eye disease for the primary efficacy endpoint, percentage of subjects achieving a 10-mm increase or more in Schirmer's tear test scores. NDA 217370 was reviewed by a multidisciplinary team as a Standard Review and was not presented at an advisory committee.
L33: ## 1.2. Conclusions on Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
L34: Substantial evidence of effectiveness (SEE) was established with two or more adequate and well-controlled clinical investigations.
L35: Phase 3 studies COMET-2 and COMET-3 demonstrated safety and efficacy for the treatment of dry eye disease for the primary efficacy endpoint, percentage of subjects achieving a 10-mm increase or more in Schirmer's tear test scores.

L37: ## 2.1. Benefit-Risk Framework
L38: Table 2. Benefit-Risk Framework
L39: Dimension	Evidence and Uncertainties	Conclusions and Reasons
Analysis of condition	Dry eye disease (DED) is a multifactorial, age-related, chronic progressive disease of the ocular surface.
Chronic DED can cause severe pain, visual impairment, decreased tear production, and tear film hyperosmolarity and instability.Patients with corneal staining are more susceptible than others to eye infections and damage to the surface of the eye (cornea).

Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% is a TRPM8 agonist. The mechanism of action by which it may increase tear production is uncertain. The drug effect is negligible under topical anesthesia, so it appears to stimulate tear production through the afferent pathway.
Current treatment options	Restasis (cyclosporine ophthalmic emulsion) 0.05%
Xiidra (lifitegrast ophthalmic solution) 0.05%
Cequa (cyclosporine ophthalmic solution) 0.09%
(b) (4) (varenicline tartrate) (b) (4)
Miebo (perfluorohexyloctane ophthalmic solution)
Vevye (Cyclosporine ophthalmic solution) 0.1%
OTC Monograph eye drops
Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% would provide an alternative product for the treatment of DED by increasing tear production.
Benefit	Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% demonstrated a statistically significant increase in Schirmer tear score, improvement in tear production in two (COMET-2 and COMET-3) randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled multisite clinical studies in subjects with a diagnosis of dry eyes.
Uncertainties: The duration of action of Tryptyr is unknown. Successful tear production endpoints all began Schirmer Tear Tests at three minutes postdrop. This provides no data to evaluate tear production through the remainder of the nearly 12 hours before the next drop.
The increase in tear production by 10 mm on a Schirmer tear test is considered a clinically significant change on its own. The amount of change in tearing has been associated with a change in clinical symptoms.

L42: Dimension	Evidence and Uncertainties	Conclusions and Reasons
Risk and risk management	The most common adverse event experienced with Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% was instillation site pain, at rate of 49.7% in the integrated safety population.
There were no deaths and no serious complications related to the study drug.
Urinary tract infections, COVID-19 infections, and sinusitis were the only nonocular TEAEs reported in ≥1% of the 0.003% acoltremon arm in the safety population (2.1%, 1.0%, and 1.0%, respectively, compared to 1.7%, 1.3%, and 0.6% in the corresponding vehicle control.
In the PK substudy of COMET-4, systemic exposure to 0.003% acoltremon was evaluated in a subset of subjects. Overall, the plasma concentrations of acoltremon in most (22 of 25) subjects was below the limit of quantitation, indicating that systemic exposure via the topical ocular route is negligible.
Routine monitoring and reporting of all adverse events are expected to be adequate to monitor for potential new adverse reactions.

L44: ## 2.2. Conclusions Regarding Benefit-Risk
L45: Studies COMET-2 and COMET-3 demonstrated that Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% dosed twice daily in each eye was statistically superior to vehicle in the proportion of subjects experiencing a \ge 10-mm increase in Schirmer score on Day 14. These were identical, randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled studies evaluated in adult subjects with dry eye disease.
L46: The primary efficacy endpoint, the proportion of subjects achieving a \ge 10-mm increase in Schirmer's tear test scores, is considered a clinically significant change without a corresponding symptom change. The amount of change in tearing has been associated with a change in clinical symptoms.
L47: The safety of Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% was assessed in over 766 subjects dosed twice a day. The mean for treatment exposure was 141.3 days; the median was 92 days. The safety profile of Tryptyr is acceptable. The most common treatment-emergent adverse event experienced during the clinical studies was instillation site pain (burning or stinging) events: 49.7% in the Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% group and 4.1% in the vehicle group. The majority of instillation site pain (burning or stinging) events were mild in severity: 97.6% of these events were mild in the Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% group, and 96.4% in the vehicle group.

L92: ## 5. Pharmacologic Activity, Pharmacokinetics, and Clinical Pharmacology
L93: ## 5.1. Nonclinical Assessment of Potential Effectiveness
L94: The published literature established that acoltremon is a transient receptor potential melastatin 8 (TRPM8) agonist like menthol. TRPM8, a calcium-permeable cation channel, is a cold sensor in humans. The activation of TRPM8 opens the channel and allows influx of calcium ions into the cell, resulting in cold thermal transduction and pain detection, among other effects. The
L95: Applicant verified some of these activities in vitro using cells transfected with rat TRPM8, ex vivo, and in vivo on Guinea pig corneas:
L96: - In vitro, acoltremon increased cytosolic calcium influx in TRPM8 transfected cells at a half maximal effective concentration (EC_{50}) as low as 1\mu M (289 pg/mL).
- Ex vivo, acoltremon activated the corneal cold thermoreceptor (TRPM8) nerve endings in the Guinea pig with an EC_{50} of 47.7\mu M (13,805 pg/mL).
- A single topical ocular (10-\mu L) application of acoltremon in the Guinea pig increased blink and possibly tear rates, compared to vehicle with an EC_{50} of 9.1\mu M (2,634 pg/mL or 0.026 \mu g/eye).
L97: ## 5.2. Clinical Pharmacology/Pharmacokinetics
L98: Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% is proposed for topical ophthalmic use and the site of action is the eye. Acoltremon systemic exposure was evaluated in a subset of subjects with DED in the ongoing long-term safety study COMET-4, in which subjects received acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% or vehicle one drop in each eye twice daily for 12 months. Plasma samples were collected on Days 1, 14, and 90 from 25 subjects dosed with acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003%. Acoltremon was detected in 6 samples from 3 subjects with plasma concentrations ranging from 22.8 pg/mL to 213 pg/mL but was below the limit of quantification (lower limit of quantification [LLOQ] of 20 pg/mL) in all other samples (refer to Section 14 for more details). Given the negligible systemic exposure of acoltremon following topical ocular administration, the impact of intrinsic and extrinsic factors on acoltremon pharmacokinetics (PK) has not been assessed.

L104: ## 6. Efficacy (Evaluation of Benefit)
L105: ## 6.1. Assessment of Dose and Potential Effectiveness
L106: Based on nonclinical studies, the high protein binding of acoltremon, and negligible systemic (plasma) exposure following the clinically intended route of administration (topical ocular), there is limited potential for clinically significant systemic effect levels with topical ocular dosing. A systemic pharmacokinetic evaluation was incorporated in the long-term safety study (Phase 3, COMET-4). Results from this study are included in Module 2.7.2 and the final analysis reported included in Module 5.3.5.4.
L107: Initial dose finding and dosing frequency were evaluated in the Phase 1/2a study conducted by the prior sponsor (AVX012-CT-001). Results demonstrated that both 0.0007% and 0.0014% acoltremon administered as a topical ophthalmic solution three times a day (TID), had an acceptable safety profile. Additionally, after 28 days of either BID or TID administration of 0.0014% acoltremon ophthalmic solution, both DED symptoms (SANDE score) and signs (tear production) were improved. Review of the totality of efficacy data concluded that TID administration provided no additional treatment benefit relative to BID, and BID dosing was chosen to maximize subject compliance.
L108: The concentration of acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% used in the final formulation was based on results from the Phase 2b study (COMET-1), which evaluated 0.0014% and 0.003% acoltremon. In this study, the 0.003% concentration yielded the most consistent and robust treatment effects across multiple signs and symptoms. This included a dose dependent increase in tear production on Days 1 and 14 based on unanesthetized Schirmer scores (Day 1: least squares [LS] mean difference from vehicle in change from baseline: 12.2 and 6.1 mm for 0.003% and 0.0014%, respectively; Day 14: LS mean difference from vehicle in change from baseline: 13.4 and 9.2 mm for 0.003% and 0.0014%, respectively) (COMET-1 clinical study report Table 14.2.3.10.1), and reduction in global SANDE scores on Days 28 and 84 (Day 28: LS mean difference from vehicle in change from baseline: -7.9 and -4.3 mm for 0.003% and 0.0014%, respectively; Day 84: LS mean difference from vehicle in change from baseline: -8.9 and -1.1 mm for 0.003% and 0.0014%, respectively).
L109: Overall, evidence from preclinical safety and clinical studies support the concentration and dosing frequency of acoltremon chosen for Phase 3 development studies. The recommended dosage for Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% is one drop in each eye, twice daily.
L110: ## 6.1.1. Proposed Dosage Regimen
L111: The proposed dosage regimen is Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% one drop instilled twice daily in each eye (approximately 12 hours apart). This dosing regimen was tested in two independent Phase 3 studies (COMET-2 and COMET-3) as well as a Phase 2 study and a long-term safety study.
L112: ## 6.2. Clinical Studies/Trials Intended To Demonstrate Efficacy
L113: Three randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled studies were conducted to support the efficacy of repeat dosing of Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003% for up to 90 days in subjects of either sex and of any race with DED who met all other eligibility criteria.
L114: All three efficacy studies were prospective, randomized, double-masked, vehicle-controlled, parallel group, multicenter studies. The Phase 3 studies COMET-2 and COMET-3 are intended to demonstrate efficacy for the treatment of dry eye disease. Refer to Section 15 for a detailed description of the study design and endpoints.
L115: Subject inclusion and exclusion criteria were the same among all studies. Subjects 30 years and older with a history of artificial tear use for DED were qualified based on signs and symptoms of DED at Screening as outlined below, and were requalified at Baseline (Day 1) following a 14-day vehicle run-in.
L116: Both of the following signs of DED in the same eye:
L117: - Total corneal fluorescein staining score of \ge 2 and \le 15 (modified National Eye Institute), and no region &gt;3 at Screening only
- Anesthetized Schirmer test score \ge 2 and &lt;10 mm at 5 min at both Screening and Baseline visits
- ODS- visual analog scale (VAS) and global SANDE \ge 50 at both Screening and Baseline
- Increased signs and symptoms of DED based on CAE exposure at Screening
L118: Subjects were prohibited from wearing contact lenses, from using lid hygiene or lid heating therapies, and from using concomitant artificial tears, topical ocular anti-inflammatories, steroids, or other ophthalmic medications for DED. Short term use of some medications was allowed within a specified period relative to study visits. No rescue medication was provided or allowed in any study. Overall, any use of medications likely to bias the study results was minimized.
L119: Eligible subjects were randomized at baseline to administer acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% or vehicle as one drop BID OU in the morning and evening. Treatment compliance was measured by count of used and unused vials with subjects deemed noncompliant entered as protocol deviations.
L120: Design differences between Phase 2b and pivotal Phase 3 studies included additional exposure to CAE on Days 1, 28, and 84; omission of Day 7 and Day 60 (dispense only) study visits; and a total study duration of 84 days (instead of 90 days) for the Phase 2b study. Both Phase 3 studies had identical design and visit schedules.
L121: ## **Study Endpoints**
L122: Efficacy measures and endpoint differences between the Phase 2b and both pivotal Phase 3 studies were considered substantive and impacted the overall pooling strategy for primary efficacy evaluation based on tear production. The anesthetized Schirmer test was used in the Phase 2b study, whereas the pivotal Phase 3 studies utilized the unanesthetized Schirmer test. This difference alone was substantive, as anesthetic was shown to adversely impact accurate quantification of tear production following acoltremon ophthalmic solution administration (21-110-A). In addition, the postdrop unanesthetized Schirmer test used in the Phase 2b study was conducted 1 minute following drop instillation, as compared to 3 minutes following drop instillation in the pivotal Phase 3 studies. Lastly, different primary endpoints and timepoints were used between the Phase 2b and pivotal Phase 3 studies (Table 6).
L123: DED symptoms based on the global SANDE scores were assessed in the Phase 2b and pivotal Phase 3 studies, however, the pooling strategy (only Phase 3 studies pooled) was done at the study level for consistency of the strategy across all endpoints.

L148: ## 6.2.1.3. Statistical Analysis Plan, Study COMET-2
L149: Two studies, COMET-2 and COMET-3, were submitted in support of the treatment of dry eye disease. These studies had the same study design and efficacy endpoints and analyses.
L150: The primary null and alternative hypotheses for the comparison of acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% to vehicle with respect to the primary efficacy endpoint is:
L151: - Hypothesis Testing: H0: pT - pV = 0, H1: pT - pV \neq 0. Where pT is the proportion of subjects with \ge 10 mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14=0 and whose eyes treated with acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% and pV is the proportion of subjects with \ge 10 mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14=0 and whose eyes treated with vehicle control.
- Type I error Control. The study-wise Type I Error rate is controlled at a two-sided significance level of 5% for testing the superiority of acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% to vehicle in the primary and secondary efficacy endpoints.
- Per the protocol, superiority in the secondary efficacy endpoints was tested hierarchically if superiority in the primary efficacy endpoint was established.
L152: - Analysis Populations: The analysis populations were defined as follows:Intent-to-treat (ITT) population includes all randomized subjects. The primary efficacy analysis was performed on the ITT population.
- Per-protocol (PP) population includes all randomized subjects who do not have a significant protocol deviation likely to seriously affect the primary outcome of the study and who complete the study through Day 90.
- Safety population includes all randomized subjects who received at least one dose of the investigational product.
- Analysis of the primary endpoint: The comparison between acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% and vehicle in the ITT population. Testing of the difference in proportion of subjects with \ge 10 mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14 used Pearson's Chi-square test. The differences in proportions along with corresponding two-sided 95% CIs and p-values from Pearson's Chi-square test were presented. Multiple imputations assuming missing not at random, using vehicle treatment group regardless of randomized treatment group, was used to impute missing due to withdrawal due to lack of efficacy, adverse events (AEs), or disallowed concurrent treatment. For missing data from other reasons listed above, multiple imputations using randomized treatment were used assuming missing at random.
- Supportive and sensitivity analysis of the primary endpoint: A logistic regression analysis with fixed effects of treatment, predrop measure at baseline, and treatment center was conducted and the adjusted odds ratios, along with corresponding two-sided 95% CIs and p-values, were presented.
- Analysis of secondary endpoints: Recall that the key secondary endpoint in both studies was change from baseline in global SANDE score on Day 28. Using the ITT population, the key secondary endpoint was analyzed using an analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) model with terms for baseline value, treatment, and analysis center. LS means for each treatment group and for the difference between treatment groups were presented from the model, together with two-sided p-values and 95% CIs.
- Exploratory endpoints: The exploratory efficacy endpoints were analyzed using the ITT population with available data only. Quantitative exploratory efficacy variables were summarized using continuous descriptive statistics and analyzed using ANCOVA at each scheduled visit for change from baseline. Least squares means for each treatment group and for the difference between treatment groups were presented from the model, together with two-sided p-values and 95% CIs. Two-sample t-tests and Wilcoxon rank sum tests were also presented where appropriate as sensitivity analyses. Dichotomous exploratory efficacy variables were summarized descriptively (frequency and percentage) and analyzed at each measured visit similarly to the primary endpoint of proportion of subjects with \ge 10-mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14.

L227: ## 6.2.2.3. Statistical Analysis Plan, Study COMET-3
L228: Two studies, COMET-2 and COMET-3, were submitted in support of the treatment of dry eye disease. These studies had the same study design and efficacy endpoints and analyses.
L229: The primary null and alternative hypotheses for the comparison of acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% to vehicle with respect to the primary efficacy endpoint are:
L230: - Hypothesis testing: H_0: pT - pV = 0, H_1: pT - pV \neq 0. Where pT is the proportion of subjects with \ge 10-mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14=0 and whose eyes were treated with acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003%, and pV is the proportion of subjects with \ge 10-mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14=0 and whose eyes were treated with vehicle control.
- Type I error control. The study-wise Type I error rate is controlled at a two-sided significance level of 5% for testing the superiority of acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% to vehicle in the primary and secondary efficacy endpoints.
- Per the protocol, superiority in the secondary efficacy endpoints was tested hierarchically if superiority in the primary efficacy endpoint was established.
- Analysis populations: The analysis populations were defined as follows:ITT population includes all randomized subjects. The primary efficacy analysis was performed on the ITT population.
- PP population includes all randomized subjects who do not have a significant protocol deviation likely to seriously affect the primary outcome of the study and who complete the study through Day 90.
- Safety population includes all randomized subjects who received at least one dose of the investigational product.
- Analysis of the primary endpoint: The comparison between acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% and vehicle in the ITT population. Testing of the difference in proportion of subjects with \ge 10-mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14 used Pearson's Chi-square test. The differences in proportions, along with corresponding two-sided 95% CIs and p-values from Pearson's Chi-square test, were presented. Multiple imputations assuming missing not at random, using vehicle treatment group regardless of randomized treatment group, was used to impute missing due to withdrawal due to lack of efficacy, AEs, or disallowed concurrent treatment. For missing data from other reasons listed above, multiple imputations using randomized treatment is used assuming missing at random.
- Supportive and sensitivity analysis of the primary endpoint: A logistic regression analysis with fixed effects of treatment, predrop measure at baseline, and treatment center was conducted and the adjusted odds ratios along with corresponding two-sided 95% CIs and p-values were presented.
- Analysis of secondary endpoints: Recall that the key secondary endpoint in both studies was change from baseline in global SANDE score on Day 28. Using ITT population, the key secondary endpoint was analyzed using an ANCOVA model with terms for baseline value, treatment, and analysis center. LS mean for each treatment group and for the difference between treatment groups were presented from the model, together with two-sided p-values and 95% CIs.
L231: - Exploratory endpoints: The exploratory efficacy endpoints were analyzed using the ITT population with available data only. Quantitative exploratory efficacy variables were summarized using continuous descriptive statistics and analyzed using ANCOVA at each scheduled visit for change from baseline. Least-squares means for each treatment group and for the difference between treatment groups were presented from the model, together with two-sided p-values and 95% CIs. Two-sample t-tests and Wilcoxon rank sum tests were also presented where appropriate as sensitivity analyses. Dichotomous exploratory efficacy variables were summarized descriptively (frequency and percentage) and analyzed at each measured visit, similarly to the primary endpoint of proportion of subjects with \ge 10 mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14.

L295: ## 6.3. Key Efficacy Review Issues
L296: ## 6.3.1. Uncertainty Regarding the Duration of the Treatment Effect
L297: ## Issue
L298: The duration of the treatment effect was not assessed.
L299: ## Background
L300: The primary endpoint was met in two randomized, double-masked, well-controlled studies. The FDA has previously accepted drugs for the treatment of signs and symptoms of dry eye disease with two replicated studies that succeeded in demonstrating a statistically significant difference between the percentage of subjects achieving a 10-mm increase or more in Schirmer scores, without requiring a demonstration of symptom relief. Because this magnitude of increased tear production has been associated with a reduction in symptoms, the Division has accepted this primary efficacy endpoint without a corresponding symptom endpoint to support a demonstration of safety and efficacy.
L301: ## Assessment
L302: The postdrop Schirmer tests were all initiated at 2 minutes and 45 seconds postdrop for the first eye and 3 minutes postdrop for the second eye. The Schirmer test strips were removed 5 minutes later, then evaluated and compared to baseline predrop Schirmer. See the study instructions for predrop and postdrop unanesthetized Schirmer test below.
L303: Figure 3. Study Instructions for Predrop and Postdrop Unanesthetized Schirmer Test
L304: Source: COMET-3 Manual of Procedures, Version 1.0, Appendix 2
L305: Abbreviations: OD, right eye; OS, left eye
L306: The data obtained using this methodology provides an 8-minute sampling to assess the benefit in tear production in a drug seeking every 12 hours dosing.
L307: The only tear production measurements that were measured at an interval exceeding 8 minutes were the predrop unanesthetized Schirmer tear tests performed at every visit in the COMET-2, COMET-3, and COMET-4 studies. Predrop unanesthetized Schirmer tear tests when performed at each visit, in both Tryptyr and vehicle groups when compared to Day 1 predrop unanesthetized Schirmer tear tests demonstrated &lt;1 mm mean change from baseline in every study. These post Day 1 predrop Schirmer tests do tell us that any increase in tear production that had been present after taking the second drop on the previous day was no longer inducing increased tear production at the time of the testing visit. We do not know the time of the most recent drop prior to the testing visit so we don't have information to provide a degradation curve between the first 8 minutes of increased tear production and the unknown interval that led to the &lt;1-mm increase the following day.
L308: At each visit, the postdrop Schirmer tear test showed a higher proportion of Tryptyr subjects than vehicle subjects with a \ge10-mm mean increase postdrop on day of visit versus predrop at baseline. This data demonstrates that Tryptyr provides a convincing repeated increase in tear production after each drop in a 90-day period, with the effect of each drop lasting at least 8 minutes. The window of time between 8 minutes and 12 hours postdrop is not assessed.
L309: The FDA has previously accepted drugs for the treatment of signs and symptoms of dry eye disease with two replicated studies that succeeded in demonstrating a statistically significant difference between the percentage of subjects achieving a 10-mm increase or more in Schirmer scores, without requiring a demonstration of symptom relief.
L310: This study demonstrated a lack of correlation between meeting the primary endpoint and meaningful benefit to the patient. The lack of statistical evidence of reduction of symptoms from baseline on all but one day of one study while the primary Schirmer test endpoint was replicated consistently, casts doubt upon the clinical relevance of the Schirmer test as performed in this study. The study provides little or no evidence that Trypyr, dosed twice daily, improves the way dry eye patients feel or function.
L311: The statistical reviewer noted that for DED signs, an evaluation of change in Schirmer score from predrop at baseline to pretreatment instillation at each visit, did not show a statistically significant difference at any of the 5 visits in both COMET-2 and COMET-3 studies. This may suggest that the twice daily dosing regimen of acoltremon is not sufficient to maintain the treatment effect. Evidence is sufficient to recommend approval of Trypyr
L312: (b) (4)
L313: ## Conclusion
L314: The treatment group effect in the proportion of subjects who achieve a \ge 10-mm increase in Schirmer test score on Day 14 is considered a clinically significant endpoint without a corresponding symptom endpoint.
L315: ## 6.3.2. Clinical Significance of Corresponding Symptom Relief
L316: ## Issue
L317: Is evidence of symptom relief clinically significant?
L318: ## Background
L319: A statistically significant treatment group difference in the change from baseline in SANDE symptom score was only demonstrated in COMET-2. The Phase 2 study, COMET-1, failed its primary endpoint; thus, the secondary endpoints including change from baseline in SANDE score cannot be evaluated. In COMET-3, the change from baseline in SANDE score at Day 28 was not statistically significant. Clinically significant symptom relief was not demonstrated in clinical studies.
L320: ## Assessment
L321: Evidence of symptom relief represented by the global SANDE score was not demonstrated to be statistically superior to vehicle in relief of symptoms in replicated studies.
L322: ## Conclusion
L323: A separate demonstration of safety with a symptom endpoint was not demonstrated in replicated studies. However, the sign endpoint, the proportion of subjects who achieved a \ge 10-mm increase in Schirmer test score, is considered a clinically significant endpoint without a corresponding symptom endpoint.

L337: ## 7.4. FDA Approach to the Safety Review
L338: The clinical safety review focused on the safety database from COMET-2, COMET-3, and COMET-4. The COMET-1 study evaluated two different concentrations of acoltremon. The other studies all evaluated 0.003% acoltremon compared to placebo. These studies were reviewed to assess the safety of acoltremon ophthalmic solution.
L339: No major data quality or integrity issues were identified that would preclude performing a safety review for this NDA. There were no major identified issues with respect to recording, coding, and categorizing AEs. The Applicant's translations of verbatim terms to the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (Version 25.0) preferred terms for the events reported in COMET-2, COMET-3, and COMET-4 were reviewed and found to be acceptable. Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were protocol-defined as any event that occurred or worsened on or after the day that randomized study intervention was initiated. Percentages are based on the total number of subjects in the respective treatment for the population being analyzed.
L340: For the presentation of the overall summary of TEAEs, TEAEs related to study intervention included possibly related or related TEAEs. TEAEs were also classified by strongest relationship to study intervention (not related, unlikely related, possibly related, or related). The relationship of each AE was determined by the investigator. TEAEs were also classified by maximum severity (mild, moderate, or severe). Severity was defined as a qualitative assessment of the degree of intensity of an AE as determined by the investigator or reported to them by the subject.
L341: In addition, burning or stinging TEAEs that occurred upon study intervention instillation were identified by preferred term (PT) and/or reported AETERM (i.e., PT was "Instillation site pain" and AETERM contained "burning" or "stinging"). The number of subjects with burning or stinging upon study intervention instillation TEAEs, burning upon study intervention instillation TEAEs, and stinging upon study intervention instillation TEAEs were summarized separately in the same table using counts and percentages by maximum severity for each treatment group. The subjects who had a TEAE reporting both burning and stinging symptoms were counted once toward burning upon study intervention instillation TEAEs and once toward stinging upon study intervention instillation TEAEs.
L342: ## 7.5. Adequacy of the Clinical Safety Database
L343: Data contributing to the overall safety evaluation of 0.003% acoltremon were collected within the Phase 2b and all Phase 3 clinical studies. The clinical safety database from the submitted studies sufficiently represented the population expected to use acoltremon ophthalmic solution, 0.003% so that its safety could be assessed.
L344: Safety assessments included AEs, corrected visual acuity (CVA), slit lamp biomicroscopy parameters, dilated fundus examination, and intraocular pressure (IOP) for all studies. The two pivotal Phase 3 efficacy studies and the long-term safety study (LTSS) also evaluated subjects' vital signs and clinical labs for safety (hematology, chemistry, and urinalysis). In addition, the Phase 3 LTSS included assessments for best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), endothelial cell count (ECC), and total ocular staining.
L345: Integrated analysis of all safety parameters among studies was not supported due to differences in exposure to the CAE and timing of safety assessments in relation to study drug dosing. Specifically, IOP and dilated fundus examinations were conducted post-CAE at Screening (Baseline) and at Day 84/Exit in COMET-1 and were not collected on Day 1. This differed from the Phase 3 studies, which conducted IOP and dilated fundus assessments post-CAE (pivotal Phase 3 studies) or without CAE exposure (LTSS) at Screening (Baseline), and at follow-up visits without CAE exposure. Furthermore, while CVA and slit lamp examination were
L346: conducted prior to CAE exposure in COMET-1, the follow-up timepoints differed, with no Day 7 evaluation within the study. Therefore, to accurately reflect the safety profile of 0.003% acoltremon, integration of ocular safety assessments was conducted according to the pooling strategy outlined in Module 2.7.4, Section 2.1.2.

L535: ## Pharmacology
L536: The published literature establishes that acoltremon (as WS-12) is a TRPM8 agonist like menthol. TRPM8, a calcium-permeable cation channel, is a cold sensor in humans. The activation of TRPM8 opens the channel and allows influx of calcium ions into the cell, resulting in cold thermal transduction and pain detection, among other effects (Izquierdo et al. 2021). The Applicant verified this activity in vitro (using cells transfected with rat TRPM8) and ex vivo
L537: NDA 217370
L538: Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution) 0.003%
L539: (using guinea pig corneas). In vivo, topical ocular acoltremon increased blinking immediately after administration.
L540: - Acoltremon activity compared to menthol was studied in vitro on rat TRPM8-transfected HEK cells. In this assay, acoltremon activated TRPM8 (increasing cytosolic calcium influx) with an EC_{50} of 0.9 to 26.0\muM (measured by microfluorometry) and 13.5 to 20.8\muM (measured by patch clamp electrophysiology).**Reviewer's comment:** *The large range of EC_{50}s obtained in this assay may reflect the lack of solubility of the test compound in the cell medium.*
- Acoltremon toxicity was also studied in vitro on HEK cells. In this MTT assay to determine cell viability, no cytotoxicity was observed at 1\muM, whereas slight dose-dependent responses occurred at 10\muM (-4.0% cell viability) and 100\muM (-10.1% cell viability).
- Acoltremon activated the corneal cold thermoreceptor (TRPM8) nerve endings ex vivo in the Guinea pig with an EC_{50} of 47.7\muM (measured by nerve terminal impulse [NTI] activity in response to exposure to cold).
- A single topical ocular dose of acoltremon (10 \muL/eye/OU at 0, 10, 50, or 200\muM acoltremon into the lower conjunctival sac) increased blink rate in guinea pigs (measured over 5 minutes) and in parallel, a slight but not statistically significant increase in tear rate was measured compared to vehicle, with an EC_{50} of 9.1\muM.
L541: The literature indicated that acoltremon is a specific agonist of TRPM8 without activity on other transient receptor potential (TRP) channels such as TRPV1, TRPV2, TRPV3, TRPV4 and TRPA1 (Sherkheli et al. 2008).
L542: There was no evidence of test-article-related adverse effects on cardiovascular and central nervous system functions, when evaluated during the good laboratory practice (GLP) toxicity studies in the dog and the rat.
L543: **Reviewer's comment:** *Taking into account the most conservative EC_{50} of approximately 1\muM (289 pg/mL) (range 1 to 50\muM) observed in pharmacology studies in vitro, and at least 90% protein binding in all species tested, and in the absence of observable systemic toxicity in all GLP ocular studies in the rabbit and the dog, this reviewer judged that the systemic exposure to the test article (free maximum plasma concentration [C_{max}]) can be considered negligible and hence, neither on- nor off-target activity of the test article could be expected other than locally in the ocular tissues.*

L820: Study COMET-2 was a Phase 3, multicenter, vehicle-controlled, double-masked, randomized study conducted at 23 sites in the United States. Efficacy was assessed at the Baseline visit and on Day 7, Day 14, Day 28, and Day 90. At the end of the Day 90 visit, subjects exited the study. Safety assessments were conducted at each study visit.

L891: ## **Planned Statistical Analysis Methods**
L892: ## **Sample Size Determination**
L893: Two hundred and two subjects per treatment group yields 99% power to conclude superiority of 0.003% acoltremon over vehicle in the proportion of subjects with \ge 10-mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14, assuming a true difference of proportions (acoltremon versus vehicle) of 80% versus 30% and a two-sided \alpha=0.05.
L894: Additionally, 202 subjects per treatment group yields 99% power to conclude superiority of 0.003% acoltremon over vehicle in the mean change from baseline in SANDE score on Day 28 assuming a true difference (acoltremon minus vehicle) of -7.9, a common standard deviation of 17.72, and a two-sided \alpha=0.05.
L895: Accounting for subject discontinuations, approximately 460 total subjects (230 per treatment arm) were planned to be randomized assuming a dropout rate of 10%.
L896: ## **Analysis of Primary Endpoint**
L897: The primary comparison in this study was between 0.003% acoltremon and vehicle in the ITT population using Estimand 1.
L898: Analysis of primary efficacy was based on the difference of proportions using Pearson's Chi-square test. Available data only were used if the discontinuation rate was &lt;5\% in each arm; otherwise, an estimand based on multiple imputations was used for the primary analysis. Inference across multiple imputed datasets was obtained by pooling Chi-square distributed statistics as proposed by Ratitch (2013), Rubin (1987), and Li (1991) (Rubin D.B. 1987; Li 1991; Ratitch et al. 2013).
L899: The primary endpoint was tested as follows:
L900: - H_0: The difference between study eyes treated with 0.003% acoltremon and study eyes treated with vehicle, in the proportion of subjects with \ge 10-mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14=0.
- H_1: The difference between study eyes treated with 0.003% acoltremon and study eyes treated with vehicle, in the proportion of subjects with \ge 10-mm increase in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14 \neq 0.
L901: The primary estimand (Estimand 1) was based on the ITT population targeting the treatment effect attributable to the randomly assigned treatment, regardless of compliance to study treatment and assuming subjects who discontinued due to lack of efficacy, AEs, or disallowed concurrent treatments followed the behavior of subjects in the reference arm for the duration of the study and subjects who discontinued for other reasons (outside of lack of efficacy, AEs, or use of disallowed treatment) would follow the behavior of subjects who were still in the study under same randomly assigned treatment. For further details on Estimands 1, 2, and 3 refer to Statistical Analysis Plan.

L903: ## Analysis of Secondary Efficacy Endpoints
L904: The secondary efficacy endpoints were analyzed using the ITT population and repeated using the PP population.
L905: Analysis of secondary efficacy was conducted as described for the primary efficacy endpoints, using the difference of proportions tests, or ANCOVA for difference of change from baseline means. Available data only was used if the discontinuation rate was &lt;5% in each arm and multiple imputation otherwise. For details on Estimands 1, 2, and 3 as applicable to secondary efficacy endpoints refer to Section 9.7.1.6.2.
L906: Two-sample t-tests comparing treatment groups were performed as sensitivity analyses. Wilcoxon rank sum tests were also performed as sensitivity analyses. The secondary dichotomous efficacy endpoints were analyzed similarly to the primary endpoint.
L907: To ensure Type 1 error rate was controlled at the 5% significance level (2-sided) over all set of study hypotheses, a fixed-sequence hierarchical strategy was employed, requiring all preceding endpoints in the testing hierarchy to be statistically significant at the 5% level before an endpoint could be evaluated for statistical significance. For example, the primary endpoint must have shown statistical significance at the 5% significance level before the first (key) secondary endpoint could be evaluated for statistical significance and so on.

L951: ## **Protocols AR-15512-CS301 and AR-15512-CS302**
L952: These protocols were very similar in design with both studies being designed to assess the safety, efficacy, and tolerability of Tryptyr (acoltremon) in the treatment of signs and symptoms of dry eye disease over a 15-week study period.
L953: Both studies shared the same efficacy objectives, treatment schedules, and subject qualification criteria. L954: The study consisted of six clinic visits over a period of 15 weeks. L955: The primary efficacy endpoint of the study was the increase (postdrop on Day 14 versus predrop at baseline) in unanesthetized Schirmer score on Day 14.
L956: Each protocol was conducted at 23 sites in the United States.
