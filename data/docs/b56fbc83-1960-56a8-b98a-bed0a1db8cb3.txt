L51: ## 2.0 DISCUSSION
L52: - FDA is concerned with the anaphylaxis safety signal and would like to propose a post-marketing requirement (PMR) to the Sponsor.The Sponsor's response will most likely effect labeling.FDA noted that this safety signal has also been identified with other enzyme replacement therapies. A black box warning was added to the labeling for such products.
- FDA noted that the goal date is approaching (~ November 2017), in which they are leaning towards an approval of the product. FDA concurs with and that robust data for efficacy is lacking. FDA acknowledged that there are limitations. However, the application does show modest efficacy with long-term exposure to the product. FDA is also using expanded access data and reviewing improvement in the 6 minute walk test to aid in their application decision.FDA provided a brief summary of the potential clinical post-marketing requirement/commitments (PMRs/PMCs) to be proposed to the Sponsor.PMR: 3-year immunogenicity/hypersensitivity reactions study20 participants (including 6 new participants less than 1 year of age)
- Collect information on genotype/phenotype
- Treatment to be provided. Collect safety and efficacy data
- PMC: 10-year registry/observational studyCollect safety and efficacy data at annual visits
- Collect clinical outcome information regarding liver &amp; spleen size, pulmonary function, motor function, and neurocognitive function.
L53: - FDA also noted that the Sponsor recently submitted an amendment to protocol 401, which is intended to serve as the registry/observational study. FDA is looking to include additional endpoints

L162: ## **FDA Response:**
L163: We are concerned that the data that you are proposing at this time to include in a BLA may not be sufficient to adequately assess the safety and efficacy of UX003 at the currently proposed dose (4.0mg/kg/QOW). While we recognize the rarity of MPS 7 and the challenges associated with a clinical program for this rare and heterogeneous condition, after reviewing the briefing package, we have reservations as to whether you have provided enough evidence of effectiveness to allow for a meaningful review. The absence of rigorously collected natural history data to ensure a reliable comparator for efficacy analyses, combined with very heterogeneous clinical manifestations expected and observed in this small cohort, raise serious challenges in assessing and demonstrating a treatment effect to support definitive efficacy conclusions for your product. L229: ## Division of Medication Error Prevention and Analysis (DMEPA)
L230: You are proposing that the product will be available as an injection in a 10 mg/5 mL (2 mg/mL) vial. However, since the proposed dose is 4 mg/kg every other week, the pharmacist would need 28 vials to prepare a dose of 280 mg using the 10 mg/5 mL vials for a 70 kg patient. FDA is concerned with the large number of vials that will be needed to prepare a dose. This may lead to pharmacy confusion and increase the likelihood of pharmacy dispensing errors resulting in the wrong dose. Therefore, please clarify whether you intend to pursue an appropriate vial size and fill volume to accommodate this dosing regimen.
L231: ## Ultragenyx's Response:
L232: - *Ultragenyx acknowledges the FDA's concern and want to clarify that the 10mg/5 mL (2 mg/mL) vial is the intended commercial presentation based on available clinical development and CMC data to support the BLA submission. An advantage with the smaller vial size is more precise dosing for a variety of patients of different sizes with limited product waste. A similar situation of multiple vial doses exists for other MPS ERTs. Most infusion sites are familiar with MPS ERT therapies and are comfortable with handling multiple vials.*
- *For the approved ERTs (Aldurazyme®, Elaprase®, Vimizim® and Naglazyme®) for treatment of MPS I, II, IVA, VI diseases, respectively, a presentation of 5 mL extractable volume (or less in the case of Elaprase®) has been developed and approved by Regulatory Authorities worldwide. This 5 ml (or less) presentation has been used successfully in the commercial setting for over a decade, with no issues noted in the public literature regarding the size of vial presentation. As such, Ultragenyx does not expect any issues with the current vial presentation given the public knowledge learned from the ERTs for the other 4 MPS disorders.*
- *To date, out of 453 UX003 infusions in total, including 345 infusions at 4mg/kg dose, there has been no pharmacy dispensing errors due to confusion resulting from handling of large vial numbers.*
- *Upon BLA approval, UX003 will be available through prescription only via Specialty Pharmacy, who will be responsible for ensuring correct number of vials based on current patient weight is delivered to the infusion site on a per-infusion order basis. There would not be a need to store the vials at the infusion site.*
- *MPS VII is an ultra-rare disease, with only 19 living patients identified in the US currently. Based on current data and commercial distribution consideration upon approval, Ultragenyx considers the 10 mg/5 mL vial is a suitable presentation for commercial patient use to cover all patients with accurate dosing and minimal waste,*
L233: *including infants and young children. We agree this makes dose preparation a challenging process for the full size adult, but overall this is the optimal vial size.*
