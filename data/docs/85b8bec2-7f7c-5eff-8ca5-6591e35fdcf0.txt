L1: Study assessments and procedures were similar among the phase 2 and phase 3 studies. The phase 3 studies had a 2 to 4 week screening/baseline period. Study visits were QW during the core treatment period in all studies except BA16738 (in patients not on dialysis). In BA16738, patients visited the study clinic Q2W during the anemia correction and evaluation periods and Q4W during the extension period. Samples for determining hemoglobin and hematocrit levels were collected regularly in all studies:
L2: - QW in BA16736 and BA17284
- Q2W (anemia correction period) and Q4W (extension period) in BA16738
- QW (screening through evaluation periods) in BA16739, BA16740, and BA17283
- Q2W (long term safety observation period) in BA16739, BA16740, and BA17283
L3: Concomitant medication information was updated as needed, and adverse event information was collected during the treatment period in all phase 3 studies. Electrocardiograms were performed, and samples for anti-erythropoietin antibody tests, safety laboratory tests, and iron parameters were collected at scheduled intervals throughout all of the studies. In addition, blood pressure and heart rate were assessed at every visit during the core treatment period in all phase 3 studies.
L4: ## Data Analysis
L5: ## *Analysis Populations*
L6: General rules for inclusion in or exclusion from an efficacy analysis population were similar among the phase 3 studies. For the phase 3 studies, efficacy was analyzed primarily in the intent-to-treat (ITT), eligible, and per-protocol (PP) populations. The ITT population was defined for all studies as all patients randomized. The eligible and PP populations are described further below:
L7: ## Eligible Population
L8: In anemia correction studies, the eligible population was defined as the subset of the ITT population after exclusion based on the following study-specific protocol violations at baseline (exclusion criteria):
L9: - BA16736Hemoglobinopathies or hemolysis
- RBC transfusions or blood loss requiring treatment prior to randomization
- BA16738Hemoglobinopathies, hemolysis, or RBC transfusions within 8 weeks before screening or during the screening period
- Immunosuppressive therapy within 12 weeks of screening, except cyclosporine, mono/polyclonal antibodies, and chronic corticosteroids
L10: In hemoglobin maintenance studies (BA16739, BA16740, BA17283 and BA17284), the definition for eligible population differed from the definition in anemia correction studies in that patients who did not receive at least one dose of the study medication after randomization was also excluded, in addition to exclusion based on the following study-specific protocol violations at baseline (exclusion criteria):
L11: - Lack of a stable baseline hemoglobin (absolute difference &lt; 1.0 g/dL between mean hemoglobin at weeks -4 and -3 and values at weeks -2 and -1); hemoglobinopathies, hemolysis, or blood transfusion at baseline.
- Inadequate iron stores (mean serum ferritin &lt; 100 \mug/L and mean TSAT &lt; 20\%, or mean hypochromic RBC &gt; 10\%) at baseline (weeks -4 and -3)
L12: ## PP Population
L13: In both the anemia correction and the hemoglobin studies, the PP population was defined as a subset of the eligible population after excluding for the following pre-specified protocol violations:
L14: - BA16736 (Anemia Correction)Patients with RBC transfusions (not related to lack of effect of study medication) or blood loss requiring treatment prior to randomization or within weeks 1-24, before achieving hemoglobin response
- Patients who fulfilled the following exclusion criteria before the first dose: hemoglobinopathies or hemolysis
- Patients who had less than 75% of the scheduled post-baseline hemoglobin measurements during the first 24 weeks (through day 173)
- Patients who received an incorrect dose or missed an administration on two consecutive applications or more than three times before day 173 (24 weeks of treatment)
- BA16738 (Anemia Correction)RBC transfusions (not related to lack of effect of study medication) or blood loss (bleeding episode or surgery) within 8 weeks before randomization or within weeks 1-28
- Hemoglobinopathies or hemolysis, administration of immunosuppressive therapy other than cyclosporine, mono/polyclonal antibodies, or chronic corticosteroids
- Having fewer than 75% of the scheduled post-baseline hemoglobin measurements during the first 28 weeks, or fewer than four during evaluation
- Incorrect or missing dose on two consecutive occasions, or more than three times before week 28; receiving an erythropoietin product not specified in the protocol during 2 consecutive weeks between week 1 and week 28
- Erythropoietin therapy within 12 weeks prior to screening or dialysis therapy between week 18 and week 28
- BA16739, BA16740, BA17283, BA17284 (Hemoglobin Maintenance)Lack of a stable baseline hemoglobin (absolute difference &lt; 1.0 g/dL between mean hemoglobin at weeks -4 and -3 and values at weeks -2 and -1)
- Inadequate iron stores at baseline (weeks -4 and -3) or at evaluation (mean serum ferritin &lt; 100 \mug/L AND mean TSAT &lt; 20\%, or mean hypochromic RBC &gt; 10\%)
- RBC transfusion (not related to lack of effect of study medication) during baseline or within weeks 21 to 33.
L15: - Patients receiving RBC transfusions or undergoing major surgery (including transplantation) after week 33 were included in the PP population.
- In these patients, hemoglobin values after the event were censored and: the last observed value was carried forward in BA17283 and BA16740, or regarded as missing in BA16739 and BA17284.
- Hemoglobinopathies or hemolysis at baseline; fewer than five recorded hemoglobin values during evaluation; missing a dose during weeks 26 to 35.
L16: ## Efficacy Analyses in Phase 3 Correction Studies
L17: ## *Primary Efficacy*
L18: Summaries and plots of hemoglobin over time or hemoglobin change from baseline over time were produced using monthly time points. The analyses were limited to the end of the anemia correction period (BA16736) and end of the evaluation period (BA16738) so that only complete data was used.
L19: - The primary efficacy parameter in the phase 3 anemia correction studies (BA16736 and BA16738) was the hemoglobin response rate. Response was defined as an increase in hemoglobin of at least 1.0 g/dL compared to baseline and a hemoglobin of at least 11.0 g/dL without RBC transfusion during the anemia correction period (first 24 weeks after first dose) of BA16736, and during the anemia correction and evaluation periods (first 28 weeks after first dose) of BA16738.
- The average baseline value was estimated using all values recorded between the day of first study dose and the previous 20 days (protocol requirement was 2 values taken at least 1 day apart). The value on the day of first dose was included in the baseline calculation as this assessment was performed before the first dose was given.
- The observed response rate during the anemia correction or anemia correction and evaluation study period was shown and presented graphically. The confidence intervals for the response rate were calculated using the Clopper-Pearson method.
- BA16738 had a second primary efficacy parameter: the difference in the mean change in hemoglobin between the baseline and evaluation periods. The evaluation period was week 20 up to and including week 28 (after the 18-week anemia correction period). The average hemoglobin for each individual during this evaluation period was estimated by AUC.
L20: ## *Secondary Efficacy*
L21: Secondary efficacy analyses in the anemia correction studies included hemoglobin values and their change from baseline over time and incidence of RBC transfusion. The anemia correction studies also assessed time to hemoglobin response (via Kaplan-Meier methods). Censoring occurred on the day of RBC transfusion or at the time of last hemoglobin determination for patients who withdrew early from the study or who reached the end of the anemia correction period without achieving the criteria for response.
L22: ## Efficacy Analyses in Phase 3 Maintenance Studies
L23: ## Primary Efficacy
L24: Summaries and plots of hemoglobin over time and hemoglobin change from baseline over time were produced using monthly time points. In addition, outputs of the number and percentage of patients with mean hemoglobin during the evaluation period within, below, or above target range were created.
L25: - The primary efficacy variable in the hemoglobin maintenance studies was the change in hemoglobin between baseline and the evaluation period.
- The baseline period was defined as all assessments between the day of first study dose and the previous 30 days. The hemoglobin value on the day of first dose was included in the baseline calculation, as this assessment was performed before the first dose is given. The evaluation period was between 29 and 36 weeks of treatment (after a 28-week titration period).
- The average hemoglobin for each individual during the baseline and the evaluation periods was calculated using the AUC method. Subtracting the individual baseline value from the evaluation period value gave the final endpoint.
L26: ## Secondary Efficacy
L27: Standard summary tables using means, standard deviations, percentiles, minima and maxima were produced by visit for:
L28: - Number of patients maintaining their average hemoglobin during the evaluation period within \pm 1g/dL of their average baseline concentration,
- Incidence of RBC transfusions during the dose titration and evaluation periods.
L29: ## Missing Data
L30: Efficacy data missing at the end of the evaluation period (i.e., the last measured hemoglobin value was before week 36, or possibly before start of the evaluation period for the ITT and eligible population analyses) were handled using the last observation carried forward (LOCF) method. It was assumed that missing hemoglobin assessments would generally follow one or several hemoglobin values outside the target range, since patients may have been withdrawn if hemoglobins were not maintained. Carrying forward hemoglobin values outside the target range can therefore be seen as a conservative approach. Values missing between recorded assessments were automatically interpolated by the trapezoidal rule. If a blood transfusion occurred during the titration or evaluation period, the hemoglobin values measured in the following 3 weeks were replaced by the hemoglobin value measured immediately before the transfusion to correct for the increase caused by the transfusion.
L31: ## Test of Non-Inferiority
L32: Comparisons were made between Mircera Q2W vs reference medication and, when applicable, Mircera Q4W vs reference medication.
L33: The test of the primary endpoint (hemoglobin change from baseline) was based on an ANCOVA model used to compare each Mircera group to the reference group. The independent variable in the model was treatment group; hemoglobin at baseline was a covariate in all studies. Additional covariates (e.g., geographical region and route of administration) were added as appropriate.
L34: Two-sided confidence intervals for the difference between treatments in mean change in hemoglobin between the baseline and evaluation periods were calculated using the estimates from this model. A 95% confidence level was used for BA16738, BA17283 and BA17284. The confidence level of 97.5% was used for BA16739 and BA16740, in order to adjust for multiplicity resulting from the independent comparisons of two Mircera groups with one reference group.
L35: In each case, the Mircera treatment was regarded as non-inferior to the reference group if the lower limit of the CI was greater than -0.75 \text{ g/dL}. A p-value for the non-inferiority test was derived via the t-test.
L36: ## Subgroup Analyses
L37: The summaries of hemoglobin change from baseline, average hemoglobin over time, and responders were also produced for the following subgroups of patients in the phase 3 studies:
L38: - Dialysis type (hemodialysis, peritoneal dialysis, not on dialysis),
- Medication administration type (IV, SC),
- Previous erythropoietin (epoetin alfa, epoetin beta, darbepoetin alfa),
- Age (&lt;65, 65 - 74, = 75),
- Weight (&lt;65, 65 - 79, = 80),
- Gender (male, female),
- Race (Black, Caucasian, Oriental, other),
- Ethnicity (Hispanic, non-Hispanic),
- Geographic region (US, non-US),
- Etiology of CRF (diabetes, glomerulonephritis, hypertension),
- Diabetes status (diabetic, non-diabetic),
- Iron status (iron deficient, iron replete).
L39: ## 6.1.4 Efficacy Findings
L40: ## Phase 3 Correction Studies
L41: The two active-controlled, randomized, anemia correction studies (IV, vs epoetin in BA16736; SC, vs darbepoetin alfa in BA16738) were conducted open-label at multiple sites worldwide in patients not previously treated with an erythropoietin product.
L42: ## *Subject Exclusion for Elevated CRP*
L43: Subject selection criteria in all studies (phase 2 and phase 3) specified exclusion for an elevated C-reactive protein (CRP) level. In 5 of the 6 phase 3 studies (BA16736, BA16739, BA16740, BA17283, and BA17284), patients were excluded for \text{CRP} &gt; 30 \text{ mg/L}; in the remaining phase 3 study (BA16738), patients were excluded for \text{CRP} &gt; 15 \text{ mg/L}.

L50: ## Review Comments:
L51: Active inflammation (including infection), with or without a documented elevation in the CRP level, is an important subject exclusion criterion since active inflammation may increase the risk of serious treatment-related adverse events.
L52: - The number of patients excluded from participation in a phase 3 study was not a significant fraction of those screened (3.3%). It appears unnecessary to specify in the product label for Mircera that a CRP level should be obtained to determine treatment eligibility.

L108: ## *Primary Efficacy Results*

In addition, response rates in the MirCera groups were comparable to those in the reference groups: 91% in BA16736 and 96% in BA16738. In all instances, the lower level of the CI interval was well above 60% with statistically significant p &lt; 0.0001 (Tables 18 and 9).

L160: ## *Summary of Efficacy Results and Dosing Conclusions: Anemia Correction*
L161: - Study BA16736 was in patients with CRF receiving dialysis treatment. The starting dose of MirCera was 0.4 ug/kg administered IV every 2 weeks.The response rate in the MirCera group at the end of the anemia correction period was 93% (p&lt;0.0001; 95% CI 87.7-96.9); and the lower limit of the CI was well above 60%, confirming that MirCera resulted in the anemia correction of anemia.
- The time to response data indicated that the anemia correction of anemia was slower in the MirCera group compared with the epoetin group (median time to response was 57 days in the MirCera group and 31 days in the epoetin group).
- Majority of patients in both treatment arms had dose adjustments (90% and 98% of patients in the MirCera and epoetin groups, respectively). At the time of hemoglobin response, with dose adjustments, the median dose of MirCera was 0.6 ug/kg/2-wk and the median dose in the epoetin group was similar to the median starting dose.
- These results indicate that the dosing regimen (MirCera 0.4 ug/kg/2-wk) was effective as tested, but dose optimization could be considered. Subgroup analysis by age, weight, gender,
L162: race, ethnicity, geographic region, CRF etiology, and diabetes revealed no significant difference in hemoglobin response.
L163: - Study BA16738 was in patients with CRF who did not require dialysis treatment. The starting dose of MirCera was 0.6 ug/kg administered SC every 2 weeks.The response rate in the MirCera group during the anemia correction and evaluation periods was similar to that seen in BA16736, 98% (p&lt;0.0001; 95% CI 93.8-99.3%) with the lower limit of the CI well above 60%, confirming that MirCera resulted in anemia correction of anemia.
- An ANCOVA showed that the adjusted mean change from baseline hemoglobin in the MirCera group (2.18 g/dL) was not inferior to that of the darbepoetin alfa reference group (1.95 g/dL), in the ITT population. The results were consistent in the PP, ITT, and eligible populations (p&lt;0.0001).
- Time to response and hemoglobin over time data indicated that the anemia correction of anemia was slower in the MirCera group compared with the darbepoetin alfa group. The median time to response was 43 days (approximately 6 weeks) in the MirCera group compared with 29 days (approximately 4 weeks) in the darbepoetin alfa group.
- The rate of hemoglobin increase observed in the MirCera group appeared appropriate in this patient population, since a high response rate was achieved (98%) without an increased incidence of AE, serious adverse events (SAE), withdrawals due to AE, or RBC transfusions.
- Fewer patients in the MirCera group reached a maximum hemoglobin value &gt; 14.0 g/dL during the first 4 months of the study than in the reference group.
- These results indicate that the dosing regimen (MirCera 0.6 ug/kg/2-wk SC) was effective as tested. Subgroup analysis by age, weight, gender, race, ethnicity, geographic region, CRF etiology, and diabetes revealed no significant difference in hemoglobin response.

L226: ## Primary Efficacy Results
L227: The primary objective of BA16739, BA16740, BA17283, and BA17284 was to demonstrate that MirCera administered IV or SC would maintain hemoglobins in dialysis patients on prior IV or SC erythropoiesis stimulating agents. The primary efficacy parameter was the change in hemoglobins between baseline and the evaluation period. The analysis was performed on the PP population (Table 39).
L228: - The change in hemoglobin between baseline and the evaluation period was comparable between the MirCera treatment group(s) and the reference group within each of the four studies, and most were close to zero (no change). The largest changes from baseline occurred in the MirCera Q4W group of BA16740 (-0.21 g/dL) and the MirCera Q2W group of BA17284 (0.25g/dL). Both were within the range of expected variation over time in this patient population.
- Differences in MirCera dosing regimen had no discernible effect on changes in mean hemoglobins from baseline to the evaluation period. In studies with a Q4W MirCera treatment group (BA16739 and BA16740), changes in hemoglobins were similar to changes in both the MirCera Q2W group and the reference groups.
- In BA16739 and BA17283 MirCera was administered intravenously. In BA16740 MirCera was administered subcutaneously, and in BA16284 MirCera was administered IV or SC using PFS. Within each study, the route of administration did not affect the comparability of MirCera to the reference medications in maintaining hemoglobins. This is discussed in more detail in the analyses of subpopulations. BA16739, BA16740 and BA17284 used epoetin (alfa or beta) as the reference medication.
- Study BA17283 used darbepoetin alfa as the reference medication. In each study, median changes from baseline in mean hemoglobins were comparable between the MirCera group(s) and the reference group, indicating that MirCera adequately maintained hemoglobins in patients regardless of their previous medication: epoetin alfa, epoetin beta, or darbepoetin alfa.

L232: Non-inferiority testing was performed on the change from baseline in mean hemoglobin, comparing the Mircera groups to the reference groups. L238: ## Secondary Efficacy Results
L239: The secondary efficacy endpoints for the phase 3 hemoglobin maintenance studies were the number of patients maintaining average hemoglobins during the evaluation period within \pm 1 g/dL of their average baseline hemoglobin, and the incidence of RBC transfusions during the dose titration and evaluation periods.
L240: ## *Number of Patients Maintaining Hemoglobin*
L241: The majority of patients in each of the treatment groups (MirCera or reference) maintained average hemoglobins during the evaluation period within \pm 1 g/dL of their average baseline hemoglobins (66% to 76%). A summary of patients maintaining average hemoglobin during the evaluation period within \pm 1 g/dL of their average baseline hemoglobin is provided for the ITT population in **Table 41**.
L242: In the ITT population, the percentages of patients who maintained hemoglobins within this range were generally similar between the MirCera treatment group(s) and the epoetin or darbepoetin alfa reference groups in each study. The largest between-treatment differences were seen in BA16740 and BA17283. The percentages of patients who maintained average hemoglobin within \pm 1 g/dL of their average baseline hemoglobins in each treatment group of BA17284 and BA16739 ranged from 67% to 69%. The different MirCera treatment regimens (Q2W or Q4W) were comparable to each other and to the reference group in BA16739. In BA16740, the Q4W group was not comparable to the reference group. The percentages of patients with hemoglobin increases &gt; 1 g/dL were similar among treatment groups within each study, with the exception of BA17283, in which 20% of patients in the MirCera group compared with 8% in the reference group had hemoglobin increases greater than 1 g/dL. In all studies, the proportions of patients with hemoglobin decreases over 1 g/dL were similar between the MirCera and reference groups. L245: ## RBC Transfusions
L246: The percentage of patients requiring blood transfusions during the titration and evaluation periods was similar between treatment groups within each of the hemoglobin maintenance studies. Blood transfusion results in the ITT population and in the eligible population were similar to those in the safety population. Median changes from baseline in mean hemoglobin (ITT population) were close to zero (no change) in the MirCera treatment group(s) and the reference group of each study during the evaluation period. L295: ## Phase 3 Studies
L296: In each of the phase 3 studies, crude mortality rates in the Mircera and reference groups were generally similar (Table 50). With Mircera, the mortality rates ranged from 3.6% to 10.0%, a range that appears to be significantly greater than the rate seen with the reference agents (6.0% to 8.6%). The highest mortality rate of 10% was seen with Mircera SC Q2W for anemia correction in non-dialysis patients. The lowest mortality rate of 3.6% was seen with Mircera IV or SC using prefilled syringes for hemoglobin maintenance in dialysis patients. The magnitude of the difference in mortality rate was highest in favor of Mircera (-2.9%) in BA16736 (study medication administered IV), and highest in favor of the reference agents (1.9%) in BA16740 (study medication administered SC).
L297: It remains possible that, for Mircera, IV administration may be safer than SC administration. Although the PK data indicate that the half-life of Mircera is not appreciably dependent on the route of administration, the PD effect may be greater with SC than with IV administration. The greater PD effect may in turn have a significant effect on treatment-related mortality, if: (1) raising the hemoglobin can be associated with treatment-related mortality, and (2) SC Mircera raises the hemoglobin more effectively than IV Mircera when given according to the same dose administration guidelines.

L302: ## Review Comments:
L303: - It is unexpected that the highest mortality rate was seen in the only phase 3 study involving non-dialysis patients (BA16738); mortality rates that appear to be significantly lower were seen in all other phase 3 studies involving dialysis patients (higher risk group relative to non-dialysis patients). It remains unclear whether SC use of Mircera for anemia correction is associated with a greater risk of death than with other combinations of administration route (IV or SC) and previous therapy (anemia correction or hemoglobin maintenance).
- This observation is consistent with the hypothesis that greater treatment risks may be associated with achieving and sustaining higher target hemoglobin levels. Although protocol-specified target hemoglobin levels do not appear to be appreciably different among the six phase 3 studies, an analysis that adjusts for the hemoglobin level over time (i.e., determination of AUC for the hemoglobin response curve) may show that Mircera therapy in BA16738 (anemia correction in non-dialysis patients using SC Mircera dosing) sustained the highest hemoglobin level over time.
- Controlled comparisons within each study and uncontrolled comparisons across the controlled studies suggest that higher doses, SC route, and anemia correction may be associated with higher mortality rates than lower doses, IV route, and hemoglobin maintenance:In BA16736, the mortality rate with Mircera was lower than that with the reference agent when a Mircera dose of 0.4 ug/kg was used IV.
- In BA16738, the mortality rate with Mircera was higher than that with the reference agent when a Mircera dose of 0.6 ug/kg was used SC.
- In BA16739, using Mircera at fixed doses Q2W or Q4W IV resulted in a mortality rate comparable to that seen with the reference agent.
- In BA16740, using Mircera at fixed doses Q2W or Q4W SC resulted in a mortality rate higher than seen with the reference agent. Although the sponsor claims that Mircera IV and Mircera SC have comparable PK and PD profiles, IV and SC routes may be significantly different, at least with respect to its potential association with mortality and other adverse events. Currently approved erythropoietin products (epoetin alfa, epoetin beta, and darbepoetin alfa) exhibit more prolonged PK profiles and PD responses with SC than with IV dosing, and Mircera may not be an exception.
L304: - In BA17284, using MirCera in pre-filled syringes resulted in a mortality rate lower than that seen with the reference agent. The pre-filled syringes tended to deliver conservative doses of MirCera, in comparison with rigorous weight-based dosing.
- To explore a potential relationship between administration route and mortality, the sponsor should present IV and SC data separately in BA17284. Similarly, separate data analyses for Q2W and Q4W dosing schedules in studies BA16739 and BA16740 will be helpful in confirming the lack of a potential schedule effect.
- A comparison of pooled data (all studies) does not suggest an appreciable difference in the mortality rate between MirCera and the reference agents (7.3% with MirCera vs 7.1% with reference agents).Although the sponsor claims that safety of MirCera is not different from that of the reference agents based on analyses of pooled (and therefore uncontrolled) data, analyses of controlled (individual studies) data may reveal safety differences. The safety differences are likely to be related to the degree of induction of erythropoietic (or other) activity (dose, route, and target hemoglobin), rather than to an effect intrinsic to MirCera.
- These observations support approval of MirCera with the product label specifying a more conservative dosing in anemia correction (perhaps 0.6 \text{ ug/kg}, instead of 0.6 \text{ ug/kg} as proposed) and in hemoglobin maintenance (hemoglobin target lower than 12.0 \text{ g/dL} as specified in labeling of approved erythropoietin products). A hemoglobin target of 12.0 \text{ g/dL} may be more appropriate than 12.0 \text{ g/dL}, since 12.0 \text{ g/dL} was tested in the CHOIR study whereas as 12.0 \text{ g/dL} is an arbitrary selection not yet tested in a clinical study.

L316: Although the rates of overall and cardiac mortality do not appear to be significantly different between MirCera and reference groups, the data suggest a trend for higher mortality rates with MirCera, as well as a statistically significant imbalance in sudden deaths, or at least in the use of the term "sudden death" in reporting deaths. Five of seven phase 3 cases had a hemoglobin level that exceeded 12.0 g/dL within one week of sudden death. Although the number of patients is small, these observations raise the following considerations in evaluating the safety of erythropoietin therapy in renal anemia:
L318: - At hemoglobin levels above baseline, higher hemoglobin levels are less safe than lower levels.
- Patients with more severe CRF are at greater risk than those with less severe CRF.
- For an erythropoietin product, a longer circulating half-life may be less safe than a shorter one.
L319: Although anemia correction is an unstable situation than hemoglobin maintenance (hemoglobin level rises over time), the relatively low hemoglobin level at initiation of anemia correction may serve as a "safety buffer" before the hemoglobin level rises to levels that place the patient at significant risk for sudden deaths. Mircera may be less safe than the reference erythropoietin products, not because of an intrinsic difference in product safety, but because of its prolonged circulating half-life places the patient at an increased risk of exceeding the "safe" hemoglobin level, particularly with SC dosing.
L320: A closer examination of the Mircera regimens used (dose, interval, route), the baseline disease status (severity of CRF, risk factors for sudden deaths, baseline hemoglobin), and the treatment duration may be helpful in investigating these concerns.

L403: ## Summary of Review Comments (All Narratives and Table 51):
L404: The 8 narratives and Table 51 above suggest that the risk of sudden death may be increased when the following factors are present in treating renal anemia using an erythropoietin product:
L405: - Hemoglobin maintenance therapy (as opposed to anemia correction)
- Excessive erythropoietin treatment, as evidenced by:Brisk hemoglobin response that results in hemoglobin overshoot
- Difficulty in increasing the hemoglobin level to a desired level
L406: - Hemodialysis
- Infection
- Uncontrolled hypertension
- Underlying conditions that increase thrombotic risk, including cancer
L407: A slightly increased risk of coronary thrombosis, acute myocardial infarction, malignant cardiac arrhythmias, and sudden death may be expected with hemodialysis, infection, uncontrolled hypertension, or underlying conditions that predispose to thrombotic risk, including cancer. In the setting of erythropoietin therapy for renal anemia, these conditions are routinely encountered, and excessive erythropoietin therapy appears to increase the baseline risk. The increase in risk appears to be greater in hemoglobin maintenance than in anemia correction; in anemia correction, the baseline anemia may serve as a "safety buffer" against excessive treatment. The use of long-acting preparations (such as Mircera), particularly when administered at infrequent intervals (such as Q4W) using high doses and by SC injection (prolonged effect, in comparison with IV route) may magnify the risk of excessive treatment.
L408: An imbalance in the incidence of sudden deaths was observed between Mircera and reference treatments: 7 cases with Mircera versus none with reference agents in controlled phase 3 studies using 1:1 or 2:1 Mircera-to-reference agent allocation. The observed imbalance may reflect a significantly greater than expected increase in the risk of sudden death with the use of Mircera, a long-acting erythropoietin product, than with the use of shorter-acting reference agents.
L409: The magnitude of the increase in risk appears to be greatest in hemoglobin maintenance therapy of renal anemia requiring hemodialysis, a setting in which patients with a relatively high treated "baseline" hemoglobin (near 12.0 g/dL) routinely experience dialysis-induced hematologic activation (complement system, platelets, other), sudden hemoconcentration, and potential electrolyte imbalance. The increase in the risk of sudden death may be magnified further in the presence of inflammation or infection. The increase in risk may be greater in hemoglobin maintenance than in anemia correction. These risk considerations may be applicable to serious adverse events other than sudden deaths.
L410: Current erythropoietin product labels may not be adequate in assuring safety of erythropoietin therapy. In particular, the extent to which the hemoglobin may be safely increased above baseline likely differs with each patient (based on underlying disease and conditions). The current erythropoietin product labels specify 12.0 g/dL as the maximum hemoglobin. This recommendation is undesirable, since it implies that a hemoglobin level of 12.0 g/dL is based on clinical data and is applicable equally to all patients. Further, the value of 12.0 g/dL is often regarded in the clinical community as the desirable target rather than a maximum permissible hemoglobin level. The labeling for Mircera may be acceptable if:
L411: - The label states that the risk of serious adverse events including myocardial infarction, stroke, and sudden death have been shown to increased at target hemoglobin levels above 13.5 g/dL.

L420: A comparison of crude mortality rates shown in Table 46 and in Table 52 shows an increase in mortality rates, as might be expected from extended reporting (from November 2005 through March 2006, 2.2% higher for Mircera and 3.1% higher for the reference agents). In interpreting the adjudicated "leveling" of the incidence of sudden deaths, it is important to note the differences between CMAC and the clinical investigators in the criteria for selecting the term "sudden death."
L421: - CMAC: (all of the following)sudden or unexplained death
- no documentation of myocardial infarction or stroke
- not observed to have had an arrhythmia
- no indication of congestive heart failure
- Clinical investigators: (unspecified but probable criteria; both of the following)unexpected death
- unexplained death
L422: Although the clinical investigators did not use pre-specified criteria in selecting the term "sudden death," the term was likely intended to indicate that the deaths were unexpected AND unexplained (occurred outside the hospital setting without medical evaluation near time of death). The adjudicated selection of the term "sudden death" was based on criteria that were independent of the "criteria" used by the clinical investigators, and the adjudicated and reported incidences of sudden death describe two independent clinical situations. The adjudication process, therefore, cannot explain or discredit the initial imbalance in sudden deaths as reported by the clinical
L423: investigators; in the absence of a clear definition of "sudden death," the clinical investigators reported 8 "sudden deaths" with Mircera versus none with the reference agents, for whatever reason, in the controlled phase 3 studies. The imbalance in investigator-reported sudden deaths remains an important safety concern, and in the absence of a clear explanation, new clinical data may be needed to resolve this concern.

L482: ## *Deaths in Relation to Absolute Hemoglobin Level*
L483: Overall there was a similar trend seen in both treatment groups for a higher percentage of deaths in the two lowest hemoglobin quintiles. By quintile, the percentages of deaths in each of the five increasing hemoglobin quintiles were (%): 29, 24, 13, 12, and 23 percent in the Mircera group and 41, 24, 16, 10, and 9 percent in the reference group (quintiles of &lt; 10.6, 10.6 to &lt; 11.4, 11.4 to &lt; 12, 12 to &lt; 12.7, and &gt; 12.7 g/dL of hemoglobin, respectively). Using the same criteria used for AE, none of the deaths were associated with a particular hemoglobin level in both the Mircera or reference groups.
L484: The results of the analysis of AE leading to death by quintiles is generally confirmed in the analysis by hemoglobin categories, but 23% of deaths occurred in the highest hemoglobin quintile in the Mircera group. A total of 15 of 126 AE leading to death (11.9%) occurred in association with hemoglobin values &gt; 13 g/dL, which is lower than the prevalence of hemoglobin values &gt; 13 g/dL (17.8%) in the population. In the &gt; 14.0 g/dL category, there was a total of 6 of 126 AE leading to death (4.7%), which was slightly higher than the prevalence of hemoglobin values &gt; 14.0 g/dL in the population (3.7%). However, these findings are based on very small numbers.
