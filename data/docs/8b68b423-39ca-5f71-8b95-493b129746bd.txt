L58: 1 Executive Summary
L59: 1.1. Product Introduction
L60: Sevabertinib (HYRNUO) is a small molecule kinase inhibitor of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2, also known as Erb-B2 receptor tyrosine kinase 2 [ERBB2]), including wild-type HER2 and HER2 activating mutations. Sevabertinib is not currently approved for marketing in any country.
L61: The indication proposed by the Applicant is for the treatment of adult patients with advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) whose tumors have activating HER 2 (ERBB2) mutations, as detected by an FDA-approved test, and who have received a prior systemic therapy.
L62: The recommended dosage regimen for sevabertinib is 20 mg taken orally twice daily (BID) with food until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.
L63: 1.2. Conclusions on the Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness
L64: Substantial Evidence of Effectiveness (SEE) was established with one adequate and well-controlled clinical investigation and confirmatory evidence.
L65: The submitted data provide substantial evidence of effectiveness to support the accelerated approval of sevabertinib for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous NSCLC whose tumors have HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) activating mutations, as detected by an FDA-approved test, and who have received prior systemic therapy.
L66: The recommendation for accelerated approval is primarily supported by results from Study 21607 (SOHO-01), a non-randomized, open-label, single arm, multi-center, multi-cohort trial. The primary efficacy populations considered by FDA comprise 122 patients with locally advanced or metastatic, non-squamous NSCLC with HER2 (ERBB2) TKD mutations who had received at least one line of prior systemic therapy and received sevabertinib 20 mg orally BID in SOHO-01. This primary efficacy population was comprised of two groups: patients naïve to anti-HER2 therapy (Group D) and patients that had progressed on prior anti-HER2 directed therapy (Group E). Across the two groups, most patients received prior platinum-based chemotherapy (94% and 77% in Groups D and E, respectively), immunotherapy (71% and 56% in Groups D and E, respectively), or platinum-based chemotherapy in combination with immunotherapy (69% and 56% in Groups D and E, respectively). In Group D (n=70), the objective response rate (ORR) per blinded independent central review (BICR) per Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1 was 71% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 59, 82), and the median duration of response (DOR) was 9.2 months (95% CI: 6.3, 15.0), with 54% of responders having an observed DOR \geq 6 months and 18% of responders having an observed DOR \geq 12 months. L71: The FDA’s Assessment:
L72: FDA’s analysis identified 6% of patients in the Group D +E safety population with an event of cardiac arrhythmia. L91: 8.2.4.1. Gastrointestinal toxicities
L92: Gastrointestinal toxicity, especially diarrhea is the most frequently reported and well described toxicity with HER2/EGFR TKIs, with incidence rates varying depending on the agent used and dosage, and occur as frequent as in &gt;95\% of patients (42). Diarrhea is caused by intestinal epithelial cell damage and inflammation, and HER2 inhibition leads to excessive chloride translocation into the intestinal lumen, resulting in secretory diarrhea (43, 44).
L93: In Study 21607, TEAEs within the SOC of gastrointestinal disorders were the most commonly reported events across the safety populations, with 88.9% of participants in Group D and 90.7% of participants in the Whole Study 20 mg BID population. L168: FDA reviewed narratives of events of arrhythmia. All cases were confounded by at least one factor contributing to arrhythmia. The case of AV block occurred in a patient with prior history of mediastinal irradiation for Hodgkin lymphoma. Other cases were confounded by concomitant medication use (e.g., loperamide) and diarrhea with resulting electrolyte derangement. Notably the label for loperamide carries a black box warning for Torsades de Pointes and sudden death with higher than recommended doses of loperamide; cases of syncope and ventricular tachycardia have also been reported in adult patients receiving the recommended dosage of loperamide.
L169: Cardiac event narratives were reviewed with one non-arrhythmia case described below.
L170: • Patient ID (b) (6): This is a case of cardio-pulmonary arrest that occurred 45 days after starting treatment resulting in a Grade 5 toxicity. This occurred in a patient in Group F included in the pooled 20mg population but not in the combined Group D + E population. Sinus tachycardia was noted along with left anterior fascicular block and U wave changes. L172: Investigators attributed three Grade 3-4 cardiac arrhythmias to study drug (AV block complete, arrhythmia, and sinus tachycardia) and in all cases the Applicant disagreed with that attribution.

Both show a more gradual increase in probability towards the upper/flat part of exposure-response relationship for exposures above versus below the median exposure after 20 mg BID. For exposures below the median, the probability decreases more rapidly with decreasing exposure.
L329: For probability of diarrhea Grade \geq 3 and dose modifications due to TEAEs, the exposure range reached for a dose range of 10–20 mg BID was in the steep phase of their exposure-response relationship, showing a more or less linear increase in probability with increasing exposure. This increase was steeper for diarrhea Grade \geq 3 compared to dose modifications due to TEAEs. As a result, the decrease in probability for diarrhea Grade \geq 3 and dose modifications due to TEAEs at exposures below the median exposure after 20 mg BID is as strong as the increase in probability at exposures above this median.
L330: The shapes of the exposure-response for ORR and BTR are similar to those for all grade diarrhea and all grade rash. At exposures above the median exposure after 20 mg BID, the upper/flat part of the exposure-response relationship was reached, whereas ORR and BTR rapidly decreased at exposures below this median. These similar shapes for safety and efficacy indicate that the therapeutic window is small, i.e. a lower probability for safety events will coincide with lower efficacy and vice versa.
L331: Within the exposure range between the median exposure after a dose of 10 and 20 mg BID respectively, the exposure response relationships for efficacy (ORR) and diarrhea Grade \geq 3 were separated with (relative) ORR being above the diarrhea Grade \geq 3 curve. This indicates that the relative decrease in probability of diarrhea Grade \geq 3 was larger compared to the relative decrease in ORR with a 50% reduction in exposure by lowering the dose from 20 to 10 mg BID.
L332: Based on the integrative analyses including PK-PD analyses/modeling, the clinical efficacy and safety data presented, and exposure-response evaluations, a dose of 20 mg BID was selected as
L333: the optimal dosage. While exposure of doses &gt;20 mg BID was in the upper/flat part of the exposure-response curve for ORR and maximal change in tumor size, more participants are expected to reach &gt;80% reduction in pEGFR over 24 hours with doses &gt;20 mg BID and patients might further benefit from these higher doses. On the other hand, exposure of doses &gt;20 mg BID showed a steep increase in incidence of diarrhea Grade \geq 3 and TEAEs leading to dose modifications, which may limit the clinical application of these higher doses. While exposure of doses &lt;20 mg BID showed a decrease in AE incidence, as exposure was in the steep phase of the exposure-response relationship, exposure was also in the steep phase of the exposure-response for efficacy suggesting lower probability of efficacy (ORR and maximal change in tumor size) compared to the 20 mg BID dose.
L334: The FDA's Assessment:
L335: The proposed recommended dosage of 20 mg BID is supported by the clinical efficacy and safety, numerically higher ORR compared to 10 mg BID, positive trends observed in exposure-efficacy and exposure-safety, and PK/PD analysis
